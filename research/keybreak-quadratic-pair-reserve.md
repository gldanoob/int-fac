# An ordinary and shared-root algebraic pair bound at quadratic width two

For two distinct primitive fundamental negative quadratic marks of height \(V(\log V)^{O(1)}\) as in the one-mark construction, one can bound the algebraic pair moment by a polynomial times the product of the one-mark references. The Gaussian coefficient width for this argument is \(\sigma\ge V^{2+\xi}\) with a fixed \(\xi>0\). The argument also applies after imposing one root shared by both marks. It leaves a factor of fixed-power size \(K=V^L\) available in each value after choosing its main prime-bin factors. Its size is sufficient to count lattice points in every pair progression directly. This is the algebraic part of the wider quadratic proposal. No quadratic rational second moment or narrow-width pair estimate follows. ## A countable predicate and common reference

For one mark of positive leading coefficient \(a\) write \(I=(a,\omega)\), \(z=a^d F(\theta)\), and \(X=a^d M^2\sigma^2\). Use roots in a fixed disk \(|\theta|\le s<1\) with \(\Im\theta\gg(\log V)^{-C}\) and \(|Q\theta-r|\) between \(M/(\log V)^C\) and \(M(\log V)^C\). These bounds permit a polylogarithmic rather than constant condition number for a single mark. Use a core product of one degree-one prime ideal per chosen bin with fixed orientation and distinct rational primes, exactly as in the one-mark construction. Its norm is in \([x,\sqrt2x]\), \(x=X/K\). Define
\[
 U_h=\sum_{\mathfrak a\ \mathrm{core}}1/N\mathfrak a.
\]
It has the ordinary leading density, as \(\log K=O(\log V)\) removes only a bounded number from the smoothness argument. The test requires that at least one such core divide the relative ideal \((z)I^{-d}\). It can also require the full norm bound, the smoothness or semismoothness cutoff below and the unique-good-root condition of the one-mark argument. Dropping these extra checks gives the upper bound. For inclusion in an algorithm the core's fixed orientations and divisibilities can be tested after numerical factorization, by ideal arithmetic in the known quadratic field. The total degree and all numerical bit lengths are polynomial in the input length. On the disk or the polynomial Gaussian cutoff, the residual norm is \(V^{O_L(1)}\), so only a bounded number of primes in the core size range can be put in the residual part. Thus at most polynomially many core subsets need testing once the intended prime factors are available.

Within a fixed band one can keep the number of factors, family of bin index sets and their index sum fixed from a reference scale. Shift all bin centers by \(\log(x/x_{\rm ref})/k\) for the actual mark. Across a polylogarithmic range of coefficients this shift is \(O(\log\log V)\), far smaller than the available headroom in \(\log y\). The harmonic split-prime sum per bin agrees, by Dirichlet GRH, with half its prime integral to relative \(1+V^{-c_1}\) for some fixed \(c_1>0\). Changing its log-center has relative effect \(1+O(\log\log V/\log V)\) on that integral. The products over \(k\asymp d\) bins, and hence \(U_h\), are comparable uniformly since \(d\log\log V=o(\log V)\). No selection by prime-count rank in a bin is needed.

For an ordinary mark the actual one-mark success probability averaged over the multiplier is \(\gg U_h/(\log V)^{O_L(1)}\). To see this keep each core and fill the reserved size using a fixed number of further split primes below \(y=V^{c_0+o(1)}\), one split guard of size comparable to \(V\), and a coprime primitive correcting ideal of norm \(\le V(\log V)^C\). For large fixed \(L>2\), choose the further prime scales as fixed positive powers below \(y\) whose product has order \(K/(V^2(\log V)^{C'})\), with \(C'\) large enough for the correcting bound and the one-mark disk loss. This can use any fixed number greater than \((L-2)/c_0\), with small disjoint dyadic intervals and, if needed, a logarithmic reduction in one scale. The number of choices for them is their product size divided by a power of \(\log V\). Require distinct primes, exclude core primes and all prime divisors of \(2h(r,Q)\). For the cores themselves this exclusion loses a vanishing relative fraction, uniformly, because only a bounded number of bin primes divide that integer. The guard removes classes with unbalanced representative as in the class-count lemma, and the correction avoids every earlier prime. Thus every core has at least \((K/V)/(\log V)^{O_L(1)}\) completions in the required class at norm within the inner one-mark disk. A final ideal has only a bounded number of eligible primes in addition to its prescribed core slots, and their choices, the extra slots and guard can be recovered with polynomial multiplicity. The one-mark phase and Gaussian lower bound applies with at most a polynomial-logarithmic loss in the size of its inner disk and its mass. This proves the stated comparison. When \(c_0<1\) the guard and correction permit a bounded number of factors above \(y\), all at most \(V(\log V)^{O(1)}\); when \(y\ge V^{1+\epsilon}\) the full norm is smooth.

## Cartesian periods

Write \(H_i=h_i(r,Q)\), \(D_{12}=|\operatorname{Res}(h_1,h_2)|>0\), and \(\gamma_i=Q\omega_i-a_i r\). If \(d\ge4\), the joint image at fixed parity and anchor splits into complete cosets of the Cartesian lattice \(m I_1^d\oplus m I_2^d\) for \(m=2D_{12}\operatorname{lcm}(H_1,H_2)\). Indeed the degree-three map \((U,W)\mapsto h_1U+h_2W\), on pairs of linear binary forms, has Sylvester determinant of absolute value \(D_{12}\). Its adjugate supplies every degree-three form multiplied by \(D_{12}\). Multiplication by monomials gives the same containment in every degree \(n\ge3\). Lifting an arbitrary value in \(I_i^n\) to a form and decomposing its multiple this way gives that value times \(D_{12}\) in coordinate \(i\) with zero in the other. Apply this with \(n=d-1\), then multiply by the fixed parity and anchor difference \(2(QX-rY)\). The one-mark index identity gives \(H_i I_i^d\subset\gamma_i I_i^{d-1}\), proving the assertion. For a nonempty additional affine coefficient congruence modulo a prime \(q\) the raw period \(qm\) always suffices; in this setting \(q=V^{O(1)}\). The period bound is \(V^{O(1)}\) with constants fixed per band. [Homogeneous Sylvester determinant](https://math.berkeley.edu/~bernd/sparse_elimination.pdf)

## Counting cores in a phase

In a specified phase and coordinate, discard from each core every prime ideal above a rational prime dividing the scalar period (including the extra \(q\) when present). Call the remaining ideal \(\mathfrak a'\). Then the divisibility condition that remains is either empty or a coset of \(m I^d\mathfrak a'\) in the coordinate, using \(m\) to denote the actual scalar period. It has index \(N\mathfrak a'\) in the phase period. This follows by coprimality of \(m\mathcal O\) and \(\mathfrak a'\). Uniformly for these phases,
\[
 \sum_{\mathfrak a\ \mathrm{core}}\frac1{N\mathfrak a'}
 \le (\log V)^{O(1)}U_h .
\]
In fact \(m\) has at most \(O(1)\) rational divisors in the prime bin size range. Under the harmonic weights of each fixed pattern, the probability of a specified prime in its slot is at most \(O(d\log V/p)\), by the bin prime lower bound. The slots are independent inside that pattern; boundedly many exceptional primes in the same slot can be summed there. Restoring their norm factors in the weighted expectation costs at most a power of \(\log V\). This bound includes a prescribed incoming core prime; its occurrence is not discarded as an unlikely event after conditioning.

Scale coordinate \(i\) by \(a_i^{-d/2}\). The covolume of the thinned coordinate lattice is \(m^2 N\mathfrak a'\sqrt{|D_i|}/2\), with \(D_i\) the quadratic field discriminant. Its shortest length is at least \(m\sqrt{N\mathfrak a'}\) by integrality of the relative element norm. A reduced planar basis then has lengths \(\ll mV\sqrt{N\mathfrak a'}\), including when \(\mathfrak a'\) is skew. Relative to the coordinate scale \(\sqrt X\), this is \(O(mV/\sqrt K)\) since \(N\mathfrak a'\le\sqrt2 X/K\).

In the four-dimensional pair Gaussian, after additionally standardizing the coordinates by their respective \(\sqrt{X_i}\), the smallest standard deviation is at least \(V^{-C_6}\), uniformly over all *distinct* marks in this box. Indeed the four powers of degrees zero through three give a Vandermonde minor at the two roots and their conjugates. Each imaginary part is at least an inverse polylogarithm. The resultant formula and the bound on the leading coefficients give \(|\theta_1-\theta_2|\gg V^{-2-o(1)}\); the cross-conjugate differences are also bounded below by the imaginary parts. All entries are bounded, and multiplication by the individual anchor factors costs at most further polynomial logarithms under the stated hypotheses. These give a fixed power lower bound for the least singular value. Higher powers only increase the Gram matrix.

Choose \(L\) large enough for \(mV^{1+C_6}/\sqrt K\le V^{-c_2}\) with fixed \(c_2>0\), at every one of the finitely many bands. In Mahalanobis units the thinned product lattice has a basis of length \(O(V^{-c_2})\), as does the original phase lattice. Gaussian sums on all their translates are therefore their continuous integrals divided by their covolumes to relative \(1+o(1)\), by the fixed-dimensional dual Gaussian estimate. The conditional Gaussian mass in that phase passing the two remaining core divisibilities is at most \((1+o(1))/(N\mathfrak a'_1 N\mathfrak a'_2)\). Summing over the cores bounds the two-mark test by \((\log V)^{O_L(1)} U_{h_1}U_{h_2}\). This upper bound holds in every phase; no independence of the two phase residues or uniform distribution of class-group characters is used.

At the stated wide coefficient width the actual pair law has the same upper bound. The coefficient kernel for the two exact evaluations of \(S\) is generated by monomial multiples of \(h_1h_2\), of lengths \(V^{2+o(1)}\), by primitive divisibility. Its dual Gaussian tail is negligible by the basis and packing argument of the one-mark proof. When the imposed root \(s\bmod q\) is shared by both marks, this kernel vanishes at that root. The condition restricts the image without increasing the kernel smoothing length; one uses the restricted image for normalization. This gives the same pair upper for fixed multiplier and admissible root. Equal marks belong to the diagonal and require a separate population count in an application.

The proof establishes only the algebraic pair statement at \(\sigma\ge V^{2+\xi}\). A rational two-mark estimate for the common physical anchor, the modified child cutoffs and incidence normalization, and a narrow coefficient comparison would all be needed to improve the incumbent bound.

## Consequence for the common-anchor experiment

In the fixed bands with \(\log y/\log V>9/10\) by a fixed margin, the same comparison applies with every algebraic norm factor at most \(y\). Use the two smooth ideal factors in the complementary class after selecting the core and intermediate split primes. The upper bound still drops the conditions on the remainder. [Fully smooth completion](keybreak-quadratic-class-correction.md#fully-smooth-correction-above-the-nine-tenths-threshold)

For a parent in the rational first-moment conclusion write \(H=d\log d\), \(R_0=V^3/P\) and \(\rho_R=\exp(-u_R\log u_R)\). At width \(\sigma\ge V^{2+\xi}\), a fraction at least \(\rho_R\exp(o(H))\) of the joint anchor, multiplier and Gaussian draws have at least \(R_0\rho_R U\exp(o(H))\) marks with **both** predicates passing, where \(U\) is any common band reference for the algebraic core weights. Indeed the raw pair bound permits trimming the exceptional roots at a power-saving cost in the first moment. The resulting deterministic cap \(R_0(\log V)^{O(1)}\) and the preserved smooth mean imply that a fraction at least \(\rho_R\exp(o(H))\) of anchors have \(m\ge R_0\rho_R\exp(o(H))\) rationally suitable marks in the shape-restricted first moment. For each such anchor the expected number \(W\) of joint successes is at least \(mU/(\log V)^{O(1)}\) by the multiplier-averaged one-mark bound. The wide algebraic pair upper gives \(\mathbb E W^2\ll m+m^2 U^2(\log V)^{O(1)}\); even the first term is negligible since \(m=V^{\Omega(1)}\) and \(U=V^{-o(1)}\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives probability at least an inverse power of \(\log V\) of achieving half that conditional mean. Combining the two probabilities proves the assertion. On the prime-discriminant families and shape ranges of the averaged rational variance estimate, replace the first anchor probability by \(\exp[-(\log U_R-\log T)\log d/\log B+o(H)]\). Here \(U_R\) is the size of the odd rational cofactor, \(B\) its prime cutoff, and \(T=(U_R/V)^{(1-\epsilon)/2}\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\) satisfying the tabulated overlap margin. Intersect the two typical-parent sets and apply the same conditional algebraic moment to the resulting anchors. Since the conditional factor costs only powers of \(\log V\), this is also the improved lower bound on the fraction of joint draws. An inverse-polynomial fraction at the original cutoff remains open. ## A necessary width for the full two-value Gaussian approximation

Consider the polynomial coefficient vector before any extra root congruence. Let \(H\) be a primitive integral polynomial of fixed degree \(r\), with leading coefficient of absolute value \(B\), and let \(n>r\) be the number of coefficient slots. For two distinct irreducible quadratic marks, take their primitive product, so \(r=4\) and \(B=|a_1a_2|\). Simultaneous exact evaluation has the same fibers as orthogonal projection \(\pi\) off the real space of multiples of \(H\). In the Euclidean metric of this projection the two laws in question are the actual image of \(D_{\mathbb Z^n,\sigma,c}\) and the Gaussian \(D_{L,\sigma,\pi c}\) on \(L=\pi\mathbb Z^n\). The Gaussian weight convention is \(\exp(-\pi\|x-c\|^2/\sigma^2)\). An invertible linear change of the output metric preserves the comparison.

For \(\sigma\ge1\) and \(R\) above an absolute constant, their overlap obeys
\[
 1-d_{\rm TV}\ \le\ e^{-c_1 nR^2}+(C R)^n(\sigma/B)^{n-r}.
\]
Constants are uniform in the shift. In particular, if \(B=V^{2-o(1)}\), \(n\to\infty\) and \(\sigma\le V^{2-\xi}\) for fixed \(\xi>0\), the overlap is \(V^{-\Omega(\xi n)}\). Take \(R=V^{\xi/4}\). This rules out a full-image Gaussian approximation at those widths. A predicate on the two values can still have much closer probabilities under the two laws.

Indeed the integral kernel \(K\) has basis \(H,XH,\ldots,X^{n-r-1}H\) by Gauss's lemma. Its top-degree square minor is triangular with absolute determinant \(B^{n-r}\), so Cauchy–Binet gives \(\det K\ge B^{n-r}\), writing \(\det\) for Euclidean covolume. The kernel is saturated and its basis extends to an integral basis of \(\mathbb Z^n\). Orthogonal projection of the remaining basis vectors therefore gives a basis of \(L\) with \(\det L=(\det K)^{-1}\). Further, \(L^*=\mathbb Z^n\cap K_{\mathbb R}^{\perp}\) has minimum at least one. Poisson summation and fixed-rank packing give the shifted normalizing mass \((1+O_r(e^{-c_2\sigma^2}))\sigma^r/\det L\) for large \(\sigma\). For \(1\le\sigma\le\sigma_0(r)\), use the same lower bound up to a fixed constant: \(L\) contains \(r\) independent projected unit vectors of length at most one, so has covering radius at most \(r/2\). Integrating over its Voronoi cells in a ball of fixed radius gives a uniform lower bound \(\gg_r1/\det L\). Thus the target atom cap is \(O_r(1/(\sigma^r\det K))\).

Independence gives \(\mathbb P(\|x-c\|>R\sigma\sqrt n)\le e^{-c_1 nR^2}\), since each centered squared coordinate divided by \(\sigma^2\) has a uniformly bounded exponential moment by comparison of one-dimensional Gaussian sums. Unit cubes and the ball volume bound give at most \((C\sigma R)^n\) integral points in the retained ball. Its image contains at most as many atoms. Apply the finite-set total variation inequality and the target atom cap. A fixed coefficient parity translate rescales \(\sigma\) by two.
