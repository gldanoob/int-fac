# Computing monic ideal valuations at named primes

The named-prime replay builds a local integral basis at each supplied prime, computes exact ideal powers by Hermite normal form, and obtains the valuations of the marks by integer lattice membership. The reusable GP function `named_parity(coefficients,points,named)` returns the odd rows for each mark. Its row labels are `[field,p,j]` with a separate index $j$ for each prime ideal above $p$. The library call uses the field polynomials and the supplied primes. [GP implementation and replay](../experiments/cogentic-monic-named-primes.gp)

## From the partial basis to the named prime ideals

Let $P$ be monic irreducible with root $\alpha$ and set $R=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. PARI documents that `nfbasis([P,[p]])` returns an order $C$ with $p\nmid[\mathcal O_K:C]$. It can compute additional parts of the integral basis when the discriminant is small. A conditional `nfinit([P,[p]],4)` likewise gives a $p$-maximal order, and the manual guarantees `idealprimedec` at a prime included in that list. The single-element vector `[p]` selects a specific prime. An integer supplied in place of `[p]` is instead a bound on the primes. [PARI documentation for local integral bases](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/ref/General_number_fields.html#nfbasis) · [Conditional `nfinit`](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/ref/General_number_fields.html#nfinit)

Write $[C:R]=p^v h$ with $p\nmid h$. The computation takes the $p$-primary part of the returned basis by forming $B=R+hC$ with integer lattice arithmetic. In $C/R$, multiplication by $h$ kills the prime-to-$p$ subgroup and acts invertibly on the $p$-primary subgroup. Therefore $[B:R]=p^v$, and $B$ is still $p$-maximal. This also identifies $B$ intrinsically as the preimage of the $p$-primary subgroup of $\mathcal O_K/R$.

Use the named-prime decomposition in the conditional `nf` to get the two generators of each ideal over $p$. Multiply the second generator by the prime-to-$p$ part of that order's index over $R$ and denote the result by $u$. This puts the generator in $B$ and scales its reduction by a unit in $\mathbb F_p$. All these orders have the same quotient modulo $p$ as $\mathcal O_K$. Hence the resulting ideal is exactly $\mathfrak q=pB+uB$ in $B$ and extends to a unique prime ideal in the full ring of integers. The program uses different labels even for two ideals with the same power-order residue root. [PARI prime-ideal encoding](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/ref/General_number_fields.html#idealprimedec)

## Exact local membership and cost

Let $U$ be the integer multiplication matrix of the scaled generator $u$ in the basis of $B$. Compute it by reducing products modulo $P$ and changing coordinates. If the columns of $J$ describe a lattice, the columns of the Hermite normal form of $[pJ\mid UJ]$ describe $\mathfrak q J$. Start with the identity matrix and repeat to compute the required ideal powers. The replay checks that the determinant for power $j$ is $p^{jf}$, where $f$ is the residue degree of the chosen ideal.

For $z=a-b\alpha$, its valuation is the greatest $j$ for which the coordinates of $z$ in the basis for $\mathfrak q^j$ are integral. This membership can be tested in $B$. Away from $p$, $\mathfrak q$ contains the unit $p$. At $p$, $B$ is maximal, so the ideal-power criterion agrees with the normalized valuation in $\mathcal O_K$. The largest required exponent is at most $v_p(|P_h(a,b)|)/f$. Compute the rational exponent by repeated division at the supplied prime. The implementation checks the sum of the resulting ideal exponents weighted by their residue degrees against that rational exponent. It uses HNF and exact rational coordinate solving for these membership tests.

The power-lattice operations have bit complexity polynomial in the explicit input sizes. Each $\mathfrak q^j$ lies between $p^jB$ and $B$, so its HNF entries have $O(j\log p)$ bits. The basis index bound follows from $[C:R]^2\mid\operatorname{disc}(P)$, and a basis of $C$ can be represented through its rational coordinate lattice over $R$. The deterministic theoretical construction can obtain the $p$-maximal order by Buchmann–Lenstra and split its semisimple residue algebra by scanning $\mathbb F_p$, as in the [singular-prime proof](cogentic-monic-singular-ideals.md). Those bounds apply to that construction. The finite GP replay uses PARI for the partial bases and the named-prime decompositions. It does not certify the backend's factorization behavior or its running time.

## Differential replay

Run `gp -fq cogentic-monic-named-primes.gp` from `experiments`. The library mode uses `cogentic_as_library=1` before `read`, which skips the oracle and fixture code. `named_parity` expects ascending coefficient vectors, points `[a,b]`, and a sorted list of distinct primes. All marks in the replay are nonzero as field elements.

- On the raw $N=895$ pair, the 425 eligible marks of height 20 were checked at $2,3,5,7,11,13$. The test matched 8,500 individual ideal valuations. For each ideal it extended the local lattice into the full-order lattice modulo $p$ and matched the HNF, ramification index, and residue degree. It also compared the translated parity columns with the [all-ideal reference](cogentic-all-ideal-reference.md).
- The cubic of the $895$ pair has power-order index $15$. Its isolated basis at 5 has index $5$ over the power order. At the mark $(-3,1)$ it gives distinct odd rows for the two ideals above 5 with contraction root 2.
- Three singular fixtures add 102 exact valuation checks. The quadratic $X^2+2X+10$ has one degree-two ideal at 3 with exponent one on $(-1,1)$, even though the rational norm exponent is two. The cubic $X^3+2X^2+10X+10$ includes a ramified degree-one ideal at 5. The cubic $X^3+2X^2+26X+338$ splits the mark $(0,1)$ into two exponent-one ideals above 13.
- For $P=X^2+2rq^2$ with primes $q=1000000007$ and $r=1000050017$, the full index is $q$ because $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\alpha/q]$. The installed PARI 2.19.1 returned the power basis when only 2 was named. This strict suborder still gave the single prime over 2 with ramification index two and the valuations $1,0$ for $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$. Here the full index and valuations follow directly from the quadratic field, so this control needs no full-order oracle.
