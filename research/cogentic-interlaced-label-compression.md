# Rational label compression for interlaced quadratic circuits

A short Euclidean fraction cannot represent even one mark at a root of the interlaced quadratic modulo a large modulus. This holds for every center, translation and cofactor. The same obstruction applies to bounded products of marks, except for words that are already rational in the field. In the clean six-mark construction those exceptional words are generated by three reflection pairs and the exact circuit. [Interlaced fields and four-chart fixture](cogentic-exact-local-circuits.md)

## A bound uniform over the root-center join

Let \(W>h>0\), \(A_j=a+jW\), \(B_j=A_j+h\) for \(0\le j\le2\), and let \(A(X),B(X)\) be their two monic products. Write
\[
 F(X)=\frac{A(X)-B(X)}h=3\left(X-a-W-\frac h2\right)^2+\frac{h^2}{4}-W^2.
\]
Assume \(F\) is irreducible over \(\mathbb Q\) and \(F(m)=0\bmod N\), with \(m\) integral. The two real roots lie between the successive \(A_j\), by the alternating signs of \(F(A_j)\). Thus each root is at distance less than \(3W\) from each of the six marks.

Take a signed word of mark differences \(P(X)/Q(X)\), where \(P,Q\) are products of factors \(X-A_j,X-B_j\) and \(K=\max(\deg P,\deg Q)\). Suppose its value at \(m\) agrees with a fraction \(c/d\) modulo some \(M>1\) dividing \(N\), with \(Q(m),d\) units modulo \(M\) and \(\max(|c|,|d|)\le H_0\). Put \(G=dP-cQ\). For the exact integer resultant one has \(M\mid\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\) when \(G\ne0\), by the Sylvester Bézout identity evaluated at \(m\). The product formula at the two real roots gives
\[
 \left|\operatorname{Res}(F,G)\right|
 \le 4H_0^2\,3^K(3W)^{2K}.
\]
Indeed the leading-coefficient factor is at most \(3^K\) and each root contribution has size at most \(2H_0(3W)^K\). If \(M\) exceeds the bound, then \(F\mid G\) over \(\mathbb Q\), including the case \(G=0\). Thus \(P(\theta)/Q(\theta)=c/d\) exactly. [Sylvester determinant and product formula](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-972-algebraic-techniques-and-semidefinite-optimization-spring-2006/46bf636ecc6195991e396703bfe1a1ae_lecture_06.pdf)

For one mark and for the ratio of two distinct marks, \(G\) is nonzero of degree at most one. Consequently every such rational reconstruction modulo \(N\) needs \(H_0\ge\sqrt{N/108}/W\). If only a prime factor \(p\) is used, the same bound holds with \(p\). If \(\log W,\log H_0=O((\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3})\), the entire set of such labels is empty at any prime factor of size \(N^{\Omega(1)}\). The bound permits all input-dependent choices of \(a,m\) and all integers \(F(m)/N\). For bounded \(K\), the same scales allow only exact rational words. More generally the exactness conclusion applies for \(K=o(\log N/\log W)\) at primes of size \(N^{\Omega(1)}\) when \(\log H_0=o(\log N)\).

The cofactor \(F(m)/N=0\) cannot occur for an irreducible quadratic at an integer center. If the quotient in the reconstruction equation \(d(m-A_j)-c=kN\) is zero, then the proposed representative equals the integer mark evaluation. This is also excluded by the resultant bound at the stated heights. If reducible quadratics are included, the discriminant square can instead be used to compare their two explicit linear factors modulo \(N\).

## The exact rational words in the clean family

Let \(u_j,v_j\) be the exponents at \(A_j,B_j\). Suppose each of \(W-h,W+h\) has a prime divisor coprime to \(6W\), as in the interlaced construction with clean cross differences. Put \(d_j=u_j-v_{2-j}\). Then the word evaluated at a root \(\theta\) is rational if and only if
\[
                        d_0=d_1=d_2\in 2\mathbb Z.
\]

For a prime \(\ell\nmid6W\) dividing \(W-h\), the collisions \(A_1=B_0\) and \(A_2=B_1\) modulo \(\ell\) give the two distinct simple roots of \(F\) there. At the two corresponding local embeddings, the valuations of the participating marks both equal \(v_\ell(W-h)\). To see this, the valuation of the norm of each participating mark equals \(v_\ell(W-h)\) by its explicit cross-difference product and \(\ell\ne3\). The mark is a unit under the other embedding. A rational word has equal valuations at the two embeddings, implying \(u_1+v_0=u_2+v_1\), or \(d_1=d_2\). The prime for \(W+h\) gives the collisions \(A_0=B_1\) and \(A_1=B_2\) and forces \(d_0=d_1\). [Simple-root lifting](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04GE)

Writing \(T=\theta-a-W-h/2\), the reflection pair products at \((A_j,B_{2-j})\) are the rational constants
\[
 -\frac{(W+h)(2W+h)}3,\qquad
 \frac{(W-h)(W+h)}3,\qquad
 -\frac{(W-h)(2W-h)}3 .
\]
Remove these rational factors from the word. The remaining word is \(\prod_j(\theta-A_j)^{d_j}\), hence a power of \(A(\theta)\) once the three differences agree. Direct substitution gives \(A(\theta)=2(h^2-W^2)T/3\). Its square is rational and it is nonzero and irrational, giving the parity condition. Subtracting a power of the six-mark circuit removes every even common difference. The residual exponents lie in the three reflection pairs. Thus tightly compressed whole-circuit labels yield the rational value one, and other bounded words can only reproduce these explicit rational identities.

## Two charts and the relative choice of branch

Consider a bounded word across two irreducible interlaced charts, with each chart's mark differences evaluated at its own integral root modulo \(N\). Let \(W_*\) be the larger width and take \(K\) to be the larger total degree on either side of the word. Multiply the numerator of a claimed relation to \(c/d\) by \(3^K\) after clearing the signed mark denominator. The resulting element is an algebraic integer, since three times any marked element is integral. Each conjugate has size at most \(2H_0(9W_*)^K\).

For distinct quadratic fields, the compositum has degree four and has independent choices of the two roots. Evaluation of the two integral quadratic generators defines a joint ring map after inverting three. If the relation holds modulo \(M\mid N\), its integer norm is divisible by \(M\), by multiplying all four conjugates in this localization and applying the map. Consequently if \(M>(2H_0(9W_*)^K)^4\), the norm must vanish and the relation is exact in the compositum. If a product of one field element from each field is rational, each factor is rational since the fields intersect in \(\mathbb Q\). Thus the join creates no new short labels across distinct quadratic fields.

For the same quadratic field, put \(\Delta_i=3(4W_i^2-h_i^2)\) and \(z_i=3(2(m_i-a_i-W_i)-h_i)\). Then \(z_i^2\equiv\Delta_i\pmod N\). At odd \(N\) coprime to \(6\Delta_1\Delta_2\), the two values \(z_1\) and \(z_2\sqrt{\Delta_1\Delta_2}/\Delta_2\) are explicit roots of \(\Delta_1\). The square root in the coefficient is an integer. Either the two sign gcds split \(N\), or the relative branch is uniform modulo \(N\). In the latter case there is one coherent choice of embedding across both charts. The same algebraic norm argument in degree two forces every relation under the corresponding bound to be exact in that quadratic field, with the chosen orientation. Mixed relative branches therefore already give a direct divisor certificate before any small-fraction search.

For example, use widths and shifts \((21,16)\), \((42,32)\), both with \(a=0\), at \(N=25000820006699=5000077\cdot5000087\). Their modular roots are \(22178783406807\) and \(24362233890229\), with all mark evaluations units. Their discriminants are \(4524\) and \(4\cdot4524\). The two normalized roots of \(4524\) have difference gcd \(5000077\) and sum gcd \(5000087\). Both primes exceed the single-mark bound at height five for both charts, so this mixed-branch certificate exists despite the absence of any individual rational label of that height.
