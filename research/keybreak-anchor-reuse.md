# Hidden sign rank when private anchors are reused

A known modular square multiplier lets one root serve several completed rows. Each row then uses the same hidden sign. The extra rows can change the extractor's rank through their certified public differences. They carry no additional hidden signs. For the current squarefree private shapes, known rational multipliers of height \(\exp(O(H))\) also cannot make two distinct good shapes share expensive top-prime requests with appreciable probability. These conclusions allow arbitrary choices based on the public square data within the stated size bounds. [KeyBreak, Theorem E.5 and §H.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=93) · [Private-coordinate conditions](keybreak-private-band-stages.md)

## Conditional sign law

Let \(N\) be odd, \(U=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\), and \(K=U[2]\). Write \(k=\omega(N)\), so \(K\simeq\mathbf F_2^k\) by CRT, including for powers of odd primes. Sample \(s\) independent uniform unit roots \(h_1,\ldots,h_s\), and expose their squares. Fix any positive-probability public transcript depending only on the squares and independent public randomness. Relative to any reference roots of those squares, the hidden signs are uniform in \(K^s\). This continues to hold with ordinary public shape filters and public descent failures. On nonunit draws one may take a proper gcd with \(N\) or discard the draw before applying the unit-root law.

For a completed row indexed by \(j\), suppose its root is represented as
\[
 \rho_j=m_j\prod_{i=1}^s h_i^{e_{ji}},
\]
where the exponents are integers and the unit multiplier \(m_j\) is public. The root expression can be kept as a word until extraction. Its binary label is \(e_j\bmod 2\); the public multiplier and all even powers contribute only a known factor. If the transcript also exposes the values of some root words, let \(C\) be the binary matrix of their exponents. Assume the matrices and all other transcript data are functions of the squares and independent public randomness, and that the only extra information about the roots is those word values. Relative to the reference roots, the exposed values prescribe \(Cz=c\) on \(z\in K^s\). Thus the conditional hidden fiber is an affine translate of \((\ker C)\otimes_{\mathbf F_2}K\). If no root words are exposed, use the zero-row matrix.

Writing \(E=(e_{ji}\bmod 2)\), the joint word-root signs are uniform on an affine image. Their entropy in bits is
\[
 k\,\operatorname{rank}(E|_{\ker C}).
\]
For signs modulo the global sign subgroup \(\{1,-1\}\), the entropy is \((k-1)\operatorname{rank}(E|_{\ker C})\). This follows by applying the same linear map separately in every CRT coordinate, or in a basis of the quotient. The same rule applies to any publicly selected products of rows by left-multiplying \(E\) by the selection matrix. In particular, one selection whose label is nonzero on \(\ker C\) yields, after successful exact square certification, a uniform comparison sign. It gives a proper gcd with probability \(1-2/2^k\). A family of \(r\) such certified selections with linearly independent restricted labels has probability \((2/2^k)^r\) of all failing the gcd comparison. Here all selections and their certification predicates are determined before evaluating those hidden words. Signs conditioned by other kinds of root leakage need a separate analysis.

The same conclusion is immediate for a pair of public square targets \(R,R'\) with witnessed multiplier \(m\), \(R'=m^2R\). Reusing one root via \(\rho'=m\rho\) gives a repeated label. Obtaining independent signs conditional on the two squares would require additional hidden randomness. In particular, if \(h,h'\) were independent before the squares were exposed, their quotient \(h'/(mh)\) would be uniform in \(K\) on this event, giving the ordinary split probability upon evaluation. Revealing the quotient as a public root-word value either supplies a proper gcd immediately or removes the new useful sign direction.

## Exact gain from the extra completed rows

Let \(A\) map a binary row selection to its sum in the parity and character space. Let \(T\) map selections to their restricted labels, as above, and let \(F\) be the space of all row selections. Put \(W=A(\ker T)\). The map from \(T(F)\) to \(A(F)/W\) sending \(Tx\) to \(Ax+W\) is well-defined and onto, with kernel \(T(\ker A)\). Hence
\[
 \dim T(\ker A)=\operatorname{rank} T-\dim(A(F)/W).
\]
A selection in \(\ker T\) has label in the row span of \(C\). Binary elimination expresses its root word as a product of exposed root words and even powers of the base roots, together with its public multipliers. This gives a public comparison certificate from the completed rows.

In the ordinary repeated-anchor model, choose one representative completed row for each anchor that occurs. The space \(W\) is spanned by the public rows and the difference of each other row with its representative. The difference has witnessed root quotient \(m_j/m_{\rm rep}\), or witnessed root product \((m_jm_{\rm rep})h_i^2\). It is consumable using the two existing completed certificates. Eliminate using these differences first, then use one row per anchor in the quotient. This gives exactly the same set of attainable dependency labels. An anchor can supply a split by itself if its representative lies in \(W\), even if its repeated rows are individually nonzero. If each anchor's representative projects to a distinct independent vector modulo \(W\), any number of extra rows with these repeated labels gives zero usable dependency rank.

For products of *different* private roots there is a concrete alternative coupling. Take independent hidden roots \(h_i\) up front. For an oriented edge \(ij\) use \(h_j/h_i\) and optionally multiply by a known unit. For a forest, the edge targets are jointly independent uniform squares, since the vertex-square tuple is bijective with one square per component and the edge ratios. Every pair of distinct edges of a simple graph is a forest, so its two square targets are independent. The tag rank of the accepted edge rows is the rank of the binary incidence matrix, namely the number of vertices minus the number of components of the accepted graph, including isolated vertices. Cycles give certified public comparisons after completion, with root quotient obtained by telescoping. If a predetermined list of edges is screened by the ordinary public tests, with fixed public multipliers independent of the vertex squares, each attempted edge has the ordinary marginal acceptance probability. Thus \(M\) such attempts with acceptance probability at most \(p\) supply at most \(Mp\) expected accepted tag rank and have probability at most \(Mp/r\) to supply rank \(r\). This bounds direct edge screening. It leaves open a search that finds passing edges among many possible pairs without paying for each individual screen.

## Shared shapes and shared descents

Write \(D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}\) and \(H=D\log\log N\). Consider positive primitive pairs \((u,v)\), \((u',v')\) of unit coordinates at most \(X=\sqrt N\exp(O(H))\). Assume each coordinate is a squarefree product of permitted primes at least \(P=\exp(\Omega(DH))\), as in the counted private shapes. Let their residues be related by \((a/b)^2\), with \(a,b\) units of absolute value at most \(A_0=\exp(O(H))\). Then
\[
 b^2u'v-a^2uv'=jN,\qquad |j|\le 2A_0^2X^2/N.
\]
A common permitted prime in the two numerators or in the two denominators divides \(j\). Since it exceeds the bound on \(|j|\), this forces \(j=0\). The two rational targets then have the same rational square class. Both targets are supported above \(A_0\), whereas the square quotient is supported on primes dividing \(ab\). Their valuation difference is therefore zero at every prime. It follows that the pairs coincide. Thus a distinct pair sharing a permitted prime must share it in opposite roles.

For fixed \(a,b\), suppose a permitted prime \(q\) divides \(u\) and \(v'\). Choose these two multiples of \(q\), with at most \((X/q)^2\) possibilities. There are \(O(1+A_0^2X^2/N)\) possible \(j\), and once these data are fixed the equation determines \(u'v\). If it is a positive integer at most \(X^2\), its prime factors at least \(P\) have total multiplicity \(O(D)\). Consequently at most \(2^{O(D)}\) divisors can supply the remaining good coordinate, with the last coordinate then determined. Sum over \(q\ge P\) using \(\sum_{q\ge P}q^{-2}=O(P^{-1})\). The other opposite-role orientation follows by exchanging the two pairs and replacing \(a/b\) by its inverse. Each tuple determines the original square target. For a uniform unit square on the rough-input branch, its probability is \(1/|U^2|=2^k/\varphi(N)=N^{-1}\exp(o(H))\). The probability of any such tuple for a fixed multiplier and one uniform square is therefore at most
\[
 \frac{\exp(O(H))}{P}.
\]
The bound survives a union over all height-\(\exp(O(H))\) rational multipliers and \(\exp(O(H))\) original draws. It also applies to any two members of a family whose targets are obtained from a uniform square by multiplying by squares of such rationals: union over all possible first multipliers, then use the relative multiplier, whose height still satisfies the bound. For each fixed first multiplier the first target remains uniform. This covers adaptive selection within this rational-height family. Known multipliers of larger rational height are outside the counting argument. Even with no height bound on a witnessed multiplier, the sign-rank calculation still applies. [Related argument for equal targets](keybreak-enlarged-private-lattice.md#shared-factors-and-sieve-cutoffs)

For a fresh independent uniform square, reuse of an already recognized denominator gives a different barrier. Conditional on the prior transcript, a fixed unit denominator \(v\) and at most \(X\) positive numerator candidates name at most \(X\) square targets. A public menu of \(\exp(O(H))\) denominators selected before exposing the fresh square gives probability at most \(N^{-1/2}\exp(O(H))\). The same bound applies to fixing numerators. In the single-input budget this rules out obtaining fresh independent labels by keeping one recognized coordinate and only screening the other. It leaves open a new coupling that supplies many independent square targets already having short good coordinates without screening them individually.

These bounds identify the precise benefit available from known-multiplier reuse: certified differences may enlarge the public parity span. Within the bounded-height rational family, the differences from distinct good shapes still require separate top-prime descents unless their certificates arise by another method. No leading private or terminal cost saving follows from repeated roots, direct edge screening, or reusing a short menu of recognized coordinates.
