# Smooth residuals in a fixed intermediate quadratic window

For the same unknown odd modulus \(N\), set \(X=\lfloor N^{97/100}\rfloor\). Random sampling of the integer quotient of \(h^2/N\) yields a certified smooth residual \(u=h^2\bmod N\) at the one-coordinate loss appropriate to \(\log X=.97\log N+O(1)\). This uses a composite-modulus estimate for the roots of products of two bounded sets, the existing good-parent band counts, and Dirichlet GRH. The same sampling law has upper bounds for all stages of the band recognizer. It also admits signed counts against products of arbitrary bounded weights on those two sets, with an individual count for each CRT root sign relative to chosen block roots. These are statistical estimates for the sampled rows. They leave the terminal dependency argument and its cost to a separate calculation.

## The actual sampler

Let \(I=[X/8,X/4]\cap\mathbb Z\). Let \(\mathcal K\) be the range from \(\lfloor(\min I)^2/N\rfloor\) to \(\lfloor(\max I)^2/N\rfloor\). Sample \(k\) exactly uniformly from this range and a slot from a fixed constant size list. At that slot, return a root in the increasing order of
\[
 I\cap[\lfloor\sqrt{kN}\rfloor+1,\lfloor\sqrt{kN+X}\rfloor]
\]
or return an empty slot if this index exceeds the list length. Successive squares in \(I\) differ by at least \(X/5\) for large \(N\), so eight slots suffice. Each unsigned pair \((h,u)\) with \(h\in I\), \(u=h^2-kN\in[1,X]\) occurs exactly once. Its unconditional probability is \(1/(8|\mathcal K|)\), and \(|\mathcal K|\asymp X^2/N=N^{.94+o(1)}\). The sampler uses integer powers and roots for the rational exponent \(97/100\), and \(O(\log N)\) random bits in expectation by rejection. A polynomial rejection cap has negligible failure probability, independent of the sampled value conditional on success. Each trial takes polynomial bit work. The sampler never enumerates the range. A proper gcd at initialization gives a factor; otherwise retain the unit samples and keep the root separate from the subsequent choices that operate on \(u\). Distinct small roots with the same residual also give a proper gcd if encountered. [Exact window and collision proof](keybreak-public-quadratic-window.md#exact-enumeration-and-certification)

## Root sums for two blocks, including signed weights

Write \(G=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\), \(K=G[2]\), and \(Q=G/G^2\). In each class in \(Q\), choose a unit representative \(j\). For each integer \(a\) in the first block and \(b\) in the second block of that class, choose reference roots \(x_a^2=ja\), \(y_b^2=jb\) modulo \(N\). Every unit root of \(ab\) is uniquely \(t j^{-1}x_a y_b\) for \(t\in K\). Integers from different classes contribute no roots. The reference choices are only for this proof. The sampling algorithm computes the short root with integer square roots and needs no CRT decomposition.

Here is the bound used for the root distribution. For an odd modulus \(r\), let two sets of unit radicands lie in \([1,M_1]\) and \([1,M_2]\), with \(r^{1/3}\le M_i\le r\). For chosen roots of a common unit multiple of their radicands, arbitrary block weights of modulus at most one, and a unit frequency \(c\), the sum of \(e_r(cxy)\) over all weighted pairs of those roots is
\[
 \ll M_1M_2\,r^{-1/24+o(1)}.
\]
To see the application of restricted energy, partition each radicand interval into blocks of width \(W=\lfloor r^{1/3}\rfloor\). For nonnegative weights the two successive Cauchy bounds partition first the \(x\) sum and then the \(y\) sum. If \(R_1,R_2\) count the weighted within-block root differences, this gives
\[
 |S|^4\ll \frac{(M_1M_2)^3}{W^2}
       \left|\sum_{d,e} R_1(d)R_2(e)e_r(cde)\right|
 \le \frac{(M_1M_2)^3}{W^2}\sqrt{r}\,\|R_1\|_2\|R_2\|_2.
\]
The first inequality uses at most \(M_i/W+1\) blocks and \(\sum_d R_1(d)\le M_1 W\) for one root per radicand. The last inequality is additive Parseval for the unit frequency. The restricted energies, including pairs of equal radicands, give \(\|R_i\|_2^2\ll M_i^2 r^{o(1)}\) at this width. The composite energy proof accounts for nonunit differences and retains the short-progression remainder in the rank-one lattice contribution. Signed real weights follow by separating positive and negative parts, and complex weights by doing the same for real and imaginary parts. This argument allows arbitrary selection of the roots, arbitrary supports inside the radicand bounds, and weights chosen from a fixed public environment. [Composite restricted energy proof](keybreak-composite-root-energy.md) · [Baier, Theorem 4 and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15448v4#S2.SS2)

Apply Erdős–Turán to the roots divided by \(N\), with cutoff \(\lfloor N^{.031}\rfloor\). For each nonzero frequency \(\ell\) up to the cutoff, reduce to \(r=N/\gcd(\ell,N)\), so \(r\ge N^{.969}\) and the reduced frequency is a unit. Use the *chosen* block roots reduced modulo \(r\). In each application below \(N^{.437+o(1)}\le M_i\le N^{.533+o(1)}\), so \(r^{1/3}\le M_i<r\). The reductions are injective on radicands in either block and the analytic estimate needs no knowledge of \(r\) in the sampler. The harmonic sum costs \(O(\log N)\). There are \(|Q|=|K|=N^{o(1)}\) classes and signs, so summing over any desired subset changes only the subleading power.

More explicitly, take arbitrary weights \(w_1,w_2\) of modulus at most one, possibly different in each square class. For one specified class and one specified sign \(t\), the weighted number of short roots is
\[
 \frac{|I|}{N}\sum_{a,b}w_1(a)w_2(b)
        +O\big(M_1M_2N^{-.031+o(1)}\big).
\]
For the discrepancy step, one first applies the ordinary positive-measure inequality to the nonnegative parts of the product weights, using layer-cake integration for weights in \([0,1]\). The same absolute bound then holds for signed or complex weights. It also holds after summing the per-sign estimates against any CRT sign character. In that case the main term cancels for every nontrivial sign character. In comparison, taking the full-width \(W=M_i\) in the general composite restricted energy yields only a saving \((1-.97)/8\) on the balanced blocks, which does not beat the relative interval length \(N^{-.03}\). The prime-modulus energy of Kerr, Shkredov, Shparlinski and Zaharescu gives adequate savings on these sizes, but alone would leave the composite case open. [Prime-modulus energy and bilinear corollary](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1474748023000397)

## Counting a fixed two-block recipe

Take a fixed staged recognition schedule with band weights \(w_i\) for the old two-coordinate boxes of size about \(\sqrt N\). Write \(S_j=\sum_{i<j}w_i\), \(S=S_J\), and \(H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\). For each new block use the H.20 product count with total logarithmic size \(\log X/2\) and the same stage fractions. Round each half to a power of two so their product is at most \(X\). The counts of prime positions remain \(\Theta(D)\) in either block, \(D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}\). The prime bands remain near their original stage cutoffs and retain the finite H.20 central-index and interval checks. Choose the stage threshold \(T_i\) for the combined product as the product of the two guaranteed lower stage factors. This changes threshold logarithms by \(O(D)\) from the corresponding real length allocations. In the first block allow only primes \(1\bmod 4\), and in the second only primes \(3\bmod 4\).

A fully factored squarefree target from this recipe has a unique pair of blocks. Each prime identifies its residue-class block and its unique stage and dyadic band. Inside a block and stage, all allowed products have exactly \(m_i!\) orderings in the H.20 sum when there are \(m_i\) prescribed prime positions. The central-index constraint is symmetric. This exact multiplicity avoids a change of measure on the admitted targets. The congruence restriction costs only a constant factor per prime choice under Dirichlet GRH modulo \(4N\). Any nontrivial quadratic character modulo \(N\) remains nontrivial after twisting by the character modulo four. On the good-field event from G.12, use its quantitative bad-parent upper bound to delete at most \(D^{-B_0}\) of each band for arbitrarily large fixed \(B_0\). Within either residue-class half of the band the bad fraction remains \(O(D^{-B_0})\). As in the full-class argument, every nontrivial quadratic probe on a tuple of independently chosen good primes has mean at most \((O(D^{-B_0}))^{\Theta(D)}\), negligible relative to the reciprocal number of square classes on the rough-input branch. Repeated prime choices have probability \(\exp(-\Omega(DH))\) uniformly per ordered tuple, also negligible at that scale. Summing over band tuples and dividing by the exact multiplicities leaves the same relative square-class bounds for distinct products. [Square-class mixing and the quantitative good-field event](keybreak-public-quadratic-window.md#there-are-enough-modular-square-products-before-the-root-restriction)

The two good block sets therefore have product of cardinalities \(X\exp(-(.97 S+o(1))H)\), with the lower count from H.20 and the upper count from Rankin. The number of equal-class pairs is \((1+o(1))|A||B|/|Q|\). Summing their short-root count over all \(|K|=|Q|\) signs gives
\[
 \frac{|I|}{N}|A||B|(1+o(1))
   =\frac{X^2}{N}\exp(-(.97S+o(1))H).
\]
The discrepancy error is negligible since \(.031>.03\) and \(H=o(\log N)\). Dividing by the sampler denominator gives raw probability \(\exp(-(.97S+o(1))H)\).

For the signed law, fix all independent descent coins for the current trial by block, ordered prime position, and subsequent bounded call. A completed occurrence and its records then give a function of its block alone. A probe whose value on the comparison data factors as one unit-bounded weight per completed block can include a failure indicator in each weight. With a nontrivial character of the CRT sign relative to the chosen block roots, its unnormalized signed expectation is \(O(N^{-.031+o(1)}X/(X^2/N))=O(N^{-.001+o(1)})\). Integration over the independent coins preserves the bound. This includes probes obtained by multiplying arbitrary block signs with a nontrivial sign character. It does not assert conditional uniformity of the root given the entire residual or permit an arbitrary nonfactorizable weight on the product.

A multiplicative comparison probe on certified pairs \((g,h)\) with \(\epsilon(g)=h^2\) has this factorization even if individual block targets are nonsquares. Within a common class, fix one completed reference record and chosen root in each block, say \(g_{1,0},x_{a_0}\) and \(g_{2,0},y_{b_0}\). The pairs \((g_1/g_{1,0},x_a/x_{a_0})\), \((g_2/g_{2,0},y_b/y_{b_0})\), and \((g_{1,0}g_{2,0},j^{-1}x_{a_0}y_{b_0})\) all satisfy the square-evaluation identity. Their product times \((1,t)\) is the full pair \((g_1g_2,h)\). Evaluating the probe gives a fixed class factor, one weight per block and its restriction to the CRT sign group. A class with no completed record in either block gives no contribution. This reduction is used in the probability analysis and needs no explicit computation of the reference roots.

The recognizer can test the exact recipe from the verified factorization, without deciding whether the field's abstract good-parent predicate holds. For the bounded uniform genus-two tests, couple to exact recognition of every recipe input using polynomial confidence amplification. The resulting failure probability on certified recipe inputs can be smaller than any inverse polynomial in \(N\), with no leading cost. For an elliptic recognizer with the separate top-prime eligibility condition, the top dyads have exceptional fraction \(\exp(-\Omega(H))\). Within either residue-class half of a dyad the same bound holds up to a constant. By the exact ordered-tuple comparison, the fraction of block products using any exceptional prime is \(\exp(-\Omega(H))\) relative to the unrestricted block recipe. Applying the short-root bound to the exceptional subblock and the other full block shows that their raw probability is \(o(\exp(-(.97S+o(1))H))\) relative to the counted completions. This gives an \(o(1)\) error in normalized signed probes for the faster recognizer. The statement uses the fixed recipe and uniform confidence cap. Early false failures cannot add prefix survivors, and the recognized factorizations and complete descents are all verified. On counted good parents the established top descent guarantees give a completed row or an immediate split. In an analysis that needs a lower completion probability, immediate splits must be counted as success or bounded separately. ## Upper bounds for actual prefix gates

For a sample that passes through stage \(j-1\), at each earlier stage greedily stop multiplying certified prime copies when the band factor first reaches \(T_i\). This gives a divisor \(d_i\in[T_i,T_iY_0]\) of \(u\) supported in its assigned band, where \(Y_0=N^{o(1)}\) is the largest cutoff. The \(d_i\) are pairwise coprime. The thresholds are deterministic. For the prefix \(j=0\) use the raw window count. For later prefixes let \(D_j^{\rm size}\) denote the product of the upper divisor caps \(T_iY_0\) over \(i<j\). The corresponding lower product differs by a factor \(N^{o(1)}\).

If \(D_j^{\rm size}\le N^{.438}\), fix the divisor \(d=\prod_{i<j}d_i\). It is a unit on all continuing samples. The condition that the residual be divisible by \(d\) is exactly that \(d^{-1}h^2mod N\) lie in \([1,\lfloor X/d\rfloor]\). Additive completion of the quadratic Gauss sum gives an upper main term \(|I|X/(Nd)\) and an error \(N^{1/2+o(1)}\), uniformly in the unit multiplier. There are at most \(N^{.438}\) distinct \(d\), with a unique factorization among stages. The total error \(N^{.938+o(1)}\) is negligible against the raw exponent \(.94\) and any fixed staged loss in \(H\) units. Rankin on the sum of reciprocal divisors gives an upper main term \((X^2/N)\exp(-(.97S_j+o(1))H)\). [Gauss completion](keybreak-public-quadratic-window.md#the-short-root-distribution-is-a-separate-condition) · [Staged Rankin estimate](keybreak-private-band-stages.md#testing-and-counting-a-prefix)

If \(D_j^{\rm size}>N^{.438}\), split the certified prime copies of \(d\) at the first partial product \(a\ge N^{.437}\). The lower product bound ensures this is possible, and \(a\le N^{.437}Y_0\). Write each \(d_i=a_i b_i\) according to this split. The two root-sum blocks are \(a=\prod a_i\) and \(b=u/a\), where \(b\) is a multiple of \(\prod b_i\) and is at most \(N^{.533}\). Cover by dyadic bins for each \(a_i,b_i\), keeping only bins intersecting the constraints \(T_i\le a_ib_i\le T_iY_0\). For one bin tuple let \(A\) be the allowed products \(a\) in its bins, and \(A_{\min}\) the larger of \(N^{.437}\) and the product of the lower endpoints of the \(a_i\) bins. Let \(B\) be the unit integers up to \(X/A_{\min}\) divisible by some product of the \(b_i\) in their bins. These independent sets give an upper cover. Since every \(a\le 2^J A_{\min}\) in the tuple, the cardinality product is at most
\[
 2^J X\sum_{(a_i,b_i)\ \text{in the bins}}\prod_{i<j}(a_i b_i)^{-1}.
\]
The factor \(|K|\) in an unrestricted upper bound on modular roots per pair is \(\exp(o(H))\) on the rough-input branch. Sum over the polynomial number of bin tuples. Within a kept tuple \(a_ib_i\ge T_i/4\). The number of ways to split a fixed \(d_i\) into \(a_i,b_i\) is at most \(2^{O(D)}\), since all prime copies have size \(\exp(\Omega(DH))\) and \(d_i\le N^{O(1)}\). This costs \(\exp(o(H))\). The remaining reciprocal sum is the same staged Rankin upper bound. The combined absolute discrepancy error across the bins is \(N^{.939+o(1)}\), smaller than the desired main-term scale. Thus both ranges give raw prefix probability at most \(\exp(-(.97S_j+o(1))H)\).

Collecting \(\exp((b+o(1))H)\) completed attempts now uses a raw allowance with coefficient \(b+.97S\), with slack for the subleading factors and completion probabilities. Cap stage \(j\) with coefficient \(b+.97(S-S_j)\) plus slack, by Markov applied to the fixed raw attempt allowance or by an independent concentration bound. At stage per-call cost coefficient \(c_j\), the recognition charge is \(b+.97(S-S_j)+c_j\). Each launched shape still pays for its complete capped field descents, and samples whose bounded trial gives a factor can stop immediately. A terminal split proof using the signed-probe estimates must specify the completed-block comparison characters and how the dependency is selected. Ordinary conditional hidden-root uniformity does not apply to this sampler.
