# Small-order centers in the elliptic four-mark chart

On the fixed elliptic four-mark curve, a finite chart root reached from a local point of order at most $R$ by one of $2B+1$ short offsets is exceptional. For primes $p\in[Y,2Y]$, the proportion of such primes is $O((R+1)^3(B+1)^3/Y)$. The center can depend arbitrarily on $p$ and on the input. In particular, a scalar multiple that returns to the identity adds no local chart roots beyond the short offsets themselves. [Four-mark curve and inverse chart](cogentic-elliptic-mark-cycle.md)

## The target has no rational multiple

Use $E\colon y^2=x^3+1964x^2+753984x+80568576$ and let $P$ be any fixed rational point on $E$. Put
$$
 A(m)=(m-51)(m-95),\qquad B(m)=(m-57)(m-85),\qquad
 F(m)=m+A(m)B(m)(A(m)-1).
$$
The polynomial $F$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$ by reduction modulo 13. For a root $m$ of $F$, the identity $A-B(1-2A)^2=-4F$ gives an elliptic point
$$
 T=\left(-\frac{8976}{m-51},-\frac{8976 B(m)(1-2A(m))}{(m-51)^2}\right).
$$
The finite chart zeros on $E$ are the conjugates of $T$ and their negatives, away from finitely many primes of bad reduction, zero chart denominators, or repeated chart roots.

In the splitting field of $F$, take a prime above 31. The roots $25$ and $22$ modulo 31 are simple, so two conjugates $T_1,T_2$ reduce there to $(9,6)$ and $(7,10)$. Their difference reduces to $D=(6,18)$, with $[4]D=(17,13)$ and $[20]D=O$. Thus the local order has a nontrivial 5-part. Modulo 23 the polynomial is separable with irreducible factors $m+3$, $m^2+18$, and $m^3+16m+20$. The splitting field is unramified at 23 with residue degree $6$, since the factor degrees are the Frobenius cycle lengths. The curve has $32$ points over $\mathbb F_{23}$. Its Frobenius trace is $-8$, and the trace recurrence gives $\#E(\mathbb F_{23^6})=148058624$, prime to 5. Prime-to-characteristic torsion injects at both good reductions. Consequently $T_1-T_2$ has infinite order. If any nonzero multiple of $T$ were rational, the same multiple of every conjugate would equal that rational point, killing $T_1-T_2$.

## Counting the primes reached by offsets

Exclude the fixed bad primes. Suppose a local center $Q\in E(\mathbb F_p)$ has order $1\le r\le R$ and $Q+[b]P$ is a finite chart zero for $|b|\le B$. At some degree-one prime of $\mathbb Q(m)$ above $p$, the reduction of $T$ or $-T$ equals that chart point. Thus
$$
 D_{r,b,\pm}=[r](\pm T)-[rb]P
$$
reduces to the identity there. Every such point is nonzero over $\mathbb Q(m)$ because $[r]T$ is never rational. The quadratic canonical height on the subgroup generated by $T,P$ bounds the logarithmic abscissa height of $D_{r,b,\pm}$ by $O(r^2(1+b^2))$, with a fixed constant. At a good prime, reduction of a finite point to the identity forces a negative valuation of its abscissa. The logarithm of the norm of the denominator ideal is at most the field degree times that height. Each underlying rational prime in $[Y,2Y]$ contributes at least $\log Y$, so summing over $r,b$ and the two signs gives $O((R+1)^3(B+1)^3/\log Y)$ primes. Dividing by the number of dyadic primes gives the stated proportion. The same proof for a fixed translate of a box in $d$ rational directions gives $O((R+1)^3(B+1)^{d+2}/Y)$. [Canonical height comparison](https://www.math.brown.edu/johsilve/Wyoming/WyomingEllipticCurveJune7.pdf#page=80)

A smooth scalar or a full supersingular order multiple can provide a small-order center, but this bound applies regardless of how the center is obtained. It concerns finite zeros of this sextic. A factor exposed by a group-law denominator is a separate scalar or point-comparison test. The proof also depends on the absence of rational multiples of the chart target. For a chart with $[r]T=[r]S$ and $S$ rational, $T-S$ is $r$-torsion, and a chart hit on that branch requires the point minus $S$ to lie in $E[r]$. The scalar test can screen candidates before checking the specified chart root.
