# A deterministic conditional score for Cogentic's two polynomial fields

In the [two-thirds construction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md), put \(L=\log N\), \(z=\log L\), \(D=(L/z)^{1/3}\), \(H=Dz\) and \(V=DH\). Suppose \(\log U=(v+o(1))V\), \(\log m=(1/x+o(1))V\) with \(v>1/x\), and \(\log(A B K_1/w)=O(H)\). Use any fixed schedule satisfying the moment and rank estimates in that note. For every target prime these estimates give a deterministic successful search in **\(U^2\exp(O(H))\) bit operations**, replacing exhaustive enumeration of the Cartesian product of both polynomial boxes. The reduction below also gives a score suitable for conditioning on each of the four scalar coefficients. It uses the two-thirds moment estimates with empty exceptional-conductor sets, obtained from Dirichlet GRH over \(\mathbb Q\). [Cogentic, Proposition 10.1 and Lemmas 7.4–7.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=52) · [Uniform window estimates](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) · [Alpöge](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Sutherland](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## A computable nonnegative score

Fix the generator prime \(\ell\). Let \(s=(s_0,s_1)\) and \(t=(t_0,t_1)\) be the coefficient pairs for the two fields. Use the paper's notation \(C=\widehat C\), \(A_{jk}=\widetilde A^\circ_{jk}\), \(D_A,F_A^2,F_\ell^2\), root rows \(l_1,\ldots,l_d\), and \(F_i=F^\circ_i\). Here \(F_{\rm med}=\varnothing\). Write \(\alpha=\alpha_s\) for the reference saddle, \(\mu(F)=\sum_{(i,q,r)\in F}q^{-\alpha}\), \(w_i=(q^{-\alpha/2}\log q)_{(i,q,r)\in F_i}\), \(\mu_0=\sum_{q\in J_*}q^{-\alpha}\), and \(W_0=\varphi''(\alpha)>0\). Put \(T_0=(7/.15)z^2\), \(\tau=e^{-.0375T_0}\) and \(\mu_*=e^{-.07T_0}\), the thresholds from the [two-thirds peeling proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md). Define
\[
\begin{aligned}
 P(s,t)={}&64(C-1)^2
 +\sum_{i=1}^2\left\{4(\mu(F_i)-\mu_0)^2/u^2+
   (\|w_i\|^2-W_0)^2/(B^{-.4}W_0^2)\right\}\\
 &+\frac{2}{\mu_*}\left(\mu(F_{\rm mult})+\mu(F_{\rm shared})+D_A/\tau^2\right)
 +\tau^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^d A_{l_j,l_j}^2\\
 &+e^{.148T_0}\left(F_A^2+D_A+\ell^{\alpha}F_\ell^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\]

Every summand is nonnegative. Under the original product discrete Gaussian, \(\mathbf E P=o(1)\): \(\operatorname{Var}C=o(1)\), the two root-mass statistics have variances \(O(B^{-.45})\) and respective means \(\mu_0+O(B^{-.45})\), \((1+O(B^{-.45}))W_0\). The multiple- and shared-root masses have expectations \(O(B^{-.49})\). The source bounds \(\mathbf E D_A\ll u e^{-.15T_0}\), \(\mathbf E A_{l_j,l_j}^2\le e^{-.15T_0}\), and \(\mathbf E(F_A^2+D_A+\ell^\alpha F_\ell^2)\le L^{O(1)}e^{-.15T_0}\). In particular \(e^{-.15T_0}/(\mu_*\tau^2)=e^{-.005T_0}\) and \(L^{O(1)}e^{-.002T_0}\to0\). These comparisons give the claim even with the small gap between the last two exponents. [Original moment bounds, printed pp. 52–54](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=52)

For any pair inside the allowed boxes with \(P<1\), all the hypotheses of the deterministic peeling step on the paper's event \(E_{\rm good}\) hold. Indeed \(|C-1|<1/8\), and \(\mu_0\le(2+o(1))u\) gives \(\mu(F_i)\le3u\). The displayed \(w_i\) terms give their required relative accuracy. The bad diagonal rows, defined by \(|A_{q,q}|>\tau\), have mass at most \(D_A/\tau^2\). Together with the two root defects their initial mass is less than \(\mu_*/2\). No protected root row is diagonal-bad, while construction makes every protected root simple and absent from the other field. The last line simultaneously gives its three norm thresholds. All conditional terms from medium-conductor deletions are zero. Therefore strict Gram positivity holds and the exact \(\operatorname{TryPair}\) returns a verified exponent. Only this implication and \(\mathbf EP=o(1)\) will be used; the algorithm need not reconstruct the peeling sequence.

## Fixing the two fields

For the \(U^2\) bound, give each coefficient pair the uniform distribution on the rectangle \(|s_j-\bar u_j|\le S_j/2\), independently across the fields. These are integer points inside the prescribed boxes. Their uniform point masses are at most an absolute constant times the original Gaussian point masses, by \(Z_j\asymp S_j\) and the exponent bound on the rectangle. Positivity gives \(\mathbf E_{\rm unif}P=o(1)\). There are \(O(U^2 A)\) pairs per field, where \(S_0=UA,S_1=U\).

For a marked integer \(a\in\mathcal A_*\), let \(I_i(a,s)\) be the squarefree smooth-value indicator for field \(i\). For a universal row pair \(j,k\), let \(e_i(s)\) indicate the presence of its field-\(i\) root rows. Each such condition is a congruence \(f_{i,s}(r)=0\bmod q\); the protected rows and the real rows are fixed. For an admitted pair the centered Gram entry is exactly
\[
     A_{jk}=\sum_a h_{jk}(a) I_1(a,s)I_2(a,t),\qquad
 h_{jk}(a)=w_{\rm eq}(a)\big(r_j(a)r_k(a)-\widetilde U^{\rm ex}_{jk}\big).
\]
Here the real rows use the fixed target roots, as in the source's pre-peeling matrix. All \(h\)'s are independent of both coefficient pairs. Expand its squared contribution as
\[
 \sum_{a,a'} h_{jk}(a)h_{jk}(a')
   [e_1(s)I_1(a,s)I_1(a',s)]
   [e_2(t)I_2(a,t)I_2(a',t)].
\]
The scalar \((C-1)^2\) has the same expansion with additional linear and constant terms. The root-mass and \(w_i\) squares expand into at most two root-presence tests in a single field; a repeated root adds its derivative congruence, and a shared root is a product of one presence test per field. Thus \(P\) is a sum of \(\operatorname{poly}(A,B,d)\) separated terms. This count includes the universal root list, which has at most \(2B^2+d\) elements. Each single-field factor tests at most two marks and finitely many congruences on the two coefficients. Computing the factors from their polynomial values by trial division over the factor base costs \(\operatorname{poly}(A,B,L)\) bit operations.

Accumulate the uniform averages of the second-field factors once over all \(t\). A pass through all \(s\) now evaluates \(\mathbf E_t P(s,t)\) from those stored numbers. Fix an \(s\) minimizing the computed conditional mean. In a further pass through \(t\), select a pair minimizing the computed \(P(s,t)\), and run the exact extractor. Uniform score errors below \(1/16\) in both passes suffice, since for all sufficiently large \(N\) the global mean is below \(1/4\). Each selected minimum is at most its true minimum plus twice the absolute numerical error. This yields \(P(s,t)<1\). For any small input on which the extractor fails, the ordinary verified trial-division fallback preserves a total algorithm. The three separate passes each cost \(U^2\exp(O(H))\) bit operations. Polynomially many tables in \(A,B,L\) suffice in workspace. This costs \(\exp((2v+o(1))V)\) on a fixed schedule, including a complete sweep of target primes.

## Computing the real coefficients without a smoothness oracle

All quantities in the score admit deterministic approximations to absolute error \(e^{-L^2}\), with preprocessing cost \(\exp(O(H))+O(A\sqrt m\,\operatorname{poly}(L))\). The latter term is smaller than \(U\) for the stated schedules. At each of the \(\operatorname{poly}(A,B,L)\) choices of marks, rows and at most two omitted primes, sums over the prime band cost \(\operatorname{poly}(B,L)\). The factors \(q_0/\phi(q_0)\) in \(Z_i^{(0)}(a)\), \(q_0=P_0\ell|a-m|\), are computable by factoring \(|a-m|\) by trial division up to \(\sqrt m\), taking the union with the known prime divisors of \(P_0\ell\). Computing the grid membership and masses \(\pi_0(a)\) requires gcds and integer counts. The heights of rational means \(Y=X_{\rm sc}|P_i^*(a/A)|/Q\) and of the squared Gaussian widths are polynomial in \(L\) bits. The normalizing \(I_{\rm pure}\) and \(I\) denominators used in the schedule are at least \(\exp(-O(H))\), by the saddle estimates and their principal Gaussian approximation. The total number of weighted terms is \(\exp(O(H))\), and the total number of coefficients swept is \(\exp(O(V))\). Their sizes, the reciprocals of scalar denominators, row factors and thresholds are bounded by \(\exp(O(L))\). Thus computing elementary coefficients to \(e^{-cL^2}\), taking a sufficiently large fixed \(c\), allows a uniform score error below \(1/16\), including finite summation and averaging. Dyadic arithmetic or rational interval enclosures suffice.

The normalized saddle product is finite. Bisection solves \(-\varphi'(\alpha_Y)=\log Y\) using positive derivative magnitude \(\varphi''\asymp u(\log B)^2\) near its solution, with a polynomial number of precision bits. The same evaluation gives \(\varphi''\). For \(I_{\rm pure}\) set \(\tau=t\log B\). On the complex disk \(|\tau|\le2\) the product has absolute value at most \(\exp(e^2\sum_{p\in J_*}p^{-\alpha_Y})=\exp(O(u))\); the phase \(e^{i\tau\log Y/\log B}\) has the same bound. The integrated Taylor polynomial of degree \(\lceil L^4\rceil\) on \([-1,1]\) therefore gives more than \(e^{-cL^2}\) absolute precision by Cauchy's inequality. Its coefficients come from products of the ordinary exponential series, using polynomially many bit operations per prime and per coefficient. This calculation includes \(I_{\rm pure}\) with up to two prime factors omitted, so it computes the exact centered model before its Edgeworth approximation.

For the Gaussian principal coefficient \(I(Y,S;S_{\rm om})\) of source §8.3 use the actual cutoff window and its Mellin integral on the saddle. Write \(R=Y/S\), \(K=\lceil L^4\rceil\), and truncate the outer frequency integral at \(T=RL^{10}\). The integration-by-parts estimate in the proof of Lemma 8.4(iv), valid as soon as \(|t|/R\) exceeds an absolute constant, bounds its tail by \(\exp(-c' L^{20/3}+O(\log R+u))\). The Euler product on the saddle has size at most \(\exp(O(u))\). In the inner Mellin integral put \(h=(x-Y)/S\) and keep \(|h|\le K\). The source cutoff equals one throughout this range because \(K/R\to0\), and discarding its remaining compact tail costs \(O(e^{-c'K^2})\) before the harmless factors \(T\) and the Euler product. The remaining double integrand is
\[
 Y^{\alpha_Y-1+it}\, e^{-\pi h^2}(1+h/R)^{\alpha_Y-1+it}
       \prod_{p\in J_*\setminus S_{\rm om}}(1+p^{-\alpha_Y-it}).
\]
Partition the real rectangle \([-T,T]\times[-K,K]\) into intervals of half-width at most \(1/(4\log B)\) and \(1/4\). On the concentric complex polydisks of radii \(1/(2\log B)\) and \(1/2\), the integrand is holomorphic and has modulus at most \(\exp(O(L^{11}))\). In particular \(|t|/R=O(L^{10})\), \(|\arg(1+h/R)|=O(1/R)\) there, and moving \(t\) by this radius preserves the Euler-product bound \(\exp(O(u))\). Integrate its bivariate Taylor polynomial of separate degrees \(\lceil L^{13}\rceil\) on each rectangle. Two geometric tails from Cauchy's coefficient bound, summed over \(O(TK\log B)\) rectangles, cost \(o(e^{-cL^2})\). This number of rectangles is \(\exp(O(H))\); elementary series operations, including range-reduced trigonometric functions for the phase, need at most \(\operatorname{poly}(L,B)\) bit operations per rectangle. For example working in coordinates scaled to the two complex radii keeps coefficient magnitudes bounded by \(\exp(O(L^{11}))\), and \(L^{O(1)}\) bits absorb this growth, the number of polynomial operations, and the polynomial-degree elementary recurrences. This provides the coefficients at the required precision without enumerating integers through \(Y\).
