# Starting and descending the high-degree cofactor score

The five-coordinate family in each field admits a low-mean initial box and a descent of both low polynomial coordinates to their correction floors. The cofactor and the two high coordinates stay averaged. This uses the fixed-schedule moment and extractor hypotheses and the real-coefficient evaluators already used for the [enlarged cofactor score](cogentic-cofactor-dispersion.md), together with the [exact evaluation lattice for two high directions](cogentic-high-degree-two-directions.md). The argument evaluates that score on every intermediate box. It does not select a final tuple.

## The initial box

Use the same \(g=P_0\ell\), digit polynomial \(R\), and fixed starting polynomial as in the high-direction family. In each field write the perturbation as
\[
 g\left[kR(X)+(m-X)\bigl(x+Xy+u(A+X)^r+v(A+X)^{r+1}\bigr)\right],\qquad r=d-3.
\]
Keep \(k\) in its original full cofactor interval of length \(K=m^2e^{C_1H}+O(1)\). The two high intervals have length \(T=\lceil e^{40000V}\rceil\). Initially use the old uniform rectangles for \(x,y\), with widths of leading exponent \(100000\) on the \(V\) scale and ratio \(e^{O(H)}\). The sign change from \(X-m\) to \(m-X\) just reverses these intervals. As on the fixed schedule, any specified widths may be rounded to nearby powers of two using polynomial-bit approximations to the logarithmic scales. Take the upper rounding for the correction floors.

Fix \(k,u,v\) in both fields. The additional polynomial has degree at most \(d-1\). It preserves the leading coefficient, the defining congruences modulo \(g\), the cofactor \(k_0+gk\), and the protected roots modulo \(\ell\). On \(|X|\le A\) its size is at most \(e^{(50001+o(1))V}\), versus the original mark-height scale \(e^{(100001+o(1))V}\). The correction term alone is smaller still. Thus after moving these fixed terms into the starting polynomial, the real-root and marked-value margins remain valid uniformly. Cofactor heights and the finite band-deletion bounds are the same as before. Nothing here changes the Gaussian widths in \(x,y\). The original fixed-schedule moment argument consequently gives mean score \(o(1)\) in the old Gaussian law, with the actual score for these fixed fields, cofactors and omissions. On the old uniform rectangles the point masses are at most a fixed multiple of those of the Gaussian. Nonnegativity gives the same \(o(1)\) bound there. Averaging over \(k,u,v\) gives the required initial mean. This argument does not compare the row laws on the old and new coefficient boxes. [Uniform fixed-cofactor moment comparison](cogentic-cofactor-dispersion.md#a-low-mean-initial-score-for-the-enlarged-family)

## Exact score terms and congruences

Use the finite Gram and mass expansions from the [cofactor score](cogentic-cofactor-dispersion.md#the-centered-gram-expectations-while-full-cycles-remain). Each resulting term has at most two actual smoothness marks per field. The nominal archimedean factors are unchanged. The analytic coefficients have finite expansions in the cofactor band omissions, and the varying cell densities use the finite expansion in ordinary grid eligibility. These introduce only cofactor predicates and auxiliary grid marks, which carry no smoothness tests. The original truncation orders give any prescribed error \(e^{-CH}\), with \(e^{O(H)}\) terms and absolute coefficient sum.

Ordinary and protected row predicates, repeated-root and shared-root flags, and bounded prime insertions are still exact after fixing all five coordinates in each field modulo the term's retained small modulus \(M=e^{O(H)}\). In particular one does not evaluate a row at a polynomial produced by changing or freezing a coordinate. Use the original congruence class and its exact probability. For protected mark values divide by \(\ell\) as before. More generally a prescribed squarefree divisor is enforced in the residue split, divided out of the marked progression, and omitted from its remaining band.

The cofactor-omission Bonferroni tail remains valid. The high variables may be fixed for that bound. At any eligible prime the cofactor witness fixes one \(k\) class and the marked-value witness then fixes one \(x\) class conditional on the others, since the mark modifier \(g(m-a)\) is a unit there. Both lengths exceed \(B^b\) for every fixed \(b\) used in the truncations. Auxiliary cofactor avoidance predicates are also truncated on the actual intervals before introducing any approximating law. The finite expansion tails are therefore bounded on the original box. A term with no smoothness test just uses the retained exact residue counts. For one smoothness mark use a one-mark count, and for a repeated mark combine its omissions and reject incompatible prescribed valuations. [Omission and auxiliary-sieve bounds](cogentic-cofactor-dispersion.md#modular-conditions-and-changing-cofactor-omissions)

Fix the desired score precision first. Choose the analytic and grid truncations, auxiliary avoidance and omission precisions using the existing coefficient-sum bounds. This fixes the \(e^{O(H)}\) modulus and enumeration bounds. Choose \(C_1\) in the correction widths sufficiently large after these choices. All subsequent geometric and scalar accuracies can absorb the remaining finite weights.

## Two estimates along one descent

Let \(Z=T A^r\), so \(\log Z=(50000+o(1))V\). Keep the four low side lengths, across both fields, within a factor \(e^{O(H)}\) during descent by bisecting a longest side whose length still exceeds twice its floor by at least two. The initial ratio satisfies this bound. When any side reaches its floor, the remaining sides are already at most the same factor larger, and the ratio continues to satisfy the bound. Use floor \(X_0=\lceil(2A)^{r+2}e^{C_1H}\rceil\) for both low axes. All counts below are made after fixing the retained residue classes. Their integer intervals are shorter by at most \(e^{O(H)}\) factors. All panels have exact weights from their integer lengths, and a partition into panels uses balanced lengths to avoid a short end piece.

### Broad low intervals

Suppose the smaller low width in this field is at least \(Z\) before the class split. At marks \(a_1,a_2\), the value of the high polynomial
\[
 H_0(X)=u(A+X)^r+v(A+X)^{r+1}
\]
can be represented exactly by an integral linear polynomial \(x_0+Xy_0\) at those two integers. Its slope is the divided difference, which is an integer. Its coefficients vary by at most \(Z e^{O(H)}\) across the full high box, uniformly in the marks. For a single mark use an intercept shift. Varying \(u,v\) only within an existing class modulo \(M\) changes the two interpolation coefficients by multiples of \(M\).

Subdivide the two high intervals into \(e^{O(H)}\) pieces so that the change of either interpolation coefficient in a piece is at most \(e^{-C'H}\) times the smaller low width. This is possible since the full variation is at most \(Z e^{O(H)}\). Freeze the interpolation shift at one point of each nonempty high panel. The law of the two smooth mark values differs by at most \(O(e^{-C'H})\) in total variation, by coupling the low rectangles to their small integer translates on the residue class. The retained row values and weights are those of the original residue split. No claim about their agreement after translating the evaluation coordinates is needed.

After freezing, the scalar averaging problem is the old \((k,x,y)\) problem with translated mark origins. The low widths have leading exponent at least \(50000\), so the full-cofactor-cycle estimator applies with strict square-root margin. The physical height and sign margins hold since the frozen evaluations are actual values at a high panel point. The cofactor shifts and modulus splits meet the same hypotheses as in the previous estimate. [Full-cycle scalar estimator](cogentic-cofactor-dispersion.md#one--and-two-mark-counts)

### Narrow low intervals

Otherwise the ratio invariant implies both low widths are at most \(Z e^{O(H)}\). Use the full high parallelogram and the low-coefficient quotient coupling from the high-direction note. Its smaller physical singular width before reinstating \(g\) is \(mZ e^{-O(H)}\). The two low columns have norms at most \(m e^{O(H)}\). If needed, divide each low interval into balanced panels of width at most \(Z e^{-C''H}\) with the implicit scale guard for the singular value. The number of panels is \(e^{O(H)}\). Shorter intervals are used without splitting. A split panel still has leading width \(50000V\), so it does not approach the correction floor of leading width \(10000V\).

Subtract the new interval origins before applying the high-lattice comparison. The physical variation within a low panel is \(e^{-C'H}\) times the smaller high singular width. The cofactor contribution varies only by \(e^{(10003+o(1))V}\) before reinstating \(g\), likewise negligible on that scale. Thus the conditional high rectangle is coupled to the corresponding translated lattice coset in the frozen parallelogram with error \(O(e^{-C'H})\). Each low panel is still long enough for the quotient mixing by \(k,x,y\). The exact representative bounds \(m^2\) and \((2A)^{r+2}e^{O(H)}\) and the correction floors absorb the losses from the common class split. Consequently the unconditional law within a fixed original coefficient class, with the three corrections averaged, is close to uniform on the translated full evaluation lattice inside the high parallelogram. This replacement makes no assertion about the full cofactor coordinate. All cofactor-dependent factors of the retained term are already constant on that class.

Use the axis-aligned small-modulus progressions inside that parallelogram as in the [high-direction estimate](cogentic-high-degree-two-directions.md#scalar-panels-and-the-residual-search-cost). The physical progression lengths have leading logarithm \(50001V\), which strictly exceeds the square-root threshold \(50000.5V\). Geometric boundary, coset and scalar errors can each be made \(e^{-C'H}\) with fixed larger constants. The estimate averages with the original residue-class and panel weights.

## Resulting box

The broad and narrow regimes cover each intermediate box, including both children of every split. Their scale comparison uses \(Z=T A^r\) itself, so no unknown \(e^{o(V)}\) factor is used as a panel budget. Independent field factors combine by telescoping since their normalized fractions are bounded by one. Inserting all exact weights and summing the finite score expansion yields additive error \(e^{-CH}\) at cost \(e^{O(H)}\) per comparison.

Choose the smaller estimated child until all four low widths are between \(X_0\) and \(2X_0+O(1)\). Integer child weights give the parent mean as their weighted average. The chosen child's true mean exceeds the parent's by at most twice the estimator error. There are \(O(V)\) splits, so the resulting mean is still \(o(1)\). In each field the resulting leading coordinate exponents in the order \((k,x,y,u,v)\) are
\[
 (2,10000,10000,40000,40000).
\]
This construction assumes the existing fixed-schedule moment and conditional-score extractor implications, rather than a separate proof of those analytic and rank inputs. The mean bound guarantees that some tuple in the final product box has score below one. The remaining averaging in all five coordinates is essential to the estimates here. Their exhaustive product still has leading exponent \(100002\) per field on the \(V\) scale, and no efficient final selection follows from this descent.
