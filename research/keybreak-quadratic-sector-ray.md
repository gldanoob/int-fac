# Smooth quadratic ideals in ray classes and angular sectors

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of fundamental discriminant \(D\), with \(V=\sqrt{|D|}\). Let \(\mathfrak m\) be a finite integral ideal modulus with \(m_0=\mathrm N\mathfrak m=V^{o(1)}\), let \(h_{\mathfrak m}\) be the ray class number, and take an integer \(J\ge1\) with \(J=V^{o(1)}\). Fix a smoothness exponent \(c>e^{-1/2}\). Choose a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small that \(s=1+\varepsilon\) satisfies \(s<2c\) and \(\log(s/c)<1/2\), and put \(X=V^s\). If \(c\ge1\), one may use any smaller fixed cutoff exponent in \((e^{-1/2},1)\). Fix an integer \(Z\) with \(\log Z\le(\log V)^C\) for a fixed \(C\). Enlarge the forbidden rational primes to include those dividing \(2Z\), those dividing \(m_0\), and all primes at most \(z=(\log V)^4\). Let \(Z_*\) be the product of these distinct primes.

For each ordinary class \(a\), fix an integral ideal \(I_a\) in the inverse class coprime to the rational primes dividing \(m_0\). Such a representative can be obtained from any representative by multiplication by an element in its inverse ideal that is a local generator at the specified primes. For an integral ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of ordinary class \(a\), define its angle by a generator of \(\mathfrak a I_a\), taken modulo the rotations by all units of \(K\). Normalize this angular circle to \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).

Under Dirichlet GRH, every target ray class and every angular arc of length \(1/J\) contain
\[
 \gg \frac{X^2}{h_{\mathfrak m}J(\log V)^{O(1)}}
\]
distinct primitive integral ideals of norm at most \(X^2\), with \(V^c\)-smooth norms coprime to \(Z_*\). Each is a product of two primitive ideals of norm at most \(X\) with relatively prime rational norms. The argument uses a majority of the ray-class and sector cells and the global ordinary smoothness estimate. [Ordinary smoothness estimate](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md) · [Ordinary completion argument](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md)

## Counting one ray and angular cell

Let \(w\) be the number of units of \(K\), let \(w_{\mathfrak m}\) count the units congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak m\), and put \(\Phi=|(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m)^\times|\). Each fibre over an ordinary class has size \(\Phi w_{\mathfrak m}/w\), so \(h_{\mathfrak m}=h\Phi w_{\mathfrak m}/w\). Indeed, an ideal of ordinary class \(a\) coprime to \(\mathfrak m\) has \(\mathfrak a I_a=(\alpha)\) with \(\alpha\in I_a\) a unit modulo \(\mathfrak m\). Two such ideals lie in the same ray class exactly when their generator residues differ by a unit of \(K\). Fix one of the residues in the associated orbit in \(I_a/\mathfrak m I_a\). Each ideal in the ray class corresponds to exactly \(w_{\mathfrak m}\) generators in this fixed affine coset, and all of those generators have the same angle modulo unit rotations.

Write \(\lambda_a\) for the shortest length in the normalized lattice \(I_a/\sqrt{\mathrm N I_a}\). For all but \(o(h)\) ordinary classes, \(\lambda_a\ge\sqrt V/(\log V)^{K_0}\) for a fixed sufficiently large \(K_0\). These are the balanced classes of the ordinary count. The proportion of rays over unbalanced classes is also \(o(1)\) since every ordinary class has an equally sized ray fibre. [Balanced class bound](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md#uniform-counts-in-balanced-classes)

In normalized generator coordinates, the constraints of norm at most \(U\) and angle in an arc of length \(\beta\) give a union of at most \(w\) sectors of the disk of radius \(R=\sqrt U\), with total area \(\pi U\beta\). In a translate of a lattice of covolume \(\Delta\) and shortest length \(\lambda\), the count equals its area divided by \(\Delta\) with error \(O(R/\lambda+1)\), uniformly in the angle and translation. To see this, use rows parallel to a shortest vector, with spacing \(\Delta/\lambda\gg\lambda\). Count points along each chord and then approximate the sum of the chord lengths by its area integral. Each of the sectors has opening at most \(\pi\), and its chord length as a function of the row coordinate has total variation \(O(R)\) by convexity. The chord approximation and the row-rounding errors are thus both \(O(R/\lambda+1)\). The same bound includes points on the arc boundaries when half-open cells are used.

For a ray class the affine lattice is a translate of \(\mathfrak m I_a\), with normalized covolume \(Vm_0/2\) and shortest length at least \(\lambda_a\). To impose the exclusions at rational primes dividing \(m_0\), put \(Q=\operatorname{rad}(m_0)\) and refine the fixed affine coset modulo \(\mathfrak m Q I_a\). There are at most \(Q^2\le m_0^2\) resulting cosets. At each of these rational primes, requiring the residue to be a unit at every prime above it gives the usual local coprimality density divided by the unit density already imposed by the corresponding part of \(\mathfrak m\). This follows because the units modulo the larger local modulus map surjectively, with equal fibre size, onto the units modulo the local part of \(\mathfrak m\). At rational primes away from \(m_0\), the primitivity and coprimality densities are the same as in the ordinary count by CRT.

Let \(\mathcal D_{Z_*}\) be the product of ordinary primitivity and coprimality densities and let \(C_{Z_*}=L(1,\chi_D)\mathcal D_{Z_*}\). Both are bounded below by \((\log\log V)^{-O(1)}\). The conditional density of these restrictions in the fixed unit residue is \(\mathcal D_{Z_*}m_0/\Phi\). For completeness, use a fixed large polylogarithmic prime cutoff \(U_0\) as in the ordinary count. For primes up to \(U_0\) away from \(m_0\), Bonferroni truncation at consecutive orders of size \(\asymp\log\log V\) uses \(V^{o(1)}\) CRT cosets. Including the preceding refinement, the total sector discrepancy is at most \(m_0^2 V^{o(1)}(\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\). The omitted terms of the independent density product are smaller than any fixed negative power of \(\log\log V\).

For primitivity at rational primes \(p>U_0\) away from \(m_0\), the intersection with \(pI_a\) has lattice \(p\mathfrak m I_a\), with covolume increased by \(p^2\) and shortest length at least \(p\lambda_a\). A nonzero point in \(pI_a\) requires \(p\le\sqrt X/\lambda_a\). Summing the errors costs \(O((\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\log X)\), and summing the sector areas costs \(O(X\beta/(Vm_0U_0))\). For each remaining forbidden prime \(p\mid Z_*\), the intersection with a prime ideal above \(p\) gives covolume increased by at least \(p\) and shortest length at least \(\lambda_a\). The corresponding totals are \(O((\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\log Z_*)\) and \(O(X\beta\log Z_* /(Vm_0U_0))\). Take \(U_0\) large enough to make these area contributions negligible relative to the allowed density. When \(a\) is balanced, the discrepancy terms are \(V^{\varepsilon/2+o(1)}\). For \(\beta^{-1}=V^{o(1)}\), the main scale is at least \(V^{\varepsilon-o(1)}\), uniformly in the affine coset and the angular arc.

It follows that the number of primitive ideals of norm at most \(X\) coprime to \(Z_*\) in a balanced ray and angular arc of length \(\beta\) is uniformly
\[
 (1+o(1))\frac{2\pi X\beta}{w_{\mathfrak m}V\Phi}\mathcal D_{Z_*}
 =(1+o(1))\frac{C_{Z_*}X\beta}{h_{\mathfrak m}}.
\]
The last equality is the imaginary quadratic class number formula and the ray fibre size above.

## Matching good cells into a prescribed sector

Partition the angular circle into \(L=8J\) equal half-open cells. Each ray and cell above a balanced ordinary class contains \((1+o(1))\mu\) admitted primitive ideals, where \(\mu=C_{Z_*}X/(h_{\mathfrak m}L)\). The global number of admitted ideals with a norm prime factor above \(V^c\) is at most \((\log(s/c)+o(1))C_{Z_*}X\) by the ordinary smoothness estimate. It applies to \(Z_*\) since \(\log Z_*\) is bounded by a fixed power of \(\log V\). The ray and angular cells partition the ideals in that estimate. Choose a fixed \(\tau>0\) so that \(\log(s/c)/(1-\tau)<1/2\). Any balanced cell with fewer than \(\tau\mu\) admitted smooth ideals contributes at least \((1-\tau-o(1))\mu\) bad ideals. Since unbalanced cells are a proportion \(o(1)\), a proportion at least \(1/2+\xi\) of all ray and angular cells are balanced and contain at least \(\tau\mu\) admitted smooth ideals, for some fixed \(\xi>0\). [Global bound from real moments](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md#sharper-cutoff-by-real-moments)

Fix a target ray and angular arc. A first ray forces a unique complementary ray. If the underlying ordinary classes of the two rays are \(a,b\), the product angle is the sum of the two angles plus a fixed rotation \(\theta_{a,b}\). This rotation is the angle of a generator of the principal fractional ideal \(I_{ab}(I_aI_b)^{-1}\). For each first ray, match angular cells with indices \(i,j\) so that \(i+j\) is fixed modulo \(L\). Choose that sum to put the two cell centres, after rotation, as close as possible to the centre of the target arc. Centre rounding costs at most \(1/(2L)\), and the two points within their cells add at most \(1/L\) of angular error. This total is less than \(1/(2J)\), so the entire product of each matched pair of cells is in the target arc. The ray and cell matching is a bijection. By the majority bound, at least \(2\xi h_{\mathfrak m}L\) first cells are good and have a good partner cell.

For each first ideal in such a cell, discard partners sharing any prime \(q\) in its rational norm. All these primes exceed \(z\) and are coprime to \(m_0\). Count divisible partners in the original balanced ray and angular cell: for a prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of norm \(q\) above \(q\), the intersected affine lattice has difference lattice \(\mathfrak m\mathfrak q I_b\), and there are at most two choices of \(\mathfrak q\). Its sector count is bounded by
\[
 O\left(\frac{X}{Vm_0Lq}+\frac{\sqrt X}{\lambda_b}+1\right).
\]
Summing over \(O(\log X)\) prime divisors of the first norm gives \(o(\mu)\), since the area term is reduced by \((\log X)/z\) and the total discrepancy is only \(V^{\varepsilon/2+o(1)}\). Thus there are \(\gg h_{\mathfrak m} L\mu^2\) surviving ordered pairs. Their norms are mutually coprime, so their products are primitive.

Finally discard pairs having either ideal with more than \(T=(\log V)^B\) integral ideal divisors. The global number of such ideals of norm at most \(X\) is \(O(X(1+\log X)^3/T)\) by coefficientwise \(\zeta_K^2\le\zeta^4\). Each has one possible complementary ray and cell under the matching, with at most \(O(X/(Vm_0L))\) possible partners if that cell is balanced. The same estimate holds when the high-divisor ideal is the second factor since the matching is bijective. The total loss is negligible compared to \(h_{\mathfrak m}L\mu^2\) for sufficiently large fixed \(B\), using \(h_{\mathfrak m}\ll Vm_0(\log V)^{O(1)}\) and \(C_{Z_*}\ge(\log\log V)^{-O(1)}\). Each surviving product has at most \(T^2\) integral ideal divisors and hence at most \(T^2\) ordered factorizations. Division by this bound and \(L=8J\) give the result.

## Arbitrary fixed smoothness in logarithmically small sectors and ray classes

Fix positive constants \(c,M,C,A\). Put \(T\le |D|\le2T\), \(V=\sqrt{|D|}\), and restrict to \(m_0=\mathrm N\mathfrak m\le(\log V)^M\), \(1\le J\le(\log V)^M\), \(\log Z\le(\log V)^C\). Outside \(O(T/(\log T)^A)\) negative fundamental discriminants, every ray class and every ordinary angular arc of length \(1/J\) contain
\[
 \gg \frac{V^2Y}{h_{\mathfrak m}J(\log V)^{O(1)}}
\]
distinct primitive ideals of norm at most \(V^2Y\), with every rational prime at most \(V^c\) and coprime to \(2Dm_0Z\). Here \(Y=(\log V)^b\), with \(b\) fixed in terms of \(c,M,C,A\). The same exceptional set works for all the stated choices. The earlier majority proof permits subpower moduli and sectors at its larger smoothness cutoff. [Ordinary-class product estimate](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md#every-class-at-arbitrary-fixed-smoothness-for-almost-all-discriminants)

On principal ideals take the unit-circle character \((\alpha)\mapsto(\alpha/|\alpha|)^w\), where \(w\) is the number of roots of unity. Extend it to a character \(\Omega\) on fractional ideals. Such an extension exists since the circle is divisible (equivalently, choose roots on generators of the finite class group). It is an unramified finite-place Hecke character with the indicated infinity type. Within a fixed ordinary class, \(\Omega\) gives the generator angle defined at the start of this note up to a fixed rotation. For a ray character \(\psi\) and an integer \(n\), put \(\xi=\psi\Omega^n\). For \(|n|\le(\log V)^{M+2}\), its cuspidal inductions have analytic conductor \(O(Tm_0(1+w|n|)^2)\). The arithmetic conductor equals \(|D|\,\mathrm N\mathfrak f_\xi\), with \(\mathfrak f_\xi\mid\mathfrak m\), by the conductor formula for quadratic induction. In the automorphic zero-density argument each bad cuspidal representation of conductor \(Q\ll T(\log T)^{O(1)}\) can affect at most \(\tau(Q_{\rm arith})=T^{o(1)}\) quadratic discriminants, since \(|D|\mid Q_{\rm arith}\). This accounts uniformly for all moduli and frequencies. Take the near-one strip narrow enough that their combined exception is \(O(T^{1/2})\). The local parameters are unitary, also for nonzero infinity type; the zero count and explicit formula used for the ordinary guard remain uniform with this conductor and bounded degree. Thus the normalized log-scale prime guard has transform \(O((\log V)^{-H})\) at all these cuspidal frequencies for any prescribed \(H\), by choosing \(b\). [Family zero estimate, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.07717#page=3)

An induction is noncuspidal precisely when \(\xi=\xi^{\mathrm{conj}}\). In that case \(n=0\), since conjugation reverses the infinity type. The invariant finite-order character descends through the norm, giving two Dirichlet characters \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda\chi_D\) on induction. Equivalently, extend the invariant one-dimensional Weil character over the index-two subgroup by choosing a square root of its value on the square of a coset representative; the two choices differ by \(\chi_D\). Unless the ray character is trivial both Dirichlet factors are nonprincipal. Their primitive conductor product is \(|D|\,\mathrm N\mathfrak f_\xi\). For a fixed bad conductor \(q\asymp U\), existence of any admissible modulus therefore implies \(q\mid |D|f\) for some integer \(f\le L=(\log(2T))^M\) (increasing the constant in \(L\) if necessary). For fixed \(f\), the number of possible \(D\) is \(O(T\gcd(q,f)/q)\), since the nonzero integer \(|D|\) is a multiple of \(q/\gcd(q,f)\) and is at most \(2T\). Summing over \(f\le L\) gives \(O(TL^2/q)\).

At polynomial prime scales the small primitive conductors \(q\le(\log T)^B\) obey Siegel–Walfisz. In each larger dyad the GL(1) zero-density estimate leaves \(O(U^{1/2})\) bad primitive characters for a fixed narrow strip up to height \(2U\). For the good ones the smoothed explicit formula at \(y\asymp V^{1/k}\) gives relative error \(O(y^{-\eta}\log(2U)+\log(2U)/U+ y^{-1/2}\log^2(2y))\). It is \(O((\log V)^{-H})\) for large enough fixed \(B\). Summing the bad-conductor incidence bounds over dyads costs \(O(T L^2/(\log T)^{B/2})\). At each modulus the number of invariant ray characters is at most \(m_0|\mathrm{Cl}(D)[2]|\): fix the restriction to the kernel of projection to the ordinary class group, of size at most \(m_0\), then quotients with that restriction are ordinary invariant characters. As in the ordinary proof, the last factor is at most \((\log T)^{A+3}\) outside the desired exceptional set. This treats the invariant frequencies in the polynomial prime intervals.

Take exactly the two products from disjoint prime intervals and the additional oriented prime of the ordinary proof. For each frequency \(n\), weight their ray distributions by \(\Omega^n\). The absolute value at any ray of either weighted polynomial-product distribution is bounded by its ordinary class mass \(O((\log V)^k/V)\). Parseval over the ray group thus costs at most \(O((\log V)^{M+k+1})\) for either sum of squares, uniformly in \(n\). Fourier inversion on the ray group and the two-factor estimate give, at every target ray, its probability \((1+o(1))/h_{\mathfrak m}\) for \(n=0\), and weighted mass \(O((\log V)^{-H+M+k+1}/h_{\mathfrak m})\) for \(0<|n|\le(\log V)^{M+2}\). In the zeroth mode remove the invariant terms separately using their count and polynomial-interval bounds. After normalization on the target ray, the Erdős–Turán discrepancy inequality with cutoff \(\lfloor(\log V)^{M+1}\rfloor\) gives interval discrepancy \(o(1/J)\) when \(H\) is sufficiently large. Rotating to the generator angle preserves this estimate. Distinct tuples remain distinct ideals, giving the claimed count from their maximum weights.

**A geometric cost for prescribing a ray and a sector.** Suppose \(D<-4\), let \(q\ge3\) be an odd prime, and take \(\mathfrak m=q\mathcal O_K\). Write \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z+\omega\mathbb Z\), with \(\Im\omega=V/2\). Set \(y_0=(q-1)/2\) and choose \(x_0\bmod q\) so that \(x_0+y_0\omega\) is a unit modulo \(q\): its norm polynomial in \(x_0\) is monic quadratic, hence rules out at most two residues. In its principal ray class every integral generator \(x+y\omega\) has \(y\equiv\pm y_0\pmod q\), so \(|y|\ge(q-1)/2\). Here the ray class is the class of the indicated principal ideal; its ordinary ideal class is principal. Use \(I_1=\mathcal O_K\) for the angle or rotate the target sector accordingly. In the arc of normalized length \(1/J\) centred on the real axis modulo sign,
\[
  V|y|/2 \;=\; |\Im(x+y\omega)| \;\le\; |x+y\omega|\sin(\pi/(2J)) \;\le\; |x+y\omega|\pi/(2J).
\]
Thus every ideal in this ray and arc has norm at least \(V^2(q-1)^2J^2/(4\pi^2)\), even before requiring smoothness or primitivity. This gives a lower bound of order \(V^2\,\mathrm N\mathfrak m\,J^2\) for this family of prescribed targets.

## Subpower smoothness in polynomially thin angular sectors

Fix \(s>2\), put \(X=V^s\), and let \(\log Y=(\log V)^{\theta+o(1)}\) for fixed \(0<\theta<1\), \(u=\log X/\log Y\) and \(E=u\log u\). For some fixed \(\sigma>0\) depending on the fixed parameters, the preceding result extends to all \(1\le J\le V^\sigma\) at this smoothness scale. Outside \(O(T/(\log T)^A)\) discriminants the number of primitive \(Y\)-smooth ideals coprime to the stated exclusions, in each prescribed polylogarithmic ray and each arc of length \(1/J\), is
\[
                 \frac{X}{h_{\mathfrak m}J}\exp[-(1+o(1))E].
\]
In particular it includes every \(J=V^{o(1)}\). The positive exponent obtained here comes from a near-one zero-density margin; the geometric bound above still gives a separate necessary condition. [Ordinary subpower-cutoff bounds and basic estimates](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md#smooth-norms-in-every-class-at-subpower-prime-cutoffs)

First allow angular frequencies up to \(V^2\) in the earlier simultaneous zero-density argument, taking the margin sufficiently small. The analytic conductors are still polynomial in \(T\) and the arithmetic conductor still has \(|D|\) as a divisor. The smoothed normalized oriented cuspidal prime transform on any of the prescribed prime bins of scale \(y=Y^{a+o(1)}\), for fixed \(a>0\), is \(O(Y^{-a\eta'})\) for some fixed \(\eta'>0\), reducing it to allow fixed powers of logarithms in weights and interval widths. Only frequency zero has invariant terms.

For the lower bound choose fixed \(0<g<s-2\). Use three independent balanced prime-bin products, of norm scales \(V^{(s-g)/2}, V^{(s-g)/2}, V^g\), with fixed factors of room in the cutoffs to keep the total norm at most \(X\). Separate their prime supports by setting their upper prime cutoffs to \(Y\exp[-C_i\log Y/\log u]\) for three fixed, sufficiently separated constants \(C_i\ge0\). The construction occupies a log interval of width \(O(\log Y/\log u)\) just below each cutoff, and all three cutoffs are \(Y^{1-o(1)}\). The first two distributions retain the primitive per-class Parseval bound \(\exp(O(E))=V^{o(1)}\). For the third use all orientations and a smooth nonnegative prime weight within each bin, bounded by one after normalization of its amplitude. Give each admissible index pattern mass proportional to its product of bin masses. The total unnormalized mass has the same leading lower count as before because each bin has a fixed proportion of its expected prime mass. For every pattern its independent prime transforms multiply to at most \(V^{-g\eta'+o(1)}\) on all cuspidal frequencies. Convex averaging over the patterns preserves the bound. At invariant nonprincipal frequencies the logarithmic bound from even one bin suffices for the zeroth angular mode. The total of the three smoothness losses is \((1+o(1))E\). Taking \(\sigma<g\eta'/2\), the conditional angular discrepancy at each target ray is \(o(1/J)\) by Erdős–Turán up to frequency \(\lfloor V^{2\sigma}\rfloor\). Disjointness and the maximum tuple probability give the lower count.

For the upper bound, in any fixed ordinary class the number of primitive ideals of norm at most \(W\) in an arc of length \(1/J\) is \(O(1+W/(VJ))\), uniformly in the arc and class. Indeed on rotating the normalized ideal lattice, each sector is contained in an origin-centred rectangle with sides \(O(\sqrt W), O(\sqrt W/J)\). The primitive rectangle bound gives the estimate after accounting for the bounded unit group. It also holds in the target arc after removing any fixed factor, since its angular character translates the arc. The extra polylogarithmic ray index fits in \(\exp(o(E))\), so the ordinary-class upper count suffices if it retains the angular factor.

For concreteness choose small fixed \(g,\tau>0\) with \(g+\tau<s-2\), and choose \(0<a<(s-g-\tau-1)/s\). Take \(\sigma\) still smaller so \(\sigma<s/2-1\), \(a<(s-g-\tau-1-\sigma)/s\), and \(\sigma<a\eta'g/2\). The sector cap absorbs norms below \(X\exp(-2E)\). Removing ideals with powerful part of norm above \(V^\tau\) costs \(O(X^{1/2+o(1)}+(X/(VJ))V^{-\tau/2+o(1)})\), which is negligible. At a fixed remaining powerful part \(P\), put \(X'=X/\mathrm NP\). The squarefree remainder has norm between \(X'\exp(-2E)\) and \(X'\). Set \(l=\lfloor g\log V/\log Y\rfloor\). For fewer than \(l\) prime factors above \(Y^a\), the removed large part has norm at most \(V^g\) and total harmonic mass \(\exp(o(E))\). For the residual norm dyad of upper endpoint \(W\), split off a divisor near \(W/(VJ)\), within a factor \(Y^a\) below it. Use the sector cap for its complementary class and arc. The harmonic Rankin estimate at \(Y^a\) supplies a loss stronger than \(E\) by the inequality for \(a\), retaining the \(1/J\) factor throughout.

With at least \(l\) large distinct factors, count ordered selections and divide by \(l!\). The two remaining products can be put in dyads above \(V\) by the square-root split. To keep the full density without having to enumerate all guard dyads, put \(\beta=\log u/\log Y\). Bound the indicator of the total norm constraint by \((X')^{1-\beta}\) times each factor's norm to the power \(-1+\beta\), then drop that constraint. In either remaining dyad of scale \(x_i\) the total weighted mass is \(\exp(o(E))\) by Rankin; the maximum class mass is \(O(x_i^\beta/V)\). Thus its squared Fourier norm, with or without angular weighting, is \(V^{o(1)}\). The list of dyad pairs has polylogarithmic size.

The harmonic guard weight on all oriented primes over \([Y^a,Y]\) has a positive majorant of total mass \(H_1\ll u/\log u\), by summing smooth broad dyad majorants weighted by \(q^{-1+\beta}\) and partial summation. A smooth weight at each dyad can majorize the varying factor there up to a fixed multiplier. Its normalized cuspidal transform is \(O(Y^{-a\eta'})\); the corresponding convex combination retains that bound. Ramified primes can be included with relative error \(Y^{-a+o(1)}\) in their dyads. Since \(l\asymp u\), \(H_1^l/l!\le\exp(o(E))\). Majorize the angular interval by a trigonometric polynomial up to degree \(\lfloor V^{2\sigma}\rfloor\) with constant coefficient \(O(1/J)\) and sum of absolute coefficients \(O(\log V)\) (the usual Selberg interval majorant). At frequency zero the invariant contributions times the constant coefficient are \(\exp(o(E))/J\); all cuspidal contributions, including every nonzero angular mode, are \(O(V^{-a\eta'g+o(1)})\) relative to the uniform-class factor and the extracted guard mass. This gives the upper \((X')^{1-\beta}\exp(o(E))/(hJ)\). Finally sum over \(P\) using convergence of the powerful-ideal reciprocal series at \(1-\beta>1/2\) bounded away from that endpoint. Since \(X^{1-\beta}=X\exp(-E)\), the claimed exponent follows.

## Averaging angular collisions at the first norm scale

Keep \(\log Y=(\log V)^{\theta+o(1)}\), \(0<\theta<1\), and the earlier polylogarithmic bound on the ray modulus. Fix \(0\le\kappa<3\) and allow \(1\le J\le V^\kappa\). Write \(W_0=VJ\), \(u_*=\log W_0/\log Y\), \(E_*=u_*\log u_*\). For any fixed \(\delta>0\), the following hold outside \(O_A(T/(\log T)^A)\) fundamental discriminants in the dyad.

* At \(X=W_0\exp((1+\delta)E_*)\), all but an \(\exp(-c_\delta E_*)\) fraction of the ray and angular cells have at least \(X\exp(-(1+o(1))E_*)/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\) primitive smooth ideals. Take any fixed partition into \(J\) equal angular intervals at each ray. At \(X=W_0\exp((1-\delta)E_*)\), only an \(\exp(-\delta E_*+o(E_*))\) fraction of such cells can be occupied by the global Rankin bound.
* At \(X=W_0^2\exp((2+\delta)E_*)\), **every** ray and every angular interval of length \(1/J\) contains at least \(X\exp(-(2+o(1))E_*)/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\) such ideals.
* If \(X=V^s\), \(s>2+2\kappa\) is fixed, every ray and every angular interval of length \(1/J\) contain
  \[
    \frac{X}{h_{\mathfrak m}J}\exp(-(1+o(1))u\log u),\qquad
    u=\log X/\log Y
  \]
  primitive smooth ideals subject to the stated coprimality restrictions.

All angles use the ordinary unit quotient, and a change of the reference ideal translates the angular coordinate within its class. The exceptional family includes the underlying simultaneous prime estimates, now with angular frequency up to \(V^{\kappa+1}\). This changes only the choice of the fixed zero-density margin. The statements permit common parameters and bin systems fixed on each discriminant dyad. [Ordinary equal-class count](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md#averaging-equal-class-pairs-gives-the-sharp-almost-all-classes-threshold)

**Counting pairs in a small angular interval.** Use a frequency cutoff \(K=\lceil J\log^2 V\rceil\). A nonnegative triangular function on the circle of width \(1/(2K)\), height \(O(K)\), and integral one has nonnegative Fourier coefficients bounded below by an absolute constant on \(|j|\le K\). Thus the sum of squared transforms at all rays and \(|j|\le K\) for a nonnegative ideal measure is at most a constant times \(h_{\mathfrak m}K\) times its ordered same-class pair mass with angular difference \(O(1/K)\). Finite-character orthogonality first imposes equality of the rays. Dropping that condition to ordinary-class equality is allowed in the resulting nonnegative close-pair count. We use \(K=J(\log V)^{O(1)}\) throughout the upper bounds.

For a pair of primitive ideal norms \(n_1,n_2\asymp W\), define \(g\) as in the ordinary collision argument, including multiplicities. At a split prime with matching orientation remove the smaller exponent, and at a ramified prime shared by both ideals remove one rational prime. Inert rational primes cannot divide a primitive ideal. The quotient generator remains primitive and satisfies \(t^2+|D|v^2=4n_1n_2/g^2\). In the same ordinary class its argument modulo the units is precisely the angular difference, since the generator differs from a generator of \(I/J\) by a positive rational factor. Thus \(|v|\ll W/(gVK)\). Only the diagonal has \(v=0\). At odd primes the root bound in the ordinary argument applies unchanged. For even \(m=n_1n_2/g^2\), \(4\mid v\) would force \(t/2\) even and make the generator divisible by two. The two-adic number of roots is therefore bounded absolutely in this case also. The number of possible \(g\)'s and of orientations above the two norms is at most \(C^{\omega(n_1)+\omega(n_2)}\) for an absolute constant \(C\); at each shared prime the exponent of \(g\) is either zero or the smaller given exponent.

For \(W=VJ\exp(o(\log V))\le V^2\), sum the per-\(v\) bound from the ordinary proof up to \(O(W/(gVK))\). The result per norm pair and choice of \(g\) is \(O((V/K+W/(VK))\log(2W))=O((V/K)\log(2W))\). The stronger estimate needed above \(V^2\) follows by summing over the candidate norms before estimating the integer rounding error.

**Near-square products above \(V^2\).** Take \(W=VJ\exp(o(\log V))\) and \(W\le V^{4-\epsilon}\). For a nonzero \(v\), \(g|v|\ll W/(VK)=V^{o(1)}\). Fix \(g,v\), write \(l=n_1/g,\ b=n_2/g\asymp W'=W/g\) with both factors in constant-ratio intervals, and partition each by its residue modulo \(v^2\). Retain only joint residue pairs consistent with the local primitivity conditions. In each pair there are \(O(2^{\omega(v)})\) allowed residues \(r\) for \(|t|\). For each \(r\) the interval for \(|t|\) gives a translate of a periodic interval of length \(O(\Delta)\), where \(\Delta=V^2/W'\), for \(2\sqrt{lb}/v^2\) modulo one. If \(\Delta\) is bounded below, the direct estimate \(O(1+\Delta)=O(\Delta)\) counts the possible lifts of each residue. Otherwise the interval has length less than one by taking the cutoff for this case sufficiently small. Choose the Selberg majorant to degree \(H=\lceil V^\zeta/\Delta\rceil\), with fixed small \(\zeta>0\). Its constant coefficient and every other coefficient are \(O(\Delta)\). The main term retains the true product of the two nonnegative rational norm weights in that residue pair, times \(O(\Delta)\) for each allowed \(r\). Summing residue pairs and then \(1\le |v|\ll W/(gVK)\) at fixed norms and \(g\) costs \(O((V/K)\log(2W))\) times those weights and the orientation multiplicity.

Here is a power saving for all nonzero Fourier terms, allowing arbitrary separable rational norm weights bounded by \(V^{o(1)}\). For \(h\asymp H_1\), group the points \(x=h\sqrt{l/W'}\) and \(y=(2W'/v^2)\sqrt{b/W'}\). Include the phase from the residue \(r\) in the coefficient indexed by \(h\). Apply the one-dimensional double large sieve with bounding interval lengths \(O(H_1),O(W'/v^2)\). Close \(y\)-pairs number \(O(W'(1+v^2/H_1))\). A close \(x\)-pair satisfies
\[
 |h^2 l-h'^2 l'|\ll H_1 v^2 .
\]
For \(d=(h,h')\), dividing the difference by \(d^2\) leaves \(O(1+H_1 v^2/d^2)\) integer differences and \(O(1+W'd^2/H_1^2)\) solutions for each difference by a linear congruence. Summing over \(h,h'\) and using \(\sum_{h,h'\ll H_1}(h,h')^2\ll H_1^3\) gives \(O((H_1^3+W'H_1)V^{o(1)})\) close \(x\)-pairs. Since \(W'\le V^{4-\epsilon}\), choose \(\zeta<\epsilon/4\); then \(H_1^2\ll W'\). Coincident points are included in the labelled pair counts. The sieve bounds the dyadic error by \(\Delta H_1 W'^{3/2}V^{o(1)}\), including the bounded norm weights and the Selberg coefficients. Summing gives \(W'^{3/2}V^{\zeta+o(1)}\), power-smaller than \(W' V^2\). There are only \(V^{o(1)}\) choices of \(g,v\), residues, and dyads. The total error is \(O(W V^2 V^{-c_\epsilon})\). This is power-smaller than the total main scale \(W^2 V/K\); subpower smooth densities and norm multiplicities fit in the margin. **Occupancy.** For the lower count take the same independent base packets and prime guards as in the ordinary proof, at packet norm \(W\asymp VJ\exp(E_*)\). With unnormalized rational weights indicating the packet norms, their total norm count is \(W\exp(-E_*+o(E_*))\), and \(2^{O(\omega(n))}=\exp(o(E_*))\) on this support. On normalizing the squared transform sum, the averaged off-diagonal is \(\exp(o(E_*))\) by the preceding estimates, and the diagonal costs \(h_{\mathfrak m}K/S_D=\exp(o(E_*))\). Markov preserves this bound for typical fields. With one packet the small guards then give squared low-frequency cuspidal mass \(\exp(-2\eta'\delta E_*+o(E_*))\). To pass to counts in the equal angular cells use a Selberg minorant of degree \(K\), with constant coefficient \(1/J-O(1/K)\) and all coefficients \(O(1/J)\). Discrete Parseval across the \(J\) translates incurs a multiplicity \(O(1+K/J)\) when frequencies agree modulo \(J\), by Cauchy–Schwarz. Ray orthogonality gives the asserted exceptional fraction after normalizing by \(h_{\mathfrak m}J\); the invariant nonprincipal modes at frequency zero have uniform \(o(1)\) relative contribution. This argument also permits cell origins depending on the ray: first apply angular Parseval and the alias multiplicity at each ray, take absolute squares of each fixed-frequency ray inversion, then use ray orthogonality. The phases from translating the grid disappear in the squares.

With two independent packets, Cauchy–Schwarz on the *joint* list of ray and angular frequencies bounds the sum of absolute products by \(\exp(o(E_*))\). Multiplying by the guard transform and the uniform \(O(1/J)\) coefficient bound gives \(o(1/J)\) after the uniform ray factor is removed. Hence every prescribed interval acquires the lower count. The maximum probability per ideal tuple and disjoint prime supports complete the proof as before. If \(s>2+2\kappa\), an additional disjoint balanced packet uses the remaining polynomial norm allowance; each of its ideals translates the target ray and arc. The sum of the leading packet losses equals \(u\log u+o(u\log u)\) because their log norms add to \(\log X+o(\log V)\) and their prime cutoffs have logarithms \((1+o(1))\log Y\).

**Upper bound in each arc.** Put \(E=u\log u\), \(\beta=\log u/\log Y\), and take \(x=VJ\exp(3E+2\log Y)\). Below \(X\exp(-2E)\) the primitive sector cap above suffices. For each remaining smooth primitive ideal, peel off successive prime ideals until their norm product first crosses \(x/Y\). Repeat once. Both factors lie in \([x/Y,x]\); the strict margin \(s>2+2\kappa\) ensures enough norm for both selections. Each factor is primitive even when some rational primes repeat. Bound their dyadic distributions using *all* primitive smooth ideals with weights \(n^{-1+\beta}\). After extracting the common weight \(O(W^{-1+\beta})\) on a dyad, the rational norm weights and orientation divisors are \(V^{o(1)}\) as needed for the power-saving error. For the main term and diagonal use the sharper Euler bound
\[
 \sum_{n\ Y\text{-smooth}} C^{\omega(n)} n^{-1+\beta}
 \le \exp\bigl(O_C(\log\log Y+u/\log u)\bigr)=\exp(o(E)).
\]
Thus the weighted joint squared Fourier norm averaged over fields is \(\exp(o(E))\). In detail the off-diagonal main count costs \(O((V/K)\exp(o(E)))\) before averaging and multiplication by \(h_{\mathfrak m}K\). The diagonal costs \(h_{\mathfrak m}K W^{-1+\beta}\exp(o(E))=\exp(o(E))\) or less. The power error acquires \(W^{-2+2\beta}\) and remains negligible under the same normalization. Taking a logarithmically larger Markov threshold handles all the dyads simultaneously within the stated exceptional family.

Majorize the norm constraint by \(X^{1-\beta}\) times the product of all the tilted reciprocal norms. For each remaining ideal factor the target cell is translated, and the sum of its tilted weights is at most \(\exp(o(E))\) by the same Euler bound with its orientation multiplicities. For the two selected dyads, the Selberg majorant has coefficients \(O(1/J)\). Joint Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz and the typical squared-norm bounds therefore give \(O(\exp(o(E))/(h_{\mathfrak m}J))\) uniformly in the translated cell. Dropping the sharing and size restrictions only increases the positive counts after this translation. Since \(X^{1-\beta}=X\exp(-E)\), this proves the stated upper bound.

### Resolving the second logarithmic term

In the same range one can replace the smoothness loss \(u\log u+o(u\log u)\) by
\[
                 F(u)+O(u),\qquad F(u)=u(\log u+\log\log u).
\]
In particular there exist fixed constants \(C,c>0\) such that the fraction of occupied ray and arc cells at \(X=VJ\exp(F(u_*)-C u_*)\) is at most \(e^{-c u_*}\). At \(X=VJ\exp(F(u_*)+C u_*)\), all but an \(e^{-c u_*}\) fraction of cells contain at least \(X\exp(-F(u_*)-O(u_*))/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\) ideals. Increasing \(C\) if necessary, every ray and interval has at least \(X\exp(-2F(u_*)-O(u_*))/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\) ideals by \(X=(VJ)^2\exp(2F(u_*)+C u_*)\). For fixed \(s>2+2\kappa\) the count in each prescribed ray and interval at \(X=V^s\) is \(X\exp(-F(u)+O(u))/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\). The typical-discriminant exception bound stays \(O_A(T/(\log T)^A)\).

For the upper estimates tilt by \(\beta=(\log u+\log\log u)/\log Y\), taking \(u=u_*\) for the occupancy threshold. Partial summation gives
\[
 \sum_{p\le Y}p^{-1+\beta}
  \ll \log\log Y+\frac{Y^\beta}{\beta\log Y}=O(u).
\]
Higher prime powers contribute \(O(1)\) in the logarithm of the Euler product, since \(\beta=o(1)\). This remains true with \(\tau(n)^a C_0^{\omega(n)}\) for any fixed \(a,C_0\). In particular one may sum over all common divisors \(g\) in the bilinear majorant, bounding the number for fixed norms by \(\tau(n_1)\). This permits separable weights on the two residue classes even when the exact matching orientations would impose a joint valuation condition. At norm \(x\), the Rankin loss is \(F(\log x/\log Y)+O(u)\) if \(x\) is a fixed positive power of \(X\); equivalently one may retain \(x^{-\beta}\) across all the factors.

For the lower estimates the index-sum construction with \(k=\lceil u(1+1/\log u)\rceil\) gives at least \(x(c_1/(k\log u))^k/(\log V)^{O(1)}=x\exp(-F(u)-O(u))\) products at a fixed relative norm width, now with \(u=\log x/\log Y\) for the packet. Orientation choices and the separation into a fixed number of prime lists each cost at most \(\exp(O(u))\). A shift of \(O(\log Y/\log u)\) in the log cutoff changes \(F\) by \(O(u)\). Taking the base norm \(W=VJ\exp(F(u_*))\) shifts \(F(\log W/\log Y)\) from \(F(u_*)\) by \(O(u_*(\log u_*)^2/\log Y)=o(u_*)\). The normalized averaged pair energy is consequently \(\exp(O(u_*))\). The bilinear errors above save a power of \(V\), so they accommodate the norm weights at this precision as well. A polylogarithmic Markov factor costs \(\exp(o(u_*))\).

Now use \(\log Y_g=\min((\log Y)/2,\sqrt{u_*})\), reserving \(C u_*\) log norm in disjoint guard intervals. There are \(\lfloor C u_*/\log Y_g\rfloor\) intervals. Their partition and rounding lose only \(o(u_*)\) in the log count because \(\log Y_g\gg\log\log V\) and \(\log Y_g=o(u_*)\). Choose \(C\) large enough for the cuspidal saving to dominate the \(O(u_*)\) energy bound. The Cauchy and Parseval arguments above give the two occupancy bounds. For the polynomial norm range, the losses \(F(u_i)\) of a fixed number of balanced packets add to \(F(u)+O(u)\) by the entropy bound for their relative sizes. For the upper dyad extraction one may replace \(3E\) in \(\log(x/(VJ))\) by \(3F(u)\). This keeps the weighted diagonal negligible. The unknown linear term includes costs from restricting the prime products and from mixing the remaining frequencies.

### Equal shares of the actual smooth ideal count

Let \(S_D(X,Y;Z,\mathfrak m)\) be the total number of primitive integral ideals of norm at most \(X\), with \(Y\)-smooth norm and the same coprimality exclusions as in the target cells. In the polynomial range \(X=V^s\), \(0\le\kappa<3\), \(J\le V^\kappa\), \(s>2+2\kappa\), the preceding argument can compare each cell to this total with relative error. For every fixed \(A>0\), on the same type of typical discriminant family, each ray and each arc of length \(1/J\) contains
\[
   \frac{S_D(X,Y;Z,\mathfrak m)}{h_{\mathfrak m}J}
   \bigl(1+O_A((\log V)^{-A})\bigr)
\]
ideals. The strict norm margin leaves a fixed positive power of \(V\) for mixing. The following proof accounts for the boundary at \(X\) and for repeated prime factors.

**Local deletions.** Write \(E=u\log u\) and choose \(\epsilon>0\) small in terms of \(s-2-2\kappa\). Norms below \(Xe^{-2E}\) are negligible in each cell by the sector cap and the earlier lower bound. Discard large exceptional components only after bounding them in each cell, allowing for arbitrary translations when an ideal divisor is removed. If the part supported on primes \(\le Y^a\) exceeds \(V^\epsilon\), for small fixed \(a>0\) it contains an ideal divisor with norm \(d\in[V^\epsilon,V^\epsilon Y^a]\). The complementary primitive sector cap is \(O(X/(VJd))\). Rankin at \(\log u/(a\log Y)\) bounds the reciprocal sum over these divisors, with orientations counted, by \(\exp(-(\epsilon/(as))E+o(E))\). Choose \(a\) with \(\epsilon/(as)>2\). If the powerful part of the norm exceeds \(V^\epsilon\), break each prime power of exponent at least two into squares and cubes and extract a powerful divisor in \([V^\epsilon,V^\epsilon Y^3]\). Its reciprocal sum with the orientation weights is \(V^{-\epsilon/2+o(1)}\), by the convergent powerful-number series at every exponent above \(1/2\). Finally, large ramified primes divide \(D\) and have multiplicity at most one in a primitive ideal. If their product is above \(V^\epsilon\), extract a divisor in \([V^\epsilon,V^\epsilon Y]\). Its reciprocal sum costs \(O(V^{-\epsilon/4})\) because \(\prod_{q\mid D,\ q>Y^a}(1+q^{-3/4})=1+o(1)\). The same cap applies. Each deletion is negligible compared with \(S_D/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\), with a saving exceeding any fixed negative log power. Summing a partition into cells gives the corresponding global deletion estimate.

Fix the remaining exceptional prime powers, with their ideal orientations, by assigning their entire powers once, first small primes, then powerful factors, then ramified factors. Their product has norm at most \(V^{3\epsilon}\). The unfixed part has distinct rational split primes in \([Y^a,Y]\), excluding prime divisors of the fixed part and of \(2DZ\mathrm N\mathfrak m\). Partition this range into intervals of relative width \(\asymp\Delta=(\log V)^{-C}\), merging a final short interval if necessary. A pattern specifies the multiplicity of each bin, with \(k=O(u)\) slots in total. Its full product range has multiplicative width \(1+O(k\Delta)\). Contributions from patterns crossing either norm endpoint can be bounded using their full mass.

**Mixing within one pattern.** In a bin used \(k_j>0\) times, partition the \(N_j\) allowed split rational primes uniformly at random into labelled lists of size \(\lfloor N_j/k_j\rfloor\), discarding the possible remainder. Draw one rational prime uniformly from each list and choose either ideal orientation with equal probability. Averaging over the partitions yields exactly the uniform ordered sample of distinct rational primes in the bin, by permutation symmetry. Every unordered selection has the same \(k_j!\) multiplicity. This construction permits repeated bin indices without admitting nonprimitive products.

On the good discriminant family each complete sharp bin at prime scale \(q\) has normalized cuspidal transform \(O(q^{-\eta_1})\) and invariant nonprincipal transform \(O_B((\log V)^{-B})\) for any required fixed \(B\). For the sharp cuspidal estimate smooth each endpoint across relative length \(q^{-\eta_2}\); the boundary contains \(O(q^{1-\eta_2}+1)\) rational integers, and the fixed derivative losses in the explicit formula absorb \(q^{O(\eta_2)}\) by reducing \(\eta_2\). For invariant modes one can instead use a transition of relative width a sufficiently large negative log power. The bin's prime mass is \(\asymp q\Delta/\log q\); the polynomial-logarithmic denominators and excluded primes are absorbed. Concentration for sampling a bounded function without replacement now gives the same bounds, with smaller margins, for all the random lists except with probability \(O(\exp(-Y^{c}))\). Here \(q\ge Y^a\), the list sizes are \(q/(\log V)^{O(1)}\), the two orientations are kept together, and the number of characters, lists and angular modes up to \(K=J(\log V)^B\) is polynomial in \(V\). This failure bound is uniform for a given pattern; after averaging its effect on that pattern's cell probability is negligible.

For a fixed good partition, divide the slots, using only their bin labels, into two base packets and the remaining guards. Stop each base packet once its product of nominal prime scales reaches \(VJ\); the overshoot is at most \(Y\). Both packets have size scales \(W_i=VJ\exp(o(\log V))\), and the remaining slots have product at least \(V^{g_0}\) for fixed \(g_0>0\), by the strict margin and small deletions above. Every tuple in a base packet gives a different primitive ideal, with maximum probability \(V^{o(1)}/W_i\), since the number of prime choices per slot is at least its scale divided by a log power. The earlier *unweighted* close-pair bound applied to all primitive ideals in the norm dyad gives, on average, joint squared low-frequency norm \(V^{o(1)}\) after dividing the ideal masses by \(W_i\). Indeed its diagonal costs \(O(h_{\mathfrak m}K W_i^{-1}V^{o(1)})\); the off-diagonal is bounded by the angular collision estimate with divisor weights. Markov covers all \(O(\log V)\) relevant dyads on a common typical family. The kernel counting close pairs is positive. Consequently this same \(V^{o(1)}\) energy bound holds for **every** base packet of every partition, by its atom cap.

The guard product damps each cuspidal frequency by \(V^{-c_0}\) for some \(c_0>0\); one guard damps every invariant nonprincipal mode by the prescribed log power. A Selberg majorant and minorant for the target arc have constant coefficients \(1/J+O(1/K)\) and uniform coefficient bound \(O(1/J)\). Cauchy–Schwarz on both frequency indices and the two packet energies now gives the desired relative accuracy for each complete pattern, after translating the target by the fixed factors. Summation of complete patterns preserves that accuracy without a factor for their number.

**The norm boundary.** The lower boundary at \(Xe^{-2E}\), including its multiplicative \(1+O(k\Delta)\) enlargement, is negligible by the primitive cap. For patterns meeting \(X\), their complete mass is contained in a shell of relative width \(O(k\Delta)\) about \(X\). Bound the total shell mass of these patterns by deleting one designated large split prime ideal. For a given residual ideal \(I\) of norm \(m\), the shell has \(O(k\Delta X/m+1)\) rational prime choices, up to the two orientations. The interval \([X/(2m),0.9X/m]\) contains \(\gg X/(m\log Y)\) allowed split replacement primes, excluding divisors of \(m\); their size is at most \(Y\) and at least a fixed multiple of \(Y^a\). All replacement products are admissible and lie below \(X\). Each such output can be obtained from at most \(O(\log X)\) residual ideals by deleting a prime. Thus the shell mass is \(O((k\Delta+Y^{-a+o(1)})\log Y\log X)\) times \(S_D\). Taking \(C\) large makes this negligible to the claimed precision. Applying the complete-pattern upper bound to this shell controls its contribution in each cell and completes the comparison.

### Locating occupancy by the ordinary smooth-number counting function

Let \(W_*\) be the least \(w\) with \(\Psi(w,Y)\ge VJ\), using a common scale for \(V\) on the discriminant dyad. Continue to assume \(J\le V^\kappa\), \(\kappa<3\). For any prescribed \(A>0\), apart from the preceding \(O_A(T/(\log T)^A)\) discriminants, the following estimates hold with a sufficiently large fixed \(C\).

* Below \(W_*/(\log V)^C\), at most \(O((\log V)^{-A})\) of the prescribed ray and angular cells contain a primitive smooth ideal.
* By \(W_*\exp(C(\log\log V)^2)\), all but \(O((\log V)^{-A})\) of the cells contain one. By \(W_*^2\exp(C(\log\log V)^2)\), every ray and **every** angular interval of length \(1/J\) contains one.
* For prime discriminants with the ordinary class group, including ordinary angular constraints, the two upper multipliers \(\exp(C(\log\log V)^2)\) can each be replaced by \((\log V)^C\).

This gives the typical-cell transition implicitly to an additive \(O((\log\log V)^2)\) on the log norm scale, and to \(O(\log\log V)\) in the ordinary prime-discriminant case. In particular it retains terms lost by a truncation of the Dickman exponent at error \(O(u_*)\). The every-cell result is a sufficient upper norm. [Global smooth ideal count](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md#the-global-dickman-asymptotic-at-a-subpower-prime-cutoff)

The global asymptotic and its small-divisor ratio show \(W_*=VJ\exp(o(\log V))\) and \(\Psi(W_*/(\log V)^C,Y)\asymp VJ/(\log V)^C\). This proves the first assertion by counting all ideals. For both upper assertions use a base packet consisting of **all** admissible smooth primitive ideals of norm in \([W_*/2,W_*]\). Exclude from it all rational primes up to \((\log V)^{B_0}\), with \(B_0\) large, including the primes reserved for guard intervals below. The product of excluded primes remains within the allowed budget on \(\log Z\). The packet size \(S\asymp C_{Z'}\Psi(W_*,Y)\) is at least \(VJ/(\log\log V)^{O(1)}\), where \(Z'\) includes this presieve. All estimates below can first be made on the full packet before any exclusions and then divided by this lower bound.

For the close-pair count there is a sharper multiplicity bound. Once \(n_1,n_2,g,t,v,D\) are given, the principal ideal generated by \((t+v\sqrt D)/2\) determines both orientations wherever an exponent survives the common rational divisor. The only ambiguity is at shared split primes with equal exponents completely removed. This costs at most \(2^{\omega(g)}\). When summing the near-square main term one may count all \(g\mid(n_1,n_2)\), for a total factor at most \(\tau((n_1,n_2))^2\). A further factor \(1+\omega((n_1,n_2))\) is also affordable. Indeed for any fixed \(b\)
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n_1,n_2\le W_*\\ n_1,n_2\ Y\text{-smooth}}}
 \tau((n_1,n_2))^b
 \le \sum_{d\ge1}\tau(d)^b\Psi(W_*/d,Y)^2
 \ll_b \Psi(W_*,Y)^2(\log V)^{O(1)} .
\]
Use the global Rankin bound at the saddle point above and \(\sum_d \tau(d)^b d^{-2\alpha}=O_b(1)\) for \(\alpha\ge3/4\). For the diagonal with \(v=0\) the number of ideal pairs is exactly the number of ideals \(I=J\), bounded on good fields by the global asymptotic; marking a prime divisor costs at most \(O(\log V)\). The nonzero-frequency bilinear error in the near-square estimate already saves a power of \(V\); allowing the additional marked common prime in that proof can be done by fixing it as a divisor of both norms. For primes larger than a small fixed power of \(V\) this would create many cases. Here all marked primes are at most \(Y=V^{o(1)}\), so the power saving persists. The zero-frequency main term retains the divisor-moment bound above.

With \(p\) the uniform packet probability, let \(p_q\) be its restriction to norms divisible by \(q\), keeping the original denominator \(S\). The positive angular kernel and one application of Markov now give
\[
  \sum_{\chi,\ |j|\le K}
  \left(|\widehat p(\chi,j)|^2+
    \sum_{q}|\widehat {p_q}(\chi,j)|^2\right)
  \le (\log V)^{O(1)}
\]
on the retained fields, \(K=J(\log V)^{A+O(1)}\). The normalization comes from averaging \(h_{\mathfrak m}K\) times the close-pair count over the dyad and dividing by \(S^2\). The extra restrictions by \(q\) give exactly the marked-common-prime factor.

Use one obligatory oriented prime in a broad guard interval at scale \((\log V)^B\) to save an arbitrarily large inverse log power on all cuspidal transforms. In the presence of invariant nonprincipal modes use \(O(\log\log V)\) additional disjoint lists of lazy primes as in the polylogarithmic ray argument. Their real-part bound and independence give the same inverse log saving on every such mode. The whole guard costs at most \(\exp(O((\log\log V)^2))\) in norm. For ordinary prime discriminants there are no nonprincipal invariant frequencies and one prime costs only \((\log V)^B\). Take \(B_0\) large enough to cover the disjoint guard intervals. The energy estimate is uniform at this choice, since the polynomial powers in it come from the full-packet estimate and the presieve affects the packet size by at most powers of \(\log\log V\).

For one packet, the guard and the cell Parseval argument give the second assertion for typical cells. For two independent copies of the full packet, the same Cauchy argument gives probability \((1+o(1))/(h_{\mathfrak m}J)\) for every cell. This time the two packet prime sets can overlap. For \(m_q=p_q(\text{all})\), deleting a prime ideal above \(q\) in the global count gives \(m_q\ll q^{-\alpha}(\log V)^{O(1)}\) for \(q\le Y\), by the global smooth-ideal formula at \(W_*/q\) and the rational Rankin bound. Thus \(\sum_{q>(\log V)^{B_0}}m_q^2=o(1)\) for sufficiently large \(B_0\). For a fixed arc, majorize the probability of any overlap by summing the same two-packet convolution with \(p_q\) in both slots. Its principal contribution is \(O(\sum_q m_q^2/(h_{\mathfrak m}J))\). The summed nonprincipal contribution is \(o(1/(h_{\mathfrak m}J))\) by the displayed summed energy and the guard savings. Positive cell mass remains after deletion. The remaining products are primitive and coprime to all guard primes, establishing existence without requiring unique recovery of the pair.

## Odd rational ray moduli of polynomial size

Assume Dirichlet GRH. On each discriminant dyad prescribe one odd integer \(q\le V^\delta\), using the ray modulus \(q\mathcal O_K\). Fix \(\kappa,\delta\ge0\) with \(\kappa+6\delta<3\), and \(J\le V^\kappa\). If \(X=V^s\) with \(s>2(1+\kappa+2\delta)\), each ray and each arc of length \(1/J\) contains
\[
  (1+O_A((\log V)^{-A}))
      \frac{S_D(X,Y;qZ)}{h_{q\mathcal O_K}J}
\]
primitive ideals with the prescribed restrictions. The global formula evaluates the numerator to relative \(1+o(1)\). For each prescribed \(q\) the exceptional family is \(O_A(T/(\log T)^A)\); the averaging argument does not provide one such exceptional set simultaneously for every polynomial-size modulus. The relative-class argument uses the typical cuspidal prime estimates over the enlarged polynomial conductor range. Dirichlet GRH gives the sharp-bin prime estimates for all nonprincipal invariant-character factors uniformly in \(q\).

Here the packet scale is \(W=VJq^2\exp(o(\log V))\), \(K=J(\log V)^{O(1)}\), and \(h_{q\mathcal O_K}\ll Vq^2\log V\). For a same-ray pair, after passing to the primitive generator as above one may choose its sign so that \((t+v\sqrt D)/2\equiv n_2/g\bmod q\mathcal O_K\). All input norms are coprime to \(q\). In the integral basis of the quadratic field this forces \(q\mid v\) and \(t\equiv 2n_2/g\bmod q\), since \(q\) is odd. Taking the norm gives \(n_1\equiv n_2\bmod q\). For a fixed sign the root modulo a prime of \(q\) is a unit with prescribed residue, hence has a unique lift to the full prime power in \(v^2\). The remaining root multiplicity at \(v=qj\) costs \(O(2^{\omega(j)})\). The angular constraint gives \(g|v|\ll W/(VK)=q^2V^{o(1)}\).

At fixed coprime norms with the required congruence (coprimality here is to \(q\)), the zero-frequency main bound becomes \(O((V/(Kq))\log(2W))\) per admissible \(g\), by summing over \(v=qj\). For the unweighted energy the number of norm pairs in the congruence is \(O(W^2/q+W)\). Divisor and orientation multiplicities are \(V^{o(1)}\). In the elementary range \(W\le V^2\), the additional one per residue class in the integer window obeys the same bound after summation, giving the required energy directly.

For \(W>V^2\) apply the near-square product argument at \(W'=W/g\). The pairs of residue classes \(l=n_1/g,\ b=n_2/g\bmod v^2\) are now equal modulo \(q\), so there are \(O(v^4/q)\) pairs. Keep their spacing in the double large sieve. On a single class of \(b\) the points \(2W'\sqrt{b/W'}/v^2\) have spacing bounded below by a positive constant; their close-pair count is \(O(W'/v^2)\). Indeed \(v^2\ll W'\) by a power margin since \(v\ll q^2V^{o(1)}/g\) and \(\delta<1/2\). For the other coordinates, on a frequency dyad \(h\asymp H_1\) and at \(d=(h,h')\), each small difference \((h/d)^2l-(h'/d)^2l'\) lies in one specified residue class modulo \(v^2\). There are at most \(O(1+H_1/d^2)\) such differences, and on writing \(l=l_0+v^2 a,l'=l_0+v^2 a'\) each gives \(O(1+W'd^2/(v^2H_1^2))\) solutions. The close-pair count is \(O(H_1^3+W'H_1/v^2)\). The two spacing bounds give a Selberg error on this pair of classes of at most
\[
 O\bigl(\Delta[H_1^2 W'/v^2+H_1 W'^{3/2}/v^3]\bigr),\qquad
 \Delta=V^2/W'.
\]
Take \(H_1\le V^\zeta/\Delta+1\) as before in the case \(\Delta\) is small. Sum over compatible classes, then \(v\ne0\) divisible by \(q\) with \(|v|\ll q^2V^{o(1)}/g\), then over \(g\). The total is
\[
 O\bigl(V^{2\zeta+o(1)}
         [W^2q^4/V^2+W^{3/2}q^2]\bigr).
\]
The extra multiplicities of roots and dyads are subpower and already included. Cases with \(\Delta\) bounded below are treated by the direct interval estimate. Since \(Wq^4\le V^{4-\epsilon}\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\), choose \(\zeta\) small: both errors save a power against \(WV^2\), equal up to subpower factors to the raw pair scale \(W^2 V/(Kq^2)\). This proves the \(V^{o(1)}\) joint squared Fourier bound for all subpackets of the required dyads on a typical field, under the same positive-kernel and atom-cap normalization as before.

For local deletions a translated ray lattice in the normalized planar ideal lattice has determinant \(\asymp Vq^2\) and minimum difference length at least \(q\). Counting rows through an enclosing rotated rectangle of sides \(O(\sqrt Z_1),O(\sqrt Z_1/J)\) bounds its contribution at norm cutoff \(Z_1\) by
\[
 O(1+\sqrt{Z_1}/q+Z_1/(VJq^2)).
\]
This applies uniformly to the affine coset, including the finitely many unit rotations. All complementary cutoffs after the small-divisor deletions in the pattern proof exceed \((VJq)^2\) by a power, on reducing their fixed margins if needed. Thus the last term absorbs the boundary terms there. Its comparison to \(1/h_{q\mathcal O_K}\) costs at most logarithmic factors. The remaining packet allocation, guard cancellation, prime sampling without replacement, and shell estimate follow the proportional-share proof, now placing both base packets at scale \(VJq^2\) up to a subpower instead of \(VJ\).

### The inverse smooth-count threshold in a rational ray group

Under the same Dirichlet GRH hypothesis and \(\kappa+6\delta<3\), define \(W_q\) by \(\Psi(W_q,Y)\ge VJq^2\) for the first time. For any fixed \(A\), outside the stated discriminant exception, a sufficiently large fixed \(B\) gives the following. At \(W_q/(\log V)^B\) at most \(O((\log V)^{-A})\) of the ray and arc cells contain an admissible ideal; by \(W_q(\log V)^B\) all but \(O((\log V)^{-A})\) cells contain one. Every ray and every arc of length \(1/J\) contain one by \(W_q^2(\log V)^B\). The ray class number is between \(Vq^2/(\log V)^{O(1)}\) and \(Vq^2(\log V)^{O(1)}\) on the retained fields, by the ordinary class number estimate and the Euler factors of the ray index. The lower occupancy constraint follows from the global count.

To improve the collision main term from subpower to polylogarithmic accuracy, retain the congruence \(n_1\equiv n_2\bmod q\) inside the divisor-moment estimate. For \(W=W_q\), any fixed \(b\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n_1,n_2\le W,\;Y\text{-smooth}\\
    n_1\equiv n_2\bmod q,\;(n_1n_2,q)=1}}
       \tau((n_1,n_2))^b
   \ll_b \frac{\Psi(W,Y)^2}{q}(\log V)^{O(1)}.
\]
Expand as an upper sum over a common divisor \(d\) coprime to \(q\). For \(d>V^\xi\), drop smoothness; the number after division is \(O((W/d)^2/q+W/d)\), and the divisor weights give a power saving for small fixed \(\xi>0\). For \(d\le V^\xi\), put \(x=W/d\), so \(x\ge q^2 V^{1-2\xi}\). Dirichlet GRH gives an \(O(\Psi(x,Y)/\varphi(q))\) upper bound for smooth numbers in any unit residue in a fixed relative dyad at this scale. In detail take a fixed positive smooth Mellin majorant of the dyad and expand in characters modulo \(q\). The principal count is \(O(\Psi(x,Y))\). For a nonprincipal character move the integral to \(1/2+\eta\). There its \(Y\)-truncated Euler product is \(V^{o(1)}\) at polynomial height: the full primitive \(L\)-function obeys the GRH subpower bound on this fixed line, imprimitive Euler factors cost \(V^{o(1)}\), and partial summation of the GRH twisted prime sum bounds the discarded prime logarithm by \(O_\eta(Y^{-\eta}(\log V)^{O(1)})\). The prime-power tail also converges. Truncate initially at large polynomial height on the line of absolute convergence and use the rapid Mellin decay on the horizontal segments. The resulting \(x^{1/2+\eta}V^{o(1)}\) is negligible compared with \(\Psi(x,Y)/\varphi(q)\). For the congruent pair on the original dyads one residual norm can now be counted freely and the other in its unit residue. Alternatively split norms below a fixed smaller power of \(W\) off by the trivial bound and sum dyads where the same shift applies. Summing the squared smooth counts over \(d\) uses the saddle-point divisor estimate as before. The displayed prefix bound follows as well: for residual pairs with maximum entry below \(q^2V^{1/4}\), the unrestricted integer-pair bound already gives a power saving; in the remaining pairs sum the second entry in the dyad of the maximum and the first freely.

In the zero-frequency main term sum \(v=qj\) using \(O(2^{\omega(j)})\) local roots, fixing either allowed global sign; this removes any dependence on \(2^{\omega(q)}\) from the relative precision. In the power-saving nonzero terms one may again mark a shared prime before applying the residue-class double sieve. It is at most \(Y=V^{o(1)}\). The summed low-frequency energies of the full packet and its common-prime restrictions are therefore polylogarithmic at \(W_q\). Use the two full packets and exclude overlaps exactly as at \(W_*\). One broad prime guard of norm \((\log V)^{O(1)}\) suffices here: Dirichlet GRH gives a fixed-power normalized prime saving on every nonprincipal invariant mode, and the typical cuspidal rectangle does the same on the other modes. All modes enter the same joint energy sum, so no factor for the number of invariant characters is incurred. This also explains the invariant-character treatment in the proportional-share proof for polynomial moduli.
