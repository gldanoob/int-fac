# Smooth quadratic ideals in ray classes and angular sectors

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of fundamental discriminant \(D\), with \(V=\sqrt{|D|}\). Let \(\mathfrak m\) be a finite integral ideal modulus with \(m_0=\mathrm N\mathfrak m=V^{o(1)}\), let \(h_{\mathfrak m}\) be the ray class number, and take an integer \(J\ge1\) with \(J=V^{o(1)}\). Fix a smoothness exponent \(c>e^{-1/2}\). Choose a fixed \(\varepsilon>0\) sufficiently small that \(s=1+\varepsilon\) satisfies \(s<2c\) and \(\log(s/c)<1/2\), and put \(X=V^s\). If \(c\ge1\), one may use any smaller fixed cutoff exponent in \((e^{-1/2},1)\). Fix an integer \(Z\) with \(\log Z\le(\log V)^C\) for a fixed \(C\). Enlarge the forbidden rational primes to include those dividing \(2Z\), those dividing \(m_0\), and all primes at most \(z=(\log V)^4\). Let \(Z_*\) be the product of these distinct primes.

For each ordinary class \(a\), fix an integral ideal \(I_a\) in the inverse class coprime to the rational primes dividing \(m_0\). Such a representative can be obtained from any representative by multiplication by an element in its inverse ideal that is a local generator at the specified primes. For an integral ideal \(\mathfrak a\) of ordinary class \(a\), define its angle by a generator of \(\mathfrak a I_a\), taken modulo the rotations by all units of \(K\). Normalize this angular circle to \(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\).

Under Dirichlet GRH, every target ray class and every angular arc of length \(1/J\) contain
\[
 \gg \frac{X^2}{h_{\mathfrak m}J(\log V)^{O(1)}}
\]
distinct primitive integral ideals of norm at most \(X^2\), with \(V^c\)-smooth norms coprime to \(Z_*\). Each is a product of two primitive ideals of norm at most \(X\) with relatively prime rational norms. The argument uses a majority of the ray-class and sector cells and the global ordinary smoothness estimate. [Ordinary smoothness estimate](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md) · [Ordinary completion argument](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md)

## Counting one ray and angular cell

Let \(w\) be the number of units of \(K\), let \(w_{\mathfrak m}\) count the units congruent to one modulo \(\mathfrak m\), and put \(\Phi=|(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m)^\times|\). Each fibre over an ordinary class has size \(\Phi w_{\mathfrak m}/w\), so \(h_{\mathfrak m}=h\Phi w_{\mathfrak m}/w\). Indeed, an ideal of ordinary class \(a\) coprime to \(\mathfrak m\) has \(\mathfrak a I_a=(\alpha)\) with \(\alpha\in I_a\) a unit modulo \(\mathfrak m\). Two such ideals lie in the same ray class exactly when their generator residues differ by a unit of \(K\). Fix one of the residues in the associated orbit in \(I_a/\mathfrak m I_a\). Each ideal in the ray class corresponds to exactly \(w_{\mathfrak m}\) generators in this fixed affine coset, and all of those generators have the same angle modulo unit rotations.

Write \(\lambda_a\) for the shortest length in the normalized lattice \(I_a/\sqrt{\mathrm N I_a}\). For all but \(o(h)\) ordinary classes, \(\lambda_a\ge\sqrt V/(\log V)^{K_0}\) for a fixed sufficiently large \(K_0\). These are the balanced classes of the ordinary count. The proportion of rays over unbalanced classes is also \(o(1)\) since every ordinary class has an equally sized ray fibre. [Balanced class bound](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md#uniform-counts-in-balanced-classes)

In normalized generator coordinates, the constraints of norm at most \(U\) and angle in an arc of length \(\beta\) give a union of at most \(w\) sectors of the disk of radius \(R=\sqrt U\), with total area \(\pi U\beta\). In a translate of a lattice of covolume \(\Delta\) and shortest length \(\lambda\), the count equals its area divided by \(\Delta\) with error \(O(R/\lambda+1)\), uniformly in the angle and translation. To see this, use rows parallel to a shortest vector, with spacing \(\Delta/\lambda\gg\lambda\). Count points along each chord and then approximate the sum of the chord lengths by its area integral. Each of the sectors has opening at most \(\pi\), and its chord length as a function of the row coordinate has total variation \(O(R)\) by convexity. The chord approximation and the row-rounding errors are thus both \(O(R/\lambda+1)\). The same bound includes points on the arc boundaries when half-open cells are used.

For a ray class the affine lattice is a translate of \(\mathfrak m I_a\), with normalized covolume \(Vm_0/2\) and shortest length at least \(\lambda_a\). To impose the exclusions at rational primes dividing \(m_0\), put \(Q=\operatorname{rad}(m_0)\) and refine the fixed affine coset modulo \(\mathfrak m Q I_a\). There are at most \(Q^2\le m_0^2\) resulting cosets. At each of these rational primes, requiring the residue to be a unit at every prime above it gives the usual local coprimality density divided by the unit density already imposed by the corresponding part of \(\mathfrak m\). This follows because the units modulo the larger local modulus map surjectively, with equal fibre size, onto the units modulo the local part of \(\mathfrak m\). At rational primes away from \(m_0\), the primitivity and coprimality densities are the same as in the ordinary count by CRT.

Let \(\mathcal D_{Z_*}\) be the product of ordinary primitivity and coprimality densities and let \(C_{Z_*}=L(1,\chi_D)\mathcal D_{Z_*}\). Both are bounded below by \((\log\log V)^{-O(1)}\). The conditional density of these restrictions in the fixed unit residue is \(\mathcal D_{Z_*}m_0/\Phi\). For completeness, use a fixed large polylogarithmic prime cutoff \(U_0\) as in the ordinary count. For primes up to \(U_0\) away from \(m_0\), Bonferroni truncation at consecutive orders of size \(\asymp\log\log V\) uses \(V^{o(1)}\) CRT cosets. Including the preceding refinement, the total sector discrepancy is at most \(m_0^2 V^{o(1)}(\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\). The omitted terms of the independent density product are smaller than any fixed negative power of \(\log\log V\).

For primitivity at rational primes \(p>U_0\) away from \(m_0\), the intersection with \(pI_a\) has lattice \(p\mathfrak m I_a\), with covolume increased by \(p^2\) and shortest length at least \(p\lambda_a\). A nonzero point in \(pI_a\) requires \(p\le\sqrt X/\lambda_a\). Summing the errors costs \(O((\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\log X)\), and summing the sector areas costs \(O(X\beta/(Vm_0U_0))\). For each remaining forbidden prime \(p\mid Z_*\), the intersection with a prime ideal above \(p\) gives covolume increased by at least \(p\) and shortest length at least \(\lambda_a\). The corresponding totals are \(O((\sqrt X/\lambda_a+1)\log Z_*)\) and \(O(X\beta\log Z_* /(Vm_0U_0))\). Take \(U_0\) large enough to make these area contributions negligible relative to the allowed density. When \(a\) is balanced, the discrepancy terms are \(V^{\varepsilon/2+o(1)}\). For \(\beta^{-1}=V^{o(1)}\), the main scale is at least \(V^{\varepsilon-o(1)}\), uniformly in the affine coset and the angular arc.

It follows that the number of primitive ideals of norm at most \(X\) coprime to \(Z_*\) in a balanced ray and angular arc of length \(\beta\) is uniformly
\[
 (1+o(1))\frac{2\pi X\beta}{w_{\mathfrak m}V\Phi}\mathcal D_{Z_*}
 =(1+o(1))\frac{C_{Z_*}X\beta}{h_{\mathfrak m}}.
\]
The last equality is the imaginary quadratic class number formula and the ray fibre size above.

## Matching good cells into a prescribed sector

Partition the angular circle into \(L=8J\) equal half-open cells. Each ray and cell above a balanced ordinary class contains \((1+o(1))\mu\) admitted primitive ideals, where \(\mu=C_{Z_*}X/(h_{\mathfrak m}L)\). The global number of admitted ideals with a norm prime factor above \(V^c\) is at most \((\log(s/c)+o(1))C_{Z_*}X\) by the ordinary smoothness estimate. It applies to \(Z_*\) since \(\log Z_*\) is bounded by a fixed power of \(\log V\). The ray and angular cells partition the ideals in that estimate. Choose a fixed \(\tau>0\) so that \(\log(s/c)/(1-\tau)<1/2\). Any balanced cell with fewer than \(\tau\mu\) admitted smooth ideals contributes at least \((1-\tau-o(1))\mu\) bad ideals. Since unbalanced cells are a proportion \(o(1)\), a proportion at least \(1/2+\xi\) of all ray and angular cells are balanced and contain at least \(\tau\mu\) admitted smooth ideals, for some fixed \(\xi>0\). [Global bound from real moments](keybreak-quadratic-smooth-majority.md#sharper-cutoff-by-real-moments)

Fix a target ray and angular arc. A first ray forces a unique complementary ray. If the underlying ordinary classes of the two rays are \(a,b\), the product angle is the sum of the two angles plus a fixed rotation \(\theta_{a,b}\). This rotation is the angle of a generator of the principal fractional ideal \(I_{ab}(I_aI_b)^{-1}\). For each first ray, match angular cells with indices \(i,j\) so that \(i+j\) is fixed modulo \(L\). Choose that sum to put the two cell centres, after rotation, as close as possible to the centre of the target arc. Centre rounding costs at most \(1/(2L)\), and the two points within their cells add at most \(1/L\) of angular error. This total is less than \(1/(2J)\), so the entire product of each matched pair of cells is in the target arc. The ray and cell matching is a bijection. By the majority bound, at least \(2\xi h_{\mathfrak m}L\) first cells are good and have a good partner cell.

For each first ideal in such a cell, discard partners sharing any prime \(q\) in its rational norm. All these primes exceed \(z\) and are coprime to \(m_0\). Count divisible partners in the original balanced ray and angular cell: for a prime ideal \(\mathfrak q\) of norm \(q\) above \(q\), the intersected affine lattice has difference lattice \(\mathfrak m\mathfrak q I_b\), and there are at most two choices of \(\mathfrak q\). Its sector count is bounded by
\[
 O\left(\frac{X}{Vm_0Lq}+\frac{\sqrt X}{\lambda_b}+1\right).
\]
Summing over \(O(\log X)\) prime divisors of the first norm gives \(o(\mu)\), since the area term is reduced by \((\log X)/z\) and the total discrepancy is only \(V^{\varepsilon/2+o(1)}\). Thus there are \(\gg h_{\mathfrak m} L\mu^2\) surviving ordered pairs. Their norms are mutually coprime, so their products are primitive.

Finally discard pairs having either ideal with more than \(T=(\log V)^B\) integral ideal divisors. The global number of such ideals of norm at most \(X\) is \(O(X(1+\log X)^3/T)\) by coefficientwise \(\zeta_K^2\le\zeta^4\). Each has one possible complementary ray and cell under the matching, with at most \(O(X/(Vm_0L))\) possible partners if that cell is balanced. The same estimate holds when the high-divisor ideal is the second factor since the matching is bijective. The total loss is negligible compared to \(h_{\mathfrak m}L\mu^2\) for sufficiently large fixed \(B\), using \(h_{\mathfrak m}\ll Vm_0(\log V)^{O(1)}\) and \(C_{Z_*}\ge(\log\log V)^{-O(1)}\). Each surviving product has at most \(T^2\) integral ideal divisors and hence at most \(T^2\) ordered factorizations. Division by this bound and \(L=8J\) give the result.

## Arbitrary fixed smoothness in logarithmically small sectors and ray classes

Fix positive constants \(c,M,C,A\). Put \(T\le |D|\le2T\), \(V=\sqrt{|D|}\), and restrict to \(m_0=\mathrm N\mathfrak m\le(\log V)^M\), \(1\le J\le(\log V)^M\), \(\log Z\le(\log V)^C\). Outside \(O(T/(\log T)^A)\) negative fundamental discriminants, every ray class and every ordinary angular arc of length \(1/J\) contain
\[
 \gg \frac{V^2Y}{h_{\mathfrak m}J(\log V)^{O(1)}}
\]
distinct primitive ideals of norm at most \(V^2Y\), with every rational prime at most \(V^c\) and coprime to \(2Dm_0Z\). Here \(Y=(\log V)^b\), with \(b\) fixed in terms of \(c,M,C,A\). The same exceptional set works for all the stated choices. The earlier majority proof permits subpower moduli and sectors at its larger smoothness cutoff. [Ordinary-class product estimate](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md#every-class-at-arbitrary-fixed-smoothness-for-almost-all-discriminants)

On principal ideals take the unit-circle character \((\alpha)\mapsto(\alpha/|\alpha|)^w\), where \(w\) is the number of roots of unity. Extend it to a character \(\Omega\) on fractional ideals. Such an extension exists since the circle is divisible (equivalently, choose roots on generators of the finite class group). It is an unramified finite-place Hecke character with the indicated infinity type. Within a fixed ordinary class, \(\Omega\) gives the generator angle defined at the start of this note up to a fixed rotation. For a ray character \(\psi\) and an integer \(n\), put \(\xi=\psi\Omega^n\). For \(|n|\le(\log V)^{M+2}\), its cuspidal inductions have analytic conductor \(O(Tm_0(1+w|n|)^2)\). The arithmetic conductor equals \(|D|\,\mathrm N\mathfrak f_\xi\), with \(\mathfrak f_\xi\mid\mathfrak m\), by the conductor formula for quadratic induction. In the automorphic zero-density argument each bad cuspidal representation of conductor \(Q\ll T(\log T)^{O(1)}\) can affect at most \(\tau(Q_{\rm arith})=T^{o(1)}\) quadratic discriminants, since \(|D|\mid Q_{\rm arith}\). This accounts uniformly for all moduli and frequencies. Take the near-one strip narrow enough that their combined exception is \(O(T^{1/2})\). The local parameters are unitary, also for nonzero infinity type; the zero count and explicit formula used for the ordinary guard remain uniform with this conductor and bounded degree. Thus the normalized log-scale prime guard has transform \(O((\log V)^{-H})\) at all these cuspidal frequencies for any prescribed \(H\), by choosing \(b\). [Family zero estimate, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.07717#page=3)

An induction is noncuspidal precisely when \(\xi=\xi^{\mathrm{conj}}\). In that case \(n=0\), since conjugation reverses the infinity type. The invariant finite-order character descends through the norm, giving two Dirichlet characters \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda\chi_D\) on induction. Equivalently, extend the invariant one-dimensional Weil character over the index-two subgroup by choosing a square root of its value on the square of a coset representative; the two choices differ by \(\chi_D\). Unless the ray character is trivial both Dirichlet factors are nonprincipal. Their primitive conductor product is \(|D|\,\mathrm N\mathfrak f_\xi\). For a fixed bad conductor \(q\asymp U\), existence of any admissible modulus therefore implies \(q\mid |D|f\) for some integer \(f\le L=(\log(2T))^M\) (increasing the constant in \(L\) if necessary). For fixed \(f\), the number of possible \(D\) is \(O(T\gcd(q,f)/q)\), since the nonzero integer \(|D|\) is a multiple of \(q/\gcd(q,f)\) and is at most \(2T\). Summing over \(f\le L\) gives \(O(TL^2/q)\).

At polynomial prime scales the small primitive conductors \(q\le(\log T)^B\) obey Siegel–Walfisz. In each larger dyad the GL(1) zero-density estimate leaves \(O(U^{1/2})\) bad primitive characters for a fixed narrow strip up to height \(2U\). For the good ones the smoothed explicit formula at \(y\asymp V^{1/k}\) gives relative error \(O(y^{-\eta}\log(2U)+\log(2U)/U+ y^{-1/2}\log^2(2y))\). It is \(O((\log V)^{-H})\) for large enough fixed \(B\). Summing the bad-conductor incidence bounds over dyads costs \(O(T L^2/(\log T)^{B/2})\). At each modulus the number of invariant ray characters is at most \(m_0|\mathrm{Cl}(D)[2]|\): fix the restriction to the kernel of projection to the ordinary class group, of size at most \(m_0\), then quotients with that restriction are ordinary invariant characters. As in the ordinary proof, the last factor is at most \((\log T)^{A+3}\) outside the desired exceptional set. This treats the invariant frequencies in the polynomial prime intervals.

Take exactly the two products from disjoint prime intervals and the additional oriented prime of the ordinary proof. For each frequency \(n\), weight their ray distributions by \(\Omega^n\). The absolute value at any ray of either weighted polynomial-product distribution is bounded by its ordinary class mass \(O((\log V)^k/V)\). Parseval over the ray group thus costs at most \(O((\log V)^{M+k+1})\) for either sum of squares, uniformly in \(n\). Fourier inversion on the ray group and the two-factor estimate give, at every target ray, its probability \((1+o(1))/h_{\mathfrak m}\) for \(n=0\), and weighted mass \(O((\log V)^{-H+M+k+1}/h_{\mathfrak m})\) for \(0<|n|\le(\log V)^{M+2}\). In the zeroth mode remove the invariant terms separately using their count and polynomial-interval bounds. After normalization on the target ray, the Erdős–Turán discrepancy inequality with cutoff \(\lfloor(\log V)^{M+1}\rfloor\) gives interval discrepancy \(o(1/J)\) when \(H\) is sufficiently large. Rotating to the generator angle preserves this estimate. Distinct tuples remain distinct ideals, giving the claimed count from their maximum weights.

**A geometric cost for prescribing a ray and a sector.** Suppose \(D<-4\), let \(q\ge3\) be an odd prime, and take \(\mathfrak m=q\mathcal O_K\). Write \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z+\omega\mathbb Z\), with \(\Im\omega=V/2\). Set \(y_0=(q-1)/2\) and choose \(x_0\bmod q\) so that \(x_0+y_0\omega\) is a unit modulo \(q\): its norm polynomial in \(x_0\) is monic quadratic, hence rules out at most two residues. In its principal ray class every integral generator \(x+y\omega\) has \(y\equiv\pm y_0\pmod q\), so \(|y|\ge(q-1)/2\). Here the ray class is the class of the indicated principal ideal; its ordinary ideal class is principal. Use \(I_1=\mathcal O_K\) for the angle or rotate the target sector accordingly. In the arc of normalized length \(1/J\) centred on the real axis modulo sign,
\[
  V|y|/2 \;=\; |\Im(x+y\omega)| \;\le\; |x+y\omega|\sin(\pi/(2J)) \;\le\; |x+y\omega|\pi/(2J).
\]
Thus every ideal in this ray and arc has norm at least \(V^2(q-1)^2J^2/(4\pi^2)\), even before requiring smoothness or primitivity. This gives a lower bound of order \(V^2\,\mathrm N\mathfrak m\,J^2\) for this family of prescribed targets.

## Subpower smoothness in polynomially thin angular sectors

Fix \(s>2\), put \(X=V^s\), and let \(\log Y=(\log V)^{\theta+o(1)}\) for fixed \(0<\theta<1\), \(u=\log X/\log Y\) and \(E=u\log u\). For some fixed \(\sigma>0\) depending on the fixed parameters, the preceding result extends to all \(1\le J\le V^\sigma\) at this smoothness scale. Outside \(O(T/(\log T)^A)\) discriminants the number of primitive \(Y\)-smooth ideals coprime to the stated exclusions, in each prescribed polylogarithmic ray and each arc of length \(1/J\), is
\[
                 \frac{X}{h_{\mathfrak m}J}\exp[-(1+o(1))E].
\]
In particular it includes every \(J=V^{o(1)}\). The positive exponent obtained here comes from a near-one zero-density margin; the geometric bound above still gives a separate necessary condition. [Ordinary subpower-cutoff bounds and basic estimates](keybreak-quadratic-majority-products.md#smooth-norms-in-every-class-at-subpower-prime-cutoffs)

First allow angular frequencies up to \(V^2\) in the earlier simultaneous zero-density argument, taking the margin sufficiently small. The analytic conductors are still polynomial in \(T\) and the arithmetic conductor still has \(|D|\) as a divisor. The smoothed normalized oriented cuspidal prime transform on any of the prescribed prime bins of scale \(y=Y^{a+o(1)}\), for fixed \(a>0\), is \(O(Y^{-a\eta'})\) for some fixed \(\eta'>0\), reducing it to allow fixed powers of logarithms in weights and interval widths. Only frequency zero has invariant terms.

For the lower bound choose fixed \(0<g<s-2\). Use three independent balanced prime-bin products, of norm scales \(V^{(s-g)/2}, V^{(s-g)/2}, V^g\), with fixed factors of room in the cutoffs to keep the total norm at most \(X\). Separate their prime supports by setting their upper prime cutoffs to \(Y\exp[-C_i\log Y/\log u]\) for three fixed, sufficiently separated constants \(C_i\ge0\). The construction occupies a log interval of width \(O(\log Y/\log u)\) just below each cutoff, and all three cutoffs are \(Y^{1-o(1)}\). The first two distributions retain the primitive per-class Parseval bound \(\exp(O(E))=V^{o(1)}\). For the third use all orientations and a smooth nonnegative prime weight within each bin, bounded by one after normalization of its amplitude. Give each admissible index pattern mass proportional to its product of bin masses. The total unnormalized mass has the same leading lower count as before because each bin has a fixed proportion of its expected prime mass. For every pattern its independent prime transforms multiply to at most \(V^{-g\eta'+o(1)}\) on all cuspidal frequencies. Convex averaging over the patterns preserves the bound. At invariant nonprincipal frequencies the logarithmic bound from even one bin suffices for the zeroth angular mode. The total of the three smoothness losses is \((1+o(1))E\). Taking \(\sigma<g\eta'/2\), the conditional angular discrepancy at each target ray is \(o(1/J)\) by Erdős–Turán up to frequency \(\lfloor V^{2\sigma}\rfloor\). Disjointness and the maximum tuple probability give the lower count.

For the upper bound, in any fixed ordinary class the number of primitive ideals of norm at most \(W\) in an arc of length \(1/J\) is \(O(1+W/(VJ))\), uniformly in the arc and class. Indeed on rotating the normalized ideal lattice, each sector is contained in an origin-centred rectangle with sides \(O(\sqrt W), O(\sqrt W/J)\). The primitive rectangle bound gives the estimate after accounting for the bounded unit group. It also holds in the target arc after removing any fixed factor, since its angular character translates the arc. The extra polylogarithmic ray index fits in \(\exp(o(E))\), so the ordinary-class upper count suffices if it retains the angular factor.

For concreteness choose small fixed \(g,\tau>0\) with \(g+\tau<s-2\), and choose \(0<a<(s-g-\tau-1)/s\). Take \(\sigma\) still smaller so \(\sigma<s/2-1\), \(a<(s-g-\tau-1-\sigma)/s\), and \(\sigma<a\eta'g/2\). The sector cap absorbs norms below \(X\exp(-2E)\). Removing ideals with powerful part of norm above \(V^\tau\) costs \(O(X^{1/2+o(1)}+(X/(VJ))V^{-\tau/2+o(1)})\), which is negligible. At a fixed remaining powerful part \(P\), put \(X'=X/\mathrm NP\). The squarefree remainder has norm between \(X'\exp(-2E)\) and \(X'\). Set \(l=\lfloor g\log V/\log Y\rfloor\). For fewer than \(l\) prime factors above \(Y^a\), the removed large part has norm at most \(V^g\) and total harmonic mass \(\exp(o(E))\). For the residual norm dyad of upper endpoint \(W\), split off a divisor near \(W/(VJ)\), within a factor \(Y^a\) below it. Use the sector cap for its complementary class and arc. The harmonic Rankin estimate at \(Y^a\) supplies a loss stronger than \(E\) by the inequality for \(a\), retaining the \(1/J\) factor throughout.

With at least \(l\) large distinct factors, count ordered selections and divide by \(l!\). The two remaining products can be put in dyads above \(V\) by the square-root split. To keep the full density without having to enumerate all guard dyads, put \(\beta=\log u/\log Y\). Bound the indicator of the total norm constraint by \((X')^{1-\beta}\) times each factor's norm to the power \(-1+\beta\), then drop that constraint. In either remaining dyad of scale \(x_i\) the total weighted mass is \(\exp(o(E))\) by Rankin; the maximum class mass is \(O(x_i^\beta/V)\). Thus its squared Fourier norm, with or without angular weighting, is \(V^{o(1)}\). The list of dyad pairs has polylogarithmic size.

The harmonic guard weight on all oriented primes over \([Y^a,Y]\) has a positive majorant of total mass \(H_1\ll u/\log u\), by summing smooth broad dyad majorants weighted by \(q^{-1+\beta}\) and partial summation. A smooth weight at each dyad can majorize the varying factor there up to a fixed multiplier. Its normalized cuspidal transform is \(O(Y^{-a\eta'})\); the corresponding convex combination retains that bound. Ramified primes can be included with relative error \(Y^{-a+o(1)}\) in their dyads. Since \(l\asymp u\), \(H_1^l/l!\le\exp(o(E))\). Majorize the angular interval by a trigonometric polynomial up to degree \(\lfloor V^{2\sigma}\rfloor\) with constant coefficient \(O(1/J)\) and sum of absolute coefficients \(O(\log V)\) (the usual Selberg interval majorant). At frequency zero the invariant contributions times the constant coefficient are \(\exp(o(E))/J\); all cuspidal contributions, including every nonzero angular mode, are \(O(V^{-a\eta'g+o(1)})\) relative to the uniform-class factor and the extracted guard mass. This gives the upper \((X')^{1-\beta}\exp(o(E))/(hJ)\). Finally sum over \(P\) using convergence of the powerful-ideal reciprocal series at \(1-\beta>1/2\) bounded away from that endpoint. Since \(X^{1-\beta}=X\exp(-E)\), the claimed exponent follows.
