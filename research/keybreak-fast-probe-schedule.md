# Faster character arithmetic permits a larger field degree

The pinned KeyBreak construction with staged private-coordinate tests admits a single-input expected bit-time bound $L_N[1/3,2.341]$ under Dirichlet GRH and the preprint's analytic propositions. The larger degree uses the same character sampler and faster arithmetic modulo its long primes. Some selected private-prime bands lie below the top denominator interval. Keeping each selected band disjoint from that interval preserves the rational count. A second strict schedule gives $2.357$ while retaining the denominator guard for the entire private-prime interval. [KeyBreak, Appendices G and H](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105) · [Fast character arithmetic](keybreak-fast-probe-arithmetic.md)

## Fixed integer parameters

Use $L=\log N$, $D=(L/\log L)^{1/3}$, $H=D\log L$, $n=b(N)$, $m_b=b(n)$ and $t=dm_b$. Let $d$ be the least odd integer at least three with $d^3m_b\ge\delta^3n$. Set $k_0=\lceil d/(2\mu)\rceil$, $e_0=\lfloor(n-5)/(2k_0)\rfloor-1$, $e_B=\lceil\beta t\rceil$, $T_H=\lfloor h_Tdt\rfloor$ and $e_{\min}=\lceil\eta e_0\rceil$. Use $M=\lfloor N^{1/d}\rfloor$ as before. The box exponent and next cutoff are

$$v_i=\lceil a e_i+c e_B\rceil,\quad V_i=2^{v_i},\qquad e_{i+1}=\max\left(e_B,\left\lceil\frac{a e_i+c e_B+T_H/d}{\lambda}\right\rceil\right),\quad Y_i=2^{e_i}.$$

Keep the field and rational-environment sampling law from (G.2)–(G.4), including $\sigma=V_0^2n^{10}$. ## Public relation estimates

- The affine fixed point of the cutoff recurrence is below $e_B$ since $(\lambda-a-c)\beta-h_T>0$. The cutoff strictly decreases until the clamp and the number of levels is $O(\log D)$. Also $h_T>u+2x_0$, so the polynomially padded height guard (G.57) holds. The recurrence bounds every algebraic smoothness argument by $(\lambda+o(1))d$ and the integer-height bound becomes $2(T_H+d v_0)\le n-1$, with slack because $a\mu<1/2$. [KeyBreak (G.56)–(G.58)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)
- The box checks hold since $a>1/2$, $(a+c)\beta>\beta$ and $(\lambda-1)(a+c)\beta>h_T$. The latter gives strict separation from an unclamped child and the former two give the required box size and separation at the clamp. We have $x_0<u$, $y_0<u$, $2x_0>y_0$ and $2x_0>3y_1$. Thus the top means remain large and the Gaussian congruence conditioning only needs primes up to $Y_1$. For the remaining finite startup, scalar sizes are polynomial in $n$ in bits, and the height and $Y_1^3$ guards have strict margins. [KeyBreak (H.1)–(H.2), Lemma G.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=126)
- Set the GRH progression parameter to $1/100$. The (G.12) error has largest leading coefficient at the first internal box and the (G.17) error has its largest at $P\asymp Y_0$. In units $dt$ the two coefficients are $(1+\epsilon)y_1-(1/2-\epsilon)(u+x_1)$ and $(1+\epsilon)y_1-(1/2-\epsilon)(u+x_0-y_0)$. Both are below $-u/4$. The reciprocal rational densities and mesh factors contribute $e^{O(H)}$, below these margins. The top parent error powers in (G.18) and (G.53) are governed by $y_0-2x_0,-u,y_1-2x_0,y_1-u$, all negative. The internal rational root-average exponent in (G.15) decreases as $e_i/t$ increases, by the same $2a-1-O(1/d)>0$ comparison as in (G.13), and its bottom decay margin is $(2(a+c)-1)\beta-u/(3\beta)>0$. [KeyBreak (G.12), (G.15), (G.17), (G.18), (G.53)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=108)
- In (G.38), the separate period upper bound has base-two logarithm at most $\log_2 M+O(e_i+e_B)$. The padded lower endpoint has logarithm $\log_2(M\sigma)+d(a e_i+c e_B)-O(e_i+e_B)$. The determinant, parent divisions, and semismooth extra power contribute at most $O(e_i+e_B)$ to the comparison. The worst bound is at the bottom because the negative term has slope of order $d$ in $e_i$. Its limiting coefficient in $dt$ is $(1+\epsilon)u-(1/2-\epsilon)(u+2x_0+(a+c)\beta)<-1/25$. This also bounds the separate-period calculation with $m_r\vartheta$. The endpoint normalization term $4k$ is at most a constant times $V_i^2$ and fits the same lower-order size. The proof of Proposition G.10 then uses the same radius and mesh losses. [KeyBreak (G.38) and the last part of Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=124)
- The leading $H$-coefficients of the smoothness losses are at most $s_A=\delta\lambda/3$ and $s_R=\delta(u+x_1)/(3\beta)$. In the absolute mean check use the rational loss of its own box, which in units $\Lambda_d$ is at most $(u+a e_i/(dt)+c\beta/d)/(3\beta)+o(1)$. The mean grows with $e_i/t$ since $2a-1-a/(3\beta d)>0$ eventually. Its bottom limit in $\Lambda_d$ units is $(2(a+c)-1)\beta-\lambda/3-u/(3\beta)>1/50$. Normalized semismooth bands add only a logarithmic loss. Use $s_A+s$ and $s_A+s_R+s$ in the per-search budgets (H.15), with $s=1/20000$ in $H$ units. Those are exact rational exponents converted using $t/\delta$. The bottom mean check, the top means and the progression bounds preserve the original proof of G.10–G.12. [KeyBreak Proposition G.13 and Lemma H.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)

### Private bands below the denominator interval

For the $2.341$ schedule remove from the permitted private set every complete dyadic band that intersects the top denominator interval $(V_0/2,3V_0/4]$. Retain the original domain's rule excluding $p\mid b$ at a top request with parent $p$. The two uses of the old order inequality $p>b$ are as follows. [KeyBreak §G.3 and §H.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=108)

1. In the aggregate rational count for a particular complete band $J$, the convolution counts products $p u b$, with $u$ a $B$-smooth cofactor, $p\in J$ and $b$ in the denominator interval. Both primes exceed $B$. Their roles are recovered from the two disjoint intervals even if every $p$ in the band is smaller than the denominators. Thus the multiplicity-one input of the large sieve in G.3 is preserved. For an individual parent the convolution uses $u b$ and the map $r\mapsto r p^{-1}\bmod Q$ remains injective on the environment interval, whether or not the size of $u$ exceeds $Q$. All these cofactors are $Q$-units by smoothness. The analytic bounds (G.17) and (G.18) carry over as written.
2. At the top, $p>B$ and $p>Y_1$ since $\eta>a/\lambda$. Consequently $p$ cannot equal a child prime. A top algebraic child above $B$ still cannot divide $G(P)$, whose only prime above $B$ in a rationally eligible point is $p$. The denominators exceed $Y_1$ as before. In the aggregate count, the unit conditions modulo the child and the exact parent label therefore remain valid. This preserves the distinct, primitive points and the exact incidence reordering in (G.54)–(G.55). The weights and density comparisons are within each one band, so deleting other bands has no effect on the incoming estimate. [KeyBreak Proposition G.12](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=121)

In both schedules $\eta>a-a^2/\lambda$, hence the top cofactor $MV_0/p$ is eventually at most $MV_1$. This is the condition for retaining the original uniform rational loss used above. For $2.357$ one has $\eta>a$ and therefore the full private-prime interval already satisfies the unmodified separation guard. For $2.341$ replace that one guard by a finite interval-disjointness check on every admitted dyadic band. A dyadic denominator interval starts above $2^{v_0-1}$ and ends below $2^{v_0}$. Thus any complete parent band with upper exponent at most $v_0-1$, or lower exponent at least $v_0$, is admissible. The probability bound requires only a polynomial union over the admitted bands. The good child sets are the same for all of them.

## Private stages and whole-input cost

Use $J=128$, $z_i=z_0+(1-z_0)i/(J-1)$ and private right cutoff $e_i^*=\lfloor z_i^2 e_0\rfloor$. The two-coordinate loss normalization is $P=\delta/(3\mu)$ in $H$ units. Put $w_i=h(z_{i+1}-z_i)$ for $i<J-1$, $w_{J-1}=P-\sum_{i<J-1}z_i^2w_i$, and allocate size fractions $f_i=z_i^2w_i/P$. All weights are positive, $\sum f_i=1$, and $\sum f_i/z_i^2<2$ so the original $K=2k_0$ cap suffices. The initial grid values satisfy $z_0^2>\eta$. In the lower schedule every $z_i$ is rational, whereas $a=.5005$ is not a rational square, so each fixed $z_i^2$ differs from $a$. The recipe (H.18) uses bands with width $o(e_0)$ near each $z_i^2e_0$. Since $v_0/e_0\to a$, every recipe band eventually passes the finite disjointness check. All remaining band, distinctness, and central-index guards are the ones in the staged analysis. [Staged private construction](keybreak-private-band-stages.md#the-shapes-counted-below)

Use the staged test, the rough-input first-split pass, and the publicly charged stage and launch caps. Each coefficient $q$ of a cap in $H$ units is realized by $2^{\lceil q t/\delta\rceil}$. The confidence caps in (H.31)–(H.32) include all staged partial-factor trees and all raw slots. Their node counts per invocation and bit lengths are polynomially bounded as before. The per-stage curve charge is bounded by $h z_i$ since $h>(20001/10000)\sqrt{2\mu/(3\delta)}$. Success is still tested on a public transcript until the final private-root multiplication. Thus the H.8 private signs and uniform conditional epoch success apply. Trial division between bounded fresh epochs gives termination on every tape and verified recursion yields complete factorization.

Let $b=\delta\beta$. The raw descent cost is $b+s_A+s_R+2s$. The algebraic recognition cost is at most $b+s_A+2s+h\sqrt{a/\lambda}$. The private-stage maximum is at most $b+\max_i(\sum_{j\ge i}w_j+h z_i)+s$. Faster modular arithmetic keeps the original character setup and evaluations within $2\delta$ and $b+4\delta/3$, respectively. The same finite probe law and simultaneous separation event apply to all the retained classes. Matrix elimination with arithmetic exponent below $2.6$ costs at most $2.6b$. The exact products, integral square root and verification cost at most $2b$ using the small auxiliary prime procedure. These exponent bounds include all setup and retained work for the current integer.
