# Productive signs from certified relations or an exponent multiple

A finite list of verified multiplicative identities can certify a productive ordinary-mark sign without a complete S-unit presentation. The input may contain any unit pairs and any identities expressing words in those pairs as words in the ordinary marks. Binary elimination on the identity columns then computes every sign certified by those identities. A different test uses a supplied exponent multiple to detect a mixed involution in a modular subgroup, with a lifting condition when the result must come from ordinary marks. [Conductor and unit lattice](cogentic-conductor-signs.md)

## A partial relation lattice

Let $N>1$ be odd, $s_1,dots,s_k$ be ordinary mark pairs with common unit residues, and $u_1,dots,u_r$ be pairs of field elements with defined unit reductions on the two selected branches. Write $d_i$ for the ratio of the reductions of $u_i$. Supply integer columns $A_j\in\mathbb Z^r$ and $B_j\in\mathbb Z^k$ with the identities
\[
                    u^{A_j}=s^{B_j}
\]
in the product of the fields. One can always supply the pair $(-1,1)$ with its square equal to the empty mark product. This makes the certified sign image contain both global signs.

If $c$ is a binary vector in $\ker(A\bmod 2)$, treated as a vector of integers zero or one, then
\[
                 (u^{Ac/2})^2=s^{Bc},\qquad
                 z(c)=\prod_i d_i^{(Ac)_i/2},\qquad z(c)^2=1\pmod N .
\]
A binary kernel basis generates the entire sign image from the certified lattice $L=\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb Z} A$. Indeed, an exponent vector $x$ with $2x\in L$ is $Ac/2$ for an integer $c$ whose reduction lies in the binary kernel. Decompose that reduction in the chosen basis. After subtracting those binary vectors, $c$ differs by $2b$ for some integer $b$, and the resulting contribution to the root residue is $d^{Ab}=1$. Thus evaluation of basis vectors and a test against $\{1,-1\}$ either produce a proper gcd or prove that this list of identities has trivial productive sign image. The formal quotient $\mathbb Z^r/L$ can have further relations in the actual S-unit quotient. Its image on elements killed by two remains sound because all supplied relations hold.

Once the input identities and the reductions are verified, binary elimination and modular exponentiation take polynomial bit work in their explicit input sizes. A verifier can check the field identities in polynomial time in a certificate that gives the values at the nodes of multiplication and inversion circuits for the two sides. Each edge is checked by exact rational polynomial arithmetic in the respective power basis. One may give the generator reductions by power-basis expressions with scalar denominators prime to $N$, or by formulas with explicitly invertible reduced denominators. Compact binary exponents alone do not bound the lengths of intermediate exact field elements. There is no bound here on finding the identities or giving polynomial-sized field certificates for a growing pool.

### An exact input family

For integers $v\ge2$, put $m=2v^2$, $N=m^2+1$, $F=X-m$ and $G=X^2+1$. In the second field write $\beta^2=-1$. Both derivatives are units at the chosen branches and the conductor overlap is exactly $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. Use the single ordinary mark $s=2X$ and pairs
\[
 u_1=(1,\beta),\qquad u_2=(2v,1-\beta).
\]
All their residues are units, since $m$ is a unit, $\gcd(v,N)=1$, and $(1-m)(1+m)=2\pmod N$. Two certified relations have columns $(4,0)$ and $(2,2)$, with $u_1^4=1$ and $(u_1u_2)^2=s$. The second column gives root residues $2v$ and $1+m$. The two factors $m+1-2v$ and $m+1+2v$ are greater than one and multiply to $N$. They are coprime, since any common prime divides $4v$ and neither factor has a prime divisor in common with $2v$. Consequently the difference gcd returns the first factor. The columns, field coefficients and reduced roots have bit length polynomial in $\log N$, and the certificate only uses the given parameter $v$, which can be recovered by integer square roots on inputs of this form. This example uses fixed degrees and a nonprimitive mark.

## Detecting involutions with a supplied exponent multiple

Suppose modular units $a_1,\dots,a_r$ and a positive integer $E$ satisfy $a_i^E=1\pmod N$ for every $i$. This hypothesis is checked by modular exponentiation. Write $E=2^he$ with $e$ odd. The $a_i^e$ generate the full 2-primary subgroup of the group generated by the $a_i$, since the exponentiation kills the odd part and acts invertibly on the 2-primary part. Append $-1$ to the projected list. This preserves existence of a productive sign up to a global sign.

Using repeated squaring, find an element $g$ in the list of maximal order $2^t$ and inspect the final nonidentity power of each list element. Such a power has order two. If any is different from $-1$, it yields a split. Otherwise take a current element $b$ of order $2^j$. Its final involution is $-1$ and $g$ has the same final involution, so
\[
                    (b g^{-2^{t-j}})^{2^{j-1}}=1 .
\]
Replace $b$ by this product and repeat. Its order strictly decreases at each step. If every list element reaches one without producing a mixed involution, all original list elements belong to $\langle g\rangle$. The 2-primary subgroup with $-1$ appended is then cyclic and has no mixed involution. Including the verification of $E$, this takes $O((r+1)(\log E+h^2))$ modular multiplications using squaring and a table of powers of $g^{-1}$. Tracking the same group operations expresses any output as a word in the supplied generators and $-1$.

For unit-square corrections this test applies directly to the modular image of the admitted units. For ordinary marks one also needs to lift the involution to a pair whose square belongs to the mark subgroup. The odd-index condition in the [conductor note](cogentic-conductor-signs.md) supplies this lift when its hypotheses hold. Abstract 2-rank alone does not control the lift. The surjection $C_4\times C_2\to C_2\times C_2$ which reduces the first coordinate modulo two maps the source's elements killed by two into just the second factor, even though both groups have 2-rank two.

At $N=65$ the roots $8$ and $18$ both have order four and square to $-1$. With $E=4$ the descent computes $18/8=51\pmod {65}$ and splits off $5$. Testing just the final powers of the original generators would miss this sign.

The two tests certify existence without **supplying** a factorization. A positive output also gives a factor by the same polynomial-time verification. Producing useful certificates or a useful exponent multiple uniformly for arbitrary $N$ remains open. The pair of tests gives no algorithm to certify that a given finite mark pool has no productive signs unless the supplied identities capture its full image.
