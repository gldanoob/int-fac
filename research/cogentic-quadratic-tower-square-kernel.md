# Exact square kernels by descent through a quadratic tower

A supplied tower of quadratic extensions starting at $\mathbb Q$ admits unconditional square-kernel recovery for growing degrees at the one-third scale after exact ideal parity. The rank bound needed in the descent depends on the degree and discriminant of the fields, rather than on the number of marked products. For unrestricted columns the same algorithm gives a fixed-depth one-third bound and a larger growing-depth bound.

## One quadratic step

Let $K=L(\theta)$, $\theta^2=D\in L^\times\setminus L^{\times2}$, and let $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_n\in K^\times$ be explicit. For a binary mask $v$, write $\gamma(v)=\prod_{j:v_j=1}\gamma_j$. Norm induces a linear map on square classes. Recursively compute the square kernel of $N_{K/L}\gamma_j$ in $L$, with the current mask coordinates retained. If this norm map has rank $r$, choose a systematic basis $v_1,\ldots,v_s$ of its kernel. Each basis mask has at most $r+1$ nonzero coordinates. Indeed, row reduction solves the $r$ constrained coordinates in terms of the remaining free ones, and each systematic mask sets just one free coordinate to one.

For each basis mask put $\beta=\gamma(v_j)$ and recover an explicit $t\in L^\times$ with $t^2=N_{K/L}\beta$, using the exact field-square oracle. If $\beta\in L$, take its descended scalar $\delta=\beta$. Otherwise put $\delta=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/L}\beta+2t\in L$. The identity
\[
      (\beta+t)^2=(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/L}\beta+2t)\beta
\]
shows that $\delta$ is nonzero, since $\beta\notin L$ implies $\beta+t\ne0$. In this case $\beta=\delta((\beta+t)/\delta)^2$. Thus every descended scalar $\delta_j$ represents the class of its source product in $K$.

The kernel of $L^\times/L^{\times2}\to K^\times/K^{\times2}$ is exactly the span of $[D]$. In fact a square $(a+b\theta)^2$ in $L$ has $2ab=0$, so its class in $L$ is either $1$ or $D$. Consequently a second recursive call finds the joint relations of the list $\delta_1,\ldots,\delta_s,D$ in $L$. Project its kernel away from the last coordinate. The projection is injective on that kernel, since $D$ itself is nonsquare in $L$. Lifting the resulting masks by the $v_j$ gives exactly the original square kernel. To return roots for its basis, rebuild each final product once from its original mask and apply the exact square oracle. This avoids composing any of the intermediate root expressions. Independent fields can be processed separately and their returned kernel subspaces intersected in the original coordinates, including a common sign bit if required. [Exact square roots in number fields](cogentic-monic-square-extraction.md)

At the bottom $\mathbb Q$, compute a gcd-free basis of the positive numerator and denominator integers. Overlapping bases $a,b$ are split into $a/g,b/g,g$ with $g=\gcd(a,b)$, retaining integer exponent vectors and dropping units. The sum of logarithms of distinct bases decreases at each proper splitting, giving polynomially many steps. An exact integer square test identifies which pairwise coprime bases are squares. An arbitrary rational product is square precisely when its sign is positive and the exponent sum on each nonsquare base is even. Binary elimination computes this kernel without rational integer factorization.

## Why the rank stays small after parity

Let $F$ be the top field of the supplied tower and let $\Sigma_0$ contain all rational primes below any odd valuation of the original columns. Choose
\[
       \Sigma=\Sigma_0\cup\{p:p\mid 2\Delta_F\}.
\]
If the starting columns are basis products of the exact ideal-parity kernel, $\Sigma_0$ is empty. Every generator in every recursive call has even valuation away from places over $\Sigma$. For the norm call, valuations of a norm are sums of the upstairs valuations weighted by residue degrees, so evenness is preserved. For the scalar call, $\beta=\delta x^2$ in the upper field. At an unramified place above the lower place, $v(\delta)$ equals the lower normalized valuation. Thus evenness of $\beta$ upstairs forces evenness of $\delta$ downstairs. At a rational prime outside $\Sigma$, all tower steps are unramified. Also $D$ has even valuation there, since an odd valuation gives an Eisenstein quadratic after removing an even power of a uniformizer. Multiplication and the common sign preserve the same invariant. The argument uses the prime set for a bound only and does not require computing or factoring it.

For a tower subfield $M$ with degree $e$, the classes of elements with even valuations away from $\Sigma$ have dimension at most
\[
              e|\Sigma|+r_1(M)+r_2(M)+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Cl}(M)[2].
\]
The parity map at the at most $e|\Sigma|$ primes over $\Sigma$ has kernel given by even valuations everywhere. A class there maps to the ideal class of its half-divisor, which is killed by two. The kernel of that map is the image of the units modulo squares, of dimension $r_1+r_2$. This argument includes elements with nonzero even valuations outside $\Sigma$. By Minkowski's bound, $\dim\operatorname{Cl}(M)[2]\le\log_2 h_M$ is polynomial in $e$ and $\log(2|\Delta_M|)$. The tower discriminant formula gives $|\Delta_M|^{[F:M]}\le|\Delta_F|$. Thus the ranks of all norm maps used in the descent are bounded by a common polynomial in the degree of $F$, its discriminant bit length and $|\Sigma_0|$. The algorithm does not compute a class group. [Class number bound from the ideal count](cogentic-monic-square-rank.md#elementary-bound-for-the-class-group-term)

## Bit bounds and scope

Let $H$ bound the absolute logarithmic Weil heights of the current generators. The norm step increases it by at most a factor two. A systematic product has height at most $(r+1)H$. Its norm root has height at most the height of the product, since $h(t)=h(N\beta)/2$. The trace and addition bounds give $h(\operatorname{Tr}\beta+2t)\le3h(\beta)+O(1)$. Consequently at each descent level the maximum height grows by at most a factor $O(r+1)$, allowing an additive bound for the supplied relative radicands. Height here bounds element size independently of the sequence of arithmetic operations used to construct it.

For an explicit rational basis of degree $e$, the bit sizes of the coordinates of an element of height $h$ are polynomial in $e$, the basis description and $h+1$. In a power basis, recover each rational coordinate as $\operatorname{Tr}(x b_j^*)$ with $b_j^*$ from the trace-dual basis. The dual basis is obtained by inverting the rational trace matrix. Its description and height have polynomial bounds, and the height of a trace is at most $e$ times the height of its argument plus $O(e)$. This gives the claimed bit bound for the rational coordinate numerators and denominators. The same argument works in the tensor basis of a supplied quadratic tower.

For a tower given by explicit relative radicands in the tensor bases, its rational multiplication tables also have polynomial bit size in the top degree and the tower description. If a lower table has common denominator $Q$ and the next radicand denominator $q$, a common denominator for the next table is $Q^2q$. This accounts for the two lower-table multiplications in the scalar contribution involving $D$. The maximal numerator bit length obeys the same doubling bound plus the coefficient bits of the radicand and $O(\log e)$ for sums. Depth at most $\log_2[F:\mathbb Q]$ therefore gives polynomial bit lengths. Integral primitive power presentations can be computed if needed by scaling the tower generators to be integral and successively testing $\eta+t\theta$ for generation, with $\eta$ an earlier primitive element. Distinct pairs of embeddings forbid at most $\binom e2$ choices of $t$, and testing the rank of the candidate powers is exact linear algebra. If $q_i$ clears the tensor coefficients of the $i$th radicand and the earlier scaled generators $s_j\theta_j$ are integral, take $s_i=q_i\prod_{j<i}s_j$. Then $s_iD_i$ is integral because each earlier generator occurs with exponent at most one in a tensor monomial. The square of $s_i\theta_i$ is integral and hence so is $s_i\theta_i$. The scaling bit length grows by at most the sum of all earlier bit lengths plus the input denominator bit length, which is polynomial in the top degree. The exact square oracle and conversions to or from such presentations use time polynomial in the explicit table, degree and current coefficient bit lengths.

Write $\mathcal L=\log N$ and $W=\mathcal L^{1/3}(\log\mathcal L)^{2/3}$. Suppose there are $\exp(O(W))$ original marks, degree and tower presentation sizes are polynomial in $\mathcal L$, and the initial marked generators have polynomial height in $\mathcal L$. The top discriminant bit length is polynomial in $\mathcal L$ in these presentations. After [exact primitive-grid ideal parity](cogentic-monic-singular-ideals.md#kernel-and-cost), explicitly rebuilding its basis products costs $\exp(O(W))$ and their heights are at most $\exp(O(W))$. Now $\Sigma_0=\varnothing$, so $r$ is polynomial in $\mathcal L$ at every recursive node. A downward path has $O(\log\mathcal L)$ levels, giving a further height growth factor $\exp(O((\log\mathcal L)^2))=\exp(o(W))$. The two recursive calls per level give at most $O([F:\mathbb Q])$ nodes. The list at each node contains at most the original number of columns plus the tower depth. All exact tests, kernel coordinate maps and root recovery therefore take total deterministic time $\exp(O(W))$, without GRH. The same bound applies without first taking parity if $|\Sigma_0|$ is polynomial in $\mathcal L$.

For arbitrary explicitly supplied lists, each norm rank is at most the original number of columns plus the depth. This gives polynomial cost at each fixed tower depth and hence the one-third bound for $\exp(O(W))$ columns at fixed depth. At growing depth with unrestricted odd-prime support the resulting height estimate gives only $\exp(O(W\log\mathcal L))$ time. This is an estimate for the descent, with no lower-bound claim. The unconditional one-third case assumes a supplied quadratic tower or multiquadratic presentation. It does not recognize an arbitrary bare field polynomial as a tower. In particular, both degrees of the raw consecutive-degree monic pair cannot be powers of two once its smaller degree is at least two. Kernel recovery also does not guarantee a productive root ratio modulo $N$.

A finite SageMath check in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ compared the norm and scalar criteria with direct exact square testing on all 32 masks of five marked test elements. It included scalars and nonscalars and verified the scalar lift in the top field whenever the norm was square.

A [short-representative refinement](https://github.com/gldanoob/int-fac/blob/73fb386/research/cogentic-tower-short-representatives.md) now gives polynomial bit work in the explicit input size for arbitrary supplied quadratic towers with unrestricted ideal support. At each scalar child, compare the long product to a freshly descended representative in the lower field. An exact square test decides whether to multiply the representative by the relative radicand. Discard the long product after this comparison. This removes the mask-length multiplier from the stored heights at successive levels and gives the one-third scale for the growing-degree kernel under the same presentation bounds.
