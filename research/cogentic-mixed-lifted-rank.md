# Local square bases and mixed productivity rank

Certified local square bases can be combined by binary elimination while carrying their reduced roots. The zero-mask residuals generate the entire mixed CRT sign image. This gives an exact decision relative to the supplied local spaces and requires no further field-square tests on mixed tuples. For Blum semiprimes the same image dimension is a binary rank gain from one additional row, computed by Jacobi symbols without knowing the factors. ## Counts, overlap and genuine relations

Use a common coordinate set $J$ with unit residues $s_j\in R^\times$ for $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, $N$ odd. In field $i$, let $L_i\subseteq\mathbb F_2^J$ be a supplied space of certified squares on an admitted set $J_i$. Its generators come with exact roots admitting evaluation modulo $N$. The results apply to partial local spaces, and to the full domain if the local bases are complete. Ideal parity or an unverified probe kernel can overestimate these dimensions.

In the connected incidence setting, restrict each $L_i$ to zero on coordinates present in only one $J_i$. A relation cannot use such a coordinate. If $J^*$ is the remaining coordinate set, the restriction costs the rank of the projection of $L_i$ on $J_i\setminus J^*$. The same deletion can be repeated whenever a coordinate is now used by only one local space. Write $b_i$ for the resulting local dimensions, $t_{ij}=\dim(L_i\cap L_j)$, $W=\sum_iL_i$ and
\[
S:\bigoplus_i L_i\longrightarrow W,\qquad S((v_i))=\sum_i v_i .
\]
The mixed square space is $K=\ker S$, of dimension $\sum_i b_i-\dim W$. Let $B$ be the span of pair-supported tuples, generated by putting a common vector from $L_i\cap L_j$ in coordinates $i,j$. A sufficient condition for a genuine mixed square is
\[
\sum_i b_i> |J^*|+\sum_{i<j}t_{ij}.
\]
Indeed $\dim W\le |J^*|$ and $\dim B\le\sum t_{ij}$. If there are known common linear rows annihilating every $L_i$, their rank can be subtracted from $|J^*|$ in this inequality. For instance the Jacobi bit row of the common residues annihilates all local square spaces.

For three fields the exact genuine-relation space is
\[
K/B\;\simeq\;
\frac{L_1\cap(L_2+L_3)}{(L_1\cap L_2)+(L_1\cap L_3)}.
\]
Project a tuple to its first coordinate. The numerator is its full image. If that coordinate is in the denominator, subtract two corresponding pair tuples, leaving a tuple $(0,w,w)$ with $w\in L_2\cap L_3$. This proves the kernel of the quotient map is precisely $B$. In particular, if $t_{123}=\dim(L_1\cap L_2\cap L_3)$, then
\[
\dim(K/B)=b_1+b_2+b_3-\dim W-t_{12}-t_{13}-t_{23}+t_{123}.
\]
These rank statements supply a square relation. The sign calculation below decides whether it can factor $N$.

## Lifted elimination and exact productivity

For a bit mask $v$, put $s^v=\prod_j s_j^{v_j}$. Lift each $v\in W$ to a pair $(v,[a])$ with $a^2=s^v$ in $R^\times$ and $[a]$ its class modulo the global signs. Such a lift exists on $W$ since each local basis root is supplied. Give the lifts the binary addition
\[
(v,[a])+(w,[b])=
\left(v+w,\left[\frac{ab}{\prod_j s_j^{v_jw_j}}\right]\right).
\]
The overlap denominator corrects the integer exponent sum to binary addition. Associativity follows since either grouping removes $\lfloor(v_j+w_j+x_j)/2\rfloor$ powers from the same column. Every element has order at most two since $a^2=s^v$. The projection onto $W$ therefore defines a vector space with kernel $Q=\mu_2(R)/\{1,-1\}$.

A field's exact root choices give a linear lift $\iota_i:L_i\to E$, where $E$ denotes this space of lifted masks. To see this, multiply the two field roots and divide by the field overlap product. This root is defined in the localization at unit reductions. The only alternative root differs by a sign. For $v=(v_i)\in K$, the sum $\sum_i\iota_i(v_i)$ has zero mask, and its root coordinate is the normalized mixed sign $\chi(v)$. In particular
\[
\dim\operatorname{im}\chi
  =\dim\!\left(\sum_i\iota_i(L_i)\right)-\dim W .
\]
The dimension specific to genuine mixtures is $\dim\operatorname{im}\chi-\dim\chi(B)$. When all two-field signs are trivial, any positive rank in this formula requires a mixed tuple.

To compute the rank, eliminate the supplied local basis masks while carrying their root residues. An XOR with a pivot multiplies the two roots and divides by the product of the common residues in the overlap. Carry the same XOR on the generator index masks to recover witnesses. Each zero-mask residual yields a modular root of one. The dependency masks of these residuals form a basis of $K$, hence their roots span $\operatorname{im}\chi$. A proper gcd of a residual minus one with $N$ already gives a factor. No joint field products or additional field roots are needed.

If the image dimension itself is wanted, start with the single block $N$. For each residual $z$, refine each current block $D$ into $\gcd(z-1,D)$ and its complementary factor, omitting ones. Each block is a product of whole prime powers that have identical signs on all residuals processed so far. After all refinements, record the signs on the final blocks relative to the sign on the first block. The binary rank of these rows is the exact rank in $Q$, because the relative patterns distinguish every sign class in the generated subgroup. This uses gcds without requiring the prime factorization of $N$ or of any final block. Costs are polynomial in the explicit local bases and $\log N$. With complete local square bases of one-third size already recovered, this combination stays at one-third cost.

## A Jacobi row and a sufficient splitting condition

Suppose $N\equiv1\pmod4$. Let $J(a)$ be the bit of the Jacobi symbol of a unit, with $J(a)=0$ for symbol $+1$. It is a homomorphism and has $J(-1)=0$. Set $h_j=J(s_j)$ as integers in $\{0,1\}$. For a local square $v\in L_i$ with root residue $A_i(v)$ define
\[
\tau_i(v)=J(A_i(v))+\frac{\sum_j h_jv_j}{2}\pmod2 .
\]
The numerator of the half-sum is even since $s^v$ is a modular square. This half-count is necessary when individual residues have Jacobi symbol $-1$. The Jacobi bit of the overlap denominator in a root addition is $\sum_jh_jv_jw_j$. The half-count changes by that same bit when converting ordinary addition to XOR, so $\tau_i$ is linear. Since $J(-1)=0$ its values do not depend on the root signs. On a balanced tuple $v=(v_i)\in K$, the sum of the half-counts is the Jacobi bit of the mixed normalization denominator. Therefore
\[
J(\chi(v))=\sum_i\tau_i(v_i).
\]

Let $M$ be the matrix whose columns are the supplied local basis masks, retaining their field labels. Append the row of $\tau_i$ values. Its rank gain
\[
\operatorname{rank}\!\begin{pmatrix}M\\ \tau\end{pmatrix}
     -\operatorname{rank}M
\]
is exactly the rank of the Jacobi character on the mixed sign image. A positive gain is a constructive sufficient condition for splitting. Solve $Mx=0$ and $\tau x=1$, combine the selected root residues with the overlap denominators and take the difference gcd. The condition is equivalent to the local $\tau_i$ failing to extend to a single linear functional on $W$. On three fields, if the local functionals agree on pair intersections, it suffices to compare $\tau_1$ with the gluing of $\tau_2,\tau_3$ on $L_1\cap(L_2+L_3)$. The comparison is well defined on the displayed quotient $K/B$.

For a Blum semiprime $N=pq$ with distinct $p,q\equiv3\pmod4$, the Jacobi character is an isomorphism from $Q$ to $\mathbb F_2$. It multiplies the two local signs, so it takes value $-1$ exactly on the mixed signs. In this case a zero rank gain proves absence of productive relations in the supplied spaces. For general $N\equiv1\pmod4$, the Jacobi character can be blind to some CRT patterns. In particular it cannot see the sign at a prime $1\bmod4$, even when that sign differs from the others. A zero gain in that setting calls for the exact lifted elimination test if a complete decision is needed.

## Identical local ranks with opposite outcomes

The four-field example at $N=209$ has common argument $m=13$ and monic Eisenstein fields
\[
P_k(X)=(X-m)^2+2k(X-m)+N
\]
for $k=12,14,18,28$. The four rational columns $(2,-130,10,46)$ have independent rational square classes. Each field has one local square line generated by the adjacent pair of columns, all pair intersections are zero, and the four lines give one genuine mixed dependency. Replace the fourth polynomial by $P_{64}$. The fourth field's square class is unchanged because $28^2-209=23\cdot5^2$ and $64^2-209=23\cdot13^2$. Both polynomials evaluate to exactly $209$ at $13$ and have simple common branches. Thus all local spaces, overlaps and incidence ranks are identical in the two libraries.

The canonical adjacent-product root residues in the original library are $(24,69,36,53)$. Replacing the fourth field changes its root residue to $42$. Lifted elimination gives normalized roots $153$ and $208=-1\bmod209$, respectively. The first gives factor 19 and Jacobi rank one. The second has rank zero. Consequently the overlap and square-space counts alone cannot guarantee a productive sign, even with distinct Eisenstein fields, a common exact evaluation and a nonzero genuine mixed kernel. The rank test supplies the extra arithmetic condition.
