# Worst-case workspace for the deterministic order search

Write $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $H=L^{1/3}z^{2/3}$ and $V=L^{2/3}z^{1/3}$. The two-field order extractor can use **$B\,\mathrm{poly}(L)$ bits of workspace** with time polynomial in $A,B,L$ per candidate. This is useful because the search's dominant time counts perturbation choices, while $B$ is the much smaller factor-base cutoff. For each $b>0$, the resulting deterministic time and space coefficients can approach
$$
 \log T(N)\le (v_b+o(1))V,\qquad \log S(N)\le(b+o(1))H,\qquad
 v_b=\max\left\{2\sqrt b,\ \frac{2}{3b}+\sqrt{2b+\frac{4}{9b^2}}\right\}.
$$
Here $b$ is a real coefficient in the logarithm of the factor-base cutoff. At the optimal time coefficient $v_*=(32/3)^{1/3}$, one can use the space coefficient $b_*=(4/3)^{2/3}=1.21141\ldots$. The claim is a bit-model paper deduction from the [optimized two-thirds schedule](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) and the [one-intercept support](cogentic-one-intercept.md). The new linear-algebra procedure is elementary.

## Small-space integer linear algebra from traces

Suppose the entries of an $r\times r$ integer matrix $C$ can be regenerated in polynomial time from a small description, and have at most $t$ bits. To compute its determinant, choose successive primes $p>r$ of $O(\log(r+t+2))$ bits until their product exceeds twice a Hadamard bound, for example $2(r2^t)^r$. A large fixed power of $r+t+2$ supplies enough primes by the prime number theorem. For each $p$ obtain all power sums $s_k=\operatorname{tr}(C^k)\bmod p$, $1\le k\le r$. For each coordinate vector $e_j$ maintain one vector, successively applying $C$ modulo $p$ and accumulating its $j$th entry in $s_k$. The additions to one component of a matrix-vector product use a streamed row. Newton's identities
$$
 e_0=1,\qquad k e_k=\sum_{i=1}^k(-1)^{i-1} e_{k-i}s_i\pmod p
$$
give $\det C=e_r\bmod p$. Online Chinese remaindering reconstructs the signed determinant. This takes polynomially many evaluations of entries, with at most $O(r(t+\log r)+r\log(r+t+2))$ working bits besides the input generator. This procedure trades repeated matrix-vector products for storage. The prime moduli in this argument are fully enumerated and no random pivot is required.

For a rectangular binary matrix $X$ with at most $R$ rows and $A$ columns, compute its rational rank from $G=XX^T$. Its entries, each bounded by $A$, are regenerated by a scan over the allowed columns. The coefficient $e_k(G)$ is the sum of all squared $k$-minors of $X$ by Cauchy–Binet. Consequently the largest $k$ giving a nonzero coefficient is exactly its rational rank, including on rank-deficient input. It suffices to take the largest such $k$ seen modulo any of enough primes: $(2R(A+1))^{O(R)}$ bounds the magnitude of every coefficient by the principal-minor expansion. The product of the primes exceeds this bound, so a nonzero integer coefficient survives at some prime. The traces use $O(R\,\mathrm{poly}(\log(A+R+2)))$ bits. This applies to any row or column prefix. Scan the columns, recording just indices where their prefix rank increases, and do the same separately for rows. There are at most $R$ indices of either kind. The two independently chosen bases intersect in a nonsingular square minor: restriction to the selected rows is injective on the column space, since every other row is in their span.

## Using the primitive inside TryPair

In Cogentic Lemma 7.5, $R=1+|F_{\rm clean}|\le1+2dB$ and the precision $P=64d^3R L_0$ is $O(B\,\mathrm{poly}(L))$. The unperturbed source polynomials, root brackets, perturbation tuple, and each mark have polynomial bit length in $L$. List the at most $2dB$ field root rows, with small integer labels. There is no need to store the interval of $A$ distinct marks. Regenerate any column by testing field norms for squarefree smoothness, the discriminant/root conditions, and the prescribed grid gcds; drop it unless it satisfies the original tests. The same generation provides every row or column prefix rank above.

Use the selected square minor to obtain the source's inhomogeneous solution and one nullspace vector for each free column by Cramer's rule. Compute individual determinant coefficients when requested. Each coefficient has $O(R\,\mathrm{poly}(L))$ bits by Hadamard. Never keep all coefficients of a dense kernel vector simultaneously. Its scalar products with the $O(d)$ real rows can be accumulated one coefficient at a time, reevaluating the point logarithm at the corresponding pivot mark. The exact inhomogeneous check can likewise be streamed one row at a time. The integer bases selected by prefix rank suffice in steps 2–5 of Lemma 7.5: the certificate uses a nonsingular pivot minor and a full rational basis of free-column vectors, independently of how they were found.

The $2d$ real root brackets are bisected to the original precision with dyadic numerators of $O(dP+\mathrm{poly}(L))$ bits. A logarithm can be computed at the same working scale by binary range reduction to $[1,2]$ and the ordinary $\operatorname{atanh}$ series, retaining the current power and one fixed-precision sum. Its ratio argument is at most $1/3$ and $O(P+\mathrm{poly}(L))$ terms suffice with logarithmic guard bits. Integer square roots handle the normalization factors. The accumulated projected vectors have $O(P\,\mathrm{poly}(L))$ bits per entry, including their integer parts. The final greedy choice of $2d-2$ independent real columns can keep just the already selected columns and regenerate all other candidates in each round. Its Gram matrices have dimension polynomial in $L$. The continued fractions, the lcm and the final modular order verification similarly need at most $O(P\,\mathrm{poly}(L))$ working bits. All loops have a fixed nesting depth or polynomially many streamed polynomial-cost passes. In particular the prefix-rank scans and repeated Cramer determinants use $\mathrm{poly}(A,B,L)$ bit operations per candidate. On a fixed number of tapes the stored vector or short index lists may be scanned linearly on each access; even charging a further polynomial of their sizes preserves this time. ## Time–space curve

Use $\log B\sim bH$, $\log A\sim aH$, $d\sim x(L/z)^{1/3}$ and $\log U\sim vV$. The one-intercept construction uses the rank estimates for these scales. Its transfer also applies to each fixed strict choice in the optimized parameter proof: the weights, counts of row/mark conditions, reciprocal one-point masses, and reciprocal root-presence probabilities are all $\exp(O(H))$. The loss in comparing the finitely expanded moment sums is then absorbed by choosing the small-bias error $(2+ABL_0)^{-C}$ at a sufficiently large fixed $C$. In particular the same $U\exp(O(H))$ support hits a successful pair for each sufficiently large input. The cyclic support lists are generated one entry at a time by prime, numerator and modular inversion; their dimensions and their counters need only $\mathrm{poly}(L)$ bits. Each generator prime can be inspected separately. For obtaining a single divisor and then recursing it also suffices to preserve the one-base probability, so preprocessing can immediately return a proper gcd. Complete recursive factoring stores at most a polynomial in $L$ additional bits by depth-first traversal.

For a fixed desired $b$, the [four strict parameter inequalities](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md#a-smaller-coefficient-using-all-two-point-widths) admit some $x,a$ iff
$$
 v^2>4b,\qquad v^2>2b+4v/(3b).
$$
Indeed $x>1/v$, and both $4bx$ and $2bx+2(vx+1)/(3b)$ increase in $x$; it suffices to take $x$ just above $1/v$ and $a$ strictly between the required lower and upper bounds. Solving the two inequalities gives the displayed $v_b$. They coincide at $b=b_*$. For every fixed strict schedule, the base and relative precision contribute $\exp((b+o(1))H)$ bits of storage, while the candidate enumeration costs $\exp((v+o(1))V)$ independently of the polynomial in the per-pair work.

For computable $b$ the limiting pair is attainable by enumerating rational strict schedules with factor-base coefficient below $b$ tending to $b$, and time coefficient above $v_b$ tending to $v_b$. One can fix a bounded rational parameter box containing this sequence. All machines use the same extraction code. Verify every proposed divisor. At simulation stage $k$, restart and separately run the first $k$ schedules for at most $2^{k-i}$ steps each ($1\le i\le k$), reusing the same tapes. The total at the stage is $O(2^k)$ up to ordinary logarithmic simulation factors. One may impose a common space cap $\exp((b+o(1))H)$ with integer rounding, for example $2^{\lceil bH/\log2+\sqrt H\rceil}$ when the coefficient is efficiently computable. Every fixed schedule in the approximating sequence eventually lies below the cap. The outer scheduler and enumerated rational descriptions require polynomial space in $L$ up to the first successful stage. For arbitrary fixed real $b$, rational schedules arbitrarily close give the same tradeoff in the usual infimum sense. These bounds concern the entire deterministic computation, including unfavorable inputs.

The [exact trace replay](../experiments/cogentic-streamed-minors.py) checks 1,060 rational ranks and signed minors. It includes all binary $2\times5$ matrices and 36 seeded rectangular or square integer matrices. The rational rank agrees with direct minor enumeration despite 83 individual-prime rank drops. The polynomial-space question for the complete factoring algorithm remains open at the stated time coefficient.

For smaller space exponents, the [averaged divisor argument](cogentic-space-exponents.md) treats large two-mark determinants and gives a separate quasipolynomial-space schedule.
