# When distinct quadratic marks can share a modular residue

For the [two-field prototype](cogentic-quadratic-order.md) let $m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$. A mark $(a,b)$ has $1\le a\le A$, $1\le |b|\le A$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and evaluates to $a-bm$. When $2A<m$ and $2A(m+1)<N$, equality up to sign modulo $N$ forces equality of the marks. Indeed the absolute difference or sum of the two evaluated integers is less than $N$, so the congruence holds over the integers. Then $m$ divides $a\mp a'$ with $|a\mp a'|\le2A<m$. Positivity of both numerators forces the plus correspondence and gives $a=a', b=b'$. Thus at any subpower height $A=N^{o(1)}$, the simple residue join eventually gives exactly the original same-mark column space, up to row signs. For the height 220 of the finite survey the sufficient inequalities already hold for every $m\ge441$. The observed gain from 24 to 87 splits came from a range with $m<220$.

## Products of a bounded number of marks

More generally fix $k$ or allow $k=o(\log\log X)$, and take $A\ge1$ satisfying $k\log(2A)=o(\log X)$. Among $X\le N\le2X$, at most $X^{1/2+o(1)}$ integers admit two distinct multisets of at most $k$ marks whose products of evaluated residues agree up to sign. The estimate even includes nonunit residues. It implies the same exceptional count among balanced semiprimes, whose population across the dyad is $\gg X/(\log X)^2$. For example use both primes in a fixed subinterval of $(\sqrt X,\sqrt{2X})$.

To prove the bound, choose $m$ and two multisets. Their signed difference as a polynomial $F(T)$ is nonzero because the primitive linear factors $a-bT$ have a unique representative with positive constant term. This includes multisets of different lengths and the empty product. Every coefficient of $F$ has magnitude at most $2(2A)^k$. For large enough $X$ this is less than $m$. Remove any power of $T$; if $m$ were a root, the rational-root theorem would give $m$ dividing the remaining nonzero constant term, a contradiction. Every possible $N$ now divides the fixed nonzero integer $|F(m)|\le 2(A(m+1))^k=X^{O(k)}$. The classical divisor bound $\log\tau(t)\ll \log(2t)/\log\log(3t)$ yields $X^{o(1)}$ choices uniformly, since $k=o(\log\log X)$. There are $O(\sqrt X)$ choices of $m$ and at most $\exp(O(k\log(2A)))=X^{o(1)}$ choices of marks. Their product proves the claim. This extends the [exact equality argument](projective-norm-interpolation.md) to congruences on the composite moduli determined by each square block. Related estimates for products in short intervals over prime fields use different choices of modulus and shift. [Bourgain, Garaev, Konyagin and Shparlinski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.0017)

The argument counts relations distinguished by their rational marks. Two fields can still give independent smooth algebraic norms at exactly the same mark, as in Google's method. Prime-factor decompositions of the rational residues can also expose relations having coefficients outside the bounded product class. The exceptional count gives no lower bound on the running time of those methods.

The [integer replay](../experiments/quadratic-mark-collisions.py) enumerates complete square blocks. It compares direct collisions, up to sign, to the divisors of all nonzero signed polynomial differences and tests the single-mark threshold.
