# Narrow prime bands and finite-order joint polynomial samples

In the Cogentic two-field calculation let $B\to\infty$ be the smoothness bound and $u=\log X_{\rm sc}/\log B\to\infty$ its order parameter. Replace the eligible prime window by
$$
       B^{1-\delta}<p\le B,\qquad \delta=(\log u)^{-1/2}.
$$
Take $\delta<1/2$ for sufficiently large inputs. In the parameter schedules of the [variable-multiple construction](cogentic-variable-multiple-lattice.md), $\delta\log B\to\infty$, so every prescribed target prime $B/2<\ell\le B$ belongs to this band. If rational interval endpoints are needed, rounding $\delta$ with relative error $o(1)$ suffices.

The saddle in $\alpha=1-\lambda/\log B$ still has $\lambda=\log u+\log\log u+o(1)$ and the same leading smooth exponent. To see this, for any fixed $j\ge 0$ the normalized prime sum of shape $p^{-\alpha}(\log p/\log B)^j$ is asymptotic to $e^{\lambda}/\lambda$, uniformly when $\lambda\asymp\log u$. Partial summation of the prime number theorem gives the integral $\int_{1-\delta}^1 v^{j-1}e^{\lambda v}\,dv$. The substitution $r=\lambda(1-v)$ gives the asserted equivalent since $\delta\lambda\to\infty$. When comparing one- and two-prime saddle ratios keep this actual truncated integral and the actual prime sums throughout. The derivatives of every fixed normalized order remain asymptotic to the same leading scale; saddle-point Taylor and Edgeworth estimates that use the top windows $1-v\asymp1/\lambda$ continue to apply. The two short disjoint intervals at distances $O(1/\lambda)$ from the top used for decay on the first secondary Fourier peaks still fit. The square-root-line prime band estimates apply on subtracting at $B^{1-\delta}\ge\sqrt B$. The sharp GRH error is valid uniformly there, and the curved principal bound uses endpoint integration by parts on this shorter interval. Let $C_{\rm bad}$ count root residues outside the target where one of $f_1,f_2$ has a multiple root or where both have a common root, counting each of these three conditions separately. Each prescribed event has probability at most $(1+o(1))/p^2$ per residue, from the submersion of the coefficient lattice modulo $p$. There are at most $p$ possible residues. At the target the first polynomial has only simple roots and the second has none. Therefore
$$
  \mathbf E C_{\rm bad}\le(3+o(1))\sum_{B^{1-\delta}<p\le B}\frac1p
         =(3+o(1))\log\frac1{1-\delta}=o(1).
$$
The uniform Mertens error needed at a moving lower endpoint follows already from the prime number theorem with its classical logarithmic error, or from the Dirichlet RH estimate. Markov's inequality now gives clean active roots at every prime simultaneously with probability $1-o(1)$. In the fixed-multiple family omit the cofactor primes and $r=m$; the same upper bound holds since every remaining affine value-derivative pair is independent modulo $p$. Under the [one-intercept distribution](cogentic-one-intercept.md) each summand in $C_{\rm bad}$ is one product of local affine value and derivative congruences. Their total number is $O(B^2)$, and the bias $\exp(-CH)$ with sufficiently large fixed $C$ makes the total expectation error $o(1)$. Adding $C_{\rm bad}$ with coefficient one to a separable pessimistic score of expectation $o(1)$ forces complete root cleanliness whenever the selected score is strictly below one. ## Any fixed number of marks

Fix positive integers $h,j$ and distinct primitive unit marks $v_1,\ldots,v_h$ from the projective grid. In the variable-multiple construction take $m=\lceil N^{1/(d-h-1)}\rceil$, $e=d-1$, and $W=m\exp(sH+o(H))$ for fixed $s>hc+jb$. Then $\deg R\le d-h-2\le e-h$. The Gaussian projection is uniform up to relative $e^{-\Omega(L^6)}$ over all exact affine evaluations after at most $j$ prescribed prime rows. The coefficient change preserves $\log m=(1/x+o(1))V$ and the nominal logarithmic norm $(xc+1/x+o(1))V$. At each new mark $v_k$ the unrestricted evaluation step is $\prod_{i<k}|\Delta_{ik}|$. This follows directly from the exact kernel $\prod_{i<k}(b_iX-a_iY)\mathcal L_{e-k+1}$ and the integrality/surjectivity proof. Each free prescribed prime row can multiply the new step by at most that prime.

The conditional Gaussian in the $k$th homogeneous output before the fixed leading-coefficient multiplier has width between $2^{1-h}W b_k^e\prod_{i<k}|t_k-t_i|$ and $(1-0.8^2)^{-1/2}W b_k^e$, for $t_i=a_i/b_i\in[0,0.8]$. Indeed the polynomial $\prod_{i<k}(T-t_i)$ gives a vector in the perpendicular complement of the first $k-1$ normalized evaluation vectors, with Euclidean length at most $2^{k-1}$. Evaluating it at $t_k$ gives the lower bound for the perpendicular projection. Every ratio between absolute polynomial center and this width is $\exp(O_h(H))$. To control random conditional means use the Gram-Schmidt innovation coordinates of the Gaussian. Restrict each innovation to $L^3$ of its widths. The regression shift in any subsequent scalar is at most $hL^3$ times its original unconditional width by Cauchy–Schwarz in the orthogonal innovation basis. The complement contributes $e^{-\Omega(L^6)}$ to every normalized fixed-order expression below.

Consequently for any fixed $h,j$ the conditional probability of simultaneous squarefree smoothness at all $h$ distinct marks is at most $L^{O_{h,j}(1)}$ times the product of their unconditioned one-point saddle densities, uniformly under all compatible prescribed rows. To see this, expose the evaluations successively. Each progression modulus after removal of its preassigned factors is at most $P_0\ell b_k B^j \prod_{i<k}|\Delta_{ik}| =\exp(O_{h,j}(H))$. If its class has a nontrivial gcd $G$ with that modulus, every possible squarefree smooth output forces $G$ itself to be squarefree and supported on eligible primes. It then has $\log G=O_{h,j}(H)$, and division by $G$ increases the local saddle density by at most $L^{O_{h,j}(1)}$ as in the uniform two-point proof. All forced factors have the same polynomial gain. The Gaussian covolume prefactor is the actual progression step divided by width, so no dimension-sized multiplicity occurs. This proves the bound by induction. The statement supplies a uniform upper bound for terms where marks coincide after their duplicate evaluators are identified. Higher centered spectral moments also need cancellation among the genuinely distinct marks.

For a small prime $d<p\le B^{1-\delta}$ and $h$ distinct marks, the local normalizer from coincident slopes has an exact form. Denote by $\nu_p$ their number of distinct reductions in $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)$. Every occurrence after the first in its slope block forces the same nonzero residue condition modulo $p$, up to a unit. It therefore contributes $p/(p-1)$ to the coprime normalizer. The factor for one polynomial is $(p/(p-1))^{h-\nu_p}$; the independent pair squares this quantity. Multiple coincident slope blocks are counted separately. The two-mark case is the singular series in the variable-multiple note. This identifies the small-prime correlation that must be summed in a higher-moment comparison.
