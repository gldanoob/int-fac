# Deterministic genus-two selection for a fixed small prime factor

Let $p$ be a prime, $Z\ge p$ and let $q$ vary through primes in $(Z,2Z]$, excluding $p$. Let $b=\lceil\log_2(2pZ+1)\rceil$ and $L=\log(2p)$. There is a deterministic algorithm, given $N=pq$ alone, whose mean time over these partners is
$$b^{O(1)}\min\{\sqrt p,\ (1+p^4/Z)\exp(O(\sqrt{L\log(2L)}))\}.$$
The constant is uniform in $p$. In particular all $p$ have a subexponential partner average once $Z\ge p^4$. The proof uses ordinary RH and a fixed zero-free strip for finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over number fields of degree at most 100, including their Dedekind zeta functions. The recent results below state the required hypotheses. The full-family theorem of Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance supplies the order mass on the genus-two side. The construction here gives a parameter family with a rational divisor class whose two local orders have forced opposite parity. The four free coordinates of that family account for the factor $p^4/Z$. [Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance II, §1](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/pila/HyperII.pdf#page=3) · [Alpöge and independent checks of ordinary RH](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Cureton–Thorner, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3)

There is also a classical unconditional version on fixed splitting classes of primes described below. There it replaces $\sqrt{L\log(2L)}$ by $L^{2/3}(\log(2L))^{4/3}$. The general mean over all inputs in the [elliptic result](mean-time-elliptic-factoring.md) allows a sparse exceptional set of individual primes. This estimate fixes $p$ before averaging over $q$.

## A quintic norm with a prescribed point

Suppose $K/\mathbb Q$ is cyclic of degree five, with generator $\sigma$, and $p$ is inert while $q$ splits. Let $z\in K^\times$ be a unit at all places over $p,q$, and suppose its quadratic signs at the five places above $q$ are not all equal. Set $c=\sigma(z)/z$, so that $\operatorname{Norm}(c)=1$. For an element $\beta$ with coordinates in a rational basis of $K$, form
$$ f_\beta(X)=\operatorname{Norm}_{K/\mathbb Q}(X+c\beta^2),\qquad
   D_\beta=[(0,\operatorname{Norm}\beta)-\infty]\quad\text{on}\quad y^2=f_\beta(X).$$
The formula works modulo the composite integer by taking the determinant of the multiplication matrix over its residue ring. A unit discriminant and a unit norm of $\beta$ give a smooth monic quintic model and the indicated point over both primes.

At $p$, the residue field of $K$ is $\mathbb F_{p^5}$. Every monic irreducible quintic $g$ with square nonzero constant term occurs as $f_\beta$ for ten different $\beta$. For a root $-w$, the norm of $w$ is $g(0)$. The identity $\chi_{p^5}(w)=\chi_p(\operatorname{Norm}w)$, and the same identity for $c$, show that $w/c$ is a nonzero square. Each of the five conjugate roots allows two square roots.

At $q$ the quintic splits. Its roots are $-c_i\beta_i^2$ under the five embeddings into $\mathbb F_q$. Some $c_i$ is a quadratic nonresidue because the signs of $z_i$ change somewhere around the cyclic permutation $\sigma$. The two-descent coordinates of $D_\beta$ are $c_i\beta_i^2$. Their values follow by evaluating the functions $X-r_i$, where $r_i$ is a root of the monic quintic. In the usual odd-degree model $X-r_i$ has divisor $2(r_i,0)-2\infty$ and normalized leading coefficient one at infinity. Each evaluation modulo squares is a homomorphism on the rational Jacobian: for a principal divisor, Weil reciprocity evaluates its value against $2(r_i,0)-2\infty$, which is a square. Thus a single nonsquare coordinate guarantees $D_\beta\notin2J(\mathbb F_q)$ and $D_\beta$ has even order. Checking $\operatorname{Norm}\beta$ and the discriminant by gcd first deals with singular branches and vanishing coordinates. In a branch good at $p$, any such failure at $q$ already splits $N$. [Poonen, lectures on two-descent for odd-degree hyperelliptic Jacobians](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/curves.pdf)

## Odd smooth orders at every prime

Put $Y=\exp(C\sqrt{L\log(2L)})$ under ordinary RH. In the unconditional variant put $Y=\exp(C L^{2/3}(\log(2L))^{4/3})$. Set $E=\exp(O(L\log(2L)/\log Y))$, permitting a fixed sufficiently large constant in the exponent. Each $p$ sufficiently large has $\gg p^{3/2}/E$ odd $Y$-smooth integers $n$ in the interval $[p^2-p^{3/2}/2,p^2+p^{3/2}/2]$.

To verify the parity condition, fix an interval $[x,x+h]$ inside this interval with $x\asymp p^2$ and $h\asymp p^{3/2}$. In each of the two ranges for $Y$, Younis's theorem applied at $x$ and at $x/2$ shows that the count of smooth integers is $(1+o(1))h\Psi(x,Y)/x$ and the even part has size $(1+o(1))h\Psi(x/2,Y)/x$. Indeed $h\ge x^{2/3}$ and these $Y$ grow faster than every power of $\log x$. The first estimate of Theorem 1.3 includes the saddle-point coefficient $\alpha(x,Y)=1+o(1)$ at both centers, by the bound for $1-\alpha$ in the same paper, and its relative error tends to zero. Hildebrand's long-interval estimate gives $\Psi(2w,Y)=(2+o(1))\Psi(w,Y)$ at $w=x,x/2$ in this range, as also used in Younis §6. Subtracting yields the odd count $(1/2+o(1))h\Psi(x,Y)/x$. Finally $\Psi(x,Y)\gg x/E$ follows by multiplying $k\asymp\log x/\log Y$ distinct primes of size about $x^{1/k}$ in a window of relative length $1/(10k)$, as in the [smooth cofactor bound](odd-smooth-hasse.md#a-convolution-of-odd-primes-and-smooth-cofactors). [Younis, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3, §6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05761)

Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance II, Theorem 1.1, permits at most $p$ exceptional integers in the indicated central interval. Every other $n$ is represented by at least $p^{9/2}/(24000(\log p)^2(\log\log p)^2)$ nonsingular quintic models. Since $p^{3/2}/E=p^{3/2-o(1)}$, discarding the exceptional orders loses a negligible fraction. A model $y^2=g(X)$ of degree five and leading coefficient $a$ becomes monic via $a^4g(X/a)$, without changing its rational isomorphism class. Each monic model has exactly $p-1$ preimages as $a$ varies. An odd-order Jacobian forces the monic quintic to be irreducible: a proper irreducible factor gives a nontrivial rational two-torsion divisor class, using the description of $J[2]$ by even subsets of the six branch points modulo complements. Equivalently the dimension of $J[2](\mathbb F_p)$ is one less than the number of irreducible factors of the quintic.

For each monic model $g$, the Weil bound for $\sum_{t\in\mathbb F_p}\chi_p(g(t))$ shows that $(1/2+O(1/\sqrt p))p$ translations $g(X+t)$ have nonzero square constant coefficient. Translations permute the set of good models, so the same fraction of all good monic models qualify. Combining the model count, the tenfold parametrization and these translations gives at least $p^5/E'$ usable $\beta\in\mathbb F_{p^5}$, with $E'=\exp(O(L\log(2L)/\log Y))$, absorbing logarithmic factors. Their entire Jacobian has odd smooth order, hence the marked class at $p$ is killed by the odd scalar containing every odd prime $r\le Y$ to every exponent with $r^e\le 4N^2$. The constant 4 is ample for the genus-two Hasse–Weil upper bound at these $p$. [Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance II, Theorem 1.1](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/pila/HyperII.pdf#page=3)

Multiplying $\beta$ by $u\in\mathbb F_p^\times$ scales all five roots by $u^2$, giving an isomorphic curve via $X\mapsto u^2X$, $y\mapsto u^5y$. The target is invariant under scalar rescaling of all five coordinates of $\beta$. Choose coordinates in any basis reducing to a basis at $p$. The five charts fixing one coordinate equal to one cover the projective directions. At least one chart has $\gg p^4/E'$ good vectors for its remaining four coordinates. All five charts are public.

## A pointwise menu of fields and marks

Write $b$ for the input length. A preliminary trial division up to a sufficiently large fixed power of $b$ costs polynomial time and removes all primes below any of the polynomial bounds below. Let $p,q$ be two distinct remaining odd primes. Put $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$. The fields
$$ F_q=F(\sqrt[5]q),\qquad F_{pq}=F(\sqrt[5]p,\sqrt[5]q)$$
are Galois over $\mathbb Q$ with degrees 20 and 100 and discriminants of logarithm $O(b)$. For $p,q\ne5$ their fifth-power classes in $F$ are independent by the valuations at the two different primes, which are unramified there. The discriminant bound follows from the tensor power basis built from $\zeta_5,\sqrt[5]p,\sqrt[5]q$, whose integral entries and conjugates have heights $(pq)^{O(1)}$ in fixed degree.

There is a prime $\ell\le b^{O(1)}$ splitting completely in $F_q$ and failing to split completely in $F_{pq}$. Here and below a fixed zero-free strip makes the dependence on the analytic conductor logarithmic before taking a fixed power. Explicitly, for a fixed smooth nonnegative $W$ supported in $(1/2,1)$ the weighted prime-ideal sum at scale $T$ for the principal character of a degree-$d$ field has main term $T\int W$ and error $O_{d,\delta}(T^{1-\delta}(\log(DT))^{O(1)}+T^{1/2}(\log(DT))^{O(1)})$. A nonprincipal finite-order ray character has the same error with $D$ replaced by $D\operatorname{Norm}(\mathfrak m)$ and zero main term. This follows by applying the smoothed prime explicit formula, bounding each zero by $O_j(T^{1-\delta}(1+|\Im\rho|)^{-j})$, and using the unit-height zero count $O_d(\log(D\operatorname{Norm}(\mathfrak m)(2+|t|)^d))$. The prime powers and the omitted Euler factors of imprimitive characters fit the displayed bound. For a Galois field there are exactly $d$ degree-one ideals over every unramified completely split rational prime, and the higher residue degrees contribute $O_d(T^{1/2}\log^2 T)$. Subtract the resulting estimates for the two Galois fields, after division by their degrees. The main coefficient $1/20-1/100$ is positive, so $T=b^C$ suffices for large fixed $C$. [Cureton–Thorner's strip](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3)

Such an $\ell$ is $1\pmod5$, $q$ is a fifth power modulo $\ell$, and $p$ is not. The cyclic quintic subfield $K$ of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$ therefore splits $q$ and is inert at $p$. Its discriminant is at most $\ell^{O(1)}$, for example by taking a Gaussian period $\theta=\sum_{a\in H}\zeta_\ell^a$ for the subgroup $H$ of index five. Its five conjugates are distinct by the prime cyclotomic polynomial, their absolute values are at most $\ell$, and their differences bound the discriminant of the power basis. Arithmetic, Galois action and traces can be computed in polynomial time in $\ell$ in the full cyclotomic presentation. A generator $\sigma$ of the quotient is obtained by testing the finitely many residues modulo $\ell$.

It remains to provide an element with nonconstant quadratic signs at the primes over $q$. Let $\mathfrak m=q\mathcal O_K$. The ordinary ray class group modulo $\mathfrak m$ is generated by prime ideals of norm at most $T=b^{C'}$ for a sufficiently large fixed exponent. If the generated subgroup were proper, a nonprincipal ray character would equal one on all such primes. Comparing its smoothed prime-ideal sum above with the principal ray character at scale $T$ gives a contradiction. Enlarge $T$ to exceed $d_0=\sqrt{|\operatorname{disc} K|}$. The preliminary trial division ensures $p,q>T$. Take $S$ consisting of the archimedean places and all finite primes of norm $\le T$.

Some $S$-unit has differing signs at two primes over $q$. Indeed choose an integral $v$ with desired nonzero residues above $q$ by CRT. The ray class of $(v)$ is represented by a product $\mathfrak a$ of prime ideals in $S$ to integer powers. Hence $\mathfrak a=(v w)$ with $w\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m}$ locally at all its primes. The generator $v w$ is an $S$-unit with the prescribed residues. The ratio of the two quadratic signs is a group homomorphism to $\{\pm1\}$. It suffices that one member of an $S$-unit generating set has nontrivial ratio.

Lenstra's Theorem 5.4 gives generators of height $\le d_0^2$ for the $S$-unit group with the smaller cutoff $d_0$. For each new prime ideal $\mathfrak r$ with $d_0<\operatorname{Norm}\mathfrak r\le T$, the real Minkowski embedding of $\mathfrak r$ has covolume $d_0\operatorname{Norm}\mathfrak r$. The symmetric cube gives a nonzero integral $a_\mathfrak r\in\mathfrak r$ with every conjugate of absolute value at most $(d_0\operatorname{Norm}\mathfrak r)^{1/5}$. Hence $(a_\mathfrak r)=\mathfrak r\mathfrak b$ with $\operatorname{Norm}\mathfrak b\le d_0$. Adjoining these generators covers every new valuation independently, proving generation of the enlarged $S$-unit group by elements of height $\le d_0T$. Some such element $z$ thus has uneven signs. These heights use Lenstra's product normalization, without a fifth root. [Lenstra, Theorem 5.4](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1992a/art.pdf#page=23)

This gives a polynomial public list. If $z$ has height at most $H=d_0T$, its denominator ideal has norm at most $H$ and each conjugate has absolute value at most $H$. Multiplication by that integer norm clears the denominator. For the resulting algebraic integer $z_0$, invert the trace matrix of $1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^4$ to bound its rational power-basis coordinates. The matrix entries, its nonzero integral determinant, its cofactors and $\operatorname{Tr}(z_0\theta^j)$ are all polynomial in $\ell,H$. Multiplying by the determinant clears all remaining denominators and leaves integer coordinates of magnitude $b^{O(1)}$. These rational scalings leave $\sigma(z)/z$ unchanged. The enumerator scans $\ell\le b^C$ and the resulting boxes of five polynomially bounded coefficients. Its polynomial count uses the fixed dimension. Checks of the field discriminant, basis determinant, denominators and norm by gcd expose any bad reduction at just one prime. In a witness case, the trial cutoff exceeds the polynomially bounded nonzero integers introduced by denominator clearing, so no witness is lost.

## Deterministic selection from a long partner interval

Run an instance for each plausible small-prime dyad $(P,2P]$, $P$ a power of two, by round-robin scheduling. There are $O(b)$ instances. In the instance for the true $p$, let $2^k=2P$ and use $Y$ at scale $2P$. The machine also tries every public bit shift $j\le b$. For fixed $p$ choose $j=\lfloor\log_2 p\rfloor$. Then the integer $\lfloor N/2^j\rfloor=\lfloor vq\rfloor$, $1\le v<2$, is injective in the integer partner $q$. On $(Z,2Z]$ it has range length $O(Z)$. View its bottom $4k$ bits as four $k$-bit coefficient words. Each cube word occurs $O(1+Z/2^{4k})$ times. The full cube with $2^{4k}\asymp p^4$ elements covers each tuple of four residues at $p$ at least once and at most 16 times. Thus whichever chart succeeds has a target of cube density $\gg1/E'$.

At stage $a$ use the $4^a$ explicit XOR shifts from the [binary small-bias construction](mean-time-elliptic-factoring.md#a-deterministic-search-with-a-summable-cost-per-stage), now with $4k$ binary coordinates. For each target of density $\gamma$ the missed fraction is at most $(4k)^2/(4^a\gamma)$. For fixed $p$ take the union over the polynomially many possible fields, marks and chart choices in the $b$-bit input range, retaining only those giving a target of the stated density. This counting absorbs dependence of $K,z$ and the successful chart on $q$. The number of partners reaching stage $a+1$ is consequently
$$O\!\left(\min\{Z,(Z+p^4)b^{O(1)}E'/4^a\}\right).$$
The instance enumerates all charts and their candidate coefficients, forms the monic norm quintic and computes the scalar multiple of the marked divisor class. Cantor's group law for a fixed-genus odd-degree model uses bounded-degree polynomial Euclidean algorithms, normalization, and reduction of Mumford representatives. Before any field inversion or degree decision check the leading coefficient by gcd with $N$. Before dropping a coefficient as zero, compare it in the residue ring. If a decision differs between $p$ and $q$, a proper gcd intervenes. Otherwise the reduced representatives at both primes are obtained from exactly the same computation. The identity has first Mumford polynomial 1, so the discrepancy between identity and a nonidentity class forces a factor. Each parameter costs $b^{O(1)}Y^{O(1)}$ bit operations using the complete odd scalar. [Cantor's group algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1987-0866101-0)

Stage $a+1$ costs $4^{a+1}b^{O(1)}Y^{O(1)}$, and there are $O(b)$ stages before the full cube can be covered. Sum the stage costs weighted by the partner count, and divide by the number $\asymp Z/\log Z$ of prime partners. The factor $\log Z$ is polynomial in $b$, giving the stated upper bound. Run a deterministic small-prime-factor search in parallel for the separate $\sqrt p\,b^{O(1)}$ cap. Round-robin scheduling preserves the cost of the correct small-prime scale up to the factor $O(b)$, including when $\log q$ is much larger than $\log p$.

## Fixed fields under classical unconditional theorems

Take $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1})$, $\theta=\zeta_{11}+\zeta_{11}^{-1}$, $\sigma(\theta)=\theta^2-2$, and $z=3-\theta$. Its norm is 199, a prime equal to 1 modulo 11. Each of the five conjugates $z_i$ has a distinct prime ideal of norm 199 to odd valuation. Their squareclasses are therefore independent. The ratios $z_{i+1}/z_i$ span the four-dimensional subspace of even exponent sum. Thus the compositum $K'$ obtained by adjoining the square roots of all five conjugates of $c$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$ of degree $5\cdot16=80$. The $q$ splitting in $K$ but not in $K'$ have absolute prime density $1/5-1/80=3/16$. For such $q$ at least one conjugate of $c$ is a nonresidue. Each $p$ inert in $K$ is admissible, a prime set of density $4/5$. Remove the finitely many bad primes $2,11,199$. In this fixed family, classical Chebotarev in $(Z,2Z]$ gives the required denominator $\asymp Z/\log Z$ for the partner average. Field and mark selection are then fixed rational arithmetic, and Younis's classical smoothness cutoff gives the unconditional version above.

## Finite replay

Run `gp -q experiments/quintic-field-genus-two.gp`. It exhausts the nonzero parameters over $\mathbb F_{3^5}$ and $\mathbb F_{5^5}$, obtaining all 24 and 312 monic irreducible quintics with square constant, each with exactly ten preimages. Their Jacobian orders were all odd. At the larger toy primes it verified 555 smooth odd-order/forced even-order discrepancies by independent local group orders and by gcd-checked Cantor multiplication on the composite. A separate search found admissible quintic fields and marks for 108 ordered prime pairs, with largest conductor 691. Nine complete integer-only search examples used binary shifts, rational norm models, and the full odd scalar at cutoff 13. These computations verify finite arithmetic instances. The cost and uniformity assertions are proved above. [Exact GP replay](../experiments/quintic-field-genus-two.gp)

## Arbitrary integer partners

Fix $p$ and let $m$ vary through integers in $(Z,2Z]$ such that $p$ is the least prime factor of $N=pm$. The same mean bound, with $b=\lceil\log_2(2pZ+1)\rceil$, gives one proper split of $N$. It gives the prime factor directly if $N$ is a prime power, by exact integer roots followed by primality testing. The bound for composite partners concerns one split. The proper divisor it returns can itself be composite.

For each $m$ possessing a prime divisor $q\ne p$, apply the pointwise field and sign construction to the two primes $p,q$ using the common global length bound $b$. Its two target conditions already suffice to give a proper gcd on $N$, including when other prime divisors or repeated factors occur. The prescribed curve, significant leading coefficients, and the normalized basis are valid at $p$. A discrepancy in one of the tested coefficients or denominators at any other prime produces a proper gcd, so the full Cantor computation cannot skip a successful witness because of additional factors.

Choose $j=\lfloor\log_2 p\rfloor$ in the public seed $\lfloor N/2^j\rfloor$. This is $\lfloor (p/2^j)m\rfloor$, injective on all *integer* partners. Each $4k$-bit word occurs $O(1+Z/p^4)$ times. The union over the polynomially many possible fields, marks and charts at $p$ already accounts for the dependence of $q$ and the construction on $m$. The same stage count and factorial fallback therefore apply. The number of eligible partners is $\gg Z/\log Z$ since every prime $m>p$ in the dyad is eligible. This suffices to normalize the mean, at the cost of a polynomial factor in $b$. The same proof without the least-prime restriction applies when averaging over all $m$ and the selected $q$ may be smaller than $p$, with a small-factor fallback costing at most the displayed $\sqrt p$ bound.
