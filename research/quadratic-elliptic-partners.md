# Elliptic curves over quadratic extensions and sparse exceptional characteristics

For every sufficiently large dyadic \(D\), there is a set of at most \(\exp(O(\sqrt{\log D\log\log D}))\) exceptional prime characteristics in \((D,2D]\). For each prime \(p\) outside these sets, a deterministic search uses two word coordinates to expose \(p\) in composite inputs. Write \(\mathcal L(x)=\exp(O_r(\sqrt{\log(2x)\log(2+\log(2x))}))\). For a fixed hidden \(K\ge2\) whose prime divisors qualify, write \(P\) for its largest prime, and let \(m\) range over \(H\) consecutive positive integers. The machine on \(N=Km\) emits all the primes of \(K\), each to full multiplicity in \(N\), with \(r\)-th mean-root time
\[
 b^{O_r(1)}\min\{\sqrt P,\ (1+P^{2r}/H)^{1/r}\mathcal L(P)\},
\]
where \(b\) bounds the bit length throughout the interval. For \(Z<m\le2Z\) with \(P^2\le Z\) this gives **complete deterministic factoring in arithmetic mean** \(b^{O(1)}\mathcal L(Z)\). Both ordinary RH and Dirichlet GRH are proved inputs. The exceptional set is specified by a short-interval count. Its locations need not be computed by the algorithm. [Independent verification of the two zeta theorems](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Stream that keeps the original input and completes the residual](common-divisor-partners.md)

## Squares in the exceptional intervals

At a dyadic scale \(X\) take \(Y=\exp(C\sqrt{\log X\log\log X})\) and \(V=\exp(C'\log X\log\log X/\log Y)\) with the constants of the [ordinary-RH convolution](mean-time-elliptic-factoring.md#almost-every-prime-with-a-square-root-exceptional-count). The argument there actually bounds the logarithmic measure of **real centers** with deficient short-window convolution. In the order coordinate, cover the deficient Hasse centers by \(\mathcal L(X)\) intervals of length \(O(\sqrt X)\). Indeed a deficient center makes a whole interval of neighboring short-window centers bad, of logarithmic measure \(\gg X^{-1/2}\). The total such measure is at most \(X^{-1/2}\mathcal L(X)\). A maximal disjoint choice and bounded enlargements give the cover, including the two endpoint strips. Each center \(Q+1\), \(X<Q\le2X\), outside the cover has at least \(\sqrt X/V\) central orders \(4m\), \(m\) odd and \(Y\)-smooth, with \(|4m-(Q+1)|\le\sqrt Q\). This conclusion uses no primality of \(Q\). [Fixed-bin version of the cover](hasse-exception-clusters.md#fixed-bins-and-computable-coefficients)

For \(D<p\le2D\), cover \(D^2<p^2\le4D^2\) by two dyads. Consecutive integer squares in the range differ by more than \(2D\), so each exceptional interval meets \(O(1)\) of them. Thus at most \(\mathcal L(D)\) prime characteristics are deficient. For a qualifying \(p\), choose parameters for the correct square dyad or enlarge both cutoffs to their maxima on these two scales. Both options preserve the stated rate. The same spacing argument bounds deficient \(k\)-th powers by \(\exp(O_k(\sqrt{\log D\log\log D}))\) for any fixed \(k\ge2\). The construction below uses \(k=2\).

## Ordinary trace mass over the square field

Set \(Q=p^2\) for odd \(p\). If \(n=4m\), \(m\) odd, lies in \(|n-(Q+1)|\le p\), its trace \(t=Q+1-n\) is ordinary. The possibilities \(t=\pm p\) have odd parity, and \(t=0\) would give \(n=p^2+1\equiv2\pmod8\). For \(\Delta=t^2-4Q\) write \(H(\Delta)=\sum_{\mathcal O}h(\mathcal O)/|\mathcal O^\times|\) over overorders of the Frobenius order. The weighted ordinary mass is \(H(\Delta)\), and its full rational two-torsion submass is \(H(\Delta/4)\). Kaplan–Petrow's normalization is \(H_w=2H\). Their Theorem 3 and Lemma 1 cover odd prime powers, with \(p\nmid t\), \(t^2<4Q\), and \(t\equiv Q+1\bmod4\) in the torsion formula. [Kaplan–Petrow, Theorem 3 and Lemma 1](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10084825/1/KaplanPetrow1arxiv.pdf#page=9) · [Schoof, Theorem 4.6 and Lemma 4.8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(87)90003-3)

Katz's parameter correspondence from the universal point of order four to a two-isogenous untwisted Legendre curve works over every field of odd characteristic. The [order-four marking and conductor-doubling calculation](untwisted-legendre-partners.md#exact-untwisted-legendre-mass) gives exactly \(2(H(\Delta)-H(\Delta/4))\ge H(\Delta)\) untwisted Legendre parameters at trace \(t\). Gao had already extended his order-four formula (6) to every \(\mathbb F_{p^a}\) with \(p\ge5\), using the same isogeny and the Kaplan–Petrow ordinary torsion masses. The equality here specializes that formula to \(Q+1-t\equiv4\pmod8\). [Gao, Corollary 4.5 and supplementary Corollary S1.3](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00852v1)

For an individual lower bound write \(\Delta=D_0F^2\) with \(D_0\) fundamental. The term from the Frobenius order alone is
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{|\Delta|}}{2\pi}L(1,\chi_{D_0})
          \prod_{s\mid F,\ s\ \mathrm{prime}}(1-\chi_{D_0}(s)/s).
\]
Dirichlet GRH gives \(L(1,\chi_{D_0})\gg1/\log\log(3|D_0|)\), with the finitely many small fundamental discriminants absorbed in the constant. The product is at least \(\prod_{s\le F}(1-1/s)\gg1/\log(2F)\), with \(s\) prime. Since \(3Q\le|\Delta|\le4Q\), each central order contributes \(\gg \sqrt Q/(\log(2Q))^2\) distinct parameters. Disjointness of their traces and the square-center supply yield \(\gg Q/(V\log^2 Q)\) good parameters in \(\mathbb F_Q\). [Katz, §2](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/newratio33.pdf#page=4) · [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3595#page=4)

There is also a classical lower bound for the **sum** of these trace masses over an eligible set of \(s\) central orders. Lenstra's Proposition 1.8 gives \(H(\Delta)\gg\sqrt{|\Delta|}/\log(4Q)\) apart from discriminants from one possible exceptional fundamental field \(F\), of discriminant below \(-4\). For every fixed \(Q=p^a\), at most two ordinary traces can come from that field. To see this, an algebraic integer \(\pi\) of norm \(p^a\) and trace coprime to \(p\) forces \(p\) to split in \(F\). Its ideal is one of \(\mathfrak p^a,\bar{\mathfrak p}^a\), since an ideal divisible by both primes would give \(\pi\in p\mathcal O_F\) and trace divisible by \(p\). In the inert or ramified case, a positive valuation at the unique prime above \(p\) would force both \(\pi,\bar\pi\) into it and give the same trace contradiction. A generator is unique up to the two units of \(F\), and conjugation fixes its trace. Thus the lower bound for the sum is \(\gg(s-2)\sqrt Q/\log(4Q)\). This extends the proof of Lenstra's Proposition 1.9(b) to ordinary traces at prime powers. His stated Proposition 1.9 concerns prime fields. [Lenstra, Propositions 1.8–1.9 and proof](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~cs812-1/Lenstra1987.pdf#page=9)

Separately, the [classical smooth-number estimate](smooth-integer-slot-hasse.md) yields at most \(D^{2\sigma-1+o(1)}\) deficient prime-square centers in \((D,2D]\) at its stated cutoff, for each fixed \(\sigma>25/32\). Its logarithmic exceptional measure on the square dyad \(X\) is \(O(X^{\sigma-1})\). The radius-\(\sqrt X\) cover therefore needs \(O(X^{\sigma-1/2})\) intervals, and the same square spacing gives the count at \(X\asymp D^2\). This analytic count and the sum-of-masses bound use classical theorems alone. The earlier subexponential exceptional-characteristic count uses ordinary RH.

## A field at one prime and two copies of the prime field at another

For distinct odd primes \(p,q\mid N\), the public list through \(O(b^2)\) contains a prime \(h\) with \((h/p)=-1,(h/q)=+1\) and a prime \(f>1\) with \((f/q)=-1\). These follow from the least-prime-in-a-coset theorem for the index-four and index-two cosets, respectively. Remove small prime divisors first, or report a proper gcd as soon as one is encountered. Enumerate both choices instead of attempting to determine their signs. Form
\[
 A_d=(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)[T]/(T^2-h),
 \qquad \lambda=x+yT,\qquad c=((f+1)/(f-1))^2.
\]
Modulo \(p\) this is \(\mathbb F_Q\), and modulo \(q\) it is \(\mathbb F_q\times\mathbb F_q\). In either \(q\)-component \(c(c-1)\) is a nonsquare. Every nonzero rational-base-field value is a square in \(\mathbb F_Q\). [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3595#page=3)

Use the [rational marks](legendre-average-factoring.md#marking-the-point-and-keeping-its-order-even-at-the-other-prime) \(a_i=cu_i^2,\ a_i-1=(c-1)v_i^2\). At the true prime dyad choose \(H=\lceil2\log_2(4D)\rceil\). Trial division through a polynomial in \(b,H\) either emits \(p\) or ensures the prefix of \(H\) marks is distinct and nondegenerate at \(p\). The [logarithmic mark argument](vertical-trace-subsets.md#both-quadratic-signs-from-a-logarithmic-mark-prefix) holds over every odd finite field. For the quadratic character \(\chi_Q\) of \(\mathbb F_Q\), the number of \(\lambda\) missing a positive sign at all \(a_i-\lambda\) is at most
\[
 Q2^{-H}+(H/2-1+2^{-H})\sqrt Q+H.
\]
Indeed away from the \(H\) roots the exact indicator of a miss is \(2^{-H}\prod_i(1-\chi_Q(a_i-\lambda))\). For each nonempty subset of size \(j\), its polynomial has \(j\) distinct roots, so Weil's complete multiplicative character bound is \((j-1)\sqrt Q\). Averaging over all subsets gives the displayed coefficient and adding the roots costs at most \(H\). Since \(\sqrt Q=p\) and \(p\in(D,2D]\), at most \(O(p\log p)\) parameters are lost. This is \(o(Q/(V\log^2 Q))\) at the stated smooth cutoff. Thus the surviving good parameters still have density at least \(1/\mathcal L(p)\), with only polynomial-bit-length preprocessing and a logarithmic mark menu. [Weil's bound over finite fields, Theorem 5.41](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/finite-fields/exponential-sums/60D3D9DC74D98A5DF17D7BF9D2019B34)

For each surviving parameter use \(d_0=a(a-1)(a-\lambda)\), the marked curve \(y^2=x(x-d_0)(x-d_0\lambda)\), and its point \((d_0a,d_0^2)\). The mark giving \(\chi_Q(a-\lambda)=1\) selects the good untwisted class and its point lies in \(2E(\mathbb F_Q)\), of odd smooth order. In each nonsingular \(q\)-component the two-descent value \(a(a-1)\) is a nonsquare, so its point has even order. A bad \(q\)-component can instead give a gcd during the unit checks below. The proof-side target depends on \(p,h,f\) and the menu at that dyad, independently of \(q\). [Two-descent for a split cubic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02255)

For every intermediate \(z=a+bT\in A_d\), compute both
\[
           g_0=\gcd(d,a,b),\qquad g_1=\gcd(d,a^2-hb^2).
\]
Return any proper gcd. If \(g_0=d\) the element is zero. If \(g_1=1\) it is a unit, with inverse \((a-bT)/(a^2-hb^2)\). The remaining case \(g_0=1,\ g_1=d\) can skip that model. It cannot occur on a block containing the chosen inert \(p\), because zero norm over \(\mathbb F_Q\) forces both coefficients to vanish modulo \(p\). This test matters even at squarefree inputs. For example at \(d=35,h=2,z=-15+5T\) the norm is 175 while \(g_0=5\).

Check \(\lambda,\lambda-1,d_0\) and the rational mark denominators individually. Apply the affine chord and tangent law using the same classifier for each zero branch and inversion. A branch returning zero or unit agrees in the inert field and both split components. Thus if all probes went without a split on a block containing \(p,q\), the curve would reduce nonsingularly in all three fields and its scalar multiple would choose the same identity branch in them. The odd scalar containing the largest powers of every odd prime \(s\le Y\) up to \(4N^2\) kills the marked point at \(p\) and preserves nonidentity in both \(q\)-components, a contradiction. Working in this rank-two free algebra costs only a polynomial in the original bit length per ring operation. The bit length of the scalar is \(O(Yb)\).

## Two-coordinate selection and simultaneous extraction

Encode each candidate parameter \((x,y)\) by two \(k\)-bit coordinates with \(2^k=2D\) at the correct prime dyad. Their cube represents all of \(\mathbb F_Q\) with density loss at most four. The binary sampler of the [common-divisor algorithm](common-divisor-partners.md#the-common-shift-on-any-integer-interval) now has block length \(2k\). Its \(r\) starting blocks still come from the original \(N\). The shift \(j=\lfloor\log_2 K\rfloor\) has joint-word multiplicity \(O(1+H/p^{2r})\). Union over all eligible \(h,f\), all input lengths in the interval, and the primes of \(K\) only costs a polynomial in \(b\). The same replay on both children of every split and the same residual-factor charge prove the two bounds at the start.

One machine can interleave this search with the one-coordinate and three-coordinate searches. Write \(d(p)=1\) when \(p\) itself qualifies for the [prime-field Hasse estimate](mean-time-elliptic-factoring.md#almost-every-prime-with-a-square-root-exceptional-count), \(d(p)=2\) when \(p^2\) qualifies, taking the smaller dimension if both qualify, and \(d(p)=3\) otherwise. Take bounded small primes in the first class by trial division. For unrestricted \(K\) put \(U_K=\max_{p\mid K}p^{d(p)}\). The extraction bound holds with \(1+U_K^r/H\) in the length factor, and the complete mean bound holds whenever \(Z\ge U_K\). In particular \(Z\ge P^2\) suffices whenever every prime factor \(p>Z^{1/3}\) of \(K\) qualifies in dimension one or two. The unconditional availability of dimension three gives the earlier uniform threshold \(Z\ge P^3\). The counts of exceptional squares alone give no test for the unknown prime divisors of a fixed input. ## Finite checks and related factoring methods

The replay counts the untwisted Legendre traces by character sums on seven square fields and verifies the exact mass identity for 44 ordinary traces across 1,529 parameters. It checks both split components by independent prime-field elliptic arithmetic on 23 marked witnesses, then obtains 46 proper gcds over the composite and its repeated-prime counterpart. The classifier found 28 by the coefficient gcd and 18 by the norm gcd. The signed-prefix test counts all parameters directly for 388 combinations of conic, prefix, and prescribed signs. Eight additional input-only searches split their composites. At smooth cutoff 13, 59 of 95 checked square Hasse centers had at least one eligible order. These counts use reduced menus. [Finite replay](../experiments/quadratic-elliptic-partners.py)

Williams's \(p+1\) method is an earlier use of the multiplicative group in a quadratic extension for integer factoring. Lenstra's elliptic method uses the variation of curve orders in a Hasse interval. The square-center spacing estimate above applies the elliptic-order search over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) with two scalar coordinates, and the partner calculation bounds a deterministic computation.
