# A CRT obstruction for the planted cut labels

The rational labels of the planted norm-two construction generate no mixed square root of unity on $X^{2-o(1)}$ balanced semiprimes with factors in $[X,5X/4]$, for every sufficiently large power of two $X$. This holds at the automatic degree and permits exponents of arbitrary size. A separate bound shows that menus of polynomially many exponent words of polynomial magnitude, chosen from the common argument alone, miss almost all balanced semiprimes even if either sign gcd is accepted. [Planted norm-two construction](cogentic-planted-norm-two.md)

## Balanced inputs with diagonal local signs

Let $X=2^k$ and let $p,q$ be distinct primes in $[X,5X/4]$. Every product has bit length $2k+1$, so the automatic degree $h$ is constant on this set. Write $r=\lfloor h/2\rfloor$. For each prime $p\equiv3\pmod4$ in the interval, let $m_p$ be the greatest odd integer with $m_p^h\le p^2$. Assign $p$ to the bucket specified by $m_p$ and its $r$ Legendre symbols

$$
\left(\left(\frac{m_p-2j}{p}\right)\right)_{0\le j<r}.
$$

These symbols are nonzero for large $X$, because $m_p$ grows faster than $h$ and is smaller than $p$. If two distinct primes land in the same bucket, $m_p^h\le pq<(m_p+2)^h$. Thus the planted argument at $N=pq$ is exactly $m=m_p$. The labels $m-2j$ have matching Legendre symbols at $p$ and $q$, and the same holds for every product of their integer powers. This includes every planted unit ratio $(m-2j)/m$ and the global unit $-1$.

Both $p-1$ and $q-1$ are twice odd numbers. For an element of order dividing two in either residue field, the Legendre symbol is the element itself. Consequently a square root of unity in the subgroup generated by the labels has equal signs at the two primes. Its residue modulo $N$ is $1$ or $-1$. This is a restriction on the subgroup itself, independent of the exponent search and of any other method that could split the input.

The number of buckets is at most $B=O(X^{2/h}2^r)$. By the prime number theorem for the fixed progression $3\pmod4$, the interval contains $P\asymp X/\log X$ eligible primes. If the bucket sizes are $b_i$, the number of distinct semiprimes from matching buckets is at least

$$
\sum_i \binom{b_i}{2}\ \ge\ \tfrac12(P^2/B-P).
$$

The estimate follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and uniqueness of prime factorization. The scheduled $h$ tends to infinity and is $o(\log X)$, so $B=X^{o(1)}$ and the lower bound is $X^{2-o(1)}$. The use of a fixed-progression prime number theorem avoids any assumption about primes in short intervals around individual $m^{h/2}$. [De la Vallée Poussin, *Les fonctions de Dirichlet et les nombres premiers de la forme linéaire Mx + N*](https://circolomatematico.unipa.it/download/Charles-Jean_de_La_Valle_e_Poussin-Collected_Works-Oeuvres_Scientifiques_Vol.1.pdf)

For a modulus promised to have two distinct prime factors both $3\pmod4$, the ordinary Jacobi symbol decides whether mixed two-torsion exists in a specified generated subgroup. If every generator has Jacobi symbol $+1$, every local sign pair is diagonal. If a generator has Jacobi symbol $-1$, raising it to the least common multiple of the odd halves of the two local group orders produces a mixed sign. The decision takes only public Jacobi calculations. The existence argument does not produce that exponent from public input.

## Menus chosen from the common argument

Fix the same balanced family and suppose each observed $(m,h)$ determines at most $T$ exponent words. After expanding ratios into raw labels, let $D$ bound the number of factors on each side of each word. For one candidate, write its positive integer numerator and denominator as $A$ and $C$, so $1\le A,C\le m^D$. Labels are units modulo every modulus in this family. If $A=C$, neither sign gives a proper gcd. Otherwise the nonzero integer $A^2-C^2$ has absolute value less than $m^{2D}$, and therefore has at most $2D\log m/\log X=O(D/h)$ distinct prime divisors in $[X,5X/4]$. A proper gcd of either $A-C$ or $A+C$ with a modulus in the family requires at least one such prime divisor. This covers a sign gcd even when the word's square is unequal to one modulo the full modulus.

For fixed $m$ and a fixed exceptional prime $p$, the other factor is in the interval $[m^h/p,(m+2)^h/p)$. The interval length is $O(hX/m)$ because $h/m=o(1)$ and $m^h\asymp X^2$. There are $O(X^{2/h})$ possible arguments $m$, all of order $X^{2/h}$. Summing at most $O(1+hX/m)$ partner integers per exceptional prime bounds the total number of sign-gcd successes by $O(TDX)$. There are $\Theta(X^2/\log^2 X)$ balanced products in the interval. Thus the success proportion is $O(TD\log^2 X/X)$ and tends to zero when $T,D$ are polynomial in $\log X$.

For any individual planted input, the same numerator bound rules out a mixed square root of unity whenever $2D\le h$. In that range $|A^2-C^2|<m^{2D}\le N$, so congruence of the squares would imply $A=C$.

The menu bound uses exponent magnitude and the restriction to choices from $(m,h)$. It gives no bound for large powers of polynomial bit length or adaptive choices using information from the full modulus. Exact field identities provide inverse and norm certificates for the units. On the real-bumped pair their multiplicative independence also rules out obtaining a finite order from a monomial torsion identity in the field.
