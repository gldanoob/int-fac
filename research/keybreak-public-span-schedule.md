# How much public relation rank would change the single-input bound?

A concrete target for beating the ordinary GNFS heuristic coefficient is to leave at most $\exp((.720+o(1))H)$ independent parity constraints after adding cheap public relations from the same input and field. Here $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. The schedule below costs less than $L_N[1/3,1.922]$ **if** that public pool can itself be constructed within this budget and usable jointly with the existing good-field event with probability bounded below. Neither the construction cost nor this residual rank has been proved. The current conditional single-input bound without the new public-pool premise is $L_N[1/3,2.154630215299\ldots]$. ## The rank requirement

Work in the same binary space $V$ of parity, character, unit and sign checks used by the anchored descent. Each public column must be a valid ordinary relation with known modular comparison, zero private label, and a certificate consumable by the existing exact extraction. Let $P$ be the map whose columns are the public relations. It suffices for $V/\operatorname{im} P$ to have dimension less than the number of newly collected independent-label rows. More generally $V$ may be replaced by a certified subspace containing all public and retained anchored columns. Distinct fresh rows have distinct hidden-label coordinates; after fixing all recorded public data, a nonempty private selection has the existing uniform hidden-square fiber. This argument requires the public pool and selection to be independent of the unrevealed roots conditional on that data. [Lean quotient-dimension theorem](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedPublicSpan.lean) · [Exact integer split probability](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedOddSplit.lean)

More precisely, the calculation charges at most $\exp((b+o(1))H)$ pool rows and the same bound on their total stored support, with $b=0.960800001\ldots$. All construction, transmission through the field-specific checks and verification of the pool must fit the 1.922 allowance. Given an effective upper bound on the residual dimension at coefficient .720, collect one more anchored row than that bound. Its finite cap and the integer confidence budgets can include any prescribed subleading factors. The rank implication is deterministic. A tagged sparse search can find a suitable dependency even if many other dependencies use only public rows.

## Extracting a dependency with a private label

Let $M$ be the constraint matrix with columns corresponding to the offered rows, $n$ the number of columns, and $H_0$ a known binary linear map recording the private-label parity. We seek $Mx=0$ and $H_0x\ne0$. Over $K=\mathbf F_{2^k}$ with $8n<|K|\le16n$, independently sample uniform diagonal entries for a row diagonal $D$ and a column diagonal $E$. Run the sparse recurrence algorithm on $S=E M^\top D M$, applied by two passes through $M$. If $r=\operatorname{rank}M$, the characteristic coefficient at degree $n-r$ is a polynomial of total degree at most $2r$ in the diagonal entries. A nonsingular $r$-minor supplies a nonzero evaluation: put ones on its row and column sets and zeros elsewhere. The surviving principal minor is the square of the selected determinant. Schwartz–Zippel bounds the probability of zero by $2r/|K|$. [Lean diagonal specialization and probability bound](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedGramMinor.lean)

When the coefficient is nonzero, $S$ has rank $r$ and $\ker S=\ker M$. Zero has algebraic and geometric multiplicity $n-r$, so the space is the direct sum of $\ker S$ and $\operatorname{im} S$. A uniform starting vector therefore projects uniformly onto $\ker M$. Its projected tag is nonzero with probability at least $1-1/|K|$ whenever a tagged dependency exists. Conditional on this vector, the left scalar probe loses at most $n/|K|$ in recovering its full minimal annihilator. Write this annihilator as $tQ(t)$. Applying $Q(S)$ kills the image component and multiplies the kernel component by the nonzero $Q(0)$. Expand in the binary basis and select a nonzero tag coordinate. Verify the binary constraints and tag exactly. The union bound leaves constant success per attempt, irrespective of how large the public-only kernel is. With input support $\exp((b+o(1))H)$ the bit coefficient is $2b$ including sequential sparse products, field construction and recurrence. ## An alternative to bounding the entire residual dimension

Suppose cheap independently sampled *valid public relation vectors* have law $\mu$ conditional on the common public environment. Draw $m$ of them and let $U$ be their span. In a space of dimension $d$, the expected $\mu$-mass outside $U$ is at most $d/(m+1)$. Among any $m+1$ vectors those outside the span of all the others are linearly independent. Exchangeability makes the last position as likely to be one of these as any other. This estimate is distribution-free and applies even when many directions in the ambient space remain uncovered. [Lean leave-one-out count](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedSpanCoverage.lean)

For example, suppose the retained anchored-row law contains a component of mass $p>0$ whose normalized law $\nu$ satisfies $\nu(V\setminus S)\le L\mu(V\setminus S)$ for every linear subspace $S$. With $m+1\ge4Ld$, Markov's inequality gives probability at least one half that the pool covers at least one half of that component. On that event each independent fresh anchored row belongs to the span with probability at least $p/2$. Collect a batch of order $1/p$ anchored rows and run the tagged sparse solve once. More generally it suffices for this success lower bound to hold conditional on the history. For this version the .720 row allowance covers inverse overlap at coefficient .720, with effective subleading factors. $L=\exp(o(H))$ preserves the table's column and sparse solve exponents. A sufficient way to check the subspace comparison is to compare individual binary parity probes. Averaging all probes of the quotient gives one half on each nonzero vector and zero on the origin, so an additive error $e$ per probe gives an additive error $2e$ on the outside-span probability. [Lean parity transfer](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedSpanTransfer.lean)

The cost of actually obtaining $m$ independent valid public rows still has to fit 1.922, and the domination or overlap estimate has to hold on the same field where descent succeeds. Unrooted smooth values alone do not supply valid public comparisons.

## Why a full shared box does not supply the target at ordinary density

In a full-box prebuild, charge $2v\le C$ to scan the rational box once and $2\max(r,a+f)\le C$ to the sparse system. Here $a,r$ are algebraic and rational smoothness cutoffs, and $f$ counts fields in the common environment. Suppose the table must cover prescribed field-specific labels at scale $a$, or must collect enough flexible rows to meet the charged column exponent under the ordinary two-smoothness density. The mean then requires $2v\ge a+s_R+s_A$. For a flexible pool the total yield has exponent $2v-s_R-s_A+f$, and the algebraic columns alone have exponent $a+f$. With $s_R\ge1/(3\delta r)$ and $s_A\ge(1/\delta+3u^2/4+\delta v)/(3a)$, elimination gives
$$
6a(C-a)\ge \tfrac32u^2+2\sqrt{2C(1+a/r)}.
$$
Even charging just one private anchor retains the private work $4/(9u^2)+2u/3\le C$, hence $3u^2/2\ge2/(3C)$. With $t=2a/C\in[0,1]$ and $r\le C/2$, the displayed constraint implies
$$
3C^2t(1-t/2)\ge 2/(3C)+2\sqrt{2C(1+t)}.
$$
Lean excludes $C=1.97$ using a rational chord bound on the square root. Thus this ordinary full-box strategy, with the required label coverage or charged-column yield, cannot build the proposed 1.922 pool. The full-table calculation also explains how the height-free flexible-row ledger reaches 1.9018836 with asymmetric cutoffs. With physical height and full private screening the joint floor returns to 2.1546302153. [Shared terminal prebuild](keybreak-shared-terminal-prebuild.md)
