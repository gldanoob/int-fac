# Half-width private rows from reduced quadratic forms

For one integer \(N\), a private sampler based on the proper ideal class group of \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-N}]\) gives the manuscript-level expected bit bound
\[
 L_N[1/3,C_{\rm form}],\qquad
 2.03288100822533195<C_{\rm form}<2.03288100822533196 .
\]
The assumptions are Dirichlet GRH and the same pinned KeyBreak analytic inputs as in the [short-square schedule](keybreak-uniform-window-integration.md). All random draws, form arithmetic, field construction, relation searches and extraction are for the current input. A fixed 353-stage rational witness gives \(2.033\), and a 4096-stage witness gives \(2.03289126\). The role of the auxiliary quadratic order is to generate one small integer \(A\) and a private root of it. The original higher-degree field still provides the certified relations for the prime factors of \(A\). Quadratic forms and their use in factoring have a classical history. [Schoof, *Quadratic Fields and Factorization*](https://reneschoof.github.io/mcpaper.pdf) · [Lenstra and Pomerance, *A rigorous time bound for factoring integers*](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf) · [Exact half-width cost calculation](../experiments/keybreak-public-quadratic-budget.py)

## The private draw and the computed root

Use the existing prime, power, parity and small-prime preprocessing. Write \(\pi=\sqrt{-N}\), \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\pi]\), \(G=\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O)\), and \(H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\). One can sample an invertible integral ideal \(I\) with class within \(O(N^{-1/2})\) of uniform in \(G\) by drawing split, oriented, factored ideals of norm at most \(Y\asymp N^4\). The ideal norm and its orientation are known in polynomial expected bit time under Dirichlet GRH. Alternatively, on the rough branch the direct weighted sampler returns a canonical primitive reduced form with unit leading coefficient with class distance \(\exp(-\omega(H))\) from uniform. It draws the leading coefficient together with its prime factors, pads the number of modular roots, and tests reduction and primitivity. We use the simpler direct sampler below. Give its independent elementary trials a polynomial cap with failure smaller than any fixed inverse power of \(N\). In the comparison process a nonunit can be treated as a rejection; a separately verified divisor may end the computation earlier. The small-prime prepass handles the complementary inputs with probability \(1-o(1)\) per epoch. [Uniform factored-ideal construction](keybreak-random-quadratic-ideal.md) · [Direct form sampler, including prime powers in the input](keybreak-direct-reduced-class-sampler.md) · [Prepass](keybreak-private-shape-work.md#ensuring-the-count-suffices-on-every-input)

Here are integer formulas that cover the even leading coefficients allowed in the direct sampler. Write the sampled form as \([t,2B,C]\), \(tC=B^2+N\), and \(I=(t,B+\pi)\). With \((t,N)=1\), also \((t,B)=1\). Put \(g=(t,2B)\), \(q=t/g\) and \(a=q^2\). The integral ideal \(I^2/g\) has norm \(a\) and is generated by \(t^2/g,\ (t/g)(B+\pi),\ (B^2-N+2B\pi)/g\). Its imaginary coefficient projection is all of \(\mathbb Z\) since \((t/g,2B/g)=1\). Choose \(u,v\in\mathbb Z\) with \(u(t/g)+v(2B/g)=1\). Consequently \(I^2/g=(a,b+\pi)\), where
\[
 b\equiv u(t/g)B+v(B^2-N)/g\pmod a .
\]
Indeed an element with imaginary coefficient one has this real part, and the norm fixes the horizontal generator. This also covers \(q=1\).

Properly Gauss-reduce \([a,2b,(b^2+N)/a]\) to its canonical representative \([A,2b',C']\), retaining the first column \((x,y)\) of the determinant-one change of basis. Put
\[
 z=(a x+b y)/q\bmod N .
\]
The identity \(aA=(ax+by)^2+Ny^2\) verifies \(z^2\equiv A\bmod N\). Choose an independent fair global sign for \(z\), verify the identity directly, and reject a nonunit \(A\) or return its verified proper gcd. Reduction gives \(A\le 2\sqrt{N/3}\), and the bit lengths in squaring and binary reduction remain polynomial in \(\log N\). No computation of the class group, conductor, genus field, or square roots of prescribed values modulo \(N\) occurs. The explicit class sampler and the original smoothness recognizer are separate steps. ## Which law goes into the row

For the analysis first take \([I]\) exactly uniform in \(G\). The squared class is uniform on \(G^2\). For each fixed resulting unit reduced class, the classes above it form a torsor for \(G[2]\), the elements of order dividing two. The associated root signs modulo global sign are uniform. In particular, for a coprime decomposition \(N=de\), the invertible ideal \((d,\pi)\) has square \((d)\). Its representative \((d+e,\pi-e)\), of norm \(d+e\) prime to \(N\), has square generated by \(d-e+2\pi\). Its image on CRT signs has residue \(-1\) at \(d\) and \(+1\) at \(e\); all relative signs occur. Changing an ideal representative changes the resulting modular root only by global sign. The independent sign in the sampler therefore gives the exact uniform law on all unit roots of \(A\), conditional even on the reduced square class. This calculation applies in nonmaximal orders and for repeated prime factors of \(N\). [Sign homomorphism and proof](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md) · [Lean uniform sign counts on a square fiber](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedClassSignFibers.lean) · [Lean unitary-divisor identities](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedUnitaryDivisorSign.lean)

The leading coefficient \(A\) has the distribution of a reduced form in the principal genus. Prime splitting weights matter here. On the rough branch and a good KeyBreak field, take the ordinary H.20 one-coordinate band recipe with total bits \(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-2\). This stays below \(\sqrt N/2\). In each dyad restrict to split primes for \(\mathbb Q(\pi)\) and remove the quantitatively small bad-parent and elliptic-exception sets. The sum of the nontrivial genus characters on each resulting prime population is small by Dirichlet GRH. The genus field modulo squares has elementary abelian Galois group over \(\mathbb Q\); its characters are quadratic Dirichlet characters supported on the prime divisors of \(2N\). Multiplying the biases across \(\Theta((\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3})\) prime positions controls *all* genus classes after good-parent removal. The fraction of products in the principal genus is \((1+o(1))/[G:G^2]\). Each squarefree product \(A\) obtained has a reduced primitive representative: choose roots of \(-N\bmod A\) at the prescribed primes, center \(b'\bmod A\), and \(C'=(b'^2+N)/A>A\). The class denominator is \(|G^2|=|G|/[G:G^2]\); the genus-index factor cancels. With \(|G|=\sqrt N\exp(o(H))\), the probability of a good product is at least \(\exp(-(S+o(1))H)\), for the half-width schedule loss \(S\). The same note proves a prefix upper bound \(\exp(-(S_j+o(1))H)\): count reduced forms by their modular-root multiplicity and apply Rankin to certified band divisors. [Staged smooth lower bound and all prefix upper bounds](keybreak-reduced-form-smooth-supply.md)

The only input to the public row search is \(A\). Keep its root private and discard the sampled ideal and the reduction data. Comparisons with the ideal class law use the whole bounded transcript. Over \(\exp(O(H))\) independent slots the product total variation is \(o(1)\), and the subsequent public choices can use the same coins. Thus the class approximation need not hold conditional on one rare specific smooth product. In the ideal process, conditional on the public transcript and a nonempty dependency chosen by the verified sparse solver, the independent private signs of its rows yield a mixed CRT sign with probability \(1-2^{1-\omega(N)}\). A successful public field setup, good-parent descents, character separation and exact square certification give a constant factor-or-complete probability per epoch as before. The method needs no assumed public relation span and no guess about parity rank. ## An alternative without the ring class field

The order \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-2N}]\) gives the same half-width bound using elementary genus labels. Its canonical primitive forms have discriminant \(-8N\) and \(A\le\sqrt{8N/3}\). Use the direct factored seed proposal with this bound, keeping even seeds coprime to the original odd \(N\). The ideal squaring formulas use the radicand \(2N\) and evaluate modulo \(N\). The root law on a uniform square class still gives every relative sign: for \(de=N\) with \((d,e)=1\), the unitary divisor twist gives the quotient \((d-2e)/(d+2e)\). A separate sign coin completes the absolute root law. [Targeted construction with the unit ideal at \(v=1\)](keybreak-targeted-quadratic-class.md) · [Class and private root replay](../experiments/keybreak-quadratic-class-roots.py)

For this discriminant the norm class gives an isomorphism \(G/G^2\simeq(\mathbb Z/N)^\times/((\mathbb Z/N)^\times)^2\). Indeed the inverse of \([A,2b,C]\) is represented by the opposite middle coefficient. Ambiguity of a reduced form can occur only on a reduction boundary or at \(b=0\); the two other boundaries are impossible modulo four since \(AC-b^2=2N\equiv2\bmod4\). The remaining ambiguity consists of coprime unordered pairs \(AC=2N\), exactly \(2^{\omega(N)}\) diagonal classes. Their number equals the square-subgroup index. A split prime in the requested unit residue class, with its residue modulo eight chosen by reciprocity, shows the norm map onto the unit squareclasses. This identifies the quotient without a ring class field. [Proof for all odd inputs](keybreak-targeted-quadratic-class.md#exact-sign-fibers-and-the-genus-label) · [Lean reduction boundaries](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedEvenDiscriminantGenus.lean) · [Lean norm-class fibers](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedTargetGenus.lean)

Every nontrivial genus character on a split prime \(p\) is therefore a product \(\chi(p)=\prod_{\ell\in S}(p/\ell)\) for a nonempty subset of the odd primes dividing \(N\). Its conductor is odd. The splitting character of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2N})\) has primitive 2-part of conductor eight, so its product with \(\chi\) is also nontrivial. The same Dirichlet GRH dyadic estimate and product-of-biases argument give all the staged lower bounds after the field-good deletions. The elementary class count gives \(h=\sqrt N\exp(o(H))\) and the prefix divisor count carries over unchanged. Factors of two in the radicand change the leading norm bound by a constant and do not change the schedule coefficients. Thus this gives a second derivation of the conditional factoring bound using quadratic-form reduction, prime estimates and the same pinned public descent. ## Cost and leading limit

Use the half-coordinate grid of the earlier public quadratic budget for **all** private rows. Its moment is \(\kappa\delta/3\); every completed row already has a private comparison root. For the explicit \(2.034\) witness, use the subset of the existing replay with row exponent \(b=\delta\beta\), coordinate fraction \(1/2\) and at most \(2k_0\) rational prime occurrences. The smaller set of additional full-width anchors from the earlier public-pool proposal can be omitted. The raw, stage and full-launch caps and the cost of failed searches follow from the preceding lower and prefix estimates. Charge the class sampler once per raw slot, including its bounded attempts. Its polynomial bit cost adds \(o(H)\) to that slot's log work. After the public column bound plus one rows, the sparse solver and exact square reconstruction each have coefficient at most \(2b+o(1)\). A polynomial-size factor tree and fresh epochs give the expected bound for complete factorization. [Checked finite half-width parameters](../experiments/keybreak-public-quadratic-budget.py)

The 353-stage choice uses \(\delta=1.402688813\), \(\kappa=1.534781893\) and \(\beta=.6727839471\). Its largest charge is staged screening, below 2.032999748. At 4096 stages the choices \(\delta=1.402726234\), \(\kappa=1.535900278\) and \(\beta=.6728571597\) lower the maximum to below 2.032891253, giving the stated 2.03289126 ceiling. Both exact rational replays certify the \(2k_0\) factor cap and all stage guards at symbolic bit length \(2^{20000}\). The former takes 1,867 high and 1,829 low transitions; the latter takes 1,867 high and 1,830 low transitions.
