# Factor-preserving padding for a sparse exceptional set

The [average-time factorer](cogentic-universal-score-sampler.md) is fast on all but $\exp(O(V_b))$ integers of $b$ bits. Its counting guarantee does not by itself give a short deterministic orbit search. It does give a single reusable prime multiplier that sends **every** input of a given length to a fast input, and the multiplier can be chosen randomly with high probability of working for the whole length.

## One multiplier for a whole length

Let a correct complete factorer have cost $\exp(O(H_b))$ on all but $E_b\le\exp(CV_b)$ integers of bit length $b$, with $H_b=b^{1/3}(\log(2b))^{2/3}$ and $V_b=b^{2/3}(\log(2b))^{1/3}$. Take an integer $s=\lceil D V_b/\log 2\rceil$ for any fixed $D>C$. A product $qN$ of an $s$-bit prime $q$ and a $b$-bit $N$ has length in $\{b+s-1,b+s\}$. As $s=o(b)$, the combined exceptional set of those lengths has size at most $\exp((C+o(1))V_b)$.

An exceptional integer at either output length contains at most $(b+s)/(s-1)$ distinct primes of $s$ bits. Hence fewer than $\exp((C+o(1))V_b)$ of the $s$-bit primes can divide even one exceptional integer of the two lengths. By the prime number theorem the prime count in this range is $\exp((D+o(1))V_b)$. Every prime outside the excluded list therefore works for all $b$-bit $N$ simultaneously. Run the fast factorer on $qN$ and delete one occurrence of $q$ from the complete prime factorization. This works even if $q$ divides $N$. Since $H_{b+s}=(1+o(1))H_b$, the factorization cost with such a supplied prime is still $\exp(O(H_b))$ per input, including for the entire batch with the same prime.

The argument only uses pairwise coprimality of the labels and their common lower size bound. For any list of pairwise coprime $s$-bit labels, at most $\sum_{t\in\{b+s-1,b+s\}}E_t\lfloor t/(s-1)\rfloor$ labels can be bad. From the factorization of a product with a composite label, subtract the label's valuations by integer division. This needs no independent factorization of the label.

A uniformly sampled $s$-bit prime is universal with probability at least $1-\exp(-(D-C-o(1))V_b)$. Sampling can be done by rejection using deterministic primality testing, with a polynomial expected bit cost. A timeout on a particular product is detectable and the returned factorization is verifiable. This gives reusable random factor-preserving padding. Selecting a universal prime deterministically has only been shown here to be possible nonuniformly. In the score-sampler construction one can instead supply an arbitrary universal master as in its short-advice theorem. The prime-padding argument also applies to a factorer supplied solely as a black box with the sparse-exception guarantee.

## Why direct deterministic orbit enumeration does not follow

If a computable integer $M$ retains a hidden prime factor of $N$, try $\gcd(M,N)$ before factoring $M$. A proper gcd already splits $N$. If the gcd is $N$, the transformation is a known multiple and fast complete factorization of $M$ would give the desired factorization of $N$. At output length at most $b+s$, there are fewer than $2^{s+1}$ such multiples of a fixed $b$-bit $N$. At exactly length $b$, the only one is $N$ itself. Therefore a padding width $O(H_b)$ does not even give enough possible multiples to exceed the allowed exceptional count.

Moreover, a timeout-only deterministic search cannot derive success from the exceptional count alone, even at larger widths. Follow its all-timeout transcript for a fixed target. If it makes at most $E_t$ distinct queries of each output length $t$, put exactly those queries into the exceptional sets. This is consistent with all the cardinality bounds and defeats the entire search. Adaptivity does not increase the number of queried values along that transcript. Conversely, querying $E_t+1$ distinct candidates at one length guarantees one fast query by the same bound. The obstruction concerns a black-box timeout argument. The arithmetic structure of a particular exceptional set could still give an explicit short list or a direct derandomization.
