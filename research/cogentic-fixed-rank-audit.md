# Independent audit of the fixed-schedule rank implication

I checked the transfer of the pinned paper's Proposition 10.1 to the conservative schedule in the two-thirds note. Given the local inputs below, the retuned Gram has strictly positive least eigenvalue on a nonempty set of admissible coefficient pairs for every generator prime and every sufficiently large surviving integer. This supports the exhaustive deterministic $L_N[2/3,O(1)]$ search. The calculation uses $d\sim D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$, $V=DH$, $z=\log L$, and the specific logarithmic coefficients $(1000,10000,100,3,100000)$ of $(B,A,K_1,1/w,U)$ on the scales stated in the note. This audit covers the rank proof from its local moment inputs through the hypothesis of Lemma 7.4(3). The analytic derivation of those inputs and the final integer-extraction routine are separate parts of the proof. ## Interval counts and signed centering

The grid sieve removes primes dividing $k_0k'_0$, all larger than $d$ and numbering $O(V/\log d)$. Mertens' estimate on the smallest such primes gives reciprocal sum at most $\log2+o(1)$, hence retained density at least $1/4$. For the progression of modulus $M=hM_q\le B^4$ and a Lipschitz subdivision interval $I'$, $|I'|/M\ge\exp((5990+o(1))H)$. Bonferroni at $2\lceil z^2\rceil$ divisors of $k_0k'_0$ produces $\exp(O(z^3))$ boundary errors of at most one each, and the exponential-series tail is $e^{-\Omega(z^2\log z)}$. Primes above $e^{100z^2}$ cost relative $O(\omega(k_0k'_0)e^{-100z^2}+M\omega(k_0k'_0)/|I'|)$. Each error is below $E_{\rm Br}=e^{-4z^2}$. For $F$ with a polynomially larger Lipschitz constant one subdivides with the corresponding polynomial in $L$ in the denominator of the interval length, still leaving the same exponential margin. Conditioning on $a\equiv r_h\bmod h$, $\gcd(r_h-m,h)=1$, changes the main factor to $1/\psi_0(h)$, where $\psi_0(h)=h\prod_{p\mid(h,k_0k'_0)}(1-1/p)$. If a grid gcd with $N$ reveals a factor, this node is split directly. On the surviving rank branch all marks already have $a-m$ invertible. [Lemma 8.2(2), pp. 39–41](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=40)

For a fixed pair of row indices let $M_{jk}$ be its normalizing scale and use the $O(d)$ cells on p. 52. Each cell is contained in one class of a squarefree modulus $M_c\le B^3$ with normalized mass $O(1/M_c)$. The weighted absolute centered summand is $O(M_cM_{jk})$, and polynomial factors from summing cells do not affect the following exponents. On the true diagonal, $B^2\pi_{\max}(Z_1Z_2)^{-1}e^{0.05H}\le e^{-7900H}$ eventually, from $2000-9997+68+0.05<-7900$. The relative conditional mass of close pairs is $O(w^{-1}K_1^{-1/2}+B^3/(Aw))=e^{-(47+o(1))H}$. For $p^2\mid a-a'$, the second class has modulus at least $pM_{c'}$, and the corresponding cost is $O(1/p+B^3/(Aw))$, with $p>\sqrt B$. Both bounds absorb the two-field rough factor $e^{0.04H}$. Restoring these points in the positive singular-series main term costs at most another polynomial in $L$.

In that main term $\gamma^2=\sum_{h\mid(a-a')}\eta(h)1_{(h,a-m)=1}$ with $\eta(p)=(2p-1)/(p-1)^2$ for $d<p\le\sqrt B$. A shortest prefix $h_0\in(\sqrt B,B]$ for $h>\sqrt B$ is coprime to every hit row modulus, and $M_c h_0\le B^4$. Its normalized mass has an extra $O(1/h_0)$ factor since the unsieved grid count already bounds it. Summing the remaining divisors costs $\prod(1+\eta(p))=(\log B)^{O(1)}$, so the long part costs $B^{-0.49}M_{jk}^2$ up to polynomial factors. For $h\le\sqrt B$ and an eligible class, removing an extraneous target-prime hit in the one-point ratios costs $O(L^{O(1)}\ell^{-\alpha} M_{jk}/\psi_0(h))$. On the required full $Q_{\rm pair}$ class, the identity on p. 53 is exactly $f_*\Pi_R^\circ=Q_{\rm pair}1_{a\equiv r_*\bmod Q_{\rm pair}}\widetilde U^{\rm ex}_{jk}(a)$. Even the additional target-prime modulus used in estimating exceptional hits has $hQ_{\rm pair}\ell\le B^{7/2}<B^4$. The exact atom's class average cancels its global mean times the normalized mass to error $O(L^{O(1)}M_{jk} E_{\rm Br}/\psi_0(h))$. Summing the squares over the $\phi(h)\le\psi_0(h)$ classes uses $\sum_h\eta(h)/\psi_0(h)=O(1)$. The resulting $L^{O(1)}e^{-8z^2}$ is strictly below the claimed $e^{-7z^2}$ entry tolerance. The excess in the unconditional mean of $\widehat C$ from the $d$ fixed target residues is $O(L^{O(1)}\ell^{-\alpha})$. Expanding $\gamma^2-1$ in its variance gives $O(\sum_{p>d}p^{-2}+e^{-7z^2})=o(1)$. ## Bad roots and terminating peeling

Set $T=(7/.15)z^2$, $\tau=e^{-.0375T}$, $\delta=e^{-.01875T}$ and $\mu_*=e^{-.07T}$. Conditional entry variances and admission probabilities imply $\mathbf E D_A\ll u e^{-.15T}$, $\mathbf E F_A^2\le L^{O(1)}e^{-.15T}$ and $\ell^\alpha\mathbf E F_\ell^2\le L^{O(1)}e^{-.15T}$. The multiple/shared-root expected mass is $O(B^{-0.49})$ by the congruence probabilities. Weighting the bad diagonals costs $\tau^{-2}\mathbf E D_A\ll u e^{-.075T}=o(\mu_*)$; the $d$ specified target diagonals are simultaneously good with probability $1-o(1)$. The root counts give $\mu(F_i)\le3u$ and $\|w_i^\circ\|^2=(1+O(B^{-0.2}))\varphi''(\alpha)$ with probability $1-o(1)$, since each prime has at most $d$ roots and different-prime row tests are independent to Gaussian precision. For example the same-prime contribution to both variances is bounded by $L^{O(1)}\sum_{p>\sqrt B}p^{-2\alpha}=O(B^{-0.45})$. Thus with probability $1-o(1)$ all squared error sums are below $e^{-.148T}$ in their respective normalized scales, $|\widehat C-1|<1/8$, and the initial deletion mass is at most $\mu_*/2$.

Write $D_t$ for the deleted rows after $t$ rounds. If $\mu(D_t)\le\mu_*$ and all target rows survive, the hit loss on a protected target row is at most $2\mu_*+\sqrt{\mu_*}\ell^{\alpha/2}F_\ell<\delta$. Every row added after the initial pass has no common hit with a previously deleted row of the *same* prime: shared roots were initially removed and different residues are disjoint. For $S=D_{t+1}\setminus D_0$, sum the loss inequality at each member's *own first deletion* (all those prior deleted sets lie in $D_t$). With $F_{\rm med}=\varnothing$ Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(\delta/2)\mu(S)\le\sqrt{\mu(S)\mu(D_t)}F_A$. Hence $\mu(S)\le4\delta^{-2} F_A^2\mu_*<\mu_*/2$ since $\delta^{-2}F_A^2\le e^{-.1105T}$. This closes the mass and target induction even if several rounds are needed. The finite deletion sequence stabilizes. In the surviving error matrix the omitted same-prime off-diagonals form disjoint blocks of size at most $2d$, with operator error $O(d B^{-0.49})$. The lost total column mass is at most $2\mu_*+\sqrt{\mu_*D_A}$. Each surviving finite row has lost normalized diagonal energy at most $\delta$; each smooth column has at most $5u$ nonzero finite coordinates. Cauchy–Schwarz therefore bounds the Gram removed on all surviving coordinates by $O(dL_0^2\mu_*+u\delta)$. This and the surviving entry errors are $O(e^{-.015T})$. [Peeling, p. 54](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=54)

## Norm directions and a polynomial spectral gap

On surviving smooth columns the exact finite norm row is $w_i\cdot z_i=\log X_{\rm sc}+\mathbf1\cdot\ell_{i,\rm true}$, with $w_{ipr}=p^{-\alpha/2}\log p$ and $\|w_i\|^2\asymp u(\log B)^2$. For input $(v_0,v_{F_i},v_{U_i})$ set $t_i=-\langle w_i,v_{F_i}\rangle/\|w_i\|^2$ and use $v'_0=v_0-(t_1+t_2)\log X_{\rm sc}$, $v'_{F_i}=v_{F_i}+t_iw_i$, $v'_{A_i}=H_{\rm Helmert}^Tv_{U_i}-t_i\mathbf1$. The dot product on each column is exactly preserved using true logs, and $v'_{F_i}\perp w_i$. Since $\|v'_{A_i}\|^2=\|v_{U_i}\|^2+dt_i^2$, the inverse has norm $O(1+(\log X_{\rm sc}+\max_i\|w_i\|)/\sqrt d)=O(\sqrt{Lz})$ here.

On this perpendicular subspace $|c_i\cdot y_{F_i}|=O(u^{-1})\|y_{F_i}\|$. The exact-minus-asymptotic two-prime model error has finite-block Frobenius norm $O(u^{-2}\sum_{j\in F}p_j^{-\alpha})=O(1/u)$ and has zero entries in every other block. It therefore subtracts only $O(1/u)\|y_F\|^2$. The diagonal finite variance is $1+O(1/u)$. For $g=y_0+\ell^T y_A$, the remaining square is $(g+m\cdot y_F)^2$ with $(m\cdot y_F)^2\le12u\|y_F\|^2$. Applying $(g+c)^2\ge (g^2/2-c^2)/(64u)$ gives the lower bound $\tfrac12\|y_F\|^2+(128u)^{-1}\sum\pi g^2$. Applying the $O(1/u)$ as an unrestricted operator error would lose positivity; its block support is essential.

The $2d$ root intervals occur at spacing $1/(4d)$, with common baseline at $3/4$. Their mean-log difference matrix has diagonal magnitudes $(3+o(1))H$ because the closest distances run from $w$ to $2w$. All other entries are bounded by $\log(8d)+O(1)$, so the off-diagonal operator norm is at most $(2/3+o(1))H$ by the maximum row and column sums. Its least singular value is at least $(7/3+o(1))H$. If $s_\nu$ denotes a mean of $g$ on interval $\nu$, then $H^2\|y_A\|^2\ll d\sum_\nu s_\nu^2$. The baseline has squared log-vector norm $O(d)$, giving $y_0^2\le2s_0^2+O(d)\|y_A\|^2\ll\sum_\nu s_\nu^2$. Intervalwise Jensen therefore gives $\sum\pi g^2\gg d^{-1}(|y_0|^2+\|y_A\|^2)$. The surviving nominal Gram is positive with lower bound $c/(ud)$ on the perpendicular subspace after the $e^{-.015T}$ operator error. Replacing true logs by nominal ones incurs squared error $O(dK_1^{-1.8})\|v'\|^2$ times bounded total mass. Combining the inverse norm bound, $\widehat C\ge7/8$, and the true-log comparison gives least eigenvalue $\gg1/(Lzud)\asymp L^{-5/3}z^{-1/3}$ in original coordinates. In particular it exceeds $L^{-2}$ eventually. Lemma 7.4(3) needs only strict positivity. The [independent finite replay](../experiments/cogentic-fixed-rank-audit.py) checks the exact norm-direction dot-product rewrite to absolute tolerance $10^{-74}$ on 2,556 vector/mark evaluations from actual squarefree values of two real irreducible quadratics. It separately checks eight root grids through $d=128$; the smallest certified singular-value ratio from their row/column bounds is $2.5331H$. On integer incidence fixtures it tests 328 individual and 36 aggregate peeling inequalities and 18 final sparse-energy bounds using exact rational arithmetic. These tests exercise the signs, conditioning and cascade bounds. The asymptotic positivity conclusion uses the estimates above.
