# An obstruction to adaptive monic perturbations

The [private-grid obstruction](cogentic-private-grid-density.md#searching-a-prescribed-list-of-fields) excludes a short list of polynomials fixed before the base is chosen. Counting possible evaluations at each mark gives two bounds for coefficients chosen after seeing the input.

- Fix an integer \(k\ge0\) and positive constants \(c,\theta\) with \(\theta+(k+2)c<1/(6c)\). There are infinitely many rough composite inputs on the raw monic schedule for which every polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(k\) and coefficient height at most \(e^{\theta W}\) gives only trivial ordinary-square signs throughout the grid of height \(e^{cW}\). Every grid mark is a unit, and the derivatives at the common root are units for all the polynomials in this envelope.
- Fix \(C>0\) and \(0<\gamma<3/2\). There are infinitely many rough composite inputs on the same schedule for which the ordinary-square conclusion holds throughout the grid of height \(\exp(\gamma\sqrt d\log d)\), simultaneously for every \(P\) of degree at most \(d-2\) and coefficient height at most \(e^{CW}\). The argument in this range guarantees unit mark values. A derivative gcd for an adaptively chosen polynomial can still give a factor.

Here \(W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\) and \(d\) is the automatic scheduled degree. Both statements concern separate pairs
\[
 F_P=X^d+2+4(X-m)P(X),\qquad G_P=F_P+(X-m)X^d.
\]
The coefficients can be any functions of \(N\) and of earlier search results within the stated envelopes. For example, at \(c=1/10\), all adaptive linear perturbations of coefficient height \(e^W\) are covered. The full coefficient box of these linear perturbations has \(e^{(2+o(1))W}\) members, beyond the earlier prescribed-list bound \(e^{\sigma W}\) with \(\sigma<1/(6c)-2c=22/15\).

## Counting projected values

Use arbitrarily large odd prime degrees \(d\), the fixed scheduled base scale \(M=2^{d^2j}\) from the earlier obstruction, and its interval of bases \(m\sim M\) with \(N=m^d+2\). Put \(W_d=(d\log M)^{1/3}(\log(d\log M))^{2/3}\). The schedule gives \(\log M\sim3d^2\log d\), \(W_d\sim3d\log d\), and \(W\le W_d\). Work with the larger coefficient limits and, in the first assertion, the larger grid at \(W_d\).

For degree at most \(k\le d-2\), a primitive mark \((a,b)\) with \(b>0\) reads only the integer \(u=b^kP(a/b)\) from the coefficients. If their absolute values are at most \(H_0\), then \(|u|\le(k+1)H_0A^k\) for \(|a|,b\le A\). For \(u\ne0\), the marked homogeneous norm in \(F_P\) is
\[
 a^d+2b^d+4(a-mb)b^{d-1-k}u.
\]
For \(u=0\) and \(a\ne0\), the norm in \(G_P\) is the raw affine norm \(a^d(a+b)+2b^{d+1}-mba^d\). For \(u=0\) and \(a=0\), the primitive mark is \((0,1)\) and its norm in \(G_P\) is 2. The nonconstant affine cases give at most \(O(A^2(1+(k+1)H_0 A^k))\) forms \(B-C_1m\), fixed before choosing \(m\). The counting includes all integers in the projection interval, even those unattainable by the coefficient box.

For the fixed-degree assertion use \(H_0=e^{\theta W_d}\) and \(A=\lfloor e^{cW_d}\rfloor\). There are at most \(\exp((\theta+(k+2)c+o(1))W_d)\) forms. For the growing-degree assertion use \(k=d-2\), \(H_0=e^{CW_d}\), \(J=d^{3/2}\log d\) and \(A=\lfloor\exp(\gamma\sqrt d\log d)\rfloor\). There are at most \(\exp((\gamma+o(1))J)\) forms. In both ranges \(\log |B|,\log |C_1|\le(c_0+o(1))\log M\) for nonzero coefficients, with \(c_0=c<1\) in the first range and \(c_0=0\) in the second.

## Uniform exclusion count

Let \(Z=2^{\lfloor d^2/1000\rfloor}\). Choose the prescribed primes \(p,q\in(Z,2Z)\) and the base progression as in the [earlier rough input construction](cogentic-private-grid-density.md#a-fixed-progression-of-bases). In addition to the raw unit-mark exclusions, avoid every prime divisor of the nonzero \(C_1(B^d+2C_1^d)\) for the projected forms. The latter integer is nonzero because \(-2\) is no rational \(d\)-th power. The sum of the logarithms of these exclusions is at most the number of forms times a polynomial in \(d,\log M\). It is \(o(Z/\log Z)\) since both \(W_d\) and \(J\) are \(o(d^2)\). The same prime selection and roughness sieve give \(K=M^{1-o(1)}\) consecutive progression indices, of which at least \(Kd^{-O(1)}\) yield the required rough composite inputs. Here rough means that every prime divisor exceeds \(Z\), and the two prescribed primes occur once. The choice of \(p,q\) ensures that neither divides any nonconstant marked affine value on the progression.

Set \(y=2A^2\). Divide each nonconstant affine form by \(g=\gcd(B,C_1)>0\) and remove its 2-part at the evaluated value. The remaining odd absolute value is between \(M/4\) and \(M^{1+c_0+o(1)}\) for sufficiently large degrees. The lower bound uses \(|C_1|/g\ge1\), \(|B|=o(M)\), and the original valuation at two being at most one. That valuation bound follows directly from the formulas. An odd numerator gives an odd value, and an even numerator has odd denominator and value \(2\bmod4\). An odd divisor of the normalized value must be coprime to \(2p^2q^2 C_1/g\), so its event has density at most \(1/u_0+1/K\) for each specified odd divisor \(u_0\).

We seek a prime above \(y\) with odd valuation outside the set \(E\) of prime divisors of \(g\) above \(y\). Then its valuation stays odd on restoring the content. The size of \(E\) is \(O(d)\) in both regimes by the bounds on \(g\le |C_1|\) and on \(\log y\). Put \(T_d=W_d\) or \(J\) according to regime and \(Y_*=\exp(C_*T_d)\) with a sufficiently large fixed \(C_*\), exceeding the coefficient in the exponential form count and with \(Y_*\ge y\). Discard the indices where any prime in \(E\) above \(Y_*\) divides the normalized value. Their fraction is \(O(d/Y_*+d/K)\) per form.

If no required odd valuation remains, the odd absolute value is \(v s^2\), where the prime support of \(v\) lies among the primes through \(y\) and the members of \(E\) through \(Y_*\). Take \(D_0=M^\kappa\) with fixed \(0<\kappa<1\). If \(v<D_0\), summing the densities for the alternative square divisors \(s^2\) gives \(O(M^{-(1-\kappa)/2})+M^{-(1-c_0)/2+o(1)}\). This uses \(s\gg M^{(1-\kappa)/2}\) and \(s\le M^{(1+c_0)/2+o(1)}\). Otherwise \(v\) has a supported divisor in \([D_0/Y_*,D_0]\), by multiplying successive prime factors until reaching \(D_0/Y_*\). The union bound for these divisors is
\[
 \exp\left(-\lambda\log(D_0/Y_*)+O\big(y^\lambda\log\log(3y)+d y^{-1+\lambda}\big)\right)+D_0/K .
\]
In fact the \(1/u_0\) term is bounded by \((D_0/Y_*)^{-\lambda}\) times the weighted Euler product of the allowed primes. For \(\lambda=o(1)\), the primes through \(y\) contribute \(O(y^\lambda\log\log(3y))\) to its logarithm by the reciprocal prime sum. The additional primes contribute \(O(dy^{-1+\lambda})\). The \(1/K\) errors are summed over at most \(D_0\) positive integers.

For the fixed-degree regime take \(\lambda=(1-\epsilon)\log d/\log y\) with fixed small \(\epsilon>0\). The exponent is \(-((1-\epsilon)\kappa/(6c)+o(1))W_d\). For the growing-degree regime take \(\lambda=(3/2-\epsilon)\log d/\log y\). The Euler logarithm is \(o(J)\), and the exponent is \(-((3/2-\epsilon)3\kappa/(2\gamma)+o(1))J\). In both cases \(\lambda\log Y_*=o(T_d)\). The square-divisor losses and \(D_0/K\) are \(M^{-\Omega(1)}\). The strict hypotheses permit \(\kappa\) close to one and \(\epsilon\) close to zero so the exponent in the form count is smaller in magnitude than the negative per-form exponent. Thus the total excluded fraction is \(\exp(-\Omega(T_d))\), smaller than the roughness sieve's surviving fraction.

## Unit residues, derivatives and square signs

The unit-mark exclusions are independent of \(P\). If a prime divisor of \(N\) divides \(a-bm\), it divides \(a^d+2b^d\). The prescribed primes avoid these integers. Every other candidate prime \(\ell\) is above \(Z\) and costs at most \(1/\ell+1/K\) of the progression per mark. There are at most \(O(A^2d\log(2A))\) occurrences of such primes across the finite grid. The total loss is negligible in both regimes. Rational mark contents and pure rational marks are also units since their prime factors are at most \(A\).

For fixed \(k\), the number of entire coefficient vectors is still \(\exp(O(W_d))\). For every such vector exclude primes dividing either resultant of \(X^d+2\) with \(dX^{d-1}+4P(X)\) or with \(dX^{d-1}+4P(X)-2\). Both are nonzero by irreducibility and the degree bound. Their logarithmic sizes are polynomial in \(d,\log M\). For each prime in these exclusions, omit it during the prescribed prime selection. Otherwise, once roughness excludes primes through \(Z\), lose at most \(d(1/\ell+1/K)\) of the indices for each candidate \(\ell>Z\) by counting roots of \(m^d+2\). These losses are negligible. The two tested polynomials evaluate to the two derivatives modulo \(N\). For the growing-degree envelope the entire vectors can number \(\exp(\Theta(dW_d))\), and this derivative exclusion argument uses the full vectors. The projected count alone supplies no uniform derivative guarantee in that case.

Fix any selected \(P\) in the envelope at a surviving input. Both fields are Eisenstein at two because \(m\) is odd and the coefficient perturbation is a multiple of four. Each odd rational valuation found in a marked norm gives at least one odd prime-ideal valuation. That ideal cannot divide any other primitive direction in the same field, since it would then contain a nonzero determinant of absolute value at most \(2A^2\). Consequently any simultaneous field square, even with negative exponents, must use each marked direction with even aggregate exponent. If the zero slope with \(P(0)=0\) is still unaccounted for, its norm in \(G_P\) is 2 and the even field degree makes any rational content invisible in that two-adic parity test. The odd degree of \(F_P\) then forces the residual rational scalar to be a rational square by taking norms. The two canonical roots are the common rational square root times their respective mark half-products. They reduce to the same unit up to a global sign wherever the supplied square roots have valid reductions. This includes the power orders localized at the unit marks, regardless of the derivative tests. Seeking a factor directly from a derivative gcd or using independent unit corrections is outside the square-sign conclusion.

The [exact replay](../experiments/cogentic-adaptive-perturbation-obstruction.py) checks 3,750 marked norms, 618 projected affine forms and 250 derivative pairs. It verifies the affine identities, the normalized progression congruences and the exceptional zero direction on finite coefficient boxes.
