# Completing the finite monic primitive-grid split test

For a prescribed finite grid, exact ideal parity, local probes and exact field-square testing give a terminating deterministic decision of whether any signed relation in that grid yields a proper difference gcd. Dedekind GRH for the quadratic extensions specified below gives an $\exp(O(W))$ time bound on the monic schedule. The output can be verified unconditionally. For an odd prime power every square root of unity is a global sign. Even among inputs with at least two distinct prime factors, some prescribed fields and grids have no productive relation. A deterministic choice of fields and a grid of one-third height guaranteed to split every remaining composite is open. [Private-grid obstruction](cogentic-private-grid-density.md)

## The decision procedure

Let $N>1$ be odd and let $P_1,P_2$ be monic irreducible integer defining polynomials. Require $P_i(m)=0\bmod N$ and $\gcd(P_i'(m),N)=1$. The common-root repair gives such a pair or returns a proper divisor for the scheduled construction. Use all primitive marks $|a|\le A$, $1\le b\le A$ for which $a-bm$ is a unit modulo $N$. Each column contains $\gamma_{ij}=a_j-b_j\alpha_i$ in field $i$. Adjoin one shared sign bit, whose column is $-1$ in both fields and has zero ideal parity. A signed mask $v$ determines products $\beta_i(v)$ with the same unit value at $m$. [Common-root repair and reduction](cogentic-monic-index.md)

The exact collector of commit 0d55ab4 computes the full ideal-parity space, including singular primes and composite private residuals. Its rational cutoff is $Y\ge\max(2A^2,2\deg P_1,2\deg P_2)$. Above $Y$ no prime ideal occurs in two different primitive-mark columns. The composite test checks all ideals over a private residual, so any discarded coordinate is zero in every parity relation. Write $V_0$ for the parity kernel with those coordinates restored as zeros and the shared sign basis vector adjoined. Every signed simultaneous field-square relation belongs to $V_0$. [Exact collector](../experiments/cogentic-monic-exact-grid.py) · [Parity completeness and bit bounds](cogentic-monic-singular-ideals.md#kernel-and-cost)

In each round, extend the rational prime cutoff geometrically, for example through $2,4,8,\ldots$. Add leading-unit Legendre rows at odd simple roots in each field. These rows handle marks divisible by the local probe ideal and test the shared sign by the symbol of $-1$. Intersect the current space with the new row kernels. Each resulting space contains the true simultaneous square kernel $K$. [Normalized local probes](cogentic-monic-square-probes.md)

Apply the exact square test in both fields to each vector in a basis of the current intersection. For any simultaneous square, recover its roots and reduce them modulo $N$ by derivative clearing. A root of an algebraic integer is integral here because it satisfies the monic quadratic equation over the ring of integers. The derivative unit makes reduction defined for every such root. Check the two modular squares against the common signed product, and return a proper difference gcd after exact division if one occurs. Otherwise continue to the next cutoff whenever some basis vector fails the joint square test. Stop in a round in which every basis vector is an exact simultaneous square, including when the basis is empty. [Exact field-square oracle](cogentic-monic-square-extraction.md) · [Derivative clearing](cogentic-monic-index.md)

In an all-square round, the current intersection equals $K$ because it contains $K$ and its basis lies in $K$. On $K$ the ratio of the two modular roots defines a homomorphism into $\{u\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times:u^2=1\}/\{\pm1\}$. Each root choice changes the ratio only by a global sign. When masks are combined, their overlap factors have the same unit reduction in both fields, including the shared sign, so the ratio is multiplicative. A nontrivial class gives a proper difference gcd for odd $N$. If all basis classes are trivial, every signed relation has a trivial class. The conclusion covers repeated prime factors of $N$ and refers to this grid and pair of fields. [Basis-only splitting proof](cogentic-square-kernel-splitting.md#extraction-from-a-supplied-basis)

## Termination and time

For unconditional termination, fix $v\in V_0\setminus K$. In at least one field $K_i$ the product $\beta_i(v)$ is nonsquare, giving the quadratic extension $E=K_i(\sqrt{\beta_i(v)})$. For these fixed fields, the unconditional prime ideal theorem gives $2\psi_{K_i}(x)-\psi_E(x)=x+o(x)$. Rational norms $p^j$ with $j\ge2$ contribute $O(d_i\sqrt{x}\log x)$ in total, where $d_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]$. Excluding primes dividing two or the defining discriminants, as well as primes ramified in the extension, removes only finitely many first-power terms. At the remaining degree-one ideals, the split terms cancel and twice the weighted inert count remains. This tends to infinity. Each such inert ideal yields a simple root and its leading-unit character detects $v$, because the product has even valuation. There are finitely many masks, so a finite cutoff detects every nonsquare mask. No unconditional one-third time estimate follows from this argument. [Grenié and Molteni, §1 and Corollary 1.2](https://sites.unimi.it/molteni/research/papers-pdf/33-molteni-Explicit_versions_of_the_prime_ideal_theorem_for_Dedekind_zeta_functions_under_GRH.pdf) · [Degree-one counting](cogentic-monic-squareclass-cutoff.md#uniform-quadratic-discriminant-and-inert-prime-count)

For a uniform bound, assume GRH for $\zeta_{K_i}$ and for the Dedekind zeta functions of $K_i(\sqrt{\beta_i(v)})$ for each $i$ and every nonsquare $\beta_i(v)$ with $v\in V_0$. The Dirichlet critical-line statement over $\mathbb Q$ does not supply this hypothesis in general. At each finite place an even-valuation product is locally a square times a unit. The relative discriminant therefore divides $4\mathcal O_{K_i}$. The parity-even cutoff bounds the largest needed rational prime by a polynomial in the degrees and defining coefficient bit lengths, independently of the marked norms. It can be computed without factoring the discriminants. The algorithm stops by the first doubling at or above that cutoff under GRH. It can instead continue to the basis condition whenever a nominal bound is passed, so no analytic hypothesis is required to verify its answer. [Effective parity-even cutoff](cogentic-monic-square-probes.md#adaptive-stopping-and-the-parity-even-cutoff)

Put $L=\log N$ and $W=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}$. On the monic schedule the degrees and coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in $L$. Take $A,Y\le\exp(O(W))$ with the required lower bound on $Y$. There are at most $(2A+1)A$ marks. Their norms and the descriptions for the private ideal tests have polynomial bit lengths in $L$, so exact parity costs $\exp(O(W))$. Under the stated GRH there are $O(\log L)$ doubling rounds through a prime limit $L^{O(1)}$. Each round has at most the signed grid dimension in exact tests. Rebuild each tested product from its original mask, multiplying each marked factor at most once. Monic reduction bounds each reduced coefficient by $O(A^2\operatorname{poly}(L))$ bits. Root recovery, probe projection and the modular checks are polynomial in the explicit sizes. These bounds give $\exp(O(W))$ total conditional bit cost.

## Replaying an exact no-split certificate

For the declared input and grid, record the influential probe rows, identified by a field, rational prime and residue root. Record the final basis masks and their two exact roots as rational power-basis polynomials. At most $\dim V_0$ rows need recording, since each influential row lowers the current dimension.

1. Verify the common-root and derivative-unit assumptions and rebuild exact $V_0$ for the declared grid, including the private-coordinate exclusions.
2. Verify the odd rational primes and simple-root conditions for the logged rows. Recompute their normalized characters and impose them on $V_0$. No enumeration of intervening primes is needed.
3. Check that the supplied masks are independent, lie in that intersection and number exactly its dimension. Verify their two field-square identities in the power bases. Check integrality of each derivative-cleared numerator, reduce it modulo $N$ and verify that the two root residues agree up to global sign.

The dimension check certifies that the logged rows and explicit square identities fully determine $K$. This replays the no-split result without repeating the adaptive prime search or square-root discovery. The masks, probe rows and roots alone certify relative to a correct $V_0$. To cover the original grid, the verifier also needs the exact parity reconstruction in step 1 or independently verifiable parity and private-exclusion evidence. A positive result requires only a checked proper divisor of $N$.
