# Large-prime cycles below the full-grid relation threshold

On the full projective grid and under its Gaussian and primitive Dirichlet RH inputs, allowing $o(D)$ additional prime factors per field leaves the leading one-mark supply exponent unchanged. The exact integer valuation cancellations give a stronger restriction. Strictly below the existing relation threshold, the expected number of partial columns that can participate in any exact cancellation is smaller than the factor-base row scale by a fixed exponential factor. With one additional prime in each field, every fixed cycle length also has vanishing expected count below that threshold. These are bounds on the full-rank extraction strategy. They do not exclude a target-specific relation obtained from fewer columns. [Full-grid trace](cogentic-averaged-projective-trace.md) · [Exact extraction and cost](cogentic-fast-full-grid-cost.md)

## The additional rows are exact ideal valuations

Use the parameters $D,H,A,B,c,b,S$ of the full-grid construction and put $\rho=2S/(3b)$ and $\eta=2c-\rho$. There are at most $A^2$ distinct primitive marks, with slopes in a fixed bounded interval, and their ordinary joint smoothness probability has leading exponent $-\rho H$. Write $J$ for the ordinary narrow band. Keep the target prime and any prescribed target occurrence as in the old extractor. In each field allow the other marked factors to be a squarefree $J$-product times a squarefree product of at most $k(N)=o(D)$ primes above $B$. Use only primes coprime to $N$ and the leading coefficient, and record the simple ideal label of each extra prime, with its field and root coordinate. A nonunit can be treated by its integer gcd. Other exclusions can only reduce the supply counted here.

If an ideal above $p$ divides both linear marks at $v=(a,\beta)$ and $v'=(a',\beta')$, it contains the nonzero determinant $\Delta=a\beta'-a'\beta$. Thus $p\mid\Delta$ and $p\le2A^2$. Any larger extra row is private and forces its column's coefficient to vanish in an exact cancellation. Discard all columns carrying such a row when bounding the useful partial pool. At a fixed first mark, any two different possible partners with the same projective reduction modulo $p$ have slopes separated by at least $p/A^2$. Consequently the number of ordered pairs of distinct marks with $p\mid\Delta$ is $O(A^4/p)$ for $p\le2A^2$. The same argument for two distinct congruence primes gives $O(1+A^2/(pp'))$ choices for one mark whose two projective classes have already been prescribed. Let $T$ be the added rows, $S_0$ the original finite rows including the field sum, and $U_0$ the trace-zero log rows. For a target with zero added coordinates the available retained columns must be formed on $\ker_{\mathbb Z}T$. If $Z$ is an integer spanning matrix of that kernel, the relevant retained matrix is $S_0Z$ and the logarithm matrix is $U_0Z$. Thus projecting away $T$ in the columns without restricting the coefficient vectors would change the problem. Every partial column used by a vector in $\ker T$ must have at least one repeated added row, since a singleton added row already forces its coefficient to vanish.

There is an explicit graph count when each field has at most one added factor per mark. Make one vertex per added ideal, separately in the two fields, along with one dummy per field for the missing factor. Every mark is a bipartite edge between its two labels, allowing parallel edges. Negate the second-field rows to make oriented incidence. The omitted dummy rows are determined by the other rows and the field-sum row. In particular among vectors with field sum zero, $Tz=0$ is equivalent to full incidence cancellation. On the support including its dummy vertices, let $e,v,c_0$ denote edge count, vertex count and the number of connected components. The kernel has rank exactly $e-v+c_0$. A spanning forest gives one integer fundamental cycle per nontree edge, with alternating signs, and all vectors in the kernel are integer sums of those cycles. This also gives the deterministic lower bound $e-v$ on the cycle rank. The rank relevant to factoring remains that of the projected original rows and logarithms. A large cycle count alone gives no lower bound for a particular target direction.

## A uniform scalar upper bound with prescribed extra factors

Use the one- and two-mark Gaussian projections of the variable-multiple lattice, exposing the exact first value for a two-mark test. On the innovation cutoff, the scalar center $Y$ has $\log Y=(S+o(1))V$ and the original scalar progression step $q_0$ and relative reciprocal width have logarithms $O(H)$. After a protected division these bounds still hold. To require a specified extra product $P$, divide the potential scalar by $P$. If the class is compatible, its new step is $q_0/g$ with $g=\gcd(P,q_0)$ and its normalized Gaussian mass has prefactor $g/P$. This follows directly by substituting $n=Pm$ in the original progression sum: the width becomes $W/P$ and the original $q_0/W$ prefactor becomes $(g/P)(q_0/g)/(W/P)$. Any further forced gcd of the divided scalar must be a squarefree ordinary-band factor or the desired event is empty. Such a factor is at most $q_0$, and dividing it out leaves the same prefactor calculation with no additional gain beyond the change of density. The remaining class is a unit class.

Uniformly, $\log P\le k(N)\log(2A^2)=o(V)$. The reduced Gaussian center still has $\log(Y/P)=(S+o(1))V$, its relative reciprocal width is unchanged, and the residual step remains $\exp(O(H))$. The Dirichlet RH smooth-window estimate and the narrow-band saddle therefore bound the scalar probability by
\[
                  (g/P)\exp((-\rho/2+o(1))H).                 \tag{1}
\]
Here the progression coprimality normalization is $\exp(o(H))$ uniformly, and the shifted-contour errors are exponentially small on the $V$ scale. This argument only imposes divisibility on the already projected scalar law. It does not condition the high-dimensional Gaussian on a growing product of extra root moduli. Restrict the Gaussian innovations once for all factors under consideration. Their omitted tails are $e^{-\Omega(L^6)}$, negligible when summed over marked tuples of fixed length. [Gaussian projection and cutoff](cogentic-narrow-band-moments.md#any-fixed-number-of-marks) · [Progression estimate](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md#a-gaussian-distribution-in-progressions)

Each free extra prime costs its reciprocal in (1). Summing the free squarefree products costs at most
\[
             \prod_{B<p\le2A^2}(1+1/p)=O_{b,c}(1),
\]
by the reciprocal prime sum. At the first mark an extra divisor of the original step can only be a denominator prime above $B$, which never divides the norm. At the second mark the additional possible nonunit primes divide $\Delta$. There are $O_{b,c}(1)$ primes above $B$ in that step, so their choices, with no reciprocal cost, add at most a bounded factor. A common added row above a prescribed $p$ in one field therefore costs $1/p$ at the first mark and can be free at the second. Independence of the two fields and the two successive upper bounds show that two accepted partial marks sharing this row have probability at most $p^{-1}\exp((-2\rho+o(1))H)$, including arbitrary other allowed factors. Likewise the total one-mark acceptance probability is at most $\exp((-\rho+o(1))H)$. The ordinary full smooth pool is included, so this admission rule cannot improve its leading one-mark density exponent.

## Collision counts and fixed graph cycles

Let $C$ count unordered pairs of distinct accepted marks sharing an added ideal row, with multiplicity over shared rows. Summing (1) and the projective pair count gives
\[
 \mathbb E C\ll A^4\exp((-2\rho+o(1))H)\sum_{p>B}p^{-2}
          \le \exp((2\eta-b+o(1))H).
\]
For a realized added row of degree $d_r\ge2$ there are $\binom{d_r}{2}$ such pairs, and $d_r\le2\binom{d_r}{2}$. Hence at most $2C$ partial columns can appear in an exact cancellation. This bounds the rank gain of the partial pool over the full smooth pool, including graph cycles of unbounded length and extra-row systems with more than two incidences per column. If $\eta<b$ with a fixed margin, Markov's inequality gives both the total admitted count and the useful partial count below $\exp((b-\epsilon)H)$ with probability $1-o(1)$ for some $\epsilon>0$. This is below the $\exp((b+o(1))H)$ row scale used by the current full-grid rank proof. For $\eta<b/2$ even a single repeated extra row is absent with probability $1-o(1)$.

In the graph with exactly one added factor in each field at every edge, let $C_j$ count simple length-$j$ cycles for fixed even $j$, counting two parallel edges as a length-two cycle. Then
\[
 \mathbb E C_j\le
 \exp((j(\eta-b)+o(1))H)
 +\exp((j\eta-(j-2)b-2c+o(1))H).                       \tag{2}
\]
For $j=2$ the first term suffices. To prove this, enumerate the rational prime at each of the $j$ vertices and expose the marked edges in cyclic order. The first occurrence of each labeled vertex costs $1/p$ as in (1). In the same field, a matching projective slope at an earlier edge would force that earlier value to be divisible by $p$. If the earlier label was different this is impossible, since each field at each edge has just one extra prime and the ordinary primes lie below $p$. Thus at the first occurrence the extra prime does not divide the conditional progression step in that field. Further occurrences require the corresponding projective match and need no second reciprocal charge. This uses the full-grid fixed-$j$ Gaussian projection, with its permitted degree slack and widths. Any fixed $j$ is available by choosing that slack before the field draw.

The first edge has at most $A^2$ choices. The following $j-2$ edges each have $O(A^2/p)$ choices for the required match to the previous edge. Each of these vertices supplies a sum of $p^{-2}$, costing $\exp((-b+o(1))H)$. If the last edge has two distinct endpoint primes $p,p'$, it has $O(A^2/(pp')+1)$ choices. The first term again produces two sums of reciprocal squares. The additive term uses two reciprocal-prime sums over $(B,2A^2]$, both bounded. If the last primes coincide across the two fields, there is only one projective condition, but both first occurrences still contribute $1/p$. Summing $p^{-3}$ again costs $\exp((-2b+o(1))H)$. This proves (2), since the $j$ successive conditional smoothness bounds contribute $\exp((-j\rho+o(1))H)$. For a parallel pair with distinct endpoint primes their product must divide the same nonzero determinant, so the $+1$ term is absorbed by $A^2/(pp')$. When $\eta<b$ both exponents in (2) are strictly negative because $c>b$. Fixed-length cycles using dummy vertices are outside (2), and are already covered by the collision bound on partial columns.

An additional upper bound applies when arbitrarily many extras are allowed but all are at most $P=\exp((b'+o(1))H)$ for a fixed $b'\in[b,2c]$. Enlarge the smooth band from its original lower endpoint to $P$, which contains every admitted ordinary or extra factor other than the protected target. The enlarged smooth density exponent is $\rho'=2S/(3b')$. A shared prime $p>B$ can still be divided out at each mark in (1). It costs $1/p$ at the first mark and is forced at the second. No enumeration over other extras is needed in this upper bound. Hence the admitted count has expectation at most $\exp((2c-\rho'+o(1))H)$ and the collision count at most $\exp((4c-2\rho'-b+o(1))H)$. In particular extras with $b'=b$ cannot improve the leading supply floor even if there are many of them. For wider cutoffs the first-moment obstruction to a full factor-base-size pool persists whenever $2c-2S/(3b')<b$.

The bounds leave open whether a partial pool with $\Omega(D)$ extra factors of size $\exp(\Theta(H))$ per field and a wider cutoff can improve supply. Such a pool can remove a positive fraction of the logarithmic norm before the ordinary smoothness test. Its exact cancellation concerns many-row columns beyond the two-endpoint graph. An improvement needs a separate nonvanishing and projected-rank proof. The probability bounds also leave open extracting a particular target from a lower-rank pool.

Exact finite checks verified nullity, the dummy-row equivalence and the bound on usable partial columns for 329 bipartite multigraphs with up to seven edges. The projective spacing inequality was checked on all primitive fractions of height at most 29 for seven moduli. These checks exercise the combinatorial identities, while the asymptotic probability bounds use the stated Gaussian and smooth-window inputs.
