# Farey marks for the two-field relation method

A polynomial pair can be evaluated on primitive integer pairs $(a,b)$ with prime denominators near the same height as the numerators. A full degree-$d-2$ perturbation has small integral kernels on one or two such marks. This gives a joint smooth-relation estimate on a genuinely two-dimensional set of marks. In the subsequent randomized extraction ledger it gives the coefficient $2(32/9)^{2/3}=4.659\ldots$ at exponent $1/3$. All logarithms below are natural. This is a modification of the [two-thirds moment proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) and the [randomized extraction proof](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md). The homogeneous ideal valuations already appear in Cogentic Lemma 7.3. [Pinned paper, §§7–10](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=31)

## The family

Write $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$, $V=DH$. To distinguish a height parameter from a marked numerator, denote its logarithmic coefficient by $c>0$. Choose fixed $x,b_0,c,s>0$, with
$$
 x c>1/x,\qquad c>4b_0,\qquad
 2c>2b_0+\frac{2(xc+1/x)}{3b_0},\qquad s>2c+2b_0.             \tag{1}
$$
Set $d\sim xD$, $n=d-2$, $m=\lceil N^{1/d}\rceil$, $\log B\sim b_0H$, $\log A\sim cH$, $\log W\sim sH$, $\log K_1\sim H$ and $\log(1/w)\sim D\sqrt z$. Use the paper's $P_0$, target polynomials $P_i^*(t)$, intervals around their roots and baseline at $3/4$. Invoke the *base-polynomial* Lemma 8.1 with its real-root scale parameter set to $1$. Thus the target roots themselves lie in $(0,1/2]$ and the coefficient rounding errors are less than $P_0\ell m$. Take $c_d$ from the same admissible $P_{\rm odd}3^k$ sequence, as the first value above $100dK_1 L_0^2P_0 BmW$. Its logarithm is $(1/x+o(1))V$. For each field independently use
$$
 f_{i,u}(T)=f_i^{(0)}(T)+P_0\ell(T-m)G_u(T,1),\qquad
 G_u(X,Y)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}u_j X^jY^{n-j}.
$$
The $u_j$ are independent centered integer Gaussians of width $W$. On $|u_j|\le L_0^2 W$ the normalized sup error from $P_i^*$ on $[-1,1]$ is at most $K_1^{-1}$. All $2(n+1)$ coordinates meet this bound except with probability $O(d e^{-c' L^4})$. The ring and irreducibility preconditions in Lemma 8.1 hold for every perturbation. Sufficiently large $N$ has $4P_0 B\le c_d$, $4d\le m$, $2c_d<2^{L_0}$ and $d\ge10$.

Choose denominators $b$ prime in $[A(1-e^{-30z^2}),A]$, omitting primes dividing $k_0 k'_0$ if any, where $k_0=f_1^{(0)}(m)/N$, $k'_0=f_2^{(0)}(m)/N$. There are $\exp((c+o(1))H)$ such primes by RH, since the short interval has length $A^{1-o(1)}$ and only $L^{O(1)}$ primes are omitted. Every $b$ exceeds $B$. Take the integers $a$ with $a/b$ in one of the source's $2d+1$ grid intervals, set $v=(a,b)$, and retain $\gcd(a-mb,k_0k'_0)=1$. The initial gcd checks on $N$ apply now to $a-mb$. Since $0<a<b$ and $b$ is prime, all these marks are primitive. Two different retained pairs have different slopes. Each interval has $\exp((2c+o(1))H)$ marks. Give each interval equal mass and its surviving marks uniform weights as in §10.

Writing $F_{i,u}(a,b)=b^df_{i,u}(a/b)$, the perturbation identity is
$$
 F_{i,u}(a,b)=F_i^{(0)}(a,b)+P_0\ell\, b(a-mb)G_u(a,b).         \tag{2}
$$
In particular there is just one common denominator factor $b$ in the step modulus. The original two low-degree coefficient directions instead introduce a factor $b^{d-2}$ before this single $b$. Define the nominal center $\mu_i(v)=c_d b^d |P_i^*(a/b)|$ and the fixed reference scale $X_{\rm sc}=c_d A^d$. Put $S=xc+1/x$. The factorial spacing bound for $P_i^*$ from the preceding parameter proof gives $|\log(\mu_i(v)/X_{\rm sc})|=o(H)$, including $d|\log(b/A)|=O(de^{-30z^2})$. Hence $\log X_{\rm sc}=(S+o(1))V$, $u\sim(S/b_0)D$, and the individual saddle density is $\exp((-S/(3b_0)+o(1))H)$.

## Conditional classes and smoothness

A root prescription at $r\bmod p$ with $r\ne m$ imposes one linear congruence on the coefficients of $G_u$ at $(r,1)$, because $P_0\ell(r-m)$ is invertible. Use the [integral projection and Poisson lemma](cogentic-projective-kernel.md) at $h=1,2$. The products $Q$ of prescribed row primes are at most $B^2$ per field, and $W/(Q(2A)^2)\gg L^3$ by (1). Each admissible output distribution therefore agrees pointwise with the corresponding continuous Gaussian density times its exact lattice covolume, up to relative $O(e^{-c' L^6})$. Local multiple-root conditions have probability $p^{-2}(1+O(e^{-c' L^6}))$ because value and derivative of a degree-$n\ge1$ form at an affine point are independent mod $p$; constraints at distinct primes combine by CRT. Shared roots across the two fields likewise cost $p^{-2}$. The root mass statistics and probabilities of prescribed rows used in Proposition 10.1 follow identically.

For the one-point distribution conditioned on root rows, let $Q_{\rm hit}(v)$ designate the prescribed primes where the slope hits their row. Let $Q_{\rm pre}(v)=\ell^{\epsilon_i(v)}Q_{\rm hit}(v)$ include the fixed target root in field 1, as in the source. The step of $G_u(v)$ is $Q_{\rm hit}(v)$. The absolute polynomial value, after dividing out $Q_{\rm pre}$, therefore occupies one class modulo
$$
                P_0\ell^{1-\epsilon_i(v)} b|a-mb|.
$$
It is a coprime class: $\gcd(F_i(a,b),a-mb)=\gcd(k_iN,a-mb)=1$ by (2) and $F_i^{(0)}(a,b)\equiv b^d k_iN\pmod{a-mb}$; also $F_i(a,b)\equiv c_d a^d\not\equiv0\pmod b$, since $b>B>d$ and all prime factors of $c_d$ are small. The $P_0$ and $\ell$ congruences are those of the source. The scale of the Gaussian in $G_u(v)$ is comparable to $W b^{d-2}$, uniformly in $a/b\in[0,0.8]$. Thus the ratio of the absolute polynomial center to the absolute polynomial width is $K_1^{1+o(1)}$ uniformly, since $\log|P_i^*|=o(H)$, $b\sim A$, and constant and polynomial factors cost $e^{o(H)}$.

For distinct marks $v,v'$ put $\Delta=|ab'-a'b|$. After specifying $G_u(v)$, the step of $G_u(v')$ is exactly $\Delta$ times the product of prescribed primes hitting $v'$ alone. This follows from the projective lattice lemma, including its statement at primes hitting neither mark. The absolute second modulus after the preassigned factors have been removed is consequently
$$
 q_2=P_0\ell^{1-\epsilon_i(v')} b'|a'-mb'|
                    \frac{\Delta}{Q_{\rm both}},\qquad
 Q_{\rm both}=\prod_{p\text{ prescribed at both }v,v'}p,
$$
where the special target $\ell$ is treated in $\epsilon_i$ and is excluded from $Q_{\rm both}$. A sign reversal of the polynomial value only changes the residue. By the last covariance bound of the lattice lemma, the conditional Gaussian width in the second evaluation of $G$ is comparable to $W (b')^{d-2}|a/b-a'/b'|$. Its absolute polynomial center-to-width ratio is accordingly between $K_1^{1-o(1)}$ and $K_1^{1+o(1)} A^2$, since $|a/b-a'/b'|\ge 1/(bb')$. On the central $L_0^3$-width cutoff at the first mark, the second conditional center has relative perturbation $K_1^{-1+o(1)}$: the regression shift is bounded by $L_0^3$ times the second *unconditional* standard-deviation parameter. The direct one-point center error from rounding is at most that relative bound too. In particular the smooth-window theorem applies for every distinct pair, including close slopes. Its errors are $O(e^{-50z^2})$ relatively because $\log q_2\le(1/x+o(1))V$, $\log Y=(S+o(1))V$, and $S/2>1/x$. Additional auxiliary prime congruences and deletions cost only $e^{O(H)}$ factors at scale $V$.

For the remaining noncoprime residue put $J_*=\{p:\sqrt B<p\le B,\ p\nmid k_0k'_0\}$ and $G=\gcd(r_2,q_2)$. When the event is possible, $G$ is a squarefree product of eligible primes and divides $\ell\Delta$, hence $G\le B A^2$. Indeed the factors of $b'P_0|a'-mb'|$ cannot divide the polynomial value, by the coprimality check above. Dividing the center, modulus and width by $G$ leaves $Y/S_{\rm width}$ unchanged and increases the saddle density by at most $L^{O(1)}$ as in the optimized parameter proof. This gives the polynomial normalized rough bound for every pair.

For $p^2\nmid\Delta$ at the prescribed row primes and $\ell$, the clean formula has the same correction as in Lemma 9.5, now
$$
 \gamma(v,v')=\prod_{\substack{d<p\le\sqrt B,\ p\mid\Delta\\p\nmid(a-mb)}} p/(p-1).
$$
At $p\mid\Delta$, (projective) homogeneity gives $F(v')\equiv e^d F(v)\pmod p$ for a nonzero scale $e$, even if the common slope is at infinity. For $p\le B$ the denominator primes are units, so $e=b'/b\pmod p$. A prime in the displayed small band cannot divide a smooth first value; its new exclusion from the second class gives exactly $p/(p-1)$. Primes already in the one-point modulus yield no new factor. For a prescribed prime hit at both marks, division by $Q_{\rm both}$ removes the difference factor under the nonsquare-divisibility condition. If $\ell$ is hit at both marks, the possible extra zero of $F(v')/\ell$ modulo $\ell$ excludes one class at the first mark: the scalar multiplying $F(v)/\ell$ there is $e^d$, a unit, and the derivative term is fixed modulo $\ell$ by the preconditioning. Its relative cost is $O(\ell^{-1}L^{O(1)})$. An eligible unprescribed prime $p\mid\Delta$ can divide the first value with relative probability $O(p^{-\alpha_s}L^{O(1)})$ by the one-point formula on dividing by $p$. Removing this event before applying the clean conditional formula and using the rough bound costs $O(B^{-0.49})$. There are $O(\log A/\log B)=O(1)$ primes above $\sqrt B$ dividing $\Delta$. The remaining larger factors outside $J_*$ alter the coprime normalizers by $1+O(B^{-0.49})$. These exhaust the new factors of $q_2$. One-point weights $Z_i^{(0)}(v)$ now use $q_0=P_0\ell b |a-mb|$ and the actual variance of (2); the exact ratios $\Phi_i$ for deleting $Q_{\rm pre}$ use $\mu_i(v)$ as before. The clean one- and two-point formulas thus retain their common relative $O(e^{-50z^2})$ error.

## Rank, the field map, and time

At fixed denominator prime $b$ the required congruences on slopes are $a\equiv rb\pmod h$, and the coprime sieve is $\gcd(a-mb,k_0k'_0)=1$. All moduli $h$ for its applications are at most $B^4$. The surviving numerator interval has length $\asymp Aw$ and $c>4b_0$, so the [Brun proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) applies with its $O(e^{-4z^2})$ Lipschitz error uniformly in $b$. The density factor for primes already imposed in $h$ is unchanged since $b$ is a unit and the reduced slope differs from $m$. Each denominator's contribution to the global normalized sum can be weighted by its actual number of survivors and then summed. The derivatives of the smooth target weights on a numerator increment are bounded by $L^{O(1)}/(Aw)$, as $b\sim A$ and minimum root distance is $w$.

The normalized single-mark mass is now at most $\exp((-2c+o(1))H)$; the diagonal error in the conditional Gram square is at most $\exp((-2c+2b_0+2S/(3b_0)+o(1))H)$ times the squared entry norm. This tends to zero exponentially in $H$ by (1). For fixed $v,b'$, each divisibility $h\mid\Delta$ in the small-prime correction fixes one class of $a'$ modulo $h$; $b$ is a unit for all $h$ used in the shortest-prefix or centered estimates. The exceptional case $p^2\mid\Delta$ for a prescribed prime has conditional cell mass cost $O(1/p+B^3/(Aw))$ as before. The shortest prefix $h_0\in(\sqrt B,B]$ just enlarges the modulus to at most $B^4$. The remaining centered-sum cancellation, Gram concentration, multiple/shared-row peeling and factor-base covariance are consequently exactly the estimates of Proposition 10.1 with $E_{\rm rel}=e^{-7z^2}$. For the real-root matrix the off-diagonal row and column sums are $O(d)$ by factorial grid spacing and the diagonal magnitudes are $\log(1/w)\gg d$.

For every clean value,
$$
 \log|F_i(a,b)|=\log X_{\rm sc}+\sum_j\log|a/b-\theta_{ij}|
                              +d\log(b/A).
$$
The last term is $O(de^{-30z^2})$ uniformly. In the two norm-coordinate eliminations this leaves a scalar error at each mark of size $O(de^{-30z^2}(|t_1|+|t_2|))$, where the $t_i$ are the source's norm-direction coefficients. The squared Gram error is $L^{O(1)}e^{-30z^2}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, the bounded total row mass, and the same polynomially bounded coordinate change. It is negligible against the polynomial positive lower bound. This gives the required full rank with probability $1-o(1)$, uniformly in each target $\ell$.

In the extractor replace the linear elements $a-\theta$ by $a-b\theta$. Lemma 7.3 applies to primitive pairs; the only eligible prime ideal at $p$ is the simple degree-one ideal of slope $a/b\bmod p$. Pole offsets are canceled by the existing all-ones row. The archimedean unit coordinates are the trace-zero projections of $\log|a-b\theta_j|$, equal to the projections of $\log|a/b-\theta_j|$ because the added $\log b$ is common to all embeddings. Localization sends these elements to $a-bm\pmod N$ in both fields, known units by the initial gcd sweep. Thus the same rational ideal and real-unit cancellation gives a verified $E_\ell$. Exact root brackets now concern $\theta$ on the unit target grid, and the logarithm inputs $|a/b-\widehat\theta|$ have the same separation and precision bounds as before. The row column sparsity remains $O(u)<L_0$. [Cogentic, Lemmas 7.3–7.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=31)

Sample each of the $2(n+1)$ Gaussian coordinates by rejection from its centered interval of radius $L_0^2 W$, accepting with weight $e^{-\pi k^2/W^2}$ approximated to absolute error $e^{-2L^2}$. Each coordinate has expected $O(L_0^2)$ proposals. Polynomial-bit Taylor arithmetic suffices since $|k/W|\le L_0^2$. The resulting joint total variation error is $e^{-\Omega(L^2)}$, smaller than the individual smooth probability and the reciprocal number of marks. This uses the Gaussian itself because domination by an arbitrary coordinatewise constant would cost a factor exponential in $d$. Random prime selection and a single verified exponent have the same positive success probability as in the [randomized extraction proof](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md).

Sieve all numerator intervals at all denominators using the known roots at each eligible prime. The stream lengths are $O((dA^2+dAB+B^2)\mathrm{poly}(L))$. The expected retained relation count is at most $\exp((2c-2S/(3b_0)+o(1))H)$. The exact lifting proof therefore gives expected logarithmic cost coefficient at most
$$
         \max\{2c,4b_0,2c+2b_0-2S/(3b_0)\}.
$$
The condition $xc>1/x$ makes $S$ approach $2\sqrt c$ from above as $x\downarrow1/\sqrt c$. Choose strict parameters tending to $c_0=(32/9)^{2/3}$, $b_0=c_0/4$ and $x=1/\sqrt{c_0}$. Then $c_0=b_0+2\sqrt{c_0}/(3b_0)$, and the three cost terms are bounded by $2c_0$. Interleaving fixed rational schedules as above proves the stated per-input expected bound including full recursion. The straightforward deterministic enumeration over the $2(d-1)$ small coordinates costs $\exp((2xs+o(1))V)$; it retains exponent $2/3$. No reduced worst-case exponent follows here from the additional projective marks.
