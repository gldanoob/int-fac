# Batching the modular elliptic chart search

For the fixed [elliptic four-mark chart](cogentic-elliptic-mark-cycle.md), two lists of $L$ and $B$ reduced points can be searched for a local sextic zero or a productive global square in time quasi-linear in $L+B$, with polylogarithmic dependence on the unknown modulus. The algorithm either gives a proper divisor or completes the search on the specified lists. It can report a global sextic zero whose square comparison gives only trivial gcds. The bound holds when the polynomial product at a left point vanishes modulo the whole modulus and allows repeated prime factors.

## A polynomial for one addition

Let $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, and write
\[
 f(X)=(X+204)(X+264)(X+1496),\qquad E:Y^2=f(X).
\]
The coefficient of $X^2$ is $c=1964$. Check the fixed primes $2,3,5,7,11,17,19$ by gcd first. If $N$ is one of them, primality is immediate. Otherwise this either splits $N$ or ensures good odd reduction and makes $8976$ and $4845$ units. The chart is $m=51-8976/x$, and its sextic and two quadratic factors are
\[
 A(m)=(m-51)(m-95),\quad B(m)=(m-57)(m-85),\quad
 F(m)=m+A(m)B(m)(A(m)-1).
\]
Here $B(m)$ denotes the quadratic form, and the standalone $B$ denotes the right-list length. Let $F_h(z,w)=w^6 F(z/w)$ be the polynomial homogenization.

For an affine right point $Q=(u,v)$, in the algebra $R[X,Y]/(Y^2-f(X))$ set
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_Q&=X-u,\\
 G_Q&=f(X)+f(u)-D_Q^2(c+X+u)-2vY,\\
 H_Q&=F_h(51G_Q-8976D_Q^2,G_Q),\\
 J_Q&=D_QG_QH_Q.
 \end{aligned}
\]
If $D_Q(x,y)$ is a unit, the ordinary addition formula gives $x((x,y)+Q)=G_Q(x,y)/D_Q(x,y)^2$. If $G_Q(x,y)$ is also a unit, $m=51-8976D_Q(x,y)^2/G_Q(x,y)$ and $H_Q(x,y)=G_Q(x,y)^6F(m)$. Including $D_Q$ and $G_Q$ makes the filter retain nonunit addition and chart denominators. All three values can be checked separately at a leaf. When $D_Q$ is a unit and $G_Q=0$ modulo a prime, $H_Q=(-8976D_Q^2)^6$ there by monicity.

Replacing $Y$ by $-Y$ changes the sum to the opposite right sign at the level of $x$, since $x(-P+Q)=x(P-Q)$. The two orientations can be filtered separately by evaluating the product at $y$ and $-y$. A scalar alternative for the filter is the norm $J_Q(X,Y)J_Q(X,-Y)$ in $R[X]$. It captures the union of the two signs at each prime. The two signed values still have to be checked individually at the leaves, since different signs may be responsible at different primes. The $Y$-free part of $G_Q$ has degree at most two, by cancellation of the cubic terms. Giving $X,Y$ weights two and three bounds the weighted degree of $J_Q$ by $2+4+24=30$. A product of $s$ such terms has representation $U(X)+YV(X)$ of degree $O(s)$ in $X$.

## Distinct right abscissas and exact leaves

Check pairs with an infinity input directly by looking at their other point, at linear cost after retaining one representative of infinity per list. For affine right points group equal $u$ modulo $N$. For any two at a common $u$, $(v-v')(v+v')=0$ modulo $N$. If neither sign agrees globally, $\gcd(v-v',N)$ is proper. In the remaining case keep one representative per $u$ and record which signs occurred. A search allowing both signs uses both regardless of the original representative. A search restricted to specified signed words uses the recorded signs at the leaves.

For the remaining distinct residues $u_j$, form the monic polynomial $W(X)=\prod_j(X-u_j)$. Evaluate $W'$ at all $u_j$ by a multipoint tree. The value at $u_j$ is $\prod_{k\ne j}(u_j-u_k)$. Its gcd with $N$ either proves every difference at that $j$ is a unit or gives a divisor. When the gcd is $N$, scan once from that $j$. At least one difference is a nonunit and every difference in the scan is nonzero modulo $N$, so a proper divisor is guaranteed. If this step gives no split, the $u_j$ are pairwise distinct modulo every prime dividing $N$.

At a leaf, check $\gcd(x-u,N)$ first. If this is $N$, the two ordinates have the same square modulo $N$. They either have mixed signs, which exposes a proper gcd as in preprocessing, or are globally equal or globally opposite. For the opposite branch the sum is infinity. For the equal branch use the tangent formula with denominator $2y$; a nonzero nonunit denominator splits $N$ and the globally zero case sums to infinity. When $x-u$ is a unit, use the secant formula. In either finite case check the exact chart. A nonzero nonunit sum abscissa splits $N$, a zero sum abscissa gives no chart, and a unit sum abscissa gives $m$ and $F(m)$. A proper $\gcd(F(m),N)$ is a divisor, including when it only extracts part of a prime power. Infinity gives no chart. A rational schedule such as $P+iR_1$ and $jR_2$ can be generated by the same checked addition on the public base points, with a divisor returned if any step exposes one.

If $F(m)=0$ modulo $N$, compute $S=1-2A(m)$, $W_q=-8976y/x^2$ at the resulting elliptic point, and $C=B(m)S$. The chart equation gives $W_q^2=A(m)B(m)$, and $C^2-W_q^2=4B(m)F(m)=0$ modulo $N$. At any prime dividing $N$, a zero of $A(m)B(m)$ would force $m=0$ by the sextic, but $A(0)=B(0)=4845$ is a unit. Thus $C,W_q$ are units. At each odd prime power exactly one of $C-W_q,C+W_q$ vanishes to the whole modulus of that prime power. Checking both gcds distinguishes a productive global square from a globally uniform sign. In the uniform case the search can record the chart and continue.

## Why a globally zero product causes no quadratic scan

Build a balanced product tree of $J_Q$ in the quadratic function algebra, or of their scalar norms. At the root, evaluate the product at all left points. In the two-coefficient representation this uses two univariate multipoint evaluations and the given ordinates. Send a left query to both children only when its current gcd is greater than one, including when it is $N$. In each subsequent wave, evaluate a node polynomial only on the left queries assigned to that node. Every nonunit leaf value is visited, since its prime divisor also divides the value at every ancestor. Exact leaf checks prevent a global zero from being mistaken for a split or from terminating the remaining search.

To bound the queries, fix a left point $P_0$ and a prime $p\mid N$. The affine abscissas in the right list are distinct over $\mathbb F_p$. The case $x(P_0)=u_j$ contributes at most one leaf. In all other cases the addition formula is valid. If $G_Q$ vanishes, the sum has $x=0$, giving at most two possible sum points over $\mathbb F_p$. If $H_Q$ vanishes and $G_Q$ is nonzero, $F$ has at most six roots, each chart abscissa gives at most two elliptic points, and therefore there are at most 12 possible sums. Each candidate sum $T$ yields at most one right abscissa even when both signs are tested. The two possible right points from the two sign tests are $T-P_0$ and $P_0-T$, with the same abscissa. Thus at most 15 right leaves can have a nonunit value attributable to $p$, for both signs together.

Writing $\omega(N)$ for the number of distinct prime factors, each left query consequently has at most $15\omega(N)$ nonunit leaves. Unit values can occur at the tested siblings of their ancestors, but there are only $O(L(1+\omega(N)))$ point assignments at any depth. A node containing $s$ leaves contributes polynomial degree $O(s)$, so the sum of degrees over nodes in one wave is $O(B)$. This proves the bound without a limit on how many row products can vanish globally. It also applies if some proper gcds are postponed until leaves, which makes every returned chart check explicit.

Monic remainder trees work over $R$ regardless of zero divisors. For example, fast division reverses a monic divisor and inverts a power series with constant term one by Newton iteration. Multiplication can lift coefficients to nonnegative integers, pack them with enough guard bits for the exact convolution, then reduce modulo $N$. A product or a multipoint evaluation with total input and output coefficient count $d$ costs soft-linear time in $d(\log N+\log(d+1))$ bits. Applying this at each wave gives an overall deterministic search bound
\[
 \widetilde O\bigl((B+L(1+\omega(N)))
            (\log N+\log(B+L+1))\bigr)
\]
bit operations, including preprocessing, gcds and exact leaf checks. The bound gives quasi-linear dependence on the list lengths times polylogarithmic factors in $N$ since $\omega(N)\leq\log_2 N$. If the caller asks for all original index pairs rather than representative point pairs, expanding repeated points incurs the cost of writing that output.

## Scope

For a radius $T$ box on the two fixed directions, use $P+iR_1$ and $jR_2$. The modular search costs $\widetilde O(T\operatorname{polylog}N)$ with $O(T)$ checked group additions to form the lists, compared with $\Theta(T^2)$ pairs in direct enumeration. The proof also applies to two lists built from more general small words, with the cost of producing those lists added. It gives a finite exhaustive search on an unknown input without using that input's factorization. A completed search certifies the absence of a splitting event in the specified lists and can return any exact global charts it found. It gives no bound on the list lengths needed to guarantee a splitting event for arbitrary inputs. The [elliptic family](cogentic-elliptic-cycle-density.md) supplies productive global charts inside its height bounds.

As an arithmetic check, direct enumeration of every ordered pair of affine points, testing both signs, agreed with the augmented polynomial filter at $p=23,29,31,97,101,103$. The maximum numbers of selected right abscissas for a single left point were $5,3,11,5,7,5$ respectively.
