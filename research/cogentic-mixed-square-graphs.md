# Mixed square relations across fields

A graph of fields can yield a proper divisor when every two-field square kernel on its column pool is zero. The relevant relation is a zero sum of square-class vectors drawn from the separate field kernels. At $N=209$, four quadratic fields with one common evaluation realize this situation and split $N$ as $19\cdot11$. One field is the raw scheduled quadratic, and the same certificate works with the raw cubic as a common hub.

## Incidence and modular signs

Let $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ for odd $N$, and suppose each vertex $i$ of a connected graph has a field $K_i$ and a specified reduction $\rho_i$ on a subring. Column $j$ has an element $u_{ij}$ at each vertex with a common unit residue $s_j\in R^\times$. For marks, take $u_{ij}=a_j-b_j\alpha_i$ and $s_j=a_j-b_jm$. Work with reductions that admit inverses of the column elements and the square roots being tested.

Orient the edges. Put an integer column exponent vector $n_e$ on edge $e$. At vertex $i$ take the outgoing exponent sum minus the incoming sum, denoted by $b_i$. Then $\sum_i b_i=0$. If the vertex products
\[
P_i=\prod_j u_{ij}^{b_{ij}}
\]
have roots $r_i$ in the fields with valid reductions, their product residue
\[
z=\prod_i\rho_i(r_i)
\]
has square one, since $\prod_i\rho_i(P_i)=\prod_j s_j^{\sum_i b_{ij}}=1$. A nontrivial $z$ gives the proper divisor $\gcd(z-1,N)$. Opposite signs at different odd prime powers dividing $N$ characterize success.

Write $V=\mathbb F_2^k$ for $k$ columns and $S_i\subseteq V$ for the exact square kernel with roots that admit reduction in field $i$. The possible vertex tuples form
\[
\mathcal K=\ker\!\left(\bigoplus_i S_i\xrightarrow{\ \sum\ } V\right).
\]
Indeed the incidence map over $\mathbb F_2$ of a connected graph reaches every tuple of column vectors of total sum zero. Route a vector at each leaf toward a root of a spanning tree, clearing the leaf before proceeding. Thus graph cycles themselves are optional. A tree can carry the same vertex relation.

The class of $z$ in $\mu_2(R)/\{1,-1\}$ is well defined on $\mathcal K$. Changing an integer lift of a vertex tuple changes each $b_i$ by $2h_i$ with $\sum_i h_i=0$. The roots can then be multiplied by $\prod_j u_{ij}^{h_{ij}}$, whose total residue is one. Changing any chosen root negates $z$. Adding integer exponent lifts multiplies their root residues, so this class is a homomorphism. A basis with supplied roots therefore suffices for testing, as in the [two-field basis criterion](cogentic-square-kernel-splitting.md).

Two-field relations generate the subspace of $\mathcal K$ spanned by tuples with an identical vector in two coordinates and zero elsewhere. Each such vector belongs to $S_i\cap S_j$. If all two-field signs are trivial, the sign test descends to the quotient by this subspace. A nonzero class there can arise even when the $S_i$ have pairwise zero intersection. For instance, vectors $v,w,v+w$ on distinct lines sum to zero across three coordinates. Whether such a relation gives a proper divisor still depends on the reductions.

For rational columns, there is an equivalent compatibility test on radicals. Suppose fields provide reduced roots $A_i$ of rational units $D_i$ and take a basis subset of the $D_i$ modulo rational squares. Write each remaining $D_i$ as $s_i^2\prod_{j\in I_i}D_j$, with $I_i$ in that basis. A reduction from their common multiquadratic field would require
\[
A_i=\pm s_i\prod_{j\in I_i}A_j\pmod N
\]
with one sign for each dependent radical. These conditions are also sufficient after choosing the embeddings of the individual radicals up to sign. Over the rationals the independent radical monomials form a basis of the multiquadratic field, so the ring generated by the basis radicals over rational numbers with denominators coprime to $N$ has only the defining square relations. A reduction on that ring can assign the basis residues freely. The displayed conditions are exactly the additional equations needed for the other radical residues. A ratio with opposite signs at different primes dividing $N$ certifies a proper divisor by gcd, without using those primes as input.

## An Eisenstein cycle at 209

Set $N=209$ and take the scheduled common argument $m=13$. For an even integer $k$ define
\[
P_k(X)=(X-m)^2+2k(X-m)+N.
\]
Each $P_k$ evaluates to $N$ at $m$. Its nonleading coefficients are even and its constant is $2$ modulo four, so it is Eisenstein at two. The field root $\alpha_k$ satisfies $(\alpha_k-m+k)^2=k^2-N$, and this radical reduces to $k$ modulo $N$. For $k=12,14,18,28$ the fields have distinct squarefree radicands:

| $k$ | $P_k(X)$ | $k^2-N=D_k t_k^2$ | $D_k$ | $t_k$ |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12 | $X^2-2X+66$ | $-65$ | $-65$ | 1 |
| 14 | $X^2+2X+14$ | $-13$ | $-13$ | 1 |
| 18 | $X^2+10X-90$ | $115$ | $115$ | 1 |
| 28 | $X^2+30X-350$ | $575$ | $23$ | 5 |

Use $u_0=2,u_1=-130,u_2=10,u_3=46$ as common rational columns, with $u_4=u_0$. The vertex products in cyclic order are
\[
u_1/u_0=-65,\qquad
u_2/u_1=-1/13,\qquad
u_3/u_2=23/5,\qquad
u_4/u_3=1/23.
\]
These are squares of $\sqrt{-65},\sqrt{-13}/13,\sqrt{115}/5,\sqrt{23}/23$ in their respective fields. Their denominators and all column residues are units modulo $209$.

The rational square classes of the four columns are independent. In a square product, the valuations at 13 and 23 first exclude $u_1$ and $u_3$, then the valuations at 5 and 2 exclude $u_2$ and $u_0$. A rational number is square in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{D_k})$ exactly when its rational class is trivial or $D_k$. This follows by squaring $a+b\sqrt{D_k}$ and equating the irrational coefficient to zero. Each vertex square kernel on these four columns is consequently the line generated by its two adjacent columns. These four lines are distinct, so every two-field kernel is zero. The tuple of all four generators has total sum zero.

Evaluating the four roots at $m$ gives
\[
z=12\cdot\frac{14}{13}\cdot\frac{18}{5}\cdot\frac{28}{115}
  \equiv153\pmod{209}.
\]
It is $-1$ modulo 11 and $+1$ modulo 19. The difference gcd is 19. The same certificate can use a star with the raw cubic $X^3-12X^2+2X+14$ as hub and these four quadratics as leaves. Send each displayed rational ratio from its quadratic toward the hub. Their product is one, so the hub has root one. This version connects only fields of consecutive degrees. The hub has no nonzero square relation on the four rational columns, by odd degree and their independence over $\mathbb Q$.

The replay searches even $0\le k\le2\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and four-element subsets containing the raw quadratic. It tests whether $\prod_k(k^2-N)$ is an integer square and whether any two selected radicands represent the same rational square class. It then takes a gcd with the product of the reduced radicals after normalization. This rational case yields the same congruence of squares as a quadratic-sieve dependency on the values $k^2-N$. It illustrates what several field kernels can contribute on a fixed pool without assuming a new norm-sampling bound. [Replay](../experiments/cogentic-mixed-square-graphs.py)

## Which prescribed field libraries remain obstructed

For the two fields of the [raw obstruction](cogentic-private-grid-density.md), incidence forces the same parity vector at both vertices, so mixing recovers only their common kernel. Repeated vertices with the same field, column elements and reduction can be merged by multiplying their roots. More generally, if at most one vertex kernel is nonzero on a primitive mark pool, the sum condition forces every vertex to have zero parity and the sign class is trivial.

The prescribed constant-shift library in the same [private-valuation argument](cogentic-private-grid-density.md#searching-a-prescribed-list-of-fields) can be made to satisfy this stronger condition on the primitive grid. Use its density bound on both sides of every listed pair, wherever the mark norm has nonzero affine coefficient in $m$. Applying the bound to both sides multiplies the number of tested field and mark choices by at most two, so the grid exponent $c$ and library exponent $\sigma$ still need only satisfy $2c+\sigma<1/(6c)$. The prime exclusions, normalization and derivative tests apply unchanged to these additional affine forms, since they have the same coefficient bounds. On the selected input, each such mark has a private odd valuation in its own field.

For $F_s=X^d+2+4s(X-m)$ with $s\ne0$, every primitive mark has nonzero affine coefficient. For $G_t=F_t+(X-m)X^d$, inactive directions are rational roots of $X^d+4t$. There is at most one because $d$ is odd. Any such root is an even integer, so its fixed mark norm $a^d+2$ has valuation one at two. Once the other directions have even exponents, this exceptional direction must also have even exponent. Consequently all $G_t$ and all $F_s$ with $s\ne0$ have zero square kernel on the primitive grid. The only remaining candidate, $F_0$, cannot supply a nonzero vector by itself. This excludes arbitrary mixed ordinary products from that primitive pool for the entire constant-shift library.

For nonconstant listed $P$, the same enlarged union bound restricts each $F_P$ kernel to directions with $P(a/b)=0$ and each $G_P$ kernel to even integer roots of $X^d+4P$. On the latter exceptional set the sum of the support coordinates must be zero, by the valuation at two. A sufficient condition for obstruction is that every direction be exceptional in at most one vertex, since no coordinate could then cancel across vertices. Pairwise obstruction by itself gives no such conclusion.

Nonprimitive marks contribute rational content as well as primitive directions. For the constant-shift library the same private-prime argument still forces even total exponents of every primitive direction at each vertex. Content primes are at most the grid height, and rational norms in the even-degree fields contribute even valuations at two. In the roots, the half-exponents of each primitive direction sum to zero across vertices, so their residues cancel. The remaining sign is exactly the product of reduced square roots of the rational contents from the different vertices. Odd-degree fields force their contents to be rational squares. Every even-degree field $G_P$ on the list has a real root for sufficiently large $d$, since $G_P(m-2)=-(m-2)^d+2-8P(m-2)<0$ and $G_P(m)=N>0$. Thus sign columns alone cannot contribute a nontrivial rational class there. Positive rational nonsquares can contribute through quadratic subfields, as the four-field example illustrates for rational content in general.
