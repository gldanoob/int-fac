# Planting one smooth mark while keeping two free coefficients

Use the parameters and notation of the [diffuse separation proof](cogentic-diffuse-mark-separation.md). Write $s_0$ for an arbitrary mark in the common grid and choose a helper field with the fixed root-free polynomial modulo $\ell$. There is a deterministic $U\exp(O(H))$ construction of a field in which $s_0$ is squarefree smooth, all its prime rows survive, and a prescribed function with sufficiently large value at $s_0$ has positive squared distance from the local row space. The field root and all CRT data are the same as in the unplanted construction.

## The planted value and the perturbed field

First search the integers $|j|\le L_0^2 UA$ for a squarefree eligible value
$$
                Y_0=f^{(0)}(s_0)+P_0\ell(s_0-m)j.
$$
The reduced class is a unit modulo $P_0\ell|s_0-m|$ by the base construction and the grid gcd guards. A Gaussian in $j$ of width $UA$ gives positive probability $\exp((-\sigma+o(1))H)$ by the uniform smooth-window theorem with $v>1/x$. Tails past the stated range cost $e^{-\Omega(L^4)}$, so the search succeeds. Sign has no effect on the squarefree test. Trial division through the factor base at each candidate has the stated total cost. Put $P=\operatorname{supp}(|Y_0|)$, all larger than $\sqrt B$, with $|P|=O(u)$. No $p\in P$ divides $s_0-m$, by $f^{(0)}(m)=kN$ and the established gcd restrictions.

With $j$ fixed vary the two independent integer Gaussian coefficients $s,t$, of widths $U$ and $U/A$, in
$$
 f(X)=f^{(0)}(X)+P_0\ell(X-m)\{j+(X-s_0)(s+tX)\}.              \tag{1}
$$
Choose the constant in the original leading-coefficient threshold large enough to cover the extra factor two in the central-box perturbation. The perturbation degree remains at most $d-1$, its real-target displacement is $O(K_1^{-1})$, and $f(s_0)=Y_0$ for all draws. Real-root bracketing and irreducibility hold as before. For the following estimates omit the single mark $s_0$ from each moment calculation, renormalizing its interval. All interval and residue counting errors retain the same bounds. The anchor is returned as an extra column once its rows survive.

For an ordinary root at $(q,r)$ with $r\ne m,s_0\pmod q$, the condition again fixes $s+rt\bmod q$. At residue $r=s_0\bmod q$ the row is deterministically present exactly when $q\in P$. Call these at most $O(u)$ rows protected. At a protected row the derivative is affine in $s+s_0t$ with nonzero coefficient $P_0\ell(s_0-m)$ modulo $q$, so the probability of nonsimplicity is $O(1/q)$. The other finite-row statistics are as in the ordinary affine family: at distinct primes the prescribed conditions are jointly uniform to Gaussian precision, and the change in mean finite mass or squared norm from omitting the one residue $s_0$ per prime and adding the protected rows is $O((u+1)B^{-0.49})$ relatively up to polynomial logarithmic factors.

For an ordinary prescribed row set $R$ of size at most two at distinct primes, let $Q_R(z)$ denote the product of its primes with $z\equiv r\pmod q$, and let $P(z)=\gcd(|Y_0|,|z-s_0|)$. The primes in these two products are disjoint for each individual $z$. Divide $f(z)$ by $T(z)=Q_R(z)P(z)$ before the smooth test, omitting all primes of $T(z)$ from the remaining squarefree band. For $z\ne s_0$, $P(z)$ has boundedly many prime factors, since their product divides $|z-s_0|\le A$. Put $D(z)=P_0\ell|z-m||z-s_0|$. The exact first modulus is $D(z)/P(z)$. In particular its class is a unit: if $p\in P(z)$ occurs to multiplicity at least two in $z-s_0$, then $f(z)/p\equiv Y_0/p\not\equiv0\pmod p$. If it occurs only once, $p$ has disappeared from the first modulus. Other factors of $z-s_0$ see $Y_0$ as a unit. Factors of $P_0\ell|z-m|$ see the usual coprime class.

At two distinct nonanchor marks $z,z'$ write $\Delta=|z-z'|$, $Q_{\rm both}=\gcd(Q_R(z),Q_R(z'))$, and denote the product of ordinary prescribed primes missed by both marks by $Q_{\rm free}$. Condition on the scalar $s+zt$. The second modulus after the prescribed division is exactly
$$
             \frac{D(z')\Delta Q_{\rm free}}{P(z')Q_{\rm both}}.    \tag{2}
$$
This is the two-dimensional affine congruence calculation on $s+zt,s+z't$, multiplied by $D(z')/T(z')$. The Gaussian prefactor equals the displayed modulus divided by the divided absolute width. The ratio of the unconditional center to absolute width is between $K_1^{1-o(1)}$ and $K_1^{1+o(1)}A$, because $1\le|z-s_0|\le A$. The conditional width loses at most another factor $O(A)$ for $1\le\Delta\le A$. Conditional shifts on the central $L_0^3$ widths at the first mark are at most $L_0^3$ unconditional widths at the second. As a result all width ratios have logarithm $O(H)$, and all moduli including auxiliary $B^{O(1)}$ factors have logarithm at most $(1/x+o(1))V$. The uniform progression estimate applies by $v>1/x$. For a possible smooth second value the residual gcd after prescribed division cannot contain a prime of $z'-s_0$: an unplanted prime sees $Y_0$ as a unit, and a planted prime has already been divided and omitted. Hence the residual gcd divides $\Delta Q_{\rm free}$ and costs at most $L^{O(1)}$ in the saddle density. Both likelihoods divided by their unconditioned baselines are bounded by $L^{O(1)}$, uniformly also on the exceptional residue classes below. ## Centered moments with the protected rows

At mark $z$ use its baseline $Z(z)$ with nominal center $\mu(z)$, the actual one-point width, coprime prefactor $D(z)/\phi(D(z))$, and the usual omissions for primes of $D(z)$. The ratio after dividing the forced primes is the pure saddle ratio from $\mu(z)$ to $\mu(z)/T(z)$ with the forced primes omitted. When a planted prime vanishes from $D(z)/P(z)$ the change in coprime prefactor is $1+O(B^{-1/2})$ per prime, and the common omitted Euler factors change by $1+O(L^{O(1)}B^{-0.49})$. On a hit at a single protected row the gain is thus $p^{1-\alpha}(1+O(1/u))$ with the same *exact pure saddle ratio* as for a prescribed ordinary row. These baseline corrections are exponentially smaller than the centered entry tolerance. The minimum baseline remains $\exp((-\sigma+o(1))H)$.

To check the two-point formula needed in the centered calculation, fix its two row or real indices and let $Q\le B^2$ be the product of their distinct row primes. The large part of the centered summand is supported on one class $z\equiv r_*(Q)$. For scale $M$ from Proposition 10.1 its absolute value times its likelihood factor is bounded by $L^{O(1)}M[1+Q1_{z\equiv r_*(Q)}]$. This follows from $T(z)\le B^2 A$ and the saddle ratio bound. Discard any mark hitting a planted prime outside the specified protected rows, as follows. At a prime outside $Q$ the single-mark bracket mass is $O(1/p+Q/(Aw))$. When the prime lies in $Q$ but its row is an ordinary prescribed row of different residue, the large part vanishes there and the cost is $O(1/p+1/(Aw))$. There are only $O(u)$ planted primes. The rough two-point bound and a second bracket of bounded mean therefore make the total error $e^{-\Omega(H)}M^2$. Likewise discard $p^2\mid z-s_0$, $p^2\mid z'-s_0$, and $p^2\mid z-z'$ at any of the specified protected or ordinary row primes, at cost $L^{O(1)}(B^{-1/2}+Q/(Aw))M^2$ per prime. These discards are only estimates in a given entry's moment calculation. They make no changes to the marks belonging to the actual field.

On the resulting clean cells the two-point correction from primes in $(d,\sqrt B]$ is
$$
   \gamma_0(z,z')=\prod_{\substack{d<p\le\sqrt B,\ p\mid z-z'\\
                         p\nmid(z-m)(z-s_0)}}p/(p-1).
$$
Indeed when $p\mid z-s_0$ the congruence is already forced by the plant and included in the one-point modulus. The other new small primes forbid a zero at the second value under a smooth first value. Unprescribed primes above $\sqrt B$ dividing $\Delta$ cost $O(p^{-\alpha}L^{O(1)})$ by first dividing out $p$. There are $O(1)$ of them. For an ordinary prescribed prime the four hit/miss cases follow from (2), as in the affine proof. If it divides a pre-existing modulus factor $z'-s_0$ and is unplanted, the value is already a unit there. A planted prime with a prescribed row of different residue has its hits at $s_0$ discarded in this particular calculation.

For a specified protected prime $p$, a hit confined to the second mark cancels the factor $p$ in $D(z')$; it supplies no further modulus. When both marks hit, write $z=s_0+pk$ and $z'=s_0+pk'$ with $k,k'$ units modulo $p$. Taylor expansion gives
$$
    f(z)/p=Y_0/p+k f'(s_0),\qquad
    f(z')/p=Y_0/p+k'f'(s_0)\pmod p.
$$
The two divided values have an invertible affine dependence. The sole first-value class giving zero at the second has relative mass $O(L^{O(1)}/p)$, or zero if incompatible with squarefreeness. This follows by applying the same reduced progression formula with an extra factor $p$ in the first modulus. The additional coprime prefactor is $1+O(1/p)$. When neither mark hits, this prime is handled by the usual eligible unprescribed-prime case if it divides the difference. These verifications produce the two exact pure ratios and $\gamma_0$ up to relative $O(e^{-50(\log L)^2})$ on the retained cells.

In the centered cancellation, expand $\gamma_0$ over squarefree $h$ made of small primes. Each allowed residue $r_h\pmod h$ now obeys both $\gcd(r_h-m,h)=1$ and $\gcd(r_h-s_0,h)=1$. The Brun estimate of the original grid has the same main term on each such class, since the second condition only restricts which classes are used. Inside the signed sum for a fixed class the cancellation against the exact saddle model is unchanged. Reinstating the discarded protected-prime hits gives the same exponentially small errors, also at a given $h\le e^{100(\log L)^2}$ with the obvious extra factor $h$ in the condition $hQ/(Aw)=e^{-\Omega(H)}$. The contribution of larger $h$ is bounded by the nonnegative bracket as in the [truncated singular-series argument](cogentic-variable-multiple-lattice.md). This proves the conditional entry estimates $\mathbf E[A_{jk}^2\mid j,k\text{ admitted}]\le M_{jk}^2 e^{-7(\log L)^2}$, apart from pairs at the same prime handled in the source's separate orthogonal blocks. The diagonal uses $B^2\pi_{\max}/\rho=o(e^{-7(\log L)^2})$.

The single-field local score may now retain all protected rows at once. Sum their bad-diagonal tests, their multiple-root indicators and their $p^{\alpha}$-weighted cross-row squared errors as in the [individual-row argument](cogentic-diffuse-mark-separation.md). Their number is polynomial in $L$, so the combined score still has mean $o(1)$. On its success all primes of $Y_0$ survive on the nonanchor matrix with the same lower local Gram bound. Consequently the new $s_0$ column has no discarded row, and can safely be appended.

## Large-coordinate separation

For a fixed $h$ of mark norm one, add to the search the nonnegative statistic $\sum_{z\ne s_0}\pi(z)I(z)h(z)^2/Z(z)$ divided by a sufficiently large polynomial in $L$. Its expectation is $O(L^C)$ before division by the one-point rough bound. The combined score therefore finds a locally good field with nonanchor energy $O(L^{C'})$. All score terms involve at most two smooth marks of the free pair $s,t$, each through the indicators of a known function of $s+zt$. The [conditional convolution](cogentic-batched-affine-fibers.md) consequently finds the field within the stated complexity. Derivative tests at a protected prime are ordinary congruences on $s+s_0t$.

Give the appended anchor column weight one. For an element of the local true row space within squared error at most one of $h$ on the nonanchor surviving marks, the local norm comparison bounds its squared coefficient norm in the centered coordinates by $L^{O(1)}$. At $s_0$ at most $O(u)$ finite coordinates are nonzero before centering, of squared size at most $B$, while the squared total centering norm is $O(u)$. Its squared value at $s_0$ is therefore $O(B L^{C''})$, including the real rows by their polynomial evaluation bound. The same norm-direction reduction is valid at the anchor because all its prime factors survived. If $|h(s_0)|^2> B L^{C''+2}$, the squared distance on all the retained marks is at least one for sufficiently large $N$. Condition (1) of the diffuse lemma covers every remaining normalized $h\perp T$ after taking $\epsilon>0$ with $a-\sigma-b-\epsilon>b$. This is exactly the strict margin $a>2b+\sigma$.
