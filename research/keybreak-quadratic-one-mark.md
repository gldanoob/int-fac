# A one-mark lower bound for quadratic resultants

Fix a primitive positive definite quadratic mark \(h=[a,b,c]\) in the fundamental-discriminant box above. Thus \(D=b^2-4ac\equiv1\pmod8\) is fundamental, \(a,b\) are odd, \(c\) is even, \(a\asymp V\), and its upper-half-plane root \(\theta\) satisfies \(|\theta|\le s<1\), \(\Im\theta\gg1\). Let \(R=(r,Q)\) be the primitive public anchor with \(r,Q\asymp M\), \(H=h(r,Q)>0\) coprime to odd \(N\), and \(\log M\asymp\log V\) at the fixed knot. Use the centered degree-\(d\) law with \(F(R)=kN\), irreducible binary parity, perturbation \(2(QX-rY)S\), and independent coefficient Gaussians for \(S\) with \(\sigma\ge V^{1+\xi}\) for fixed \(\xi>0\). The multiplier \(k\) is a sufficiently large prime sampled for the current input. All relative scale and growth assumptions of the class-count lemma apply.

Put \(X=a^d M^2\sigma^2\), \(y=V^{c_0+o(1)}\) with fixed \(c_0>0\), and \(u=\log X/\log y\). Under Dirichlet GRH the probability, averaged over the multiplier and the Gaussian, is at least \(\exp(-(1+o(1))u\log u)\) of obtaining a nonzero quadratic resultant with a verified shape of this kind: all prime factors are at most \(y\) apart from at most \(O_{c_0}(1)\) occurrences of primes at most \(V\exp(O((\log\log V)^2))\), and every good prime in it has a unique degree-one child label. One can take the full norm to be \(y\)-smooth if \(y\ge V^{1+\epsilon}\) for fixed \(\epsilon>0\). This estimates one algebraic value for each eligible mark. It does not give the rational cofactor count, the two-mark estimate, or the incoming incidence bound. The extra prime sizes also change the strict quadratic cutoff ledger when \(c_0<1\). ## The affine lattice at a quadratic value

Write \(\omega=(-b+\sqrt D)/2\), \(\theta=\omega/a\), \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\omega]\) and \(I=(a,\omega)\), with norm \(a\). For every homogeneous form of degree \(d\) let
\[
 E(F)=a^d F(\theta,1).
\]
Its integral image is \(I^d\), because products of the two integral basis elements \(a,\omega\) additively generate this ideal power. Its kernel consists of \(h\) times the integral forms of degree \(d-2\), by primitive polynomial divisibility. Consequently \(E(F)\mapsto F(R)\bmod H\) is well-defined and surjective; a Bezout linear form \(J\) with \(J(R)=1\) gives \(J^d\). Write \(\gamma=Q\omega-ar\). Its norm is \(aH\), so the lattice \(\gamma I^{d-1}\) has index \(H\) in \(I^d\). Since it comes from forms divisible by \(QX-rY\), it is precisely the kernel of the residue map.

At fixed \(k\), the image of the prescribed parity and exact anchor law is one coset of \(L=2\gamma I^{d-1}\) in \(I^d\), of index \(4H\). This uses \(d\ge2\), so multiplication by the free degree-\((d-1)\) forms realizes the entire indicated difference lattice. For fixed parity the possible cosets are indexed bijectively by the residues \(F(R)\bmod 2H\) of the required parity. To see well-definedness, two lifts of the same image and parity differ by \(2hW\); differences in \(L\) have the additional form \(2(QX-rY)S\). Surjectivity follows by adding \(2jJ^d\). Given the residue, a multiple of \(2hJ^{d-2}\) adjusts to any exact anchor in that residue without changing the image or parity. This proof also works when \(H\) is even.

The integral ideal \(\mathfrak b=(E(F))I^{-d}\) has norm \(\operatorname{Res}(h,F)\); both the degree-two resultant sign and this norm are positive for nonzero values. For any generator \(z\) of such an ideal record with norm coprime to \(2H\), the reduction of \(z\) in \(I^d/2I^d\) is the prescribed parity image. Indeed \(a\) is odd and \(h(X,1)\equiv X(X+1)\bmod2\); the two-component algebra over \(\mathbb F_2\) has just one unit, and the irreducible parity form takes value one at both roots. Thus \(z\) admits a form lift of the prescribed parity. If a prime divided both \(F(R)\) and \(H\), the two binary forms would share the projective root \(R\bmod p\), forcing the homogeneous resultant to vanish modulo that prime. This includes a root at infinity and primes dividing \(a\). Hence the required multiplier residue \(k\equiv N^{-1}F(R)\bmod 2H\) is a unit. The converse implication from a unit multiplier to a unit norm is unnecessary.

For prime multipliers in \([W,2W]\), choose \(W=M^C\) with \(C\) large enough that \(\phi(2H)W^{-1/2}\log^{O(1)}(WH)=o(1)\) uniformly. Dirichlet GRH then gives probability \(\gg1/H\) for each required residue. Excluding primes dividing \(N\) is negligible under a strict power margin. The distinct cosets themselves can be highly correlated for two different marks. [Multiplier sampling and height](keybreak-multiplier-residue-phases.md#height-parity-content-and-derivative)

## The actual probability on a disk

The real-linear evaluation of the coefficients of \(S\) onto the complex value has covariance with both standard deviations comparable to \(T_0=a^dM\sigma\). In fact \(|Q\theta-r|\asymp M\), the columns corresponding to \(1,\theta\) span a well-conditioned real plane, and the sum of the squared lengths of all powers of \(\theta\) is bounded. The centered-digit term is \(o(T_0)\) uniformly in \(k\) by its \(O(d2^dM)\) coefficient bound and \(\sigma\gg V\). The integer kernel on the free coefficients of \(S\) has a basis of monomial multiples of \(h\), each of length \(O(V)\). Every nonzero kernel-dual vector has a nonzero integer pairing with some basis vector and consequently length at least \(1/O(V)\). Packing in dimension at most \(d\) bounds the full nonzero dual Gaussian tail by \(\exp(-V^{\Omega(\xi)})\). Poisson summation on each coefficient fiber therefore gives its continuous Gaussian factor to relative \(1+o(1)\), uniformly in the translate. The leading normalizer per image point is the covolume of the image lattice divided by the two-dimensional Gaussian area. This follows also by taking a primitive kernel basis, extending it to an integer coefficient basis, and projecting onto its orthogonal complement.

In particular each \(z\) in the active coset with \(|z|\le T_0\) has probability \(\gg\det L/T_0^2\). As \(\det I^d=a^d\sqrt{|D|}/2\), averaging the required prime residue gives each of the coprime points above unconditional mass \(\gg\sqrt{|D|}/X\). The finite sampler's total variation error can be subtracted once from the event probability. Apply the primitive class-count lemma at the target class \([I]^{-d}\), with \(Z=H\). Each counted ideal makes \(\mathfrak b I^d\) principal. Any one of its generators is an eligible lattice value with \(|z|^2=a^d N\mathfrak b\le T_0^2\), and distinct ideals give distinct points. This proves the asserted lower bound without an angular distribution statement or a hypothesis on non-Dirichlet Hecke characters.

Finally, for a good prime \(q\mid N\mathfrak b\), a degree-two gcd of \(\overline F,\overline h\) requires \(q\nmid a\), since otherwise \(\overline h\) has degree at most one by primitivity. If \(q\nmid a\), full divisibility \(\overline h\mid\overline F\) makes \(E(F)\in qI^d\) by evaluation in \(\mathcal O/q\), as \(I\) is a unit locally at \(q\). This would make \(\mathfrak b\) divisible by the rational ideal \((q)\), contrary to primitivity. The resultant and good leading coefficient guarantee a finite common root. The gcd thus has degree one, and for good \(q\) the norm valuation equals the valuation at that unique degree-one prime. Only odd valuations require a new child in the linear descent. The test can check the gcd degree directly on every recorded prime. The proof uses a primitive ideal to count passing values; the numerical resultant itself may have repeated prime factors. ## An eccentric one-mark Gaussian

The smooth sector estimate removes the angular loss in the one-mark probability when the upper root approaches the real axis. Let \(|\theta|\le s_0<1\), \(\eta=\Im\theta\ge V^{-o(1)}\), and take \(\|h\|_\infty\le V^{1+o(1)}\), \(V_D=\sqrt{|D|}\asymp V\). Use the same primitive mark, coprime phase and multiplier conditions as above, with \(\sigma\ge V^{1+\xi}\). Write \(T=a^d\sigma|Q\theta-r|\) and \(X_h=T^2/a^d\). Assume the centered term is \(o(\eta T)\), the phase and anchor sizes are bounded by fixed powers of \(V\), and the class-count scales hold at \(X_h\). If \(y\ge V_D^{c}\) for a fixed \(c>e^{-1/2}\), the one-mark passing probability is at least \(U_h/(\log V)^{O(1)}\) for the core reference formed at \(X_h/V^L\) and sufficiently large fixed \(L\), with every prime factor at most \(y\). The comparison is uniform for any specified subpower upper bound on \(1/\eta\). [Ray classes and angular sectors](keybreak-quadratic-sector-ray.md) · [Reserved core](keybreak-quadratic-pair-reserve.md)

In units of \(T\) before the fixed rotation by the anchor factor, the real Gaussian has a long standard deviation comparable to one. Its area is comparable to \(\eta\) because the first two columns are \(1,\theta\) and \(|\Im(\theta^j)|\le js_0^{j-1}\eta\). The short standard deviation is therefore comparable to \(\eta\). A union of opposite sectors of radius \(c_1T\) and angular width \(c_1\eta\) around the long axis lies at bounded Gaussian distance from the center, for a sufficiently small fixed \(c_1\). Each coprime point in those sectors receives prime-multiplier-averaged mass \(\gg V_D/(\eta X_h)\). The kernel basis has length \(V^{1+o(1)}\), so the fixed power margin in the Gaussian width still supplies the fiber comparison.

For each core and each intermediate fill selection, the prescribed angle for the final generator becomes a translated angular sector for the correcting ideal, with its ordinary class also fixed. The translation follows by multiplying by the fixed core, fill and \(I^d\), as in the product-angle identity. Choose the angular resolution \(J\asymp 1/\eta\) so its arc fits inside these sectors modulo the units \(\{1,-1\}\), and use the trivial ray modulus. At the two-factor correction scale \(S^2\), with \(S=V_D^{1+\varepsilon}\) as in the sector estimate, there are \(\gg S^2/[V_D J(\log V)^{O(1)}]\) admissible corrections. Filling the remaining fixed-power reserve gives \(\gg V^L/[V_D J(\log V)^{O(1)}]\) completions per core within the stated radius, including multiplicity losses. Multiplication by the point mass cancels the angular factor since \(J\eta\asymp1\). This is an estimate for a single quadratic value and its actual elliptic covariance.
