# Deterministic factoring from fields with separate smooth mark sets

The [collective extractor](cogentic-collective-field-extraction.md), [diffuse separation](cogentic-diffuse-mark-separation.md) and [planted separation](cogentic-planted-mark-separation.md) give a classical deterministic method to factor every integer in
$$
                     L_N[2/3,(16/3)^{1/3}].
$$
This is a manuscript-level deduction using the one- and two-mark smoothness estimates, the algebraic unit method and the conditional Gaussian convolutions. Its leading coefficient is about $1.74716$. The following assembly specifies the common grids, accuracy, selection and final extraction. The analytic input uses ordinary and Dirichlet RH. [Alpöge's theorem](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Sutherland's verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Cogentic's starting construction](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf)

Write $L=\log N$, $D=(L/\log L)^{1/3}$, $H=D\log L$ and $V=DH$. For a fixed rational schedule choose $d\sim xD$, $\log A\sim aH$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log U\sim vV$, and put $S=v+1/x$, $\sigma=S/(3b)$. Require
$$
              v>xa,\quad v>1/x,\quad a>4b,\quad a>2b+\sigma.       \tag{1}
$$
Take $\log(1/w)\sim D\sqrt{\log L}$. We may set $\log K_1\sim kH$ with any sufficiently large fixed $k$, since it changes $\log X_{\rm sc}$ by only $O(H)=o(V)$. For clarity, the quantities governing dimension, weight size and the number of fields below depend exponentially in $H$ only on $a,b,\sigma$. Thus $k=100(1+a+b+\sigma)$ suffices for the global error comparisons. The constants in the contour and numerical evaluation may depend on this fixed $k$.

Use the same integer-root preprocessing as in the [two-field proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md), including removing factors through $B$. For each target prime $\ell\in(B/2,B]$, construct three base polynomials by Lemma 8.1's CRT rounding. A target field has the split CRT polynomial and the first real root grid. The two helper types have the root-free CRT polynomial and the first and second real root grids respectively. The lemma constructs its CRT coefficients independently of its real target. Use the common mark grid obtained by sieving $s-m$ against all three fixed multipliers $k_i=f_i^{(0)}(m)/N$, and exclude their prime factors from the smooth base. Its Brun estimate is unchanged: $\log|\prod_i k_i|=O(V)$ and each prime divisor exceeds $d$. All field perturbations, including those planted subsequently, keep their value at $m$ fixed. Check $\gcd(s-m,N)$ as usual before using any mark. The common mark law $\pi$ gives equal mass to each of the $2d+1$ intervals and equal mass to marks within each one. In particular $\pi_{\min}^{-1},\pi_{\max}^{-1}=\exp((a+o(1))H)$.

## From local separation to a computable family

Let $T_i$ be the constant and full nominal-log span of helper type $i$. For each mean-zero $h$ of mark norm one, at least one of $r_i=h-\operatorname{proj}_{T_i}h$ has $\|r_i\|_2\ge L^{-5}$ for sufficiently large inputs. Indeed if both have norm at most $\epsilon$, the two approximating functions $g_i=h-r_i$ differ by at most $2\epsilon$. Comparing the averages of $g_1-g_2$ on the $2d$ root intervals to its baseline average gives the joint $2d$ square matrix of logarithm differences. Its diagonal has magnitude $\log(1/w)+O(1)$ and its off-diagonal operator norm is $O(d)$ by factorial spacing, so the two coefficient vectors have combined norm $O(d/\log(1/w))\epsilon$. Therefore $g_i-\mathbf E_\pi g_i$ has norm $O(\sqrt d L)\epsilon$, which together with $h\perp1$ forces $1\ll L^2\epsilon$. This proves the explicit weaker lower bound claimed above.

Choose a type with a qualifying residual and apply the two separators to its unit normalization. Choose fixed $\epsilon_0>0$ such that $a-\sigma-b-\epsilon_0>b$. For squared maximum below $\exp((a-\sigma-b-\epsilon_0)H)$ use the diffuse construction. Otherwise plant a maximum coordinate. The squared value then exceeds $B$ times any fixed power of $L$, as required in the planted proof. Both procedures give a field with a computable full set of ordinary clean marks: take all squarefree smooth marks, omitting only those hitting a multiple root. Include the anchor in the planted case. The proof provides a subset on which the local Gram is positive after peeling and the normalized residual is separated by a positive absolute constant. The extra rows of the full field vanish on this subset, so enlarging to the full clean set and its finite rows preserves the separated distance. Every incidence is given the positive weight $\pi(s)/Z(s)$ used by its field; a planted anchor instead receives weight one. Small changes from renormalizing the nonanchor interval can be used verbatim or absorbed by a fixed factor.

For computation replace the logarithms in the $d-1$ Helmert coordinates by evaluations at the nominal roots, rounded to absolute accuracy $e^{-2kH}$ including the Helmert coefficients. Approximate the weights by positive rationals with relative error $e^{-2kH}$. Let $Q_i$ be the rational quadratic form measuring squared distance to the row space of these approximate trace-zero real rows, the constant and the *unscaled* integer finite rows, on the full clean marks. Its norm on the full mark space with law $\pi$ is at most
$$
        \Lambda=\exp((\max(a,\sigma)+o(1))H),
$$
since the nonanchor weights divided by $\pi$ are at most $(1+o(1))/\rho$ and an anchor weight divided by its mass is $1/\pi(s_0)$. The form can be computed exactly by choosing a rational basis of the local row space and solving its rational positive definite Gram matrix. Its numerator and denominator bit lengths are polynomial in the dimensions and the original approximation precision.

On surviving columns the original constant, scaled finite and true trace-zero rows have Gram bounded below by $L^{-C}$ for some fixed $C$: the orthogonal norm-direction elimination has polynomial inverse, by the source proof or the single-field norm comparison. This also holds for the rational trace-zero approximations. Consequently the exponentially small entry errors cannot destroy the separator for the normalized residual. More explicitly, any fit to squared accuracy at most one on the surviving marks has trace-zero coefficients of norm at most $L^{O(1)}\sqrt{1+\Lambda}$ for a test function of norm one. Changing those rows changes its weighted residual norm by at most $L^{O(1)}(1+\Lambda)K_1^{-0.9}$. This argument can be applied before appending the planted mark in its leverage calculation, where all its prime rows survive. Finite columns need no approximation, and row rescaling does not affect their span.

For passage back from $r_i$ to $h$, the full nominal $g_i$ can be fitted on all clean marks with weighted error norm at most $L^{O(1)}\sqrt\Lambda K_1^{-0.9}$. Its log coefficient vector has polynomial norm by the joint-grid estimate, or by the one-field interval matrix and $\|g_i\|_2\le1$. Decompose it into its trace-zero part and the direction of the vector of all ones. Its first part is directly fitted by the rational trace-zero rows. In its second part, replace the sum of nominal logs by the sum of true logs, equal to $\log|f_i(s)|-\log X_{\rm sc}$ on each integer mark. This latter expression is exactly represented by the finite rows and constant on all squarefree clean marks, allowing arbitrary real coefficients. Thus the chosen field gives $Q_i(h)\ge L^{-12}$ after the above tolerances. Fixed-point input vectors of absolute error $e^{-2kH}$ at each mark have the same guarantee with a factor two decrease. The orthogonal projections in selecting $T_i$ may use rational approximations and the polynomial condition estimate from the interval matrix.

Apply the determinant assembly lemma on the mean-zero mark space with $\delta=L^{-12}/2$. Use exact rational algebra on the field forms. If the accumulated form fails to dominate the mark metric, rational symmetric elimination of their difference supplies a negative direction. Normalize and round this direction to absolute error $e^{-2kH}$ and remove its mean. Its Rayleigh quotient is at most two, since the accumulated form before termination has norm at most $K\Lambda$. The rational bit lengths before normalization, including sums and pivots, are polynomial in the field dimensions, number of fields and the input bit lengths of the forms. The lemma bounds the number of choices by $K\le\exp((a+o(1))H)$; any fixed sufficiently large polynomial in $L$ in the separation threshold gives this same bound. The final sum of rational forms dominates the mark metric. If a mean-zero unit $h$ had a true fit on every full clean field, its trace-zero coefficients would be bounded on each field by the surviving Gram and its local energy at most $\Lambda$. Replacing those coefficients' rows by the rational proxies would yield
$$
      \sum_{i\le K} Q_i(h)\le K L^{O(1)}(1+\Lambda)^2 K_1^{-1.8}<1,
$$
contradicting this domination. Thus only constants fit on every helper. This construction uses no decision about exact equality of determinants involving transcendental logarithms.

## The target, cost and limiting coefficient

Choose one target field with positive one-field Gram and all its $d$ prescribed $\ell$ rows protected by the ordinary two-coefficient local score. The proof of the one-field rank statement is the local part of the diffuse lemma, retaining the standard target factors and the original protected-row tests; its diagonal bound still uses a single smooth density. Its selection cost is $U\exp(O(H))$ by the conditional convolution. Add this field with its own full clean marks to the assembled helpers. The only simultaneous mark functions are constants and the local target Gram protects all target prime rows, so the [many-field valuation and unit calculation](cogentic-collective-field-extraction.md) produces a verified exponent of $\ell$ modulo $N$. It has polynomial cost in the $\exp(O(H))$ collection dimensions and control bit lengths. Different fields may have prime rows with the same rational residue: they occupy distinct field blocks and a separate rational mark-sum row cancels the residues at $m$.

All mark and field tables, linear algebra, accuracy bounds and the number of calls per prime have size at most $\exp(O(H))$ away from the coefficient search. Computing the coprime prefactors $D(s)/\phi(D(s))$ in the score needs trial division through $O(\sqrt m)$ for the factor $|s-m|$ and through $O(\sqrt A)$ for a planted difference. Even $\sqrt m\exp(O(H))<U$ under $v>1/x$. This is done once per table, outside its coefficient sweep. A planted scalar search and every conditional search cost $U\exp(O(H))$. The approximately orthonormal basis of $T$, the prescribed vector and its variances have polynomial logarithmic accuracy; score coefficients involving $t_j^{-1}$ are bounded by $e^{O(H)}$ by construction. The existing Mellin/quadrature and Gaussian convolution routines therefore apply at their original accuracy and cost. The finitely many small-input cases admit trial division. For all sufficiently large inputs a proper split follows from the verified exponent of a negative-character prime in each relevant dyadic interval, exactly as in the [dense-prime splitting argument](cogentic-dense-exponents.md). Recursing, testing primality and interleaving finite fallbacks preserve the leading bound.

For fixed $v$, let $x\downarrow1/v$ and hence $S\uparrow 2v$ in the limiting schedule. The least $\max(4b,2b+S/(3b))$ occurs at $b^2=S/6$, with value $4\sqrt{S/6}$. The condition $v>xa$ holds arbitrarily close precisely above $v^3=16/3$, since $x^2(v+1/x)$ increases in $x$. Interleave all rational strict schedules, each with a positive fixed fraction of simulation time. The elementary bit cost $U\exp(O(H))$ per schedule gives the stated limiting deterministic coefficient for one algorithm. The analytic and algebraic modifications described here have not been machine formalized.
