# A randomized one-coordinate schedule at 2.0741

For a single integer \(N\), with all field setup, sampling, screening and extraction charged on that input, the short rational representative schedule gives manuscript-level expected bit time
\[
 L_N[1/3,2.0741].
\]
This uses Dirichlet GRH and the analytic relation and smooth-order inputs of the pinned November 26 KeyBreak manuscript. Vallée and Gentry established short-square sampling at the two-thirds scale. The local floor-sum and factored-denominator implementations give independent derivations for fixed widths above it. [Vallée](https://doi.org/10.1145/73007.73016) · [Gentry, §4.1](https://www.iacr.org/cryptodb/archive/2004/CRYPTO/1334/1334.pdf#page=8) The Lean target checks finite probability transfer and exponent inequalities. The analytic and end-to-end complexity statements here are manuscript proofs. ## Generating the private input

Write \(H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\), \(X=\lfloor N^a\rfloor\), \(a=.667\), and choose the denominator power of two \(T\asymp N^s\) with \(s=1333/4000\). Set \(V=TX/N\). The target ideal law is uniform on the finite set
\[
 Z=\{z\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times:1\le z^2\bmod N\le X\}.
\]
The unit population estimate gives \(|Z|=X\varphi(N)/N+O(N^{1/2+o(1)})\). First run the existing small-prime pass. On inputs with a prime at most its cutoff it already finds a divisor with probability \(1-o(1)\) per epoch. On the remaining inputs \(\min_{p\mid N}\log(p)/H\to\infty\), \(\omega(N)=o(H)\), and \(\varphi(N)/N=1-o(1)\). Perfect powers, primes and even numbers are handled by the existing preliminary tests. [Unit population estimate](keybreak-primitive-denominator-discrepancy.md#large-divisors-and-the-unit-population) · [First-split pass and cutoff](keybreak-private-shape-work.md#ensuring-the-count-suffices-on-every-input)

There are two implementations of an approximate uniform draw on \(Z\). The simpler one chooses a center modulo \(N\), takes offsets below \(\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\), and counts candidate offsets using Euclidean floor sums for the linear part of the square. It returns the selected root only after checking the exact square and gcd. One trial accepts with probability \(\gg1/\log N\), and its conditional law has total variation \(N^{-\Omega(1)}\) from uniform. The proof and executable sampler include the affine discrepancy, the short-vector exceptions at the endpoint denominator exponent \(a/2\), and the exact padding probabilities. A polynomial retry cap makes exhaustion negligible. Either this sampler or the following rational-pair construction gives the same downstream proof. [Linearized square sampler](keybreak-linearized-square-sampler.md) · [Implementation and small exhaustive checks](../experiments/keybreak-linearized-square-sampler.py)

Generate the equally weighted primitive pairs \((t,h)\) of the fair proposal and compute \(z=h/t\bmod N\). For the proof partition \(Z\) using the shortest length of the lattice \(h'\equiv t'z\bmod N\) scaled by \(T,X\). At most \(N^{a-\gamma}\) roots have \(\lambda_1<N^\eta/V\) with \(\eta=1/16000\) and some fixed \(\gamma>0\). Integer rounding and \(T\) within a constant factor of \(N^s\) change the short-vector box by a bounded factor, absorbed in the strict exponent margins. On its complement the primitive count is uniformly
\[
 (1+N^{-\eta+o(1)}+O(V^{-1/2+o(1)}))
 \frac{V}{2\pi^2}\prod_{p\mid N}\frac{p}{p+1}.
\]
For every exceptional root the count is \(O(V)\), since two distinct positive primitive representatives have normalized determinant at least \(1/V\) and hence their directions are separated by \(\gg1/V\) in the bounded box. The product over \(p\mid N\) and \(\varphi(N)/N\) are both at least inverse polynomial in \(\log N\). Normalizing shows that the accepted-root law has total variation \(N^{-c}\) from uniform on \(Z\) for some \(c>0\), for every sufficiently large odd input. The sampler can keep every pair, including those on exceptional lattices. The equal-pair probability is \(1/[32T(1+\log_2 T)|\mathcal K|]\) with \(|\mathcal K|\asymp X^2/N\). Multiplying by the total primitive count gives acceptance \(\gg(\log N)^{-3}\) on every odd input, and \(\Theta(1/\log N)\) on the rough branch. Allow \(O((\log N)^5)\) independent proposals for a single root, then emit a dummy on exhaustion. A per-proposal polynomial cap coupled as in the generator proof changes this distribution by \(O(N^{-A})\) for an arbitrarily large fixed \(A\). All proposal work and discarded attempts are charged. [Short-vector exception count](keybreak-short-vector-exception.md) · [Primitive lattice weights and unrestricted input proof](keybreak-generic-lattice-weights.md) · [Exact proposal weights](keybreak-factored-denominator-proposal.md)

Store just the accepted root in its separate private record and pass just \(u=z^2\bmod N\) to the public computation. Discard \(t,h,k\), discarded proposals and any timing data before row construction. Each raw root slot uses fresh randomness. The entire bounded epoch with \(\exp(O(H))\) raw slots can be coupled to the ideal independent roots with total error \(o(1)\), by the product bound. One can expose unused slots in advance and apply the same deterministic transcript processing with shared downstream coins. No conditioning on an individual rare successful shape is required. ## Good fields, prefix tests and extraction

For each square unit \(u\le X\) the ideal law has exactly \(2^{\omega(N)}\) equal-probability lifts, including odd prime powers. Hence its public law is uniform on the square units in the window. Use one coordinate of length \(\lfloor a(n-1)\rfloor-2\) bits, for \(n=\operatorname{bitlength}(N)\). This stays inside the window and differs from \(\log_2 X\) by a bounded amount. Apply the fixed 1536-stage product recipe with its new logarithmic length. If \(w_i\) are the *new* one-coordinate losses, the grid has moment \(\sum_i a_i w_i=a(2\kappa\delta/3)\), where \(a_i\) is the cutoff fraction. Put \(S_j=\sum_{i<j}w_i\), \(S=S_J\); the schedule already includes the shortened length in these weights.

On the quantitative good-field event, use the central-index construction (H.20) independently in each disjoint prime band. In every dyad omit the bad top parents, including the relevant elliptic eligibility exceptions. Under the pinned estimates this changes the distribution of any nontrivial quadratic character by at most \(O(D^{-B_0})\), with \(D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}\) and arbitrary fixed \(B_0\). The independent \(\Theta(D)\) prime choices mix all quadratic classes, since \(\omega(N)=O(D\sqrt{\log\log N})\), and the fraction of repeated choices is negligible even relative to one square class. Dividing ordered counts by their within-stage factorials counts distinct products. Thus at least \(X\exp(-(S+o(1))H)/2^{\omega(N)}\) eligible good products are squares. Multiplying by their root-fiber size and dividing by \(|Z|\sim X\) gives an ideal raw lower probability \(\exp(-(S+o(1))H)\). Each admitted good shape calls the existing top descent independently with one recorded prime parent per occurrence. The schedule's single-coordinate count is below \(4k_0\), the original total allowance across two private coordinates. The H.4 union over top calls and all deterministic occurrence bounds therefore still apply. For every actual stage-\(j\) survivor, its positive coordinate is divisible by a product of disjoint, certified prime-band parts \(d_i\) above the stage thresholds. Counting multiples among *all* integers up to \(X\) and taking the staged reciprocal Rankin bound gives at most \(X\exp(-(S_j+o(1))H)\) targets. The unit-root multiplicity \(2^{\omega(N)}=\exp(o(H))\) and \(|Z|\sim X\) leave this ideal exponent unchanged. The prefix upper bound permits false misses. Confidence amplification and the excluded exceptional prime sets in the lower count protect good completions. Use the source's bounded search with virtual successes after an earlier verified split. Allocate raw slots with coefficient \(b+S+\epsilon/4\), prefix admissions with \(b+S-S_j+\epsilon\), including launch cap \(b+\epsilon\), where \(\epsilon=.000005\). Markov and the strict cap gaps give \(o(1)\) probability of overflow on the ideal rough branch. The good-shape bound and H.4 supply more than the required \(\exp((b+o(1))H)\) completed rows or a split with constant probability after successful field setup. Charge all launched descents including failures. [Staged Rankin and cap argument](keybreak-private-band-stages.md#testing-and-counting-a-prefix) · [Source factor-or-row guarantee](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=136)

A completed row multiplies the checked top identities for the factors of \(u\) and has rational times evaluated field component equal to \(u=z^2\bmod N\). It uses the same rational, ideal, norm and character records and the same certified algebraic square-root procedure as H.6. Stop at the public column bound plus one completed row. The sparse solver selects a verified nonempty binary dependency using its own coins. In the ideal law every retained private \(z_i\), conditional on the entire public transcript, is independently uniform on its square fiber. Multiplying the selected roots therefore makes their product uniform on its fiber. Against the independently determined public lift, the probability of a mixed CRT sign is \(1-2^{1-\omega(N)}\ge1/2\). The coupling transfers this epoch success probability to the actual sampler. This argument allows duplicate rows, and uses no assumption about their rank. ## Cost on the current input

The rational witness has \(\delta=1.3887384,\ \kappa=1.1520151,\ \beta=.6710908\) and \(b=\delta\beta\). Gate costs, field heights, top and internal descent means, local factor cutoffs and all finite inequalities use this same witness. The stage mesh is fixed as \(N\) grows, and each rounding has a strict asymptotic margin or follows directly from its local integer rounding rule. The two symbolic integer replays additionally exercise these rules at 12,000 and 16,000 bits of the input bit-length. The decisive charges are shown rounded upward.
