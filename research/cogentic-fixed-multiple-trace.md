# Finite-order trace transfer with a fixed multiple at the common root

The [projective trace estimate](cogentic-projective-trace.md) also holds for the fixed-multiple field family
$$
 f_i=f_i^{(0)}+P_0\ell(T-m)G_i(T,1),\qquad f_i(m)=k_i N,
$$
with its [full homogeneous degree-$d-2$ coefficient Gaussian](cogentic-homogeneous-marks.md). This transfer preserves the small original Gaussian coordinate width $W=\exp(sH+o(H))$. Write $N$ for an arbitrary composite after the standard primality, perfect-power and small-divisor tests, $m=\lceil N^{1/d}\rceil$, $d\sim xD$, $\log A\sim cH$, $\log B\sim bH$, $S=xc+1/x$. Take
$$
            xc>1/x,\quad c>2b,\quad 2c>b+2S/(3b),                 \tag{1}
$$
with all inequalities strict. Take a fixed even $k>2b/(2c-b-2S/(3b))$ and, for example, $s>kc+(2k+2)b$. Choose the generator $\ell$ at size $\exp((1+o(1))z^3)$, retain the prime band $B^{1-\delta}<p\le B$ of the trace proof with its single target $\ell$, and exclude from that band the prime divisors of $k_1k_2$. Every denominator is prime above $B$ and coprime to the two fixed cofactors. Every marked value $a-b_vm$ is a unit modulo $k_1k_2N$, by the grid sieve and initial gcd sweeps. Here $k$ may be increased further by a fixed amount to allow the $o(1)$ in $n$.

For $h\le k$ marks and at most $2k+2$ prescribed root rows use the full integral projective coefficient projection on the small coordinates $G_i$. Interpolation at finitely many distinct residues of the same prime is independent since $d>3k+5$ eventually. The kernel norm for these coordinates is at most $(2A)^h$, so the conditional Poisson ratio is $e^{-\Omega(L^6)}$ for our $s$. Multiplication of the mark output by the fixed factor $P_0\ell b_v(a-b_vm)$ changes its one-point modulus to $P_0\ell^{1-\epsilon_i(v)} b_v|a-b_vm|$ after division by the preassigned factors. The higher conditional steps multiply this by $\prod_{j<i}|\Delta_{ji}|$ and the necessary free row-prime factors, again dividing the immediately prescribed factors. For fixed $k$, every resulting modulus satisfies $\log q\le(1/x+o(1))V$. Its center-to-width ratio at every mark is $\exp(O_k(H))$, by the normalized vanishing-polynomial test from the finite-order moment lemma. Thus the [uniform progression theorem](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) applies up to the original large modulus since $S/2>1/x$. Real stability, the $L^3$ innovation cutoff, and the nominal center replacements are unchanged. The one-point cofactor modulus carries its actual coprime normalization, including its potentially excluded eligible primes; the source one-point weights and their row-deletion saddle ratios track these factors. [Fixed-multiple local formulas](cogentic-homogeneous-marks.md)

The $h$-point conditional upper bound remains $L^{O_k(1)}$ times the product of the one-point saddle densities. Although $q$ contains its large fixed factor $|a-b_vm|$, no prime of this factor can divide the absolute polynomial value, since $F_i(a,b_v)\equiv b_v^d k_iN\pmod{|a-b_vm|}$ and $b_v$ is coprime to $a$. Hence every possible common divisor $G$ with the conditional class still divides a product of the $O_k(1)$ determinants and prescribed row primes, and $\log G=O_k(H)$. This is exactly the range needed in the successive rough estimates. An eligible root condition at slope $r\ne m$ is invertible in the linear coefficient evaluations of $G_i$ modulo its prime. The slope $m$ cannot be any active root because $p\nmid k_i N$ for eligible $p$. Root mass and defect estimates are therefore those of the trace proof with these residues omitted. The expectation of the full root count at $p$ is $1-1/p+O(e^{-L^3})$, and the omitted normalization error summed with the smooth weights is $B^{-1+o(1)}$. Omitting $O(L)$ primes dividing $k_1k_2$ from the saddle or narrow-band Mertens sums has negligible cost.

The clean joint local formula for one field uses, at each $d<p\le B^{1-\delta}$ with $p\ne\ell$, the multiplier
$$
 (1-1/p)^{-\,[h-\nu_p-(h_m(p)-1)_+]},\qquad
 h_m(p)=\#\{i:a_i\equiv m b_i\pmod p\}.
$$
Each repeated non-$m$ slope contributes once to the extra exclusion of zero; repeated occurrences of $m$ are already coprime in the one-point modulus at every such mark. Eligible primes on a repeated $m$ slope likewise contribute no extra coprimality factor, regardless of their higher valuation in the difference. At another repeated slope outside prescribed roots the divisibility exception at the first mark in its block is free and is bounded as in the variable-multiple proof, since $p\nmid (a_i-b_i m)$. At a prescribed root, $r\ne m$ ensures that the first two residual class steps modulo $p$ needed in the first-order Taylor transfer are the same as in that proof. Every occurrence of a large prime $p\mid(a_i-b_i m)$ that also divides a new determinant is already included in the one-point modulus. All other additional prime factors larger than $B$ divide the finitely many difference products. These observations prove the fixed-multiple version of the $h$-point local formula, including its $O_k(e^{-50z^2})$ errors and the prescribed $p^2\mid\Delta_{ij}$ exclusions.

In the distinct-mark trace expansion, expand the squared local multiplier on the partition lattice of $k+1$ points: the $k$ varied slopes and the single fixed slope $m$. The term imposing no equalities still has coefficient 1, and each other coefficient is $O_k(1/p)$ by finite incidence inversion. A term can now require a varied point to equal the fixed $m$ modulo $p$. This enforces one numerator congruence with no preceding variable, which only simplifies the sequential bound. For the tail $t>T=e^{100z^2}$, retain one such equality, or one earlier-mark equality, per participating prime. Coefficient total variation is polynomial in $L$ for fixed $k$. An upper progression bound for the normalized sieved numerator distribution loses at most $L^{O(1)}$ compared with all integers: its grid interval retains a proportion at least a fixed inverse power of $L$ by the elementary Brun estimate. This loss on each of the $k$ factors is absorbed into $L^{O_k(1)}$. For $t\le T$, the small-slope average has class density zero on any class forbidden by a cofactor prime. Otherwise its reference density is
$$
        \psi(t)=t^{-1}\prod_{p\mid(t,k_1k_2)}(1-1/p)^{-1}
               \le L^{O(1)}/t.
$$
For a prescribed row pair $Q_j\le B^2$, its primes are units for the cofactor sieve. The uniform weighted Brun estimate at modulus $tQ_j\le e^{100z^2}B^2$ is available under $c>2b$. It gives the same cancellation of the two terms in $g_j$, with $1/t$ replaced by $\psi(t)$ and an additional relative $O(e^{-Cz^2})$ for arbitrary fixed $C$ upon taking fixed larger truncation constants. Extra row-hit, target and unclean-pair upper bounds use only the elementary progression upper bound, with the harmless $L^{O_k(1)}$ loss. Repeat the finite partition sum exactly as in the trace lemma. The repeated-position forest bound needs at most two main-band congruences on any one numerator; the same upper bound provides $B^{-j+o(1)}$ for $j=1,2$ by $c>2b$. Thus every part of the central and repeated trace proof, the signed peeling and extraction remains valid under (1).

For completeness, the stated weighted Brun estimate follows directly with these constants. For an arbitrary cofactor $K=k_1k_2$ with $\log(2K)=L^{O(1)}$, take $y=\lceil e^{3Cz^2}\rceil$ for the required fixed $C>0$. Sieve the primes $p\mid K$, $p\le y$ by Bonferroni at two successive truncation levels $J,J+1$, $J=\lceil C_1 z^2\rceil$. In a progressed numerator interval of length $I\asymp Aw$, length counts at each resulting squarefree product have absolute error $O(1)$. There are at most $y^{J+1}=\exp(O_{C,C_1}(z^4))$ such products, uniform in the progression. The omitted reciprocal main terms are at most $(\sum_{p\le y}1/p)^J/J!\le e^{-2Cz^2}$ when $C_1$ is sufficiently large for the requested $C$. For primes dividing the progression modulus, either all candidates violate the cofactor condition or the prime is redundant; this produces exactly the local product in $\psi$. The remaining large primes have $\sum_{p\mid K,p>y}1/p\le \log(2K)/y=e^{-(3C-o(1))z^2}$. The number of large primes is polynomial in $L$. Their endpoint errors and the truncated small-prime endpoint errors, relative to the expected progressed count, are $O(e^{O_C(z^4)}tQ_j/(Aw))=e^{-\Omega(H)}$. Partial summation supplies the weighted version for a mark dependence with the same polynomial supremum and total variation bound. The cofactor Euler product itself is $\ge L^{-O(1)}$. Denominator dependence causes no change: the sieved forbidden residue is $a\equiv m b_v\pmod p$, and $b_v$ was chosen coprime to $K$.

Together with the fixed-order numerical conditional-score computation, this transfers the spectral rank conclusion to small per-coordinate enumeration widths. The new arithmetic input here is the fixed-multiple $k$-point transfer and the trace bound. The cost of computing and optimizing the score is a separate algorithmic requirement.
