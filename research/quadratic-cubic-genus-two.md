# Genus-two factor selection with a quadratic and a cubic branch factor

Fix a prime $p$ and $Z\ge p$. Let $m$ range over the integers in $(Z,2Z]$, let $d=pm$, $b=\lceil\log_2(2pZ+1)\rceil$ and $L=\log(2p)$. There is a deterministic algorithm on $d$ alone with the following time bound for one proper split, for every fixed integer $r\ge1$:

$$ \big(\mathbb E_m T(d)^r\big)^{1/r}
 \le b^{O_r(1)}\min\{\sqrt p,\ (1+p^{4r}/Z)^{1/r}\exp(O_r(\sqrt{L\log(2L)}))\}. $$

The same bound holds when the partners are restricted to primes, or to integers for which $p$ is the least prime factor of $d$. The square-root-logarithm exponent uses Younis's Theorem 1.3 and the independently kernel-verified ordinary Riemann hypothesis announced by Alpöge on 20 November 2026. Younis's classical Theorem 1.1 independently gives the exponent $O_r(L^{2/3}(\log(2L))^{4/3})$. Both bounds are uniform in $p$. The construction only needs rational parameters and the usual finite-field results for Jacobians. [Younis, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05761) · [Alpöge's announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Sutherland's independent checks and precise statements](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## A two-torsion character of the marked point

Take a squarefree monic quintic $G=g_2g_3$ over an odd finite field, where $g_2,g_3$ are monic with degrees 2 and 3. Let $J$ be the Jacobian of $y^2=d_0G(x)$ with $d_0\ne0$ and let $\infty$ denote its rational point at infinity. The two roots of $g_2$ determine a nonzero rational $T\in J[2]$, represented by the sum of the associated branch points minus $2\infty$. The subset of two branch points is neither empty nor the full set of six, giving a nontrivial class in the even-subset description of $J[2]$.

The divisor of the function $g_2(x)$ is twice the indicated divisor of $T$. The reduced Tate pairing with $T$ on $D_P=[P-\infty]$ is therefore $\chi(g_2(x(P)))$ for a finite nonbranch $P$, where $\chi$ is the quadratic character. To evaluate at a common support point use the local leading coefficient in the divisor pairing. At infinity the local parameter $t=x^2/y$ satisfies $x\sim d_0^{-1}t^{-2}$ and consequently $g_2(x)\sim d_0^{-2}t^{-4}$. The leading coefficient is square for every $d_0$. The possible tame-symbol sign from the pole of $g_2$ is also positive since its order is four. The finite-field Tate pairing between $J(\mathbb F_p)/2J(\mathbb F_p)$ and $J[2](\mathbb F_p)$ is perfect. Equivalently, a nonzero rational two-torsion divisor has a nontrivial quadratic evaluation character. In particular, if $\#J(\mathbb F_p)=2s$ with $s$ odd, then a positive value puts $D_P$ in the odd-order subgroup. At any other prime $q$ of good reduction a negative value forces even order, irrespective of the two-primary structure at $q$. [Frey–Rück, finite-field pairing and evaluation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2153546) · [Bruin, finite-field Tate pairing for abelian varieties](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.764/)

## Uniform supply of smooth orders of twice odd size

Take $\log Y=C\sqrt{L\log(2L)}$ using ordinary RH, or $\log Y=C L^{2/3}(\log(2L))^{4/3}$ using classical estimates. Put $E=\exp(C' L\log(2L)/\log Y)$ for a sufficiently large constant $C'$. In the central interval $|n-p^2|\le p^{3/2}/2$ there are $\gg p^{3/2}/E$ distinct $n=2s$ with $s$ odd and $Y$-smooth. Indeed apply Younis on a fixed-proportion subinterval in the $s$ coordinate, at $x\asymp p^2$ and $x/2$ with the corresponding halved lengths to count even $s$. Both lengths exceed the two-thirds power of their center. The two relative short-interval densities are $(1+o(1))\Psi(x,Y)/x$, using $\Psi(x,Y)=(2+o(1))\Psi(x/2,Y)$ and the saddle point $\alpha(x,Y)=1+o(1)$ here. The difference gives an odd density $(1/2+o(1))\Psi(x,Y)/x$. Distinct products of primes of size about $x^{1/k}$ with $k\asymp\log x/\log Y$ give $\Psi(x,Y)/x\gg1/E$. [Detailed smooth-number estimate and references](quintic-field-genus-two.md#odd-smooth-orders-at-every-prime)

Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance II, Proposition 2.4(b), supplies an ordinary regular Weil polynomial of **odd trace** for each such $n$ outside at most $p$ exceptional integers. Their proof of Theorem 1.1 uses precisely this polynomial and gives $\gg p^{9/2}/((\log p)^2(\log\log p)^2)$ quintic models for it. The monic model from a leading coefficient $a$ is $a^4 g(X/a)$, with the rational change $(x,y)\mapsto (ax,a^2y)$. Each monic model has exactly $p-1$ preimages under this conversion. Thus the good monic quintics number $\gg p^5/E'$ with $E'=\exp(O(L\log(2L)/\log Y))$ absorbing polylogarithms. The finite number of small primes can be handled directly. [Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance II, Proposition 2.4 and proof of Theorem 1.1](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/pila/HyperII.pdf#page=8)

A monic quintic giving a Jacobian of order $2s$ with $s$ odd has exactly two irreducible factors: the rational two-torsion dimension is one less than the number of factors, by the even-subset description with the fixed branch point $\infty$. For the models just counted the factors have degrees **2 and 3**. In fact the odd Frobenius trace gives an odd number of points on the genus-two curve over $\mathbb F_p$, since $\#C(\mathbb F_p)=p+1-\operatorname{Tr}(\mathrm{Frob}_p)$. Its parity is one plus the number of finite roots, by pairing nonbranch points under the hyperelliptic involution. Thus there is an even number of finite roots, which eliminates the alternative factor degrees 1 and 4. Each selected monic quintic consequently has a unique monic quadratic factor and a unique monic cubic factor.

## Four parameters with a forced local sign

Fix a nonzero integer $f$ with $\chi_p(f)=+1$. On the quadratic/cubic models above use translation $X\mapsto X+t$. Uniformly in the selected model,
$$ \#\{t:\chi_p(g_2(t))=\chi_p(g_3(t))=1\}=p/4+O(\sqrt p). $$
Indeed expand the two indicators and use Weil character-sum bounds for the squarefree polynomials $g_2,g_3,g_2g_3$. The factors are both irreducible, hence have no zero at any $t\in\mathbb F_p$. Translations permute the selected set, so a positive proportion of all selected models have both constants nonzero squares. Every such model has exactly four presentations
$$ g_2(X)=X^2+aX+f u^2,\quad g_3(X)=X^3+b_1 X^2+cX+f v^2,\quad u,v\ne0. $$
The point $(0,fuv)$ is rational over the coefficient ring. Under the square scaling of roots by $w^2$ the ordered parameters become $(w^2 a,w^2 b_1,w^4c,w^2 u,w^3v)$. The curve is isomorphic by $(x,y)\mapsto(w^2x,w^5y)$, preserving its marked point. Each orbit has exactly one representative with $u=v$, obtained by $w=u/v$. Hence at least $p^4/E''$ of the four-parameter words $(a,b_1,c,u)$ have an odd-order marked class killed by the universal odd smooth scalar, for $E''=\exp(O(L\log(2L)/\log Y))$. Each such target depends only on $p$ and $f$.

For any distinct prime divisor $q$ of the input with $\chi_q(f)=-1$, the same word has quadratic evaluation $\chi_q(fu^2)=-1$ when the constant, point and discriminant are units. Its marked class then has even order. On an input divisible by $p$ and $q$, checking units by gcd catches any failure at $q$ on a word good at $p$. Otherwise the odd scalar kills the marked class at $p$ and its reduction at $q$ remains nonzero. Gcd-checked Cantor addition over the input exposes this disagreement in the identity tests or intermediate degree decisions. Use all odd prime powers $\ell^k$ up to $4d^2$ at primes $\ell\le Y$, taking the largest such power for each prime. This includes every possible odd part of the selected order. A word costs $b^{O(1)}Y^{O(1)}$ bits of work. [Cantor's group law](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1987-0866101-0) · [Gcd-checked implementation](../experiments/genus-two-cantor.gp)

## Whole partners and time moments

One may supply $f$ pointwise at every distinct pair of primes via the verified Dirichlet GRH, or classically with rare partner exceptions. The pointwise method first trial-divides up to $Cb^2$ and enumerates singles and pairs of primes through that bound. For a pair $p\ne q$ exceeding the cutoff the quadratic characters $\chi_q$ and $\chi_p\chi_q$ are both nontrivial on the menu, by the GRH least-nonresidue theorem. If no single prime has the sign pair $(+,-)$, then a prime of sign $(-,-)$ exists and a prime of sign $(-,+)$ exists. Their product has signs $(+,-)$. This argument uses Dirichlet characters of conductor $q$ and $pq$, both bounded by $d$. [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3595#page=2) · [Independent verification of Dirichlet GRH](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

For the classical alternative, enumerate products of two primes in $(R,2R]$ with $R=b_0^{C_1}$ for the actual input length $b_0$. Taking the larger $p$-sign class of primes in that interval, a partner prime provides the sign pair whenever its character takes both signs on that class. The multiplicative large sieve bounds the remaining partners admitting no such prime divisor by $O(\sqrt Z)$, after a polynomial trial-division cutoff. This count applies to whole integer partners with arbitrary multiplicities and is uniform in $p$. The fixed-$p$ inputs in a dyad need at most two different menus. Pure prime powers can also be extracted using integer roots and primality tests. [Classical sign-count proof for all integer partners](untwisted-legendre-partners.md#whole-integer-partners-and-rare-failure-of-the-conic-list)

Choose $2^k=2P$ for $P<p\le2P$, guessing this and the following shift by round robin. For the proof use $j=\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor$ and take the lowest $4rk$ bits of $\lfloor d/2^j\rfloor$ as $r$ disjoint four-coordinate starts. Its unreduced value is injective in $m$ on $(Z,2Z]$ and each joint start occurs $O(1+Z/p^{4r})$ times. At stage $a$ try the $4^a$ binary small-bias XOR shifts separately on each start and all public $f$. The target for an admissible $f$ has cube density $\gamma\gg1/E''$, since each coordinate residue is represented once or twice. The missed fraction among all joint starts is at most $((4k)^2/(4^a\gamma))^r$. Taking the union over the polynomially many $f$ thus gives $O((Z+p^{4r})b^{O_r(1)}\gamma^{-r}4^{-ar})$ usable partners reaching the next stage. The next cumulative cost to the $r$th power is $4^{(a+1)r} b^{O_r(1)}Y^{O(r)}$. Sum over $O_r(b)$ stages and normalize by at least $\gg Z/\log Z$ partners, also valid for either restricted population. The pointwise finder always has the separate $\sqrt p\,b^{O(1)}$ factorial-block fallback. In the classical sign argument its exceptional normalized $r$th-power contribution is $O(b^{O_r(1)}p^{r/2}/\sqrt Z)$, bounded by $b^{O_r(1)}(1+p^{4r}/Z)$. [Binary shifts and tail proof](untwisted-legendre-partners.md#partner-bits-and-time-moments)

## Finite checks

The replay exhausts every monic quintic modulo 3, 5 and 7. Of the models, 12, 200 and 1,176 respectively had odd trace and order twice an odd integer. Each had factor degrees 2 and 3. The Tate sign agreed with independent Cantor multiplication for all 4,634 marked $x$ values giving a point, covering both signs. The three normalized-chart orbit counts were 54, 500 and 2,058, and every orbit had the predicted $p-1$ members. For additional toy primes it certified 150 composite gcd discrepancies across five different partner factorization patterns and produced 15 integer-only splits, 12 by Cantor arithmetic. These checks use independent PARI Frobenius polynomials for the local orders. [Exact replay](../experiments/quadratic-cubic-genus-two.gp)
