# Independent checks of the two-thirds factoring argument

I checked the two new contour estimates, the conditional congruence cases from Cogentic Lemma 9.5, and the numerical scales transferring these inputs to Proposition 10.1. The claims reviewed here are in the [smooth-window proof](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md), the [two-thirds reduction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md), and [Cogentic, §§8–10](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf). The analytic input is Dirichlet GRH over the rationals. [Alpöge](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Sutherland's verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

**The contour.** The prime band starts at \(\sqrt B\). With \(\delta=D_0\log L/\log B\), moving the truncated Perron line from the absolutely convergent half-plane to \(1/2+\delta/2\) gains \(L^{-D_0/4}\) in a character sum at \(\sigma\ge1/2+\delta\). All losses from the horizontal and vertical integrals, the logarithmic derivative and the removed prime powers are polynomial in \(L\) under the stated logarithmic bounds. For the general Gaussian formula take \(D_0\) large enough for both the modulus and the height: \(q\le Y\), \(u,\log B\le L^C\) give \(\log q\le L^{2C}\), and \(\log(B^4R^2)=O(L^C)\). In particular the constant in the first lemma can be chosen for exponent \(2C+1\) at this application. The subtraction of the two endpoints makes the Perron integrand regular at zero. The untreated principal pole gives the integral over the prime band; when its imaginary part falls outside the contour the integral is itself bounded by the horizontal error.

On the new curved contour one has \(B^{1-\sigma(t)}/|t|\le e^\lambda\), and the linear Euler sum from the principal character is \(\ll e^\lambda/\log B=o(u)\) by integration by parts. Before the contour reaches \(1/2+\delta\), its second independent gain is the exact identity \(Y^{\sigma(t)-\alpha}=|t|^{-u}\). Thus the integral over that segment is \(O(Y^{\alpha-1}e^{o(u)}/(u-1))\), compared with the saddle mass \(\asymp Y^{\alpha-1}e^{(1+o(1))u}/(\sqrt u\log B)\). The remaining vertical segment gives the stated absolute error. At height \(B^4R^2\) the pointwise Gevrey estimate in the proof of source Lemma 8.4(iv) dominates even the crude product bound \(\exp(O(\sqrt B))\), uniformly on the connecting horizontal lines. This supports the use of the formula when \(\log R\) exceeds \(u\). [Original compact cutoff, pp. 42–43](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=42)

**Two marks.** Write \(x=u_0+au_1\), \(y=u_0+a'u_1\), \(\Delta=a'-a\), and impose a root condition \(u_0+ru_1=c\pmod p\). At fixed \(x\), a hit \(r=a\pmod p\) tests \(x=c\pmod p\) and leaves the slope free. A miss fixes \(u_1=(c-x)/(r-a)\pmod p\). Modulo \(p^k\), the possible \(y\) are then a single class of modulus \(\gcd(\Delta,p^k)\) or \(\gcd(p\Delta,p^k)\), respectively, each with constant multiplicity. These statements multiply by CRT over distinct root primes. If a prescribed prime divides both forced values, the clean condition \(p^2\nmid\Delta\) removes it from the additional difference modulus after dividing out the prescribed factor. For an unprescribed \(p\mid\Delta\) the two polynomial values agree mod \(p\). The excluded primes \(d<p\le\sqrt B\) with \(p\nmid(a-m)\) consequently contribute \(p/(p-1)\), giving precisely \(\gamma(a,a')\). Primes in the smooth band dividing the difference cost \(O(p^{-\alpha_s})\) in the exceptional class, by dividing a squarefree value by \(p\); an auxiliary nonzero class costs \(O(1/p)\). The clean local comparisons allow both losses. At primes dividing \(P_0|a'-m|\), the enforced coprimality of the original polynomial value is essential. The conditioning respects it on dividing the preassigned factors. [Source Lemmas 9.4–9.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=48)

**Transfer to rank.** In the displayed fixed-constant schedule of the two-thirds proof, the extra coprime progression moduli used in removing bad classes have logarithm at most \((2+o(1))V\). Dividing a class, its mean and its width by the same common factor preserves \(q/S\le1\). The Gaussian absolute remainder \(\exp(-49000V)\) therefore absorbs both inverse one-point saddle masses, together \(\exp((68+o(1))H)\). The rough two-point losses grow on the smaller \(H\) scale. For archimedean rank the diagonal target variation is \((3+o(1))H\), while the full off-diagonal operator norm is at most \((2/3+o(1))H\). The inverse coordinate change eliminating the two norm identities loses at most a polynomial in \(L\). These estimates leave strict positive rank, the condition Lemma 7.4(3) needs. This review covers these transfers under the stated model. The modified smooth-window and complexity theorems have not been independently machine checked.
