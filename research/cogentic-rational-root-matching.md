# Exact square matching across independent evaluation bases

Field squares from different common arguments can be combined whenever their **integer evaluations multiply to a rational square**. The marks may differ in every field. The decision uses polynomial bit time once local square bases and their reduced roots are available. It includes public witnesses from ordinary integer squares. On a fixed library of explicit quadratic fields with one-third-size mark grids, obtaining the local bases and deciding this larger domain together have unconditional one-third cost. Existence of a productive match for every input is open. ## The exact kernel

For each field choose its own simple evaluation at $m_i\bmod N$ and its own finite signed list of unit marks. For each column of a complete field-local square basis, retain the integer $E_j$ obtained by evaluating its signed word using the integer $m_i$, and one root residue $r_j$ with $r_j^2\equiv E_j\pmod N$. Allow additional specified rational-unit witnesses $(E_j,r_j)$ obeying the same check. A rational unit here means a reduced fraction with numerator and denominator both coprime to $N$. For ordinary grid marks the labels are nonzero integers. Local basis words may overlap and labels need not be distinct.

Take the binary matrix with columns $[E_j]\in\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}$. A sign row and a gcd-free basis of the absolute numerators times denominators suffice. Pairwise coprime nonsquare atoms give independent rows because each has an odd valuation at a prime confined to that atom. Square atoms give zero rows. Gcd refinement, integer square roots, and binary elimination have polynomial cost in the explicit data. For example the sum of bit lengths of the distinct atoms decreases, up to using the potential $\sum\log_2 a$, by at least one at each nontrivial gcd split. [Existing gcd-free implementation](../experiments/cogentic-quadratic-square-kernel.py)

For each kernel basis vector $x$, compute
\[
 D(x)=\sqrt{\prod_{j:x_j=1}E_j}\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\qquad
 z(x)=\prod_{j:x_j=1}r_j/D(x)\pmod N .
\]
Both numerator and denominator of $D(x)$ are units. The square of $z(x)$ is one. The map $x\mapsto[z(x)]$ modulo the two global signs is linear. When two supports overlap, multiplying their root products introduces the common factors $r_j^2=E_j\bmod N$. The positive rational square roots introduce their absolute values, so these corrections agree up to a global sign. Within a field, changing from basis products to the actual local square word removes even powers of the marks. Their evaluated half-product occurs on both sides, which proves completeness for the stated domain. Negative exponents and arbitrary choices of signs for local roots give the same normalized class. A square match producing a divisor exists if and only if some kernel basis vector gives $z\not\equiv\pm1$. Then $\gcd(z-1,N)$ is proper for odd $N$, including nonsquarefree $N$.

Writing the full product for an individual kernel vector uses no more bits than the sum of the bit lengths of its labels. On a library of $\exp(O(W))$ explicit quadratic fields and marks, with degrees fixed and defining coefficients and bases of polynomial bit length in $\log N$, the local quadratic kernels, the expanded basis labels, and the rational decision all take $\exp(O(W))$ bit work, where $W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$.

## Arbitrary degree chart libraries

The explicit chart adapter takes an odd modulus $N$ and charts with a monic irreducible integer polynomial $P_i$, an integer $m_i$ with $P_i(m_i)=0\bmod N$, and a finite list of integer pairs $(a,b)$. A proper gcd from a derivative, marked evaluation, or root denominator provides an immediate factor. Marks with evaluation zero modulo $N$ are omitted from the unit pool. The [arbitrary degree signed square kernel](cogentic-order-square-kernel.md) takes the unit marks $a-bX$ and a separate $-1$ column. Its exact roots are reduced directly if their power-basis denominators are units. If the denominator is a multiple of $N$ and $P_i'(m_i)$ is a unit, the integral numerator $P_i'(X)\beta\bmod P_i$ clears the root $\beta$ and can be divided by $P_i'(m_i)$ modulo $N$. If both the denominator and derivative are multiples of $N$, the adapter records an unresolved chart and still tests every available generator. A negative result is complete only when every chart's local square basis is represented.

The output includes the local basis masks in the original mark coordinates, exact roots, signed evaluation labels and modular residues. It also gives the rational matrix rank and a basis of matches with their normalized roots. A complete negative certificate has a full local square basis in every chart, a rational nullity equal to the number of returned matches, and only $\pm1$ on those matches. If the explicit input size is $S$ and the residual local ranks are $r_i$, the total bit cost is $\operatorname{poly}(S)\,2^{\max_i r_i}$. Each local square search contributes at most $D_i2^{r_i}$ tests for initial dimension $D_i$. Rational matching and verification of the returned root and rank data use polynomial work once the local kernels have been obtained. [Chart adapter and replay](../experiments/cogentic-rational-root-matching.py)

Run `python3 -B experiments/cogentic-rational-root-matching.py --charts library.json` with SageMath available. The JSON object has integer `n` and a list `charts`, each with `m`, `field` in ascending coefficient order, and `points` as pairs. An optional top-level `primes` list restricts the ideal-square kernel at the real places and the named rational primes before recovering exact roots, reusing the restricted basis directly. Each restriction with input dimension $D$ and removed rank $r$ costs at most $D2^r$ local tests. The remaining global bound uses the residual rank after these restrictions. For $N=93$, use $P=X^3+10X^2+33X+49$ at $m=1$ with mark $16-9X$. The field square $(X^2+5X+4)^2$ equals that mark modulo $P$, with label 7 and residue $\pm10$. Match it to the quadratic at base 2 in the next section to split 93. Replacing the quadratic by $X^2+35X+258$ at base 3 with mark $31-X=(X+17)^2$ in that field preserves the label 28. The second root becomes $\pm20$, so the rank-one rational matrix has a matching line whose normalized residue is a global sign. This gives a complete negative certificate despite a nonzero rational nullity. The replay also covers overlapping basis masks, an unresolved chart, and a cubic root whose denominator exposes 5 modulo 895, and derivative clearing for a cubic root with denominator 15 modulo 15.

## Joining a sheared grid to arbitrary public witnesses

The bounded sieve on a sheared evaluation grid strips primes through the determinant bound. Any two remaining grid tails are coprime or equal. Arbitrary additional integers may share only part of such a tail. Treating those partial overlaps as independent rows would discard valid matches. [Sheared-grid determinant bound](cogentic-sheared-local-squares.md)

The combined encoder strips the same bounded primes from every label. It omits square tails. Among the remaining distinct large tails, retain for gcd refinement precisely those having nontrivial gcd with the product of the absolute auxiliary labels. This includes every nonsquare auxiliary tail. Every untouched grid tail is coprime to all auxiliary labels and to the other distinct grid tails. It can keep its original private row. On the retained list use the ordinary rational gcd-free algorithm, then replace each old tail bit by its new class vector. This gives the exact joint rational kernel without factoring any auxiliary integer. At most the bit length of the auxiliary product many distinct grid tails can meet it, since those tails are pairwise coprime and each intersection consumes a distinct prime divisor of the product. The work is polynomial in the explicit sizes and the bounded sieve limit. Its use for public witnesses requires no numerical upper bound on their unfactored residuals beyond their input bit lengths. [Joint encoder](../experiments/cogentic-quadratic-grid-norms.py)

## A match between two different bases

Set $N=93$. Use one primitive marked element in each of two real quadratic fields. Both defining polynomials evaluate to exactly $N$.

| Polynomial | Base | Mark | Integer evaluation | Root residue |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| $P=X^2+20X+72$ | 1 | $9-2X$ | 7 | 10 |
| $Q=X^2+19X+51$ | 2 | $30-X$ | 28 | 11 |

The discriminants are $112,157$, both positive nonsquares. The derivatives $22,23$ and the mark values are units. In both fields the mark equals $(X+9)^2$. Each signed singleton pool has precisely the one-dimensional local square space generated by its marked element, since the field is real. The distinct labels 7 and 28 have identical nontrivial rational square class, and neither admits a match alone. Their product is $14^2$. The normalized root is $110/14=61\bmod93$, giving $\gcd(60,93)=3$. Exact integer-label coincidence has zero kernel on these pools.

## Extra public roots and small choices of lift

A deterministic public witness is $(u^2-kN,u)$ for any specified $u,k$ with $u$ a unit. Its integer label may have a different square class from other lifts of the same residue. For the preceding example $u=\lceil\sqrt{93}\rceil=10$, $k=1$, supplies the label 7. Adding that one public witness to the second field already gives the factor. Such witnesses may be attached to any finite library and decided by the same exact kernel. The calculation covers exactly the supplied generators and the proposed rational normalization.

Alternatively, assign each local generator **one fixed lift** $E'_j\equiv E_j\pmod N$. Its reduced root remains valid, and the kernel decision is exact for products of those fixed generator labels. For $N=65$, consider $P=X^2+21X+43$ at $m_1=1$ with mark $57-X$, and $Q=X^2+23X+15$ at $m_2=2$ with mark $85-3X$. Both marks are $(X+10)^2$ in their fields, both polynomials evaluate to 65, and derivatives $23,27$ are units. The evaluations $56,79$ have independent rational square classes. Replacing the second evaluation by its lift $79-65=14$ gives $56\cdot14=28^2$ with normalized root $132/28=14\bmod65$. The difference gcd is 13.

Trying a prescribed list of full lift assignments costs one decision per assignment. Admitting all lifts as independent generators defines a larger valid domain, including products that use two different lifts of the same generator. An at-most-one-lift constraint per generator is a different decision problem. With replaced labels the exact criterion concerns the chosen basis generators, since changes of local basis can alter the lifted labels.

The [exact replay](../experiments/cogentic-rational-root-matching.py) checks the examples, rational-unit normalizations and then compares the basis decision and its normalized signs to all 5,888 subsets of eight small modular witness lists. The field identities in both examples were also checked directly in PARI.
