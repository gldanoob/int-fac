# Exact square matching across independent evaluation bases

Field squares from different common arguments can be combined whenever their **integer evaluations multiply to a rational square**. The marks may differ in every field. The decision uses polynomial bit time once local square bases and their reduced roots are available. It includes public witnesses from ordinary integer squares. On a fixed library of explicit quadratic fields with one-third-size mark grids, obtaining the local bases and deciding this larger domain together have unconditional one-third cost. Existence of a productive match for every input is open. ## The exact kernel

For each field choose its own simple evaluation at $m_i\bmod N$ and its own finite signed list of unit marks. For each column of a complete field-local square basis, retain the integer $E_j$ obtained by evaluating its signed word using the integer $m_i$, and one root residue $r_j$ with $r_j^2\equiv E_j\pmod N$. Allow additional specified rational-unit witnesses $(E_j,r_j)$ obeying the same check. A rational unit here means a reduced fraction with numerator and denominator both coprime to $N$. For ordinary grid marks the labels are nonzero integers. Local basis words may overlap and labels need not be distinct.

Take the binary matrix with columns $[E_j]\in\mathbb Q^\times/\mathbb Q^{\times2}$. A sign row and a gcd-free basis of the absolute numerators times denominators suffice. Pairwise coprime nonsquare atoms give independent rows because each has an odd valuation at a prime confined to that atom. Square atoms give zero rows. Gcd refinement, integer square roots, and binary elimination have polynomial cost in the explicit data. For example the sum of bit lengths of the distinct atoms decreases, up to using the potential $\sum\log_2 a$, by at least one at each nontrivial gcd split. [Existing gcd-free implementation](../experiments/cogentic-quadratic-square-kernel.py)

For each kernel basis vector $x$, compute
\[
 D(x)=\sqrt{\prod_{j:x_j=1}E_j}\in\mathbb Q_{>0},\qquad
 z(x)=\prod_{j:x_j=1}r_j/D(x)\pmod N .
\]
Both numerator and denominator of $D(x)$ are units. The square of $z(x)$ is one. The map $x\mapsto[z(x)]$ modulo the two global signs is linear. When two supports overlap, multiplying their root products introduces the common factors $r_j^2=E_j\bmod N$. The positive rational square roots introduce their absolute values, so these corrections agree up to a global sign. The same overlap argument within a field shows completeness for the stated domain: changing from basis products to the actual local square word removes even powers of the marks, with exactly their evaluated half-product available on both sides. Negative exponents and arbitrary choices of signs for local roots give the same normalized class. A square match producing a divisor exists if and only if some kernel basis vector gives $z\not\equiv\pm1$. Then $\gcd(z-1,N)$ is proper for odd $N$, including nonsquarefree $N$.

Writing the full product for an individual kernel vector uses no more bits than the sum of the bit lengths of its labels. On a library of $\exp(O(W))$ explicit quadratic fields and marks, with degrees fixed and defining coefficients and bases of polynomial bit length in $\log N$, the local quadratic kernels, the expanded basis labels, and the rational decision all take $\exp(O(W))$ bit work, where $W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. For other fields the matching cost has the same polynomial dependence on supplied bases; the cost of constructing them is separate.

## A match between two different bases

Set $N=93$. Use one primitive marked element in each of two real quadratic fields. Both defining polynomials evaluate to exactly $N$.

| Polynomial | Base | Mark | Integer evaluation | Root residue |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| $P=X^2+20X+72$ | 1 | $9-2X$ | 7 | 10 |
| $Q=X^2+19X+51$ | 2 | $30-X$ | 28 | 11 |

The discriminants are $112,157$, both positive nonsquares. The derivatives $22,23$ and the mark values are units. In both fields the mark equals $(X+9)^2$. Each signed singleton pool has precisely the one-dimensional local square space generated by its marked element, since the field is real. The distinct labels 7 and 28 have identical nontrivial rational square class, and neither admits a match alone. Their product is $14^2$. The normalized root is $110/14=61\bmod93$, giving $\gcd(60,93)=3$. Exact integer-label coincidence has zero kernel on these pools.

## Extra public roots and small choices of lift

A deterministic public witness is $(u^2-kN,u)$ for any specified $u,k$ with $u$ a unit. Its integer label may have a different square class from other lifts of the same residue. For the preceding example $u=\lceil\sqrt{93}\rceil=10$, $k=1$, supplies the label 7. Adding that one public witness to the second field already gives the factor. Such witnesses may be attached to any finite library and decided by the same exact kernel. The calculation covers exactly the supplied generators and the proposed rational normalization.

Alternatively, assign each local generator **one fixed lift** $E'_j\equiv E_j\pmod N$. Its reduced root remains valid, and the kernel decision is exact for products of those fixed generator labels. For $N=65$, consider $P=X^2+21X+43$ at $m_1=1$ with mark $57-X$, and $Q=X^2+23X+15$ at $m_2=2$ with mark $85-3X$. Both marks are $(X+10)^2$ in their fields, both polynomials evaluate to 65, and derivatives $23,27$ are units. The evaluations $56,79$ have independent rational square classes. Replacing the second evaluation by its lift $79-65=14$ gives $56\cdot14=28^2$ with normalized root $132/28=14\bmod65$. The difference gcd is 13.

Trying a prescribed list of full lift assignments costs one decision per assignment. Admitting all lifts as independent generators defines a larger valid domain, including products that use two different lifts of the same generator. An at-most-one-lift constraint per generator is a different decision problem. With replaced labels, the exact criterion concerns the chosen basis generators; arbitrary changes of local basis need not preserve those lifted labels.

The [integer replay](../experiments/cogentic-rational-root-matching.py) checks the examples and compares the basis decision and its normalized signs to all 5,888 subsets of eight small modular witness lists. The field identities in both examples were also checked directly in PARI.
