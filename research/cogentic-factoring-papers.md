# Two different worst-case factoring bounds in the November 25 Cogentic release

Google Research commit `4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a` contains two factoring manuscripts. Put $b=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor+1$ and $L_N[\alpha,c]=\exp((c+o(1))(\ln N)^\alpha(\ln\ln N)^{1-\alpha})$ for an asymptotic upper bound. The papers state the following results on classical uniform machines. [Pinned research index](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers)

| Manuscript | Input, output and worst-case guarantee |
| --- | --- |
| *Unconditional Integer Factorization in Time* $L_N[1/3,O(1)]$, Theorem 1.1 | For every $N\ge2$, a Las Vegas machine outputs its complete certified prime factorization. The expectation over its private fair coins is at most $L_N[1/3,105]$ bit operations at each input. Workspace on every coin tape is bounded by $L_N[1/3,40]$. The paper gives effective constants and uses classical unconditional results. [Theorem and PDF](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf#page=2) |
| *Unconditional Deterministic Subexponential Integer Factoring and Quadratic Nonresidue Construction*, Theorem 1.1 | For every composite $N\ge4$, the deterministic algorithm returns a proper divisor in $O(\exp(3(\ln N)^{3/4}))$ bit operations, with no unproved number-theoretic hypotheses. In input bits this is $2^{O(b^{3/4})}$. Deterministic primality testing and recursive splitting give complete factorization with the same bit-length exponent. [Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=4) |

The deterministic claim improves the asymptotic rate in the leaderboard's public kernel-checked result $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log b})}$. Indeed $b^{3/4}=o(b/(\log b)^s)$ at every fixed $s$. The pinned Google directories contain a PDF and a README each. The authors report completed Mathlib-only Lean developments, using v4.35.0 for the randomized paper and v4.33.0 for the deterministic paper, with the proof sources to appear separately. This repository has checked the theorem statements and model against the pinned PDFs. Its local tests and the leaderboard's existing verifier do not independently certify those manuscripts. The deterministic manuscript's introduction also overlooks the earlier publicly checked deterministic subexponential result when describing the prior literature. ## Connection to our deterministic two-thirds claim

The reusable result is Theorem 1.2(a) of the deterministic companion. Our fixed four-coordinate search can return a proper divisor directly. If it returns orders, the driver forms an even integer $M\ge2$ by saturation. Lean proves that a full-rank tuple for each dyadic target makes $p-1$ divide this particular $M$ for every prime $p\mid N$. The driver's preliminary root scan handles every perfect power $x^j$ with $x,j\ge2$, including composite bases. The surviving input and $M$ therefore satisfy every input condition of Theorem 1.2(a). The proof uses our existing deduction of dyadic prime supply from the stated Dirichlet critical-line theorem. [Published Theorem 1.2(a)](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=4) · [Lean reduction](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/CompanionReduction.lean)

Write $V(N)=(\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}$. The actual search output satisfies $\ln M\le\exp(131105 V(N))$ for $N\ge64$. With the constants $C_1,C_{\rm split}$ from the paper, Lean bounds the published cost $C_1\exp(C_{\rm split}\sqrt{\ln N}\,\ln\ln(N+16))(\ln M)(\ln N)^2$ by $\exp((C_1+12C_{\rm split}+131117)V(N))$. Complete factorization preserves the exponent. If the charge per cofactor is eventually at most $\exp(DV)$, the tree costs at most $\exp((D+\varepsilon)V)$ for each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, including calls on small cofactors. [Splitter cost](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/CompanionSplitCost.lean) · [Recursion and prime multiplicities](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/CompanionFactorCost.lean)

The endpoint `factorization_of_box_rank` applies this calculation to the recursive driver. For every $N\ge2$ it returns primes with the correct product and multiplicities. Under the per-target rank hypothesis, the search succeeds whenever the driver's guards reach it on a sufficiently large input. If the remaining charge per cofactor is at most $\exp(DV)$ eventually, the combined tree charge is eventually at most $\exp((\max(D,C_1+12C_{\rm split}+131117)+\varepsilon)V)$ for every fixed $\varepsilon>0$. [Complete conditional theorem and executable guards](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/CompanionFactorization.lean)

The Lean reduction takes the splitter's published input, output and cost guarantee as a parameter. At the checked Google revision the factoring directory contains the paper and README. Importing the announced Lean proof would supply the splitter theorem once its program and cost model are matched to this interface. Our remaining mathematical obligation is to produce verified tuples for a set of distinct targets $S$ satisfying $3\beta|S|\ge2^\beta$ on each sufficiently large surviving input. The local extractor accepts the mixed row-dependency criterion proved in Lean. Our remaining computational obligation is to bound the elementary bit operations for the rest of each decision, including construction and search. Google's Theorem 1.3 supplies a qualifying divisor or exponent for our local splitter too, but its exponent-construction cost is $O(\exp(3(\ln N)^{3/4}))$. ## Where the running times come from

The randomized paper samples two compatible number fields and constructs enough relations by descending prime factors through progressively smaller smoothness cutoffs. The arithmetic stage reconstructs square roots using a 3-adic lift. A hidden unit $z$ enters the relation search only through $z^2\bmod N$. Conditional on the public transcript, the remaining CRT choices give a proper split with probability $1-2^{1-\omega(N)}$ in Lemma 5.3. A deterministic trial-division fallback guarantees termination on unlucky tapes. The fast expected time includes the recursive work factoring smaller integers during relation collection. In §6 the largest charged recursive call has argument at most $\sqrt N/2$ and the proof budgets its contribution at constant 82.56 plus 21.15 for the number of calls. [Definitions 4.5 and 6.1, Lemmas 5.2–5.3 and proof of Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf)

The deterministic paper constructs a divisor or an even multiple $\widetilde M$ of $p-1$ for **every** prime $p\mid N$, with $\ln\widetilde M\le\exp((\ln N)^{0.65})$. It searches over explicitly enumerated perturbations of two number-field polynomials. When their relations have the required rank, the algorithm obtains a certified order multiple for a small prime base $\ell$, verifying $\ell^{E_\ell}=1\bmod N$. Enough such bases generate a subgroup of bounded index at each unknown $p$; multiplying by the small prime powers up to the index bound supplies $\widetilde M$. The separate order-splitting theorem then finds the divisor. The exhaustive parameter search in §§8–11 costs $\exp((4\ln2+o(1))(\ln N)^{3/4})$ calls up to a lower-order factor, and $4\ln2<3$. For full recursive factorization, this strict slack also absorbs the polynomial number of nodes while retaining the displayed constant 3. [Lemmas 7.5 and 11.1, §11.2–3](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=55)

The deterministic companion changes the reference point for continued worst-case research. The linked randomized result also supersedes Lenstra–Pomerance as a claimed per-input expected upper bound. The deterministic arithmetic mean in this repository quantifies over the integers $N$ at a given size; the private-coin expectation in the linked paper applies separately at every $N$. [Deterministic mean proof](sharp-elliptic-mean.md) · [Lenstra–Pomerance](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-1992-1137100-0)

There is a notation discrepancy in the deterministic companion's Theorem 1.2. Its explicit elementary bound for a prime nonresidue is $\exp(O(\sqrt{\ln p}\,\ln\ln p))$. The definition printed in the paper gives $L_p[1/2,C]=\exp((C+o(1))\sqrt{\ln p\,\ln\ln p})$. The first display alone therefore does not establish the stated fixed-$C$ abbreviation. The argument above for general factoring uses the explicit elementary time expression. ## Building on the two extractors

Our deterministic construction keeps the companion's certificate-producing local order extraction (Lemma 7.5) and changes the search for polynomials that meet its hypotheses. The [fixed-parameter deduction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) gives the manuscript-level all-input bound $\exp(O((\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}))$. Its new input is an [estimate for smooth numbers in short progressions](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) using the proved Dirichlet theorem. The [independent internal review](cogentic-two-thirds-audit.md) checks the contour shift, the two-point congruences and the transfer to rank. A second, [classical parameter choice](cogentic-two-thirds.md) gives $\exp(O((\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^4))$ using the paper's original zero-density method. Both are paper deductions beyond the authors' stated theorem. The [one-base gcd extraction](cogentic-dense-exponents.md) and [finite-coin sampling argument](cogentic-finite-coins.md) can also use the original published parameters.

For the linked Las Vegas algorithm, [one-sided smoothness tests and sparse linear algebra](cogentic-smoothness-screen.md) give a separate deduction of expected $L_N[1/3,36]$ on every input, retaining the stated space bound. The last step needs CRT signs that remain random after the relation search. The [square-root support calculation](cogentic-sealed-roots.md) identifies precisely what an enumerable replacement for those coins would need to supply.

A [second derivation of the progression law](cogentic-marked-smooth-windows.md) selects the number of prime factors in the Euler product. A [two-block estimate](cogentic-two-block-characters.md) also sharpens the classical character argument. For the new deterministic schedule, a [conditional score](cogentic-conditional-score.md) and [exact square decomposition for a Gaussian](cogentic-batched-affine-fibers.md) reduce the search to one perturbation width times lower-order factors. The resulting paper coefficient is $(32/3)^{1/3}=2.20128\ldots$ in the standard $L_N[2/3,C]$ notation. The elementary [two-parameter change](cogentic-parameter-retuning.md) gives an intermediate $2^{O(b^{18/25})}$ bound from the original classical estimates.

The [two-quadratic-field prototype](cogentic-quadratic-order.md) constructs certificates from actual smooth norms and exact rational linear algebra. At cutoff 71 it returns 137 for 10,001 and 151 for 15,251. Matching smooth marks by their evaluated residues also returns 103 for 10,403 and splits 87 of 90 separate semiprimes at the same cutoff. Restricting to the same mark in both fields split 24 of those 90. The [one-intercept construction](cogentic-one-intercept.md) reduces the high-degree extractor's randomness to one large integer, and a [randomized bit-cost analysis](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md) gives the separate paper bound $L_N[1/3,(32/3)^{2/3}]$ from that deterministic companion. The full worst-case proof uses the high-degree fields.

The [streamed extraction procedure](cogentic-extractor-space.md) gives a deterministic time–space tradeoff. At the best current paper time coefficient, the workspace is $L_N[1/3,(4/3)^{2/3}]$ bits. The [finite-advice deduction](cogentic-extractor-advice.md) quantifies the remaining choice needed to turn the shorter random input into a uniform worst-case search.

The [quadratic prototype’s rational-side variant](cogentic-quadratic-order.md#partial-rational-factorizations) also splits larger inputs where distinct marks cannot share a residue. The [collision bound](quadratic-mark-collisions.md) explains why short products of marks typically do not supply new congruences at subpower height. An [averaged common-divisor estimate](cogentic-space-exponents.md) trades more time for any fixed smaller positive workspace exponent, down to a separate quasipolynomial-space schedule.

For the randomized search based on the deterministic companion, [projective marks](cogentic-homogeneous-marks.md) give a separate coefficient 4.6591 in the exponent $1/3$. A [variable multiple of the input](cogentic-variable-multiple-lattice.md) and a more economical count in the mark grid give 3.8460. These are paper deductions. The [projective audit](cogentic-two-thirds-audit.md) includes exact checks on conditioned classes. The [hidden sparse Gram kernel](cogentic-hidden-gram-kernel.md) explains a limitation of entrywise concentration when seeking fewer relations.

The [deterministic sampler](cogentic-universal-score-sampler.md) gives an $L_N[1/3,O(1)]$ fast path on all but $\exp(O(b^{2/3}(\log b)^{1/3}))$ inputs of bit length $b$, including complete factorization. This implies the same exponent for every fixed arithmetic time moment. Its exhaustive uniform preparation also gives a reusable short string for all inputs of a given bounded length. These are distinct from the authors' private-coin expected result for each input. The [Farey-mark selector](cogentic-projective-selection.md) gives a deterministic worst-case schedule with coefficient $(8/3)^{1/3}$. A separate [quartic CM implementation](cogentic-cm-torus.md) produces norm-one order certificates with finite valuations alone.
