# A single-input descent schedule with coefficient 2.268

For one integer from scratch, the following schedule gives a manuscript-level expected bit-time bound $L_N[1/3,2.268]$ under Dirichlet GRH and the analytic results of the pinned KeyBreak preprint. The two changes are to size the first search box for the prime being eliminated and to change the internal descent ratio once smaller cutoffs make algebraic recognition cheaper. The field, primes, private attempts, unsuccessful descents, characters, matrix, and extraction are all paid for on the current input. The [staged private search](keybreak-private-band-stages.md) and [single-input extraction and first-split pass](keybreak-private-shape-work.md) supply the other parts of the construction. [KeyBreak, Appendices G and H](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105)

## Integer schedule

Use the [joint schedule's notation](keybreak-joint-schedule.md): $n$ is the input bit length, $m_b$ the bit length of $n$, $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, $H=D\log\log N$, $M=\lfloor N^{1/d}\rfloor$. The decimals below are exact.

| $\delta$ | $\kappa$ | $\beta$ | $\alpha$ | $\gamma$ | $h_T$ | $\lambda_T$ | $\lambda_H$ | $\lambda_L$ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
|1.133|.93|.715|.5005|.914|1.058|2.534|2.4|2.895|

Take the least odd $d\ge3$ with $d^3m_b\ge\delta^3n$. Put $t=dm_b$, $k_0=\lceil\kappa d\rceil$, $K=2k_0$, $e_0=\lfloor(n-5)/(2k_0)\rfloor-1$, $e_B=\lceil\beta t\rceil$, $B=2^{e_B}$ and $T_H=\lfloor h_Tdt\rfloor$. For a box exponent $e$ and a ratio $\lambda$ write
$$
v(e)=\lceil\alpha e+\gamma e_B\rceil,\quad V(e)=2^{v(e)},\qquad
c(e,\lambda)=\max\{e_B,\lceil(\alpha e+\gamma e_B+T_H/d)/\lambda\rceil\}.
$$
For $j=0,\ldots,127$ put $z_j=.1+.9j/127$, $a_j=z_j^2$, and $E_j=\lfloor a_j e_0\rfloor$. A prime in the selected dyadic bands near $2^{E_j}$ gets the top box $V(E_j)$ and child exponent $c(E_j,\lambda_T)$. Use one field with the source's Gaussian width $\sigma=V(e_0)^2n^{10}$. Every child above $B$ enters the **same** first internal level with public upper exponent $e_1=c(e_0,\lambda_T)$. At internal level $i$, if $e_i^2>dt^2$ take $\lambda_i=\lambda_H$; otherwise take $\lambda_i=\lambda_L$. Set $e_{i+1}=c(e_i,\lambda_i)$. The program handles terminal primes and passes a child $q\le2^{e_{i+1}}$ through unchanged before any active search. A nonterminal $q>B$ therefore reaches the unique level with $2^{e_{i+1}}<q\le2^{e_i}$. Its active box is $V(e_i)$ independent of which top class produced it. [KeyBreak §H.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=128)

## Supply and propagation for one field

Put $A=\delta^{-3}$, $b=\delta\beta=.810095$, $y_0=1/(2\kappa)$, $v_0=\alpha y_0$, $y_1=v_0/\lambda_T$, $v_1=\alpha y_1$. The first constant is the limit of $\log_2 M/(dt)$; the last four are log sizes relative to $\log M$. The limiting coefficient of $\log_2(\sigma M)/(dt)$ is $A(1+2v_0)$, strictly between 1.057 and 1.058. This leaves room for the polynomial factors, roundings and $2^{O(d)}$ in (G.57). For every local top or internal box this gives $\log_2(2X)\le T_H+d(\alpha e+\gamma e_B)$ eventually, and the child recurrence gives an upper algebraic smoothness argument $\lambda d$. The lower comparison is $\gg d$ because the padded log height is of order $dt+d(\alpha e+\gamma e_B)$ after parent or child divisions, and the adjacent logarithms are comparable. Clamping preserves these comparisons. The largest raw algebraic value is bounded using $2(T_H+d v(e_0))\le n-1$, since $dv(e_0)/n\to v_0<1/2$. Rational values remain below $N$. The finite program tests the original scalar and geometry guards with these actual local parameters. [KeyBreak (G.25)–(G.26), (G.56)–(G.58), (H.1)–(H.2)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)

- **Internal boxes.** With $S=\alpha+\gamma$, the low recurrence has slope below one and $(\lambda_L-S)\beta-h_T=.0005575>0$. Hence its fixed point lies below $e_B$ by order $t$. The high recurrence applies only when $e_i/t>\sqrt d$, so it contracts by a constant factor there. The chain strictly descends to $e_B$ in $O(\log D)$ steps. All boxes exceed $2B$ and have $V(e_i)^2\ge2^{e_i}$ since $S>1$ and $2\alpha>1$. The high transition has a $\gg e_i$ log gap between box and child. The low transition has gap at least $[((\lambda_L-1)S\beta-h_T)/\lambda_L+o(1)]t$ when unclamped and $(S-1)e_B$ at the clamp. These exceed the constant gap for $V_i\ge33Y_{i+1}$.
- **Top boxes.** Use only the complete bands from (H.18) for each $E_j$, so $\log p/\log M=a_jy_0+o(1)$ for every accepted parent in class $j$. The box and child log ratios tend to $a_jv_0$ and $a_jy_1$. Thus every selected $p$ exceeds both its denominator interval and its local child ceiling, and the denominator exceeds the child by a fixed power of $M$. This distinguishes the primes in the rational convolution and excludes a child above $B$ from the rational numerator. With $\epsilon=.01$ the worst (G.17) progression error has log exponent $(1+\epsilon)a_j y_1-(1/2-\epsilon)[1-(1-\alpha)a_jy_0]$. It increases in $a_j$ and is below $-1/4$ at one. In (G.12) even $(1+\epsilon)y_1-(1/2-\epsilon)<-1/4$ suffices internally, since the internal rational cofactor scale is at least a constant times $M$. In (G.18) and (G.53) use the local upper parent $2^{E_j}$, local $V$, and local child ceiling $y=2^{c(E_j,\lambda_T)}$. The factors $2^{E_j}/V^2,1/M,y/V^2,y/M$ are all negative powers of $M$ uniformly because $a_j\ge .01$. This dominates the inverse densities $\exp(O(H))$.
- **Propagation.** In each band the G.54 incidence reordering takes place entirely in its own box. In G.55 the local quotient of reference densities is comparable to $q^{\gamma_j}$ with $\gamma_j\log y=O(\log D)$ by the same saddle estimate. For $B<q\le y$ the local profile $q^{\gamma_j-1}\le D^{O(1)}/q\le D^{O(1)}B_1(q)$, using the global profile $B_1(q)=q^{\gamma_{\max}-1}$ with $\gamma_{\max}\ge0$. For $q>y$ there is zero incoming mass from that class. One may therefore majorize the first and second moments and the incoming failure integrals by the common first-level profile with polynomial loss. Specifically the aggregate rational failure uses $y/V^2+y/M$ before the comparison, and the correlated child-bad term uses Cauchy–Schwarz with the same global indicator and costs $D^{O(1)}\sqrt{\delta_1}$. The internal weights include the identity transitions in (G.40). The iteration in G.11 has $O(\log D)$ levels and its $(j_{\rm depth}+10)^3$ error exponent absorbs the polynomial losses. Markov and a union over the polynomially many selected dyadic bands give the simultaneous good-parent event for this one field. [KeyBreak (G.39)–(G.55)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)
- **Conditioning and means.** The largest congruence product remains $Y_1^3$, and $2v_0-3y_1>0$ covers it with the global Gaussian. For every box the lower log endpoint in (G.38) is at least $1.057dt+d(\alpha e+\gamma e_B)-O(e+e_B)$, while the separate coordinate period has log at most $.69dt+3(\alpha e+\gamma e_B)+O(1)$. With the same $\epsilon$, even the worst bottom comparison $(1+\epsilon).69-(1/2-\epsilon)(1.057+S\beta)$ is below $-1/25$. The negative slope of order $d$ absorbs the endpoint factor $4k$ and all extra $O(e+e_B)$ terms at larger $e$. Local top means have positive $\log M$ margins from $V^2/2^{E_j}$ and $V^2/y$. For an internal mean the rational loss in $(\log 2)t$ units is at most $(A+\alpha e/(dt)+\gamma\beta/d)/(3\beta)+o(1)$, as in the joint schedule. Even using $\lambda_L$ throughout for this mean lower bound, the coefficient of $e/t$ from $V^2/2^e$ minus the rational loss is $2\alpha-1-O(1/d)>0$. The bottom margin is $(2S-1)\beta-\lambda_L/3-A/(3\beta)>.02219>1/50$. This also controls the inverse density in (G.15); the band normalization costs only logarithmic factors. The remaining mesh, truncation and numerical comparisons of G.8–G.10 have the same lower-order losses. [KeyBreak Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)

## Private gates and costs

In the staged private test take $W=2\kappa\delta/3$, $h=9/8>2.0001/\sqrt{3\kappa\delta}$, $w_j=h(z_{j+1}-z_j)$ for $j<127$, $w_{127}=W-\sum_{j<127}a_jw_j$, and $f_j=a_jw_j/W$. Then $\sum f_j=1$, all the $f_j$ are positive and $\sum f_j/a_j<1.914<2$. At each cutoff verify partial factors of both coordinates, enforcing the staged prefix gates. At final verification also require every prime in its group's actual (H.18) window. Each such group uses $k_j\sim (f_j/a_j)k_0$ primes in complete dyadic bands within $o(E_j)$ of $E_j$. They are eventually disjoint and lie in their respective local parent ranges; one may take a first private floor $\lfloor.009 e_0\rfloor$. H.20 already counts only these good shapes, so this final restriction preserves the lower bound. The upper prefix bounds continue to hold since they count the larger set. Each failed private test and each failed search tree is charged using the same capped launch and stage counts as the [private-band construction](keybreak-private-band-stages.md).

In H.15 assign raw-trial and algebraic-invocation caps **per search type**. Write $s=.0001$. A type with algebraic loss $s_A$ and rational loss $s_R$ uses caps with coefficients $s_A+s_R+s$ and $s_A+s$, respectively, converting any rational upper coefficient $q$ to $2^{\lceil qt/\delta\rceil}$ with upward rounding. The H.4 induction uses only that request's H.16 success bounds. Set $s_A=\delta\lambda/3$ separately in each type. The top rational cofactor $MV(E_j)/p$ has worst log ratio $1-(1-\alpha)(.01)y_0$. Internally the high boxes use at most $MV(e_1)$, and every low box satisfies $e_i\le t\sqrt d=o(dt)$, hence $\log(MV_i)/\log M=1+o(1)$ uniformly. Dividing these log ratios by $3\delta b$ gives the rational losses in $H$ units. Low child log size is $o(\log M)$, so H.3 recognition costs $\exp(o(H))$ there. High children have maximum log ratio $\alpha y_1/\lambda_H$ relative to $M$. Use rational upper approximations for any square roots with the strict slack below.

| Work on this input | Coefficient including cap slack, upper rounded |
|---|---:|
| Private stage $j$, $b+\sum_{i\ge j}w_i+s+h z_j$ |2.266968|
| Top raw, $b+\delta\lambda_T/3+[1-(1-\alpha).01 y_0]/(3\delta b)+2s$ |2.129500|
| Top algebraic, $b+\delta\lambda_T/3+h\sqrt{\alpha/\lambda_T}+2s$ |2.267281|
| High raw, $b+\delta\lambda_H/3+(1+v_1)/(3\delta b)+2s$ |2.099170|
| High algebraic, $b+\delta\lambda_H/3+h\sqrt{\alpha^2/(\lambda_T\lambda_H)}+2s$ |1.945017|
| Low raw, $b+\delta\lambda_L/3+1/(3\delta b)+2s$ |2.266813|
| Low algebraic, $b+\delta\lambda_L/3+2s$ |1.903640|
| Original probe setup, fast Jacobi on retained rows |2.266 and 1.566|
| Binary elimination, exact square extraction |2.107 and 1.621|

The total number of searches in all types fits $\exp((b+s+o(1))H)$ by the common launch cap and subleading tree factor. Bounding each type's trial and recognition costs separately avoids charging the most expensive smoothness call at the highest invocation count from a different region. The [probe arithmetic](keybreak-fast-probe-arithmetic.md) charges all character primes to this input. Uniformly positive conditional epoch success, exact final checks, trial division between epochs and polynomially many recursive splits then give the stated expected bit bound. The [arithmetic replay](../experiments/keybreak-tiered-descent.py) checks the parameter inequalities. The analytic count, sampling and success arguments remain manuscript dependencies; this is not an end-to-end executable or a Lean theorem.

## Polynomial-bit probes and a 2.250 schedule

For the same single-input problem the [cumulative character sampler](keybreak-cumulative-character-probes.md) permits larger degree. The resulting manuscript bound is $L_N[1/3,2.250]$ under the same GRH and preprint inputs. The independently [audited short-list sampler](keybreak-polynomial-bit-probes.md) also fits. The arithmetic below charges the more expensive cumulative version. This improvement uses the KeyBreak private-root mechanism throughout. It is independent of the [Lee–Venkatesan square-congruence result](cogentic-mnfs-sign-map.md).

Use $\delta=1.33226$, $\kappa=.74246$, $\beta=.61135$, $\alpha=.5001$, $\gamma=.88185$, $h_T=.7078$, $\lambda_H=2.4$, $\lambda_L=2.54$. The $320$ private stages have $z_j=.041+.959j/319$, $a_j=z_j^2$, $h=1.16108$, and first private floor $\lfloor.0016e_0\rfloor$. Keep the allocation rule for $W,w_j,f_j$ and use $K=4k_0$. The limiting number of factors per coordinate divided by $k_0$ is $2.104307$ rounded up. Use this $K$ everywhere in the finite procedure, including individual search tolerances, top request counts, tree sizes and confidence budgets. Changing the cap by a constant factor preserves the logarithmic depth overhead and the private shape count; $k_0$ still specifies $e_0$. In the H.20 product estimate the actual multiplicities $k_j$ still determine every factorial and every band width. No acceptance probability is inferred from the larger cap alone.

Use the local top ratio $\lambda_T(a)=1+.86a$. The outgoing log size relative to $\log M$ is $a v_0/(1+.86a)$, increasing to $y_1=v_0/1.86$. Each ratio exceeds one with fixed positive margin on this finite grid, which separates that local child range from the local denominator. The common Gaussian uses the maximum top box, so the congruence guard is $3y_1<2v_0$, independent of whether a particular ratio is below $3/2$. The local $V^2$/parent and $V^2$/child margins stay positive. The global internal recursion and pass-through rule are as above. The actual height coefficient $.707745866\ldots$ lies inside $(.7077,.7078)$, and $A<.423$ gives a separate-period margin exceeding $.33$. The low fixed-point margin is $.000173867\ldots>0$.

Two substitutions in the concentration proof matter here. First the G.12 and G.17 cell errors need to be $o(1)$ for Lemma G.3. Their exponents of $M$ are respectively at most $-.30712$ and $-.14216$, with density and mesh corrections $M^{o(1)}$. Second, in G.6 and G.10 take the absolute mean threshold $\exp(.001\Lambda_d)$, where $\Lambda_d=t\log 2$. The bound at the bottom is $.001113477\ldots$ in these units. In the G.10 proof the diagonal contribution of the indicator is at most $1/\mu$ after normalizing by $\mu^2$, since $f^2\le f$. The proxy diagonal has the uniform density estimate used there and costs at most $\exp(O(A_{\rm tail}(\log D)^2))/\mu$ using bounded first moments, where $A_{\rm tail}=(j_{\rm depth}+10)^3$. Both are $o(D^{-4A_{\rm tail}})$ for the new fixed positive threshold. The off-diagonal conditional estimates and the two relative-density bounds of H.16 use the actual eligible populations and retain their preceding proofs. [KeyBreak G.6, G.12, G.17 and Proposition G.10](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=117)

Keep slack $.0001$ per cost cap. Both the algebraic loss and the largest outgoing top cutoff are bounded using $\lambda_T(1)=1.86$ for the respective maxima, while the top rational cost is bounded by its own worst cofactor. The [exact replay](../experiments/keybreak-compressed-probe-schedule.py) checks these inequalities, the cumulative sampler cost, and the rounded transitions and 320 groups at each of two symbolic input lengths. The largest coefficients are $2.249849956$ for low raw trials, $2.249830980$ for private screening and $2.242730484$ for top algebraic testing (decimal approximations). The verified upper ceiling of $2.250$ applies to full factoring in the conditional manuscript analysis. The replay itself tests the cost and parameter inequalities.
