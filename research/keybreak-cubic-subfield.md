# Cubic subfields and relative norm evaluation in KeyBreak

The [common-evaluation height bound](keybreak-common-evaluation-height.md) leaves a cubic as the only possible nontrivial common subfield for equal-map native fields of odd degree at the current height constants. A fresh field from the source's coefficient law has a cubic subfield with probability at most \(\exp(-\Omega(D^2))\), where \(D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}\). Fields deliberately drawn from different coefficient laws are outside this estimate. Even for such fields, an exact relative norm to a cubic subfield fails to determine the square class at the chosen modular branch in the unramified setting.

## Cubic subfields in the native coefficient law

Write \(H=D\log\log N\), let \(d\asymp D\) be the field degree, and condition on the environment \(Q,r\). The raw binary form is \(F_0+2(QX-rY)R\), with the \(d\) coefficients of \(R\) drawn from an isotropic integer Gaussian of parameter \(\sigma\), where \(\log\sigma\asymp DH\). At any prime \(p\nmid 2Q\), uniform coefficient residues give the uniform affine law on polynomials of degree at most \(d\) with \(F(r/Q,1)=w\bmod p\), where \(w=NQ^{-d}\bmod p\). Multiplication by the linear factor parametrizes the kernel of evaluation bijectively. The irreducible primitive native field, when produced, has the same roots as the raw polynomial. [KeyBreak, (G.4) and Lemma H.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105) · [Coefficient residue comparison](keybreak-public-pivot-span.md)

A cubic subfield of an irreducible degree-\(d\) field gives three blocks of size \(d/3\) for the Galois action on the roots, indexed by the three embeddings of the subfield. A cycle of length \(\ell\in(d/2,3d/4)\) with \(\gcd(\ell,6)=1\) is impossible. Its induced orbit on the blocks has length in \(\{1,2,3\}\) dividing \(\ell\), so the cycle would have to fit inside one block. A squarefree reduction of full degree with an irreducible factor of such length supplies precisely a cycle of the forbidden length by Frobenius.

For each \(p\ge5\), under the uniform affine law the probability of this witness is bounded below by an absolute positive constant for large \(d\), uniformly in the prescribed value \(w\). To see this, fix an eligible \(\ell\), put \(n=d-\ell\), and choose a monic irreducible polynomial of degree \(\ell\). There are \(\gg p^\ell/\ell\) choices uniformly by the finite-field irreducible count, and none has a root at \(x_0=r/Q\bmod p\). For \(w\ne0\) choose a monic squarefree degree-\(n\) cofactor avoiding \(x_0\). There are \(\gg p^n\) choices, since the fraction with root \(x_0\) is \(1/p\) and the fraction divisible by some irreducible square is at most \(\sum_{j\ge1}p^{-j}\). The cofactor is automatically coprime to the degree-\(\ell\) factor. Exactly one nonzero scalar makes the product take value \(w\). For \(w=0\), choose the cofactor as \((X-x_0)\) times a monic squarefree degree-\((n-1)\) polynomial avoiding \(x_0\), and allow all \(p-1\) nonzero scalars. In either case there are \(\gg p^d/\ell\) distinct witnesses in the affine space of size \(p^d\). The long factor is unique, so these counts add over eligible \(\ell\). There are \(\gg d\) eligible lengths, giving the constant probability.

Choose \(\Theta(D^2)\) distinct primes of size polynomial in \(D\), omitting \(2,Q\), with the constant in the count small enough that their product \(J\) has \(\log J\le\frac12\log\sigma\). For one Gaussian coordinate, residue weights modulo \(J\) differ from their integral reference by \(O(1)\) before normalization, by summing on each monotone half. The total variation distance for all \(d\) coefficients is \(O(dJ/\sigma)\). Under the uniform residue law the CRT makes the witnesses independent. The probability of having no witness is therefore \(\exp(-\Omega(D^2))+O(dJ/\sigma)=\exp(-\Omega(D^2))\), since \(DH\gg D^2\). The source's negligible finite-sampler error can be added. This bounds the probability of drawing an irreducible native field with a cubic subfield, with no division by a field acceptance probability. A union over \(\exp(O(H))\) fresh draws preserves the bound up to the constant in its exponent. Choosing an arbitrary member of that sampled pool cannot create the missing subfield.

## Exact relative norms can lose every modular unit

Let \(K/E\) have relative degree \(n>1\), and let compatible evaluations from \(\mathcal O_K\) and \(\mathcal O_E\) to \(\mathbb Z/N\) be given. Assume every rational prime \(p\mid N\) is unramified in \(K\). The chosen primes in both fields over \(p\) have residue degree one. Their local completions are \(\mathbb Q_p\), and the chosen prime in \(K\) has local degree one in the fiber of the chosen prime of \(E\). There is another prime in that fiber because the local degrees sum to \(n\).

For any \(u\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\), construct \(z\in\mathcal O_K\) as follows. For each \(p^a\Vert N\), impose \(z\equiv1\bmod p^a\) at the selected branch and at every other branch in its \(E\)-fiber except one. In the remaining branch take \(z\) to be a unit whose local norm is \(u^{-1}\bmod p^a\). Such a unit exists: the finite-field norm is surjective, and the norm on each successive principal-unit quotient is the surjective residue trace, by expansion of the norm modulo the next power of \(p\). The CRT in \(\mathcal O_K\) realizes all the prescriptions. The relative norm \(N_{K/E}(z)\) evaluates to \(u^{-1}\), so its inverse is allowed in the localized evaluation. Consequently
\[
x=\frac{z^n}{N_{K/E}(z)}
\qquad\text{satisfies}\qquad
N_{K/E}(x)=1,\quad \psi_K(x)=u.
\]
The exact norm-one group therefore maps onto all modular units in this setting. In particular the norm cannot determine even the separate quadratic characters at the chosen branches. When \(n\) is odd, the relative norm does split the inclusion of global square classes from \(E\), since an element of \(E\) has relative norm equal to its \(n\)-th power. This algebraic splitting gives no control of the evaluated square class of the relative norm kernel.

For a finite check take \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)\) and \(K=E(t)\), \(t^3=7\). The relative degree is three by Eisenstein at a prime of \(E\) over \(7\), which is unramified in \(E\). The primes \(5,11\) are unramified in \(K\) because the discriminants of the two cubic polynomials have no such prime factors. Each pure cubic has one simple root at each prime, giving compatible evaluations modulo \(55\). The chosen \(t\) equals \(3\bmod5\) and \(6\bmod11\). With \(z=t+1\), its relative norm is \(8\), so \(z^3/8\) has norm one and evaluates to \(3\) at both primes. This is a nonsquare modulo \(5\) and a square modulo \(11\).

These conclusions rule out finding the cubic by selection from ordinary native draws and using the relative norm alone to transport evaluated square classes. They give no small-height bound on the CRT element \(z\) and leave open constructions that track branch information in addition to the norm.
