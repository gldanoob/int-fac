# Polynomial-bit character probes for KeyBreak’s odd field

KeyBreak's H.6 character sampler can use polynomial-bit primes with leading setup coefficient $\delta/3$ at degree $d\sim\delta(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$. The probability argument applies to the same single odd-degree field used for all retained rows. The two Frobenius counts in each quadratic extension suffice, including in the exceptional-zero case. [Existing square-class bounds](cogentic-single-field-square-certification.md) · [KeyBreak, §H.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=137)

## Both Frobenius counts in a dyadic interval

Let $\gamma$ be a nonidentity class in the finite group $H_D$ of classes whose valuations outside the primes over $D$ are even. Put $E=K(\sqrt\gamma)$, used only in the analysis. The field and extension discriminants, and the norm of the relative conductor, have uniform bounds in KeyBreak's field data. In particular $\Delta_E\le\Delta_K^2 8^d|D|^d$ by its Lemma E.2, and the conductor of the nontrivial quadratic character has norm equal to the norm of the relative discriminant. Choose an integer $T$ exceeding their logarithmic bounds, $d\log(2d)$, the input bit length, and the logarithmic bounds for all atom norms. Take $X=2^{T^4}$. [KeyBreak, Lemma E.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=88)

Apply Thorner–Zaman's Theorem 5.1 separately to the split and inert Frobenius classes of $E/K$. For either endpoint $y=X,X/2$, the error is relative to the signed main term and is $O(\exp(-c T^{3/2}))$ for an absolute $c>0$. The required $Q\le y^{1/c_2}$ follows from $\log Q=O(T)$, with $Q$ as in equation (3-1). Let $F=\operatorname{Li}(X)-\operatorname{Li}(X/2)$, and for a possible exceptional zero $\beta$ put $G=\operatorname{Li}(X^\beta)-\operatorname{Li}((X/2)^\beta)$. Differentiating gives $0\le G\le F$. The zero belongs to exactly one of $\zeta_K$ and the nontrivial quadratic Hecke $L$-function, by simplicity and the factorization in (2-14). [Thorner–Zaman, (2-14), Theorems 3.1 and 5.1](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf#page=19)

- With no exceptional zero, each dyadic class count is $(1/2+o(1))F$. If the zero belongs to the nontrivial Hecke character, the split and inert counts are $(F-G)/2+o(F)$ and $(F+G)/2+o(F)$, respectively. The sum is asymptotic to $F$ and the inert class has fraction at least $1/2-o(1)$.
- If the zero belongs to $\zeta_K$, both exceptional signs are $+1$ and both dyadic main terms are $(F-G)/2$. Write $r=1-\beta$ and $J(y)=\operatorname{Li}(y)-\operatorname{Li}(y^\beta)$. The exceptional zero has $\beta>1/2$, as used in (5-3), so integrating from $X^\beta$ to $X$ gives $J(X)\le 2X(1-e^{-r\log X})/\log X$. Also
  $$F-G=\int_{X/2}^X\frac{1-y^{-r}}{\log y}\,dy\gg \frac{X(1-e^{-r\log X})}{\log X}.$$
  The last inequality follows from concavity of $1-e^{-z}$ and $\log(X/2)\ge\tfrac12\log X$. Thus the endpoint errors of size $o(J(X))$ stay small relative to $F-G$, even when $r\log X$ is tiny. Both counts are $(1/2+o(1))(F-G)$. Oddness of $d$ forbids a quadratic subfield of $K$, so Stark's Lemma 8 gives $r>1/(4d!\log\Delta_K)$. Therefore $F-G\gg X/(d!\log X)$. [Stark, Lemma 8](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0023/PPN356556735_0023.pdf)

The two counts partition the prime ideals in the dyad outside ramification. The degree-one restriction discards at most $d\sqrt X$ ideals because higher-degree norms are powers of primes at most $\sqrt X$. This is negligible against $X/(d!\log X)$. Rational primes in the dyad avoid all discriminants and excluded primes, by construction. For each nonidentity class the inert degree-one fraction is at least $1/3$ eventually, uniformly over the field data and the class. The inert character is the quadratic residue character on the integralized mark. This comparison uses one application for each Frobenius class of the same extension.

## Replacing the finite caps

Use the integer bound $T^*$ from H.6, increased to $T=10(T^*+d\,b(d+2))$, and retain the original polynomial row cap $t^*=20d(h^*+b(64)+1)$. Each proposal draws an exact uniform integer $p$ in the dyad, verifies primality, finds all roots of the source's polynomial $f$ modulo $p$, and chooses a uniform rank among $d$ positions, rejecting when there are too few roots. All probes avoid the leading coefficient, so these roots are in bijection with roots of the monic integralized polynomial used for the maximal-order interpretation. Each ideal has the same proposal probability $2/(dX)$. The probability of accepting a proposal is $\gg 1/(d!T^5)$. Give each accepted probe at most $d!T^7$ proposals and use exact polynomial-time primality testing on these polynomial-bit inputs. The original random finite-field split caps and scalar caps can be used with a polynomial adjustment for the number of bits. Their conditional timeout errors, summed over $\exp(O(H))$ proposals, are $o(1)$. [KeyBreak, (H.22)–(H.27)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=138)

Analyze independent accepted probes in the unlimited procedure and couple the capped run up to the first overflow. A union bound on the finite group $H_D$ controls every eventual relation product, including products chosen by a public rank profile after the probes. The root computation, all four column families of (E.27), and the exact square and divisor checks remain as in the original certificate. The coupling error is uniform over fields, so it can be subtracted from the good-field success probability in H.8. Each expensive proposal and each retained character evaluation uses polynomial bit work. Thus setup costs $d!\operatorname{poly}(n)$ and the retained character coefficient is just the occurrence coefficient $b$. Private roots are first used in the final gcd and all probe work is public. This reduction introduces no analytic assumption beyond the ones already used for relation supply and smoothness recognition. [KeyBreak, §H.8](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=141)
