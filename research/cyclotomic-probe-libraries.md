# How many polynomial probes suffice for a universal cyclotomic search?

Fix an odd prime index $k$. A probe chooses one monic integer polynomial $f$ of degree $2$ through $k$. It reduces $x^D$ modulo $(N,f)$ and takes the gcd of $N$ with all nonconstant coefficients. Here $D$ is the odd part of the supplied positive multiple $M$. On balanced inputs $N=pq$ with $X<p,q\le 2X$, the worst-case length of a universal probe list is $\Theta_k(\log X)$. Its coefficients can have $O_k(\log X)$ bits. This measures the number of polynomial probes. The upper bound uses a list chosen for the interval, with an exhaustive deterministic construction or nonuniform advice.

The lower bound is $(1-o(1))\log_2 X$ even for a binary decision tree with unbounded coefficients. A node can choose its polynomial from the scale $X$ and the preceding failed responses, distinguished as $1$ or $N$. A separate computation that uses the numerical values of $N$ or $M$ to select a node is outside this model. The upper bound works for every positive multiple of $\Phi_k(p)$, including multiples that cover both primes. The lower bound already holds at the specific multiplier $M=\operatorname{lcm}(\Phi_k(p),\Phi_k(q))$.

Bach and Shallit introduced factoring with cyclotomic polynomials. Źrałek studied deterministic variants. Earlier fixed-list bounds in this repository concern limiting probabilities for fixed prefixes or quantitative upper bounds on the failure probability at a growing cutoff. Here a single large set of primes gives the exact scalar responses at every polynomial and at every pair in the set. ## A set of primes with pairwise coprime exponents

Put $J=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k-1)$. For each prime $X<p\le 2X$ with $p\equiv-1\pmod k$, take the sets of prime factors of the following two integers
$$
 A(p)=\Phi_k(p),\qquad B(p)=p(p^J-1).
$$
These sets are disjoint. A common prime would divide $p-1$ since $\gcd(k,J)=1$ and $A(p)\mid p^k-1$. It would then divide $k$, whereas $p\equiv-1\pmod k$ and $k$ is odd. Also $p\nmid A(p)$.

Colour every prime divisor occurring anywhere in these finite sets red or blue independently with equal probabilities. Retain $p$ when its first set is entirely red and its second entirely blue. Its probability of retention is $2^{-\omega(A(p)B(p))}$. Any two survivors $p,q$ satisfy $\gcd(A(p),B(q))=1$ in both orientations.

There is a choice retaining $S\gg_k X/(\log X)^{C_k}$ primes for a constant $C_k$. Indeed the initial progression contains $\asymp_k X/\log X$ primes. Each $A(p)B(p)$ is $X^{O_k(1)}$, so it has $O_k(1)$ prime factors above $\sqrt X$. For $\ell\le\sqrt X$, the fixed monic polynomial $A(t)B(t)$ has $O_k(1)$ roots modulo $\ell$. Its unit root classes contain $O_k(X/(\varphi(\ell)\log X))$ primes $p$ in the interval by Brun–Titchmarsh. The zero class contributes none. Summing over $\ell$ gives mean $\omega(A(p)B(p))=O_k(1+\log\log X)$ on the initial progression. Jensen's inequality gives the claimed lower bound for the expected number retained. Choosing each colour to maximize the conditional expectation makes this a finite deterministic construction if the prime factorizations used for it have been computed. [Montgomery–Vaughan, interval Brun–Titchmarsh estimate](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/large_sieve.pdf#page=3)

## The full local response on that set

For any survivor $p$, attach one bit to $f$. Set it to one exactly when $f\bmod p=x^{\deg f}$, or when $\deg f=k$ and $f\bmod p$ is irreducible. For any distinct survivors $p,q$, the coefficient gcd is exactly $p^{\operatorname{bit}_p(f)}q^{\operatorname{bit}_q(f)}$, simultaneously for every monic probe. Take $X>k$.

Indeed $M$ is odd. At an irreducible degree-$k$ fibre the norm exponent $(p^k-1)/(p-1)=\Phi_k(p)$ makes the power scalar. On a pure nilpotent fibre the power is zero. If $x$ is a nonunit and the fibre has a factor other than $x$, its power is zero at the zero root and is nonzero at another irreducible factor. In every other case $x$ is a unit and all residue degrees are at most $k-1$. Their unit exponents divide $p^J-1$. The kernel of reduction modulo the nilradical has exponent dividing $p$ since $p>k$. Thus the projective order divides $B(p)$. The entire multiplier $M$ is coprime to $B(p)$, and $x$ itself is nonscalar in the power basis. Its $M$th power stays nonscalar. The same argument applies at $q$. This also treats coefficients divisible by either input prime and arbitrarily high input coefficients.

Now send each of the $S$ primes down the binary tree using its local bit. Any two primes arriving at the same depth-$j$ leaf give a pair on which the actual tree follows that path and returns only trivial gcds. A depth-$j$ tree has at most $2^j$ leaves. Thus a universally splitting tree needs $j\ge \lceil\log_2 S\rceil\ge\log_2 X-O_k(\log\log X)$. More quantitatively there are at least $(S^2/2^j-S)/2$ unordered missed pairs. For the full monic box $|f_i|\le H$ the number of probes is $\sum_{i=2}^k(2H+1)^i$. It follows **unconditionally** that every sufficiently large dyadic interval contains a balanced two-order input missing an entire box of height $\lfloor c_k(\log X)^{1/k}\rfloor$ for a suitable $c_k>0$. The earlier balanced lower bound for the *exact single* exponent uses different hypotheses. [Single-order lower bounds](cyclotomic-order-multiples.md#balanced-lower-bounds-at-every-index)

## Matching upper bound by short lists

Sample the $k$ lower coefficients of a monic degree-$k$ polynomial independently from $[0,U)$ for an integer $U$. At fixed distinct primes $p,q$ of the interval each coefficient modulo $pq$ has total variation distance at most $pq/U$ from uniform. The joint distance is at most $kpq/U$. First consider the exactly uniform model. Irreducibility at $p$ has probability $(p^k-p)/(kp^k)\ge1/(2k)$ by counting elements of degree $k$ over the prime field.

At $q$, require two nonzero roots of opposite quadratic characters. This event has probability bounded below by an absolute positive constant for $q\ge5$. To verify it, let $Z$ count root pairs $(u,v)$ with $u$ a nonzero square and $v$ a nonsquare. Then $\mathbb E Z=((q-1)/(2q))^2\ge 4/25$. In $\mathbb E Z^2$, terms with $m=2,3,4$ distinct prescribed roots have probability at most $q^{-m}$. For $m\le k$ this follows from interpolation, and $m>k$ is impossible for a monic polynomial. There are at most $q^2,2q^3,q^4$ terms in these three cases, so $\mathbb E Z^2\le4$ and $\Pr(Z>0)\ge(4/25)^2/4$. An odd power preserves the opposite quadratic characters, so these evaluations force a nonscalar power at $q$ for *every* odd $D$. At $p$, $\Phi_k(p)$ is odd and still divides $D$. Both events together give the proper gcd $p$.

CRT makes the events independent. Taking $U\ge 10^4 k^2(2X)^2$ preserves a success probability $\gg1/k$. There are fewer than $4X^2$ ordered pairs. A union bound on independent trials supplies a single list of $O(k\log X)$ polynomials that succeeds in both orientations of every pair, simultaneously for every supplied multiplier. Verification of a prospective list only needs the local irreducibility and opposite-root tests. This proves existence with small coefficients, and gives exhaustive construction in finite time. The cost of constructing such a near-minimum list uniformly in $\operatorname{poly}(\log X)$ time is open here.

The replay constructs simultaneous coprime sets by conditional expectation, checks both cross gcds on the full sets and compares each predicted gcd to the actual modular polynomial power. At $k=3$ it retains 27 of 67 primes and checks 3,861 gcds. At $k=5$ it retains 8 of 11 and checks 308 gcds. The fixtures include scalar, irreducible, nonreduced and mixed nilpotent fibres. A separate list of 36 cubic probes covers all 110 ordered pairs of distinct primes from 17 through 59 and verifies each split at an extra multiplier.
