# Averaging the shared modulus in a rank-one coefficient cycle

A complete intercept cycle can replace the two lower correction directions by one in the fixed-cofactor high-degree construction. In the resulting two-mark lattice, averaging the scalar errors over the paired residue classes costs the square root of the shared modulus. A common translated base can also be chosen to bound the weight of large shared moduli in the actual score. Under the existing Dirichlet RH, moment and extractor hypotheses this gives a conditional descent to three averaged coordinates per field. Their raw product still has the two-thirds exponent. ## The intercept period and the remaining congruence

Keep both original cofactors. In each field add
\[
 g(m-X)\bigl[x+Xy+w+u(Z+X)^r+v(Z+X)^{r+1}\bigr]
\]
to its original polynomial, where \(Z\ge A\) and \(r\le d-3\). The original affine variables \(x,y\) start in their broad boxes. The new variables \(w,u,v\) each start in an interval with left endpoint zero. At two distinct marks \(a_1<a_2\), write \(b_i=Z+a_i\), \(n_i=m-a_i\), \(\Delta=a_2-a_1\), and \(h_0=\gcd(b_1,b_2)\). Normalize the marked perturbations by \(gn_i\). The high lattice \(L_h\) has basis with rows \(b_i^r(1,b_i)\) and determinant of magnitude \(\Delta b_1^r b_2^r\). Adjoining \((1,1)\) gives
\[
 L_w=\{(z_1,z_2):z_2-z_1\equiv0\pmod{D_{12}}\},\qquad
 D_{12}=h_0^r\gcd\bigl((b_2/h_0)^r-(b_1/h_0)^r,\Delta\bigr).
\]
Indeed the diagonal vectors are all present. The group of differences is generated by \(b_2^r-b_1^r\) and \(b_2^{r+1}-b_1^{r+1}\). Subtracting \(b_2\) times the first member from the second gives \(\Delta b_1^r\). After removing \(h_0^r\), the first member is coprime to \((b_1/h_0)^r\), which proves the formula. Thus \(w\) has exact period \(\Delta b_1^r b_2^r/D_{12}\) in \(L_w/L_h\). Fixing coefficient classes modulo the small score modulus \(M\), then using indices in those classes, gives the same period and lattice with translated origins. A fixed affine contribution changes the normalized difference by \(\Delta y\), with \(y\) now the index in its retained class.

Choose positive constants \(S,\rho\) satisfying \(2\rho<S\), and take \(r\log Z=(\rho+o(1))V\). Take both high side lengths \(T\) within a fixed factor of \(e^{SV}/Z^r\), so their normalized parallelogram has both singular widths \(e^{SV+O(H)}\). This follows from its determinant and the bounds \(Z<b_i\le Z+A\), \(\Delta\le A\) and \(r=O(H)\). Choose \(w\)'s width within a fixed factor of \((Z+A)^{2r+1}e^{C_1H}\), so its leading log width is \(2\rho V\). Choose the guard \(C_1\) after fixing the score precision and its \(M\) split. The resulting interval is longer than the intercept period by any needed factor \(e^{CH}\) within each class. Reducing that interval modulo the period therefore gives the uniform law to error \(e^{-CH}\). Its physical drift in normalized coordinates is exponentially smaller than either singular width.

For a fixed intercept class, the high law is uniform in one coset of \(L_h\) in a translated half-open parallelogram with integer side lengths in its lattice basis. Such a parallelogram contains exactly the same number of points in every coset. Replacing its location by a common one costs \(O((\text{drift})/(\text{smaller singular width})+1/T)\) by counting boundary strips in the high basis. The complete cycle then gives uniform points of a translate of \(L_w\) inside the common parallelogram.

## An RMS estimate on a fiber of reduced residues

In a single physical interval, delete the prescribed band primes and let \(A_Q(s)\) count the remaining squarefree band products in a unit class \(s\bmod Q\). The sharp scalar proof gives a common principal count \(P_Q\), independent of \(s\), and
\[
 A_Q(s)-P_Q=\frac1{\varphi(Q)}\sum_{\chi\bmod Q}\overline{\chi(s)}\,e_\chi,\qquad
 |e_\chi|\le E,\qquad E\ll w_0 h^{\sigma_0}.
\]
Here \(h\) bounds the physical height and \(w_0\) bounds its bit length together with the modulus description. For the principal character subtract the chosen low contour integral. This is a statement about exact character sums and uses the same sharp endpoint bounds as the scalar proof.

For any coset \(s_0 K\) of a subgroup \(K\) of the units modulo \(Q\),
\[
 \left(\frac1{|K|}\sum_{s\in s_0K}|A_Q(s)-P_Q|^2\right)^{1/2}
 \le E/\sqrt{|K|}.
\]
To see this, group the character expansion by restriction to \(K\). Each group has \(\varphi(Q)/|K|\) terms, so its coefficient on the coset has absolute value at most \(E/|K|\). Parseval on \(K\) gives the inequality.

Apply this to an axis-aligned rectangle inside the normalized parallelogram, with both side lengths comparable to \(e^{SV}\) up to \(e^{O(H)}\). For the translate \(z_2-z_1\equiv c\pmod{D_{12}}\), first select a residue \(j=z_1\bmod D_{12}\) and use \(z_2\equiv j+c\bmod D_{12}\). Put \(D=D_{12}\) and \(J=\gcd(D,\operatorname{rad}(\Delta)^2)=\gcd(D,\operatorname{rad}(D)^2)\), since every prime factor of \(D\) divides \(\Delta\). Factor the small integer \(\Delta\) by trial division to compute \(J=e^{O(H)}\). Group the paired residues by \(j\bmod J\).

Before this grouping, a physical test has the form \(C_i+q_i z_i\) after its fixed sign and the required small-modulus divisions. Reduce \(\gcd(C_i,q_i)\) once and perform the associated omissions, or reject an impossible test. This divisor is supported by the small part of the stride, since on the eligible grid the fixed cofactors make the value a unit modulo \(n_i\). For the resulting unit class modulo \(q_i\), the group modulo \(J\) determines whether the progression at stride \(q_iD\) has a forced square or forbidden prime. It also determines the exact forced squarefree divisor \(e_i=\gcd(C_i+q_i z_i,q_iD)\) in each admitted group. These assertions use only the residue modulo \(p\) for primes with valuation one in \(D\), and modulo \(p^2\) for higher valuations. Any forced prime is omitted after division. Rejected groups contribute zero to the product count and keep their weight in its denominator.

After dividing by \(e_i\), the reduced unit residues range over one fiber modulo \(Q_{0i}=|q_i|J/e_i\) in the units modulo \(Q_i=|q_i|D/e_i\). The group has \(D/J\) members, and \(Q_i,Q_{0i}\) have the same prime factors. Each residue in the fiber appears exactly once, by the linear progression for \(j\). Both marks use a common \(j\). The separate RMS estimates bound absolute errors even when their residues are dependent.

In a rectangle with normalized side length \(F_i\) the exact progression length for each \(j\) differs from \(F_i/D\) by at most two. The physical interval for the sharp count is constant within the group after division and inward rounding. Clamp each principal count to the interval \([0,F_i/D+2]\). Replacing all progression lengths by \(F_i/D\) in the comparison introduces \(O(D/\min F_i)\) in normalized error. The product count error then obeys the sum of the marginal absolute errors, after normalization, since both true and clamped fractions are bounded up to this length error. The RMS bound gives a marginal error
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{w_0 h^{\sigma_0}\sqrt{DJ}}{F_i}+\frac{D}{F_i}\right).
\]
If \(\log h=(\lambda+o(1))V\), \(\log D\le(\gamma+o(1))V\), the bound is exponentially small in \(V\) when \(S>\lambda/2+\gamma/2\). The individual-residue bound gives the sufficient condition \(S>\lambda/2+\gamma\). Only \(J=e^{O(H)}\) groups are evaluated. Within one group both principal counts are constant as \(j\) varies. Their products therefore use the known multiplicity \(D/J\). The same grouping and its \(O(D/\min F_i)\) bound approximate the scalar rectangle cardinality to the required relative precision. Use the sharp scalar algorithm to compute the principal integral and its truncated inverse totient. Clamping makes a relative totient error \(\epsilon\) contribute at most \(O(\epsilon(F_i/D+2))\) to each count, regardless of whether the unclamped predictor is in range. All factorizations for forced primes use the band sieve and the small factors of the stride.

The parallelogram can be tiled by interior axis-aligned squares of mesh equal to the smaller singular width times \(e^{-C_2H}\), ignoring boundary squares. There are \(e^{O(H)}\) squares. Each square has \(\#(L_w\cap\text{square})\) equal to its area divided by \(D\) up to relative error \(O(D/\mathrm{mesh})\) by the same length calculation. The upper and lower tilings give the weighted boundary error \(O(e^{-C_2H+O(H)}+D/\mathrm{mesh})\). The scalar estimates inside the squares use the original root predicates of the retained coefficient class.

## Choosing the common translation with the score weights

Let \(W_{12}\) be the sum of the absolute coefficients in the finite true-score expansion whose scalar factors use the distinct pair \(a_1,a_2\), counting both fields. These weights include the centered Gram terms and the quadratic mass term, and \(\sum_{12} W_{12}\le e^{O(H)}\). They depend on the original fixed references and the cofactors. The translated powers preserve those references and their admissible perturbation margins. Use the existing score coefficient algorithms with the truncated totient products for the weights and the child estimates. They give any fixed exponential precision in \(e^{O(H)}\) time. [Score coefficient computation without auxiliary factorizations](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md#computing-the-score-weights-without-auxiliary-factorizations)

For any \(G\ge1\), take \(Z\) uniformly over any \(A\) consecutive integers above a chosen \(Z_0\ge A\). Every divisor \(s\mid \Delta\) satisfying \(s\mid Z+a_1\) occurs with probability at most \(1/s+1/A\le2/s\). Therefore
\[
 \mathbb E_Z\sum_{12:\ \gcd(Z+a_1,Z+a_2)>G} W_{12}
 \ \le\ \frac{2e^{o(H)}}G\sum_{12}W_{12}.
\]
Here the uniform divisor bound \(\tau(\Delta)=e^{o(H)}\) holds because \(\Delta\le e^{O(H)}\). Enumerate the shifts and take a minimizing one, using integer gcds. For any fixed desired score precision \(e^{-CH}\), a sufficiently large \(G=e^{O(H)}\) makes the bound smaller than that precision. If \(G\ge A\), all pairs automatically pass. Dropping the excluded pairs in the *estimated* score costs at most their total coefficient weight, since all factors are in \([0,1]\). The actual score continues to include them.

For the remaining pairs \(D\le \Delta G^r\). Taking \(\log Z_0\sim z_0 H\) with a larger fixed \(z_0\), and \(r\sim \rho V/\log Z_0\), makes \(r\log G/V\) as small as desired at unchanged \(\rho\). Choose \(z_0\) large enough also to ensure \(r\le d-3\). Thus one can take any \(S>\lambda/2\) with \(\rho\) fixed and \(2\rho<S\), provided \(\gamma<2(S-\lambda/2)\). A worst-pair choice can replace \(G\) by \(A\) throughout. The weighted estimate allows the smaller cap whenever the required score budget gives \(G<A\).

On the conservative schedule the cap can indeed be smaller. For the centered Gram terms let \(G(a)=r(a)r(a)^{\mathsf T}-\widetilde U^{\rm ex}\) on the row pairs in the score. The triangle inequality in Frobenius norm gives
\[
 \sum_{j,k}\left(\sum_a w_{\rm eq}(a)|G(a)_{jk}|\right)^2
 \le \left(\sum_a w_{\rm eq}(a)\|G(a)\|_{\rm F}\right)^2.
\]
The left side bounds the sum of absolute coefficients of the squared Gram entries before the peeling multipliers. At any mark at most one residue row per field and prime is hit, so \(\|r(a)\|_2^2\ll B^2+L^{O(1)}\). The local exact center entries are polynomially bounded by the one- and two-prime formulas of source Lemma 9.2(2) and the logarithmic real-row bound. There are \(B^4L^{O(1)}\) entries, hence \(\|G(a)\|_{\rm F}\le B^2L^{O(1)}\). For the term with extra multiplier \(\ell^{\alpha}\le B\), the matrix uses only protected-row versus ordinary-row entries. At most one protected row is hit at any mark, so the rank-one part on that block has Frobenius norm at most \(B^{3/2}L^{O(1)}\). The center of that block has just \(B^2L^{O(1)}\) entries and Frobenius norm at most \(BL^{O(1)}\). Squaring that restricted bound and multiplying by \(B\) therefore still costs at most \(B^4L^{O(1)}\). Other Gram multipliers are \(e^{O((\log L)^2)}\), and the square of the mass has the smaller coefficient bound \(64(\sum_a w_{\rm eq}(a))^2\).

On this schedule \(\sum_a w_{\rm eq}(a)\le e^{(2\lambda/3000+o(1))H}\) by the two reciprocal reference densities and the probability weight \(\pi_0\). This yields \(\sum_{12}W_{12}\le e^{(4000+4\lambda/3000+o(1))H}\). Taking \(G=\lceil e^{4500H}\rceil<A\) gives discarded weight at most \(e^{-350H}\) for sufficiently large inputs. This suffices across all \(O(V)\) conditional comparisons. ## Conditional descent and remaining size

On the conservative fixed schedule \(\lambda=100001\), \(\log m=(1+o(1))V\), and the original affine widths have leading exponent \(100000\). Choose \(\lambda/2<S<100000\). On \(|X|\le A\), the new perturbation has magnitude at most \(e^{(1+S+o(1))V}\), with strict room below the original real-height margins. It has degree at most \(d-1\), preserves its value at \(m\), and preserves the original protected residues. Fix the new three directions and use the original broad \(x,y\) Gaussian moment estimate. The uniform-box comparison gives initial mean score \(o(1)\) after averaging the three directions.

Bisect the broader affine side in each field down to singletons, keeping the two lengths within \(e^{O(H)}\). While the smaller affine width exceeds \(e^{SV}\), panel the high variables and interpolate their evaluations at the two marks by an integer affine polynomial. Freeze each such small affine shift by interval translation as in the existing broad-box proof. The \(w\) drift is exponentially smaller than this width, and the original arbitrary-modulus scalar panels have length \(e^{SV-O(H)}\) or longer. Every shift lies in the retained coefficient class after taking differences in that class.

Below that crossover, split the affine box into \(e^{O(H)}\) panels to make the changes in \(x+a_i y\) smaller than the narrower high singular width by any required \(e^{-CH}\) factor. Scalar heights remain inside the original extraction bounds. For every fixed \(x,y\) in a panel the intercept cycle estimates the uniform law on its own translate of \(L_w\). Use a common parallelogram for the whole panel through the boundary comparison in the high basis. The paired residues in this common parallelogram use the actual class \(c=\Delta y\) with its original offset. The principal predictors and group multiplicities depend on \(c\bmod J\) only. Thus their average uses exact counts of the affine indices modulo \(J\) without enumerating classes modulo \(D\). Root predicates remain fixed by the original class modulo \(M\). A one-mark term can introduce a dummy distinct mark with constant second test. Averaging over all \(j\) then leaves exactly the full scalar progression to the rectangle length error, irrespective of \(c\). A repeated mark uses its Boolean intersection at that one mark. For the dummy case one always has \(D\le \Delta (Z+A)^r\), so the rectangle length error stays small.

This supplies an \(e^{O(H)}\) evaluator at every child, including singleton affine coordinates. The scalar RH margin absorbs the finite coefficients and the number of panels. Choose the smaller estimated child mean. In \(O(V)\) splits their cumulative error keeps the remaining true mean \(o(1)\), as in the original descent. The final widths in each field have leading exponents \(2\rho,S-\rho,S-\rho\); in particular \(\rho=S/3\) balances the three widths. Their product exponent is \(2S\) and can approach \(\lambda\). The complete intercept cycle and the two high directions remain averaged. This calculation leaves the final one-third selection open.

An exact arithmetic check compared 660 intercept periods with the defining high-lattice membership condition, enumerated 173,936 reduced unit fibers, and verified the interval endpoints and progression counts for 386,940 signed classes. The analytic estimates above use the existing RH input.
