## November 25 source check

The leaderboard still lists score $1/2$. Upstream main points to the November 22 commit 1b331929, and the latest dated factoring-board post is November 8. The November 5 *Fan Factories* working paper improves the **heuristic per-target** NFS constant from 1.902 to 1.793 after shared precomputation costing about one ordinary factorization. It treats batches and uses the standard NFS smoothness heuristics. [Verifier](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/integer-factoring/issues/28#issuecomment-6533697488) · [Pinned comparison](https://api.github.com/repos/1337isnot1337/integer-factoring/compare/1b331929...main) · [Board feed](https://board.bigospeedrun.com/api/posts?board=integer-factoring&format=md) · [Fan Factories, pp. 1–2](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/papers/Fan_Factories_Multiple_Number_Fields_in_Factorization_Factories_and_Batch_NFS.pdf)

**Carmichael numbers can already be recognized and completely factored on yes-instances in deterministic polynomial time.** Shallue–Webster, Algorithm 1, gives an ERH time bound of $O((\log N)^{10}(\log\log N)^{c_0})$. The needed small quadratic-character witnesses fall within the verified Dirichlet GRH result. Korselt's criterion makes $N-1$ a known multiple of every $p-1$ for $p\mid N$, and the same $N-1$ works throughout the factorization. This is a published algorithm on a restricted family. For instance, extending it by finding a Carmichael multiple cannot work for every squarefree input: a Carmichael multiple $K$ of 21 would require both $3\mid K$ and $6\mid K-1$. Larsen's existence theorem for infinitely many Carmichael multiples has its own coprimality hypothesis. [Shallue–Webster, Theorems 4 and 6 and §3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.09903#page=6) · [GRH verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Korselt's criterion and the November 16 application](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21013#page=1) · [Larsen, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.09056#page=6)

There is also a weaker sufficient input for an arbitrary cofactor with at least two distinct odd prime divisors. A positive polynomial-bit multiple of $p-1$ for **one unknown** prime divisor $p$ suffices for a deterministic proper split using the small witnesses under Dirichlet GRH. Supplying one such multiple per remaining cofactor gives complete factorization. A single common multiple of all the $p-1$ suffices throughout. Producing these integers is the cost that remains. [Źrałek, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=4) · [Miller, Lemma 5](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf#page=11)

Gupta–Sikarov–Volkovich's September preprint gives a separate elementary result for a simple Carmichael triprime $N=pqr$ with $p<q<r$ and $p-1$ dividing both $q-1$ and $r-1$. One exact $L=\lambda(N)$ suffices: the paper’s identities give $p=\lceil\sqrt{(N-1)/L}\rceil$ and $\lambda(qr)=L$, from which the semiprime can be recovered. In checking the **separate totient argument** in Lemma 4.11 we found a discrepancy in the printed height calculation. Its expanded polynomial is $x^2y-xy+(\varphi(N)-N)x+N$. With $X=p$ and $Y=2r$, the scaled coefficient height is $\max(2p^2r,2pr,p(N-\varphi(N)),N)\in[N,3N]$, using $p(N-\varphi(N))\le pN(1/p+1/q+1/r)\le3N$. The proof uses $\Theta(pN)$, so its extra size region needs another justification when $p$ grows. [ECCC 2026/212, Lemma 1.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/212/download/#page=12) · [Lemma 4.11](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/212/download/#page=15) · [Semiprime identity, Lemma 5.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444#page=12)

## Totients and multiplicative orders

These are different oracle contracts. In the bounds below, $L_N[1/3,c]=\exp((c+o(1))(\ln N)^{1/3}(\ln\ln N)^{2/3})$. Each time bound excludes obtaining the stated oracle answers.

| Information available | Established deterministic result |
| --- | --- |
| A single integer $\varphi(N)$ | Miller's Lemma 5 gives deterministic polynomial-time recovery under ERH. The verified Dirichlet GRH result supplies Miller’s hypothesis, giving $O((\log N)^6)$ deterministic recovery in Źrałek’s Theorem 6.2. An alternative deduction uses the $31/32$ strip with a larger polynomial. In Miller's Lemmas 2A and 2B the needed witness has a quadratic character equal to $-1$, modulo $p$ or $pq$ for distinct prime divisors. Pathak's Theorem 2 bounds the least such witness by $O((\log N)^{32})$ with $\delta=1/32$. Miller's search can enumerate bases without knowing $p,q$. The original $\varphi(N)$ remains a multiple of the unit-group exponent of every cofactor, so one answer suffices throughout. Computing that first answer is still part of an end-to-end factoring bound. [Pathak, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.13316#page=2) · [Miller](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf) · [Źrałek, Theorem 6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=13) |
| The **prime factorization** of $\varphi(N)$, or all **numeric iterates** down to 1 | Źrałek's Theorem 8.1 gives unconditional deterministic complete factorization in $L_N[1/3,1]$ time from the factored totient. Corollary 8.2 gives that bound from $N,\varphi(N),\varphi^2(N),\ldots,1$. A callable totient oracle supplies the chain with at most $1+\log_2 N$ calls. These statements do not require the caller to find discrete logs. [Theorem 8.1 and Corollary 8.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=18) |
| A procedure returning exact totients at chosen inputs | Źrałek's 2019 reduction factors arbitrary $N$ deterministically in $L_N[1/3,72^{-1/3}]$ time beyond the queries. This improves the leading constant when the reduction can choose which totients to request. [Published abstract, p. 1261](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3368) |
| A positive polynomial-bit order multiple for a requested small prime base | Bach's reduction and Dirichlet GRH give deterministic polynomial-time factoring beyond the oracle. Accumulating answers needs at most $b$ queries on one remaining cofactor. The same bound applies to nonnegative discrete logs of inverse targets. [Derivation, query contracts and Lean counting proof](order-and-crt-reductions.md#a-linear-number-of-queries-on-one-cofactor) |
| Bounded discrete logarithms at general unit bases | Fix $0<\epsilon\le1/2$. Pomykała–Źrałek give a deterministic subexponential reduction for every $N$ using $O_\epsilon((\ln N)^2)$ queries on moduli $m\le N$. Each query seeks any natural $x\le(1-\epsilon)m$ with $a^x=b\bmod m$ when such a solution exists. To compare it with a leaderboard score, both the explicit overhead and the cost per query matter. [Published abstract, p. 421](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00037-012-0037-5) |
| One multiple $U$ of $\varphi(N)$ with $0<U<N^2$, for distinct primes $N=pq$ of equal bit length | Coron–May give deterministic factor recovery in $O((\log N)^9)$ time. Their Theorem 3 is stated using $U=ed-1$ and its proof needs only $U$ and $N$. An exponent known to annihilate one element supplies a multiple of that element's order, which can be smaller than $\varphi(N)$. [Theorem 3 and proof, §4](https://www.crypto-uni.lu/jscoron/publications/equivdet.pdf#page=4) |
| Only $N$ and a target $1\le D<N-1$, with $N\ge3$ | Harvey–Hittmeir's Theorem 1.1 gives either a unit of order **greater than** $D$ or a proper divisor, without a power-of-$N$ lower threshold on $D$. For non-small $D$ its bit cost is $O(\sqrt D\log D\log N/\sqrt{\log\log D})$. A lower bound on a chosen unit's order does not give the order of a prescribed base. [2026 preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.11131#page=3) |

The [Hurwitz residue reduction](hurwitz-residues.md#one-large-index-and-quarter-bit-precision-suffice) recovers arbitrary factorizations using one large three-square or raw form count per twist at quarter-bit precision. It combines the published Hurwitz product formula with univariate small-root reconstruction. The [polynomial-overhead reductions](hurwitz-polynomial-reductions.md) use fixed-index cosets and rational reconstruction for composite twists, or a mixed-sign gcd and CRT for prime twists.

## Existing coefficient and lattice decoders

| Given information | What the source already recovers |
| --- | --- |
| Ordinary $\sigma(N)$ or $\varphi(N)$ for a squarefree triprime | Morain–Renault–Smith, Theorem 1.2, give a deterministic polynomial-time nontrivial split in three size regimes. With $p_1>p_2>p_3$ and $\alpha_i=\log_N p_i$, any of $\alpha_1>1/2$, $2\alpha_1+3\alpha_2\ge2$, or $\alpha_2>(\sqrt{17}-1)/8$ suffices. | An integer $x$ in binary, or a fundamental negative discriminant | Tulsyan's preprint applies the cited #SAT counting algorithm to compute $\sum_{m\le x}\sigma(m)$, $\sum_{m\le x}\tau(m)$ and the count of reduced forms in $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log b})}$ deterministic time.
