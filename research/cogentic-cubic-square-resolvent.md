# Cubic square resolvents and a fixed-field kernel

Square norm gives a degree-four resolvent for individual square tests in a cubic field. Over the normal closure its conjugate classes generate a multiquadratic extension. In an $S_3$ cubic that extension has degree $4^r$ for $r$ independent square-norm classes, and $r$ can be arbitrarily large even in one fixed cubic. A separate ideal-lattice algorithm decides the complete square kernel in the fixed field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)$ in polynomial bit time. [Exact replay](../experiments/cogentic-cubic-square-resolvent.gp)

## A quartic for a single square-norm class

Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be cubic and $\beta\in K^\times$. If $\beta$ is rational, its norm is $\beta^3$, so square norm already implies it is a rational square. Otherwise $\beta$ has distinct conjugates $\beta_1,\beta_2,\beta_3$. Write their elementary symmetric functions as $s_1,s_2,t^2$, with $t\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Choose square roots $\rho_i^2=\beta_i$ with product $t$. The four sums $w=\pm\rho_1\pm\rho_2\pm\rho_3$ with product of signs one are the distinct roots of

$$ R_\beta(T)=T^4-2s_1T^2-8tT+s_1^2-4s_2. $$

Expanding the elementary symmetric functions of the four sums gives this polynomial. The sums are distinct because equality of two of them would identify two of the $\beta_i$. If $\beta$ is square in $K$, the three conjugates of one square root, with a common choice of sign, have product $t$ and rational trace, giving a rational root $w$. Conversely, for a rational root $w$, orient the three radicals to sum to $w$ and use

$$ (w^2-s_1+2\beta_i)\rho_i=2(w\beta_i+t). $$

This follows by squaring $w-\rho_i$, using the product $t$, and multiplying by $\rho_i$. The denominator is nonzero since $\beta_i$ is nonrational. Thus $2(w\beta+t)/(w^2-s_1+2\beta)$ is a square root in $K$. The trace and symmetric coefficient can be read from the multiplication characteristic polynomial in the explicit rational basis. Factoring a rational quartic, or testing it for a linear factor by deterministic rational polynomial factorization, costs polynomial bit work and uses no integer factorization. ## The size of the normal-closure obstruction

Let $L$ be the normal closure of $K$. Restriction to $L$ is injective on the square-norm subspace. In the nonnormal case $[L:K]=2$ and $L=K(\sqrt\Delta)$ for the nonsquare discriminant class $\Delta\in\mathbb Q^\times$. The kernel of restriction is the span of $[\Delta]$, whose rational norm class is $[\Delta]^3=[\Delta]\ne1$. In the cyclic case $L=K$.

For a nonsquare square-norm $\beta$, the three conjugate classes in $L^\times/L^{\times2}$ are nonzero and sum to zero. Cyclic permutation of the conjugates by an automorphism of order three shows that their span has dimension two: dimension one would make the three equal and their sum nonzero. Adjoining their square roots to $L$ therefore gives an extension of degree four with group $V_4$. The splitting field of $R_\beta$ is exactly this extension of $L$ since pairwise sums of its roots recover the $2\rho_i$. The action on the four roots is faithful, and $V_4$ is transitive. Thus the quartic is irreducible, with group $S_4$ in the $S_3$ case and $A_4$ in the cyclic case.

More generally suppose the supplied norm-square classes of an $S_3$ cubic have binary rank $r$. On the span in $L$ of all their conjugates, let $\sigma$ be a three-cycle and let $\tau$ be the transposition fixing $K$. The relation $1+\sigma+\sigma^2=0$ makes this span a vector space over $\mathbb F_4$, with $\sigma$ acting by a primitive scalar. Each input class is fixed by $\tau$, which conjugates $\sigma$ to $\sigma^2$. If a relation on the input classes $x_i$ over $\mathbb F_4$ is written $\sum_i(a_i+b_i\sigma)x_i=0$, with $a_i,b_i\in\mathbb F_2$, subtract its image under $\tau$. Since $\sigma+\sigma^2=1$, this gives $\sum_i b_i x_i=0$, and then $\sum_i a_i x_i=0$. Thus binary independence of input classes is equivalent to independence over $\mathbb F_4$ in the span of their conjugates. Its binary dimension is exactly $2r$, and the compositum of the quartic splitting fields has degree $4^r$ over $L$.

There is no fixed bound on $r$ for arbitrary square-norm lists. In $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$, $\alpha^3=2$, take a fresh prime $p>3$ congruent to $2$ modulo $3$. The cube map is bijective modulo $p$, so $X^3-2$ has one simple root and an irreducible quadratic factor there. Choose $x$ at the linear root modulo $p$ and away from its lift modulo $p^2$. Then $\eta=x-\alpha$ has valuation one at the degree-one prime and zero at the degree-two prime, and $v_p(N\eta)=1$. The element $\beta=\eta\,N\eta$ has norm $(N\eta)^4$ and odd valuation at the degree-two prime over $p$. Choosing $p$ away from the norms of all earlier elements makes each new class independent. There are infinitely many primes congruent to $2$ modulo $3$: for a finite purported list, $3$ times its product minus one has a new prime factor in that class.

A nonsquare class of square norm cannot be represented by a rational scalar times a square in a cubic field: the norm class of such a scalar $s$ is $[s]^3=[s]$. This still obstructs making the element a square by compositing with any tower of quadratic extensions of $\mathbb Q$. The normal closure $T$ of such a tower is a Galois extension of $2$-power degree, by adjoining all conjugate square roots at each step. It is linearly disjoint from the cubic. Every quadratic intermediate field of $KT/K$ is therefore the base change of a rational quadratic field and would force the same rational scalar representation.

The obstruction persists in a single class after even ideal valuations and real sign checks. In $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)$ the unit $\beta=\sqrt[3]2-1$ has norm one and is positive at the only real embedding. Its resolvent is $T^4+6T^2-8T-3$, which has no rational root by the rational-root test. These facts bound what a rational squareclass descent or a bounded-degree compositum can do. They leave other ways of deciding square relations open.

## A fixed-field algorithm using ideal lattices

For a fixed field with known maximal order and ramified primes, there is an independent way to compute exact ideal parity of arbitrary explicit columns without factoring their integer norms. It also applies with supplied maximal-order and ramified-prime data in a variable field.

Clear each input denominator by a rational square so that all its principal ideals are integral. Split these ideals into pairwise coprime ideal atoms using the gcd refinement from the [rational squareclass algorithm](cogentic-quadratic-square-kernel.md). Replace overlapping distinct ideals $I,J$ by $I+J,I/(I+J),J/(I+J)$. The quotients are integral. Their lattice computation uses $I:B=\{x:xB\subset I\}$, a set of integer linear membership conditions obtained from multiplication by a basis of $B$. The sum of log norms of the distinct ideals drops by at least $\log 2$ in each proper split. Exact quotient and containment recover the atom exponents in the original columns. All lattice determinants are bounded by the original ideal norms, so the arithmetic stays polynomial in the input size and supplied field data.

For an atom $J$, compute the Smith invariants of the integer lattice quotient $\mathcal O_K/J$. At an unramified rational prime $p$, the additive completion of the quotient has one summand $(\mathbb Z_p/p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(J)}\mathbb Z_p)^{f_{\mathfrak p}}$ for each $\mathfrak p\mid p$. This holds because $p$ is a uniformizer in every unramified completion and its integer ring is free of rank $f_{\mathfrak p}$ over $\mathbb Z_p$. Thus $v_{\mathfrak p}(J)$ is even for every ideal over every unramified $p$ exactly when each Smith integer is a square after removing its factors at the named ramified primes. At the named primes themselves, use exact ideal-power membership. Coprime atom supports now turn the even-ideal condition into binary rows, with one parity bit for each nonsquare ideal atom. Signed norm adds one independent necessary condition: an even ideal already has square absolute norm.

For a varying cubic with a supplied maximal-order table and named ramified primes, the ramified-prime decompositions also take deterministic polynomial bit work. For $A=\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K$, first compute its nilradical by the kernel of Frobenius to a power $p^t\ge3$. If $p$ is totally ramified, the nilradical is the sole maximal ideal of $A$. Otherwise the only ramified cubic pattern has one ramified degree-one component of length two and one copy of $\mathbb F_p$. The nilradical consists of the maximal ideal in the first component and zero in the second. Its annihilator is the maximal ideal of $A$ corresponding to the ramified component. The square of that annihilator is zero in the first component and all of $\mathbb F_p$ in the second. Its annihilator is therefore the other maximal ideal. Computing annihilators and products is finite-field linear algebra on the supplied table, including at 2 and 3. Lift the two ideals to the full order for the ideal-power membership tests. The procedure requires no search for roots across $\mathbb F_p$.

In $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ with $\alpha^3=2$, the full integral basis is $1,\alpha,\alpha^2$. The polynomial discriminant is $-108$, and 2 and 3 are both Eisenstein primes after using $\alpha$ at 2 and $\alpha+1$ at 3. A root of an Eisenstein polynomial over $\mathbb Q_p$ generates the local integer ring, so these check both possible index primes. Both ramified primes have one degree-one prime above them, so the determinant of an ideal lattice directly gives their valuation parities. Minkowski's bound is less than 3 in this field, and the unique prime ideal of norm two is principal, generated by $\alpha$. The class number is consequently one. An even-ideal product of positive norm is a unit times a square. Dirichlet's unit theorem gives unit rank one, and the sign $-1$ has negative norm, so the norm-one unit squareclass space has dimension one. The class of $\alpha-1$ supplies its nonzero vector. Test each ideal-and-norm kernel basis product by the quartic resolvent. Its single residual class bit is zero if it is square and one otherwise. Impose this row and recover exact roots in the final basis. The replay also checks that a product assigned the nonzero bit becomes square on multiplication by $\alpha-1$.

The general fixed-field version can instead use the [adaptive square-kernel algorithm](cogentic-adaptive-square-kernel.md) after ideal parity. The residual rank in a cubic with positive norm is at most $r_1+r_2-1+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]$. The half-ideal map lands in the class group two-torsion, its kernel comes from units, and the rational norm sign kills one dimension since $-1$ has negative norm. Thus with supplied order and ramification data its cost is polynomial in the explicit list times $2^{r_1+r_2-1+\dim\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]}$. This is polynomial for a fixed field and conditional on a small class-rank bound for a varying field. It does not bound the residual rank uniformly for growing cubic discriminants.
