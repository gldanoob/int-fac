# Local roots for a reduction to the growing-degree Pell family

The two factors of a Pell input are a Lucas trace and a second explicit polynomial. A target divides a Pell input exactly when each of its prime powers lies on one of these two factors. A useful input must place different prime divisors on different factors. Every even exponent fails already modulo five. More generally, any fixed finite exponent menu misses infinitely many targets with arbitrarily large prime factors. A deterministic Lucas trace scan does give a proper factor under a local order promise. [Productive Pell field pairs](cogentic-growing-pell-pair.md)

## The two modular factors

Let the odd target be $n>1$. Use the trace parameter $Y$ and put $C=Y^2-4$. Formally take $Y=z+z^{-1}$ and $t=z-z^{-1}$, so $C=t^2$. Define

\[
A_j(Y)=z^j+z^{-j},\qquad E_j(Y)=\frac{z^j-z^{-j}}{z-z^{-1}},\qquad
B_j(Y)=\frac{(C+1)A_j(Y)+2C E_j(Y)}4 .
\]

Here $E_j$ is a polynomial, defined also at $Y=\pm2$. Both $A_j,E_j$ satisfy the recurrence $D_{j+1}=Y D_j-D_{j-1}$ with initial pairs $(2,Y)$ and $(0,1)$. In the Pell family $V+Ut=(C+t)z^j$ gives

\[
V-U=(C-1)A_j/2,\qquad V+U=2B_j,\qquad N_j=U^2+C=A_jB_j .
\]

The last equality follows from $(V-U)(V+U)=(C-1)N_j$ as a polynomial identity. Evaluation modulo an odd $n$ uses only inverses of powers of two. The original construction takes $Y=2m+1$ and $j\equiv1\bmod3$, with $m$ odd. The later exact-value construction takes $Y=m+1$ and $j$ even, again with $m$ odd. Arbitrary residues of $Y\bmod n$ can be lifted to the required parity class by CRT in either construction.

## Local solvability

Fix $p^a\mid n$ with $p$ odd, and write $\epsilon=(-1/p)$ and $M=p-\epsilon$. If $C\equiv0\bmod p$, then $Y\equiv\pm2$ and $N_j\equiv1\bmod p$. Otherwise take $z$ as one of the reciprocal roots of $X^2-YX+1$. The roots belong to the split multiplicative group if $C$ is square modulo $p$, and to the quadratic norm-one group if $C$ is nonsquare. The conditions below force the group with order $M$, since it is the only one of the two groups containing roots of order four. Write $\mathcal T_{p^a}$ for the lift of that group to modulus $p^a$. In the inert case use the unramified quadratic extension of $\mathbb Z/p^a\mathbb Z$.

The exact two conditions are

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
A_j(Y)=0 &\Longleftrightarrow z^{2j}=-1,\\[2pt]
B_j(Y)=0 &\Longleftrightarrow z^j=\pm\iota\,(t-1)/(t+1),\quad t\ne\pm1\bmod p,
\end{array}
\]

where $Y=z+z^{-1}$, $z\in\mathcal T_{p^a}$, $z\ne\pm1\bmod p$, and $\iota^2=-1$ belongs to the same group. Indeed,

\[
4B_j=(t+1)^2z^j+(t-1)^2z^{-j}.
\]

If $t=\pm1\bmod p$, one summand is a unit while the other vanishes. Otherwise $(t-1)/(t+1)$ is a unit in the split case and has norm one in the inert case. The equation for $B_j$ therefore forces a square root of $-1$ in that group. At an $A_j$ root, $z^{-j}=-z^j$ and $B_j=t z^j$ is a unit. Thus the two branches are disjoint at every odd prime.

Write $j=2^s h$ with $h$ odd. The group $\mathcal T_{p^a}$ is cyclic of order $M p^{a-1}$. The prime-to-$p$ part lifts uniquely. The kernel of reduction has order $p^{a-1}$ and is cyclic, generated by $1+p$ in the split case and $(1+p\iota)/(1-p\iota)$ in the inert case. The number of trace roots of $A_j\bmod p^a$ is consequently

\[
\begin{cases}
2^s\gcd(h,M)\,p^{\min(v_p(j),a-1)},&v_2(M)\ge s+2,\\
0,&v_2(M)<s+2 .
\end{cases}
\]

This counts the solutions of $z^{2j}=-1$ in the cyclic group, divided by two for inversion. Traces identify only reciprocal pairs because the two quadratic roots are distinct modulo $p$. In particular, for $j=4^e$ the condition is $p\equiv\pm1\bmod 4^{e+1}$.

The $B_j$ test has an additional rational-function constraint in the torus. A root modulo $p$ lifts uniquely to every $p^a$ unless

\[
j(C-1)=2Y\bmod p.
\]

To see this, put $\Phi=z^j(t+1)/(t-1)$. Its logarithmic derivative is $z\Phi'/\Phi=j-2Y/(C-1)$ and $dY/dz=t/z$ is a unit. These derivatives test simplicity at $\Phi^2=-1$. In the singular case lifting from $y\bmod p^k$ requires $B_j(y)/p^k+uB_j'(y)=0\bmod p$ for $u\bmod p$, then the same test at the next power. For example $B_4\bmod37$ has the unique root $Y=21$, which is singular, and $B_4(21)\equiv148\bmod37^2$. It has no lift, and the trace branch is absent at $37$. Divisibility by $37$ therefore does not give divisibility by $37^2$ for this exponent.

## Obstructions to an arbitrary target

At $p=5$ the forced torus has order four. Its only elements other than $\pm1$ have $t^2=1$, so $B_j$ has no root for any $j$. The trace branch has no root when $j$ is even. When $j$ is odd, $Y=0$ is a root of $A_j$ modulo every odd modulus, giving a global embedding with every prime on the same branch.

Every fixed finite exponent menu has an obstruction even after removing small primes. Take the splitting field of all the monic polynomials $4B_j$ in the menu and of $A_j$ for its even exponents. Fix a prime above five and lift its residue Frobenius to an element of the decomposition group. This lift fixes none of the listed integral roots, since a fixed root would reduce into $\mathbb F_5$. Chebotarev gives infinitely many primes whose Frobenius has that conjugacy class. After excluding the discriminants of the squarefree parts, all listed polynomials have no root at those primes. Products of two such primes admit no embedding for the even exponents and only the trace branch for the odd exponents. The argument concerns a menu fixed independently of $n$. [Chebotarev's theorem](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1996d/cheb.pdf)

For fixed $j$, an embedding exists exactly when each prime power of $n$ has a root on at least one branch, by CRT on $Y$ and the parity condition. A productive embedding further requires that two distinct prime divisors can be assigned to different branches. For any global root of $N_j\bmod n$, each prime power divides exactly one of $A_j,B_j$. A mixed choice yields the factor $\gcd(A_j,n)$, or equivalently the nontrivial CRT idempotent $A_j/(A_j+B_j)\bmod n$. Constructing a mixed root from the prime-power data is a CRT step once a local root on the required branch is known. Finding one global root alone, even when automatic for odd $j$, supplies no separation of the prime powers.

## A promised class with a bounded trace scan

Fix $Y=7$ and test $j=4^e$ for $e=0,1,\ldots,\lceil(\log_2 n)/2\rceil$. At each step take $\gcd(A_j(7),n)$. Compute the terms modulo $n$ by two doublings between successive stages,

\[
A_{2j}=A_j^2-2,\qquad E_{2j}=A_j E_j .
\]

This deterministically gives a proper divisor whenever some hidden prime of $n$ divides $A_{4^e}(7)$ and another prime of $n$ does not divide that same term. The bound suffices because $4^{e+1}\mid M$ at the first prime. The promise covers infinitely many prime supports. The positive odd integers $A_{4^e}(7)>1$ are pairwise coprime, since a prime dividing one stage sees $-2$ at the next doubling and $2$ at every later doubling. Arbitrary products with a prime from one stage and another prime outside that stage qualify. An optional $A_jB_j\equiv0\bmod n$ test at a proper gcd recognizes the stronger promise that this same trace supplies a mixed embedding.

The split step is a Lucas order test of the same type as $p\pm1$ factoring, testing the order-four stage in place of the final identity. It needs no computation of integer $N_j$. The modular recurrence is polynomial in $\log n+\log j$, but writing a whole family input uses $\Theta(j\log m)$ bits. With $j=4^e$, a polynomial-size explicit output requires $e=O(\log\log n)$. Thus the bounded modular scan is broader than a polynomial-size reduction to the explicit family. [Williams's Lucas factoring method](https://www.ams.org/mcom/1982-39-159/S0025-5718-1982-0658227-7/S0025-5718-1982-0658227-7.pdf)
