# Smooth ideals in every ordinary quadratic class with a small correction

Let \(K\) be an imaginary quadratic field of negative fundamental discriminant \(D\equiv1\pmod8\), \(\sqrt{|D|}\asymp V\), and \(Z\le V^{C_0}\) a positive coprimality modulus. Take \(\log X\asymp d\log V\), \(y=V^{c+o(1)}\) for fixed \(c>0\), \(d\to\infty\), \(\log V=d^{O(1)}\) and \(d\log\log X=o(\log V)\). Write \(u=\log X/\log y\). Under Dirichlet GRH, in **each prescribed ordinary ideal class** there are at least
\[
 \frac X{\sqrt{|D|}}\exp(-(1+o(1))u\log u)
\]
distinct integral ideals of norm at most \(X\), coprime to \(2Z\), with the following properties. All rational primes dividing their norms split or ramify in \(K\). Their norms are products of bin primes at most \(y\), a split prime of order \(V\), and an integer at most \(V\exp(O((\log\log V)^2))\). In particular at most \(O_c(1)\) prime factors counted with multiplicity exceed \(y\); each of those is at most \(V\exp(O((\log\log V)^2))\). If \(y\ge V^{1+\xi}\) for fixed \(\xi>0\), the full norm is \(y\)-smooth for large \(V\). Constants can depend on the fixed scale bounds.

This is a lower count for a disk, using an ordinary ideal class and coprimality with a modulus. The construction below also permits primitive output by keeping the correcting ideal coprime to all input primes. An affine progression for generators needs a separate argument. ## A short representative avoiding the modulus

Each class has a primitive reduced representative \([A,B,C]\), \(A\ll V\). There are \(O(W(1+\log W))\) classes with \(A\le W\). For each \(A\) their middle coefficients satisfy \(|B|\le A\) and \(B^2\equiv D\bmod 4A\). At an odd ramified prime there is one root at the first power and none at higher powers since \(D\) is squarefree. The other odd primes have at most two unit roots, and the 2-part contributes at most four. Summing the resulting divisor bound uses \(\sum_{n\le W}2^{\omega(n)}\le W(1+\log W)\). Outside \(O(V/(\log V)^3)\) classes one has \(A\ge V/(\log V)^4\) and therefore \(\max(A,|B|,C)\ll V(\log V)^4\).

For every one of the latter classes choose a primitive represented integer coprime to \(2Z\) as follows. Use \((t,v)\) with \(v=1\) or \(2\) and a fixed parity of \(t\) so the represented value is odd and \((t,v)=1\). Such a choice exists by primitivity modulo two: test \((0,1),(1,1),(1,0)\). For each odd prime \(q\mid Z\), at most two residues of \(t\) are forbidden, because the reduction at the nonzero second coordinate is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two.

Here a direct finite sieve gives \(1\le t\le T=\exp(C_1(\log\log V)^2)\) for some sufficiently large fixed \(C_1\). Parametrize the chosen parity first. Put \(z=(\log V)^4\) and sieve the primes \(q\le z\) dividing \(Z\). Their independent residue densities \(\alpha_q\le2/q\) have \(\prod(1-\alpha_q)\gg(\log z)^{-O(1)}\) and \(\sum\alpha_q=O(\log\log z)\), by the reciprocal-prime bound. Truncate inclusion-exclusion at an odd \(s\asymp\log\log V\). Its ideal truncation error is at most \(\sum_{j>s}(\sum\alpha_q)^j/j!\), negligible compared to the product. Each CRT intersection of \(j\) events costs at most \(O(2^j/T)\) in normalized interval error. There are only \(O(\log V)\) prime divisors of \(Z\), so \(C_1\) makes their total truncation and rounding cost negligible as well. The sum of the excluded densities from primes \(q>z\) dividing \(Z\) is \(O(\log V\,(1/z+1/T))\). This proves existence of the required \(t\).

A primitive representation of this integer gives a primitive integral ideal of that norm in the chosen class, by extending the representing vector to a unimodular basis and taking the corresponding form ideal. Its norm is at most \(L=V\exp(C_2(\log\log V)^2)\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(C_2\). It contains no inert rational prime in its norm: the sole prime ideal over an inert prime is the rational principal ideal \((q)\), incompatible with primitivity. Fix one such correcting ideal per good class. These representatives are used to count a subset, so finding them during a top test is unnecessary.

## Producing each class

Form the prime-bin products of the balanced strip at \(x=X/(C_3 V L)\), taking \(C_3\) large enough to leave room for the next two factors. In every bin use rational primes split in \(K\) and coprime to \(2Z\), with a fixed choice of prime ideal above each. The quadratic Dirichlet character of \(D\) and Dirichlet GRH give the required positive proportion in each prime bin. Bin endpoints are \(V^{c+o(1)}\), their relative widths are of order \(1/d\), and the primes excluded by \(2ZD\) have negligible cost. The constant loss per split-prime bin contributes \(O(d)=o(u\log u)\) to the log count. Replacing \(X\) by \(x\) changes the smoothness argument by \(O_c(1)\). Thus there are at least \(x\exp(-(1+o(1))u\log u)\) input ideals with distinct squarefree bin products.

Independently choose a split guard prime \(p\in[\sqrt{|D|}/16,\sqrt{|D|}/8]\), coprime to \(2Z\), with fixed orientation \(\mathfrak p\). Dirichlet GRH gives \(\gg V/\log V\) choices. Distinct guard primes give distinct ideal classes. Otherwise \(\mathfrak p_1\overline{\mathfrak p}_2=(\alpha)\) with \(|N(\alpha)|=p_1p_2<|D|/4\). Every nonrational algebraic integer has imaginary part of absolute size at least \(\sqrt{|D|}/2\); a rational generator instead has square norm. Both possibilities exclude distinct primes. For each input, the complementary classes determined by these choices are therefore distinct. At most \(O(V/(\log V)^3)\) are among the discarded classes. Multiply each remaining input and guard by the fixed correcting ideal of its complementary class. This gives an integral ideal of norm at most \(X\), in the prescribed class, coprime to \(2Z\).

There are \(k\asymp d\) bin primes in the input. The guard and correction contain only \(O_c(1)\) additional rational primes in the bin size range because their product norm is \(V^{2+o(1)}\). Given the output norm, at most \(k^{O_c(1)}\) choices of the original \(k\)-element subset and the guard are possible: the guard divides the final norm, and every correction has \(O(\log V)\) prime divisors in all. The fixed orientations then recover the input and guard ideals. This upper bound is polynomial in \(d\) in the stated regime. Taking numerical norms or integral ideals thus loses at most a polynomial factor. Combining the estimates gives the claimed count since \(\log(L/V)=O((\log\log V)^2)=o(u\log u)\).

A product can contain both conjugate primes above a split rational prime if the correction overlaps the input. The counting argument retains such an unstripped product. Dividing away its rational content could collapse the count and plays no role here. No conclusion about equidistribution among ray classes or in a thin angular sector is used.


## Primitive output at the same cost

First remove guard primes already used by the input, costing at most \(k\) of the \(\gg V/\log V\) choices. When constructing the correcting ideal, exclude every input prime and the guard in addition to the primes dividing \(2Z\). These extra primes exceed \(T\) for large \(V\), since they are \(V^{c+o(1)}\) or of order \(V\). Each costs at most two surviving choices of \(t\) in the previous sieve (or a fixed constant after parametrizing parity). Thus \(O(d)\) further choices are lost from a population of size \(\gg T/(\log\log V)^{O(1)}\), which still leaves a choice since \(d=o(\log V)\). Fix the correcting representative for the class and this specific list of excluded primes. The recovery bound is unaffected because the input and guard still determine the representative. Now each of the input, the guard and the correction is primitive, and their rational norms are pairwise coprime. Hence the final ideal is primitive. This version never divides an output ideal by an integer.

## A polylogarithmic correction allowance

The usual fundamental lemma of the sieve improves the uniform correction bound to \(L=V(\log V)^{C_4}\). Keep the parity choice and \(z=(\log V)^4\). On a parity interval of length \(T=(\log V)^{C_5}\), the squarefree divisibility counts for the forbidden odd residue sets have expected local factors \(\alpha_q\le2/q\) and remainder at most \(2^{\omega(e)}\) at modulus \(e\). The dimension-two product bound \(\prod_{w\le q<z}(1-\alpha_q)^{-1}\ll(\log z/\log w)^2\) follows from Mertens, uniformly in the subset of primes and the primitive form. Use a fixed sufficiently large sieve parameter \(s\), level \(z^s\), and choose \(C_5>8s\). The lower sieve leaves \(\gg T/(\log z)^2\) choices before the remaining prime divisors. The total remainder up to the level is \(O(z^s(1+\log z))\), by the squarefree divisor sum. Prime divisors of \(Z\) beyond \(z\) and the \(O(d)\) extra primes of the input and guard still cost strictly less than this lower count. The coefficients of the balanced representative and \(T^2\) are all polylogarithmic multiples of their indicated scales, proving the improvement. All constants here are fixed. The elementary truncation above remains sufficient for leading exponents.
