# Seven local half-point targets on one fixed elliptic curve

Seven fixed targets on one elliptic curve guarantee a local relation at every good odd prime, using only the subgroup generated by three public rational points. On a limiting fraction at least $315/512$ of balanced pairs of distinct primes, both primes admit a relation that fails at the other. A deterministic one-third-time algorithm finds a split on those pairs if the odd part of the local subgroup exponent at **either** prime has all prime divisors within the chosen one-third bound. Adding the curve’s two public torsion generators raises the proved separation density to $987/1024$ with the same odd-exponent condition. The density counts separation alone. No corresponding lower bound on the frequency of the smoothness condition is used here.

## Local supply and separation

Use $E:Y^2=(X+1496)(X+264)(X+204)$ and $R_1=(-1344,13680),R_2=(-544,9520),R_3=(-280,1216)$. For each nonzero $s\in\mathbb F_2^3$, interpret its entries as $0,1$, set $T_s=\sum_i s_iR_i$, and take the fiber of the doubling map above $T_s$. None of these seven fibers has a rational point. The homomorphism for the root $-1496$ sends the three generators to independent rational square classes. [Points and elementary chord proof](cogentic-elliptic-cycle-density.md#independent-directions)

For an odd good prime $p$, put $G_p=\langle R_i\bmod p\rangle$. Since $G_p[2]\subset E[2]$ has size at most four, $G_p/2G_p$ has dimension at most two. Some nonzero $s$ therefore gives $T_s\in 2G_p$. In particular an integer word in the very same generators halves $T_s$. Equivalently the local relation lattice
\[
 L_p=\{z\in\mathbb Z^3:\textstyle\sum_i z_iR_i=O\bmod p\}
\]
contains an element of parity $s$. This follows by writing the half as $\sum_i w_iR_i$ and taking $z=2w-s$. The claim also holds when the reduction of a particular target is the identity.

Use the two square-class homomorphisms associated to $-1496,-264$. With rows corresponding to those roots and columns to $R_i$, their rational values modulo squares are
\[
 \begin{pmatrix} 2\cdot19 & 2\cdot7\cdot17 & 19 \\
 -2\cdot3\cdot5 & -2\cdot5\cdot7 & -1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
At odd primes away from the fixed bad and supporting primes, taking Legendre signs (minus maps to one) gives a binary matrix $C_p$. Every local relation of parity $s$ gives $C_p s=0$, since both homomorphisms kill doubles. For $N=pq$, if the two matrices stacked vertically have rank three, no common relation can have nonzero parity. Consequently any locally attained half in $G_p$ for one of the seven targets fails at $q$, and conversely.

The six square classes in the display are independent. In a dependence use the prime 17 to eliminate the second entry of row one, the prime 3 to eliminate the first of row two, and the prime 5 to eliminate the second of row two. The remaining entries are independent by the primes 2,19 and the sign. The corresponding multiquadratic characters therefore assume all 64 patterns with equal prime density, by quadratic reciprocity and the prime number theorem in fixed progressions. Choosing distinct primes independently from $(Y,2Y]$ makes the stacked matrix asymptotically uniform. Its three columns are independent with probability
\[
 \frac{15\cdot14\cdot12}{16^3}=\frac{315}{512}.
\]
The same limiting fraction applies to unordered pairs. This is a sufficient separation test at the hidden primes. The algorithm does not receive those primes or their character matrices.

## Finding a relation after an odd public power

Fix an odd positive integer $U$ and set $Q_i=[U]R_i$ using modular curve arithmetic. The character columns for $Q_i$ equal the original ones. Choose $k=\lceil\log_2(4N)\rceil$. If the odd part of the exponent of $G_p$ divides $U$, all three $[2^k]Q_i$ are the identity at $p$, by Hasse's bound. Test these relations by repeated doubling modulo $N$.

Use the checked modular group law from the [elliptic splitter](../experiments/cogentic-elliptic-cycle-split.py). A nonzero nonunit denominator or a comparison of equal abscissas with different signs returns a proper gcd. As long as no factor has appeared, the result of every addition is either affine at both primes or the identity at both. At an equal abscissa the curve equation gives $(y_1-y_2)(y_1+y_2)=0$; compare signs and test gcds before choosing the tangent or the identity. Therefore a one-sided identity during any relation test exposes a proper factor. If the three initial tests do not split such a promised input, all three are global relations.

Start with a basis of global relations $b_i=2^ke_i$, or the smaller verified powers of two. For each nonempty subset of basis columns whose sum is coordinatewise even, test the integer half $w$ of that sum. If it is a global relation, replace any one of the selected columns by $w$ and restart. The old column equals $2w$ minus the other selected columns, so the lattice increases by index exactly two. Each test evaluates an ordinary signed integer combination of the $Q_i$, and an identity at only one prime returns a factor. At most $3k$ enlargements can occur. Using the smaller of the initial powers when available is optional.

If no enlargement and no split occur, the global lattice in hand equals both local relation lattices for the $Q_i$. Indeed any proper local superlattice is a subgroup of the finite two-group quotient of $\mathbb Z^3$ by the current lattice. Its quotient by the current lattice would contain an element of order two. For any basis, representatives of all such possible order-two cosets are exactly the integral nonempty subset-halves tested above. This proves the claim. Both equal local lattices would then contain a common nonzero parity relation by the local supply argument, impossible when the stacked matrix has rank three. This proves termination with a proper divisor under the hypotheses. More generally the same algorithm separates any unequal powered local relation lattices if its three initial powers are relations at either prime. Coordinate bit lengths stay $O(k)$ because each update at worst multiplies the largest entry by $3/2$ for at most $3k$ rounds.

For an input-only version, choose $B$ and take
\[
 U=\prod_{3\le\ell\le B\ {\rm prime}}\ell^{\lfloor\log_\ell(4N)\rfloor},
\]
omitting primes greater than $4N$. This kills the odd part of every eligible subgroup at a prime divisor of $N$ if all its odd exponent primes are at most $B$. A sieve, the construction of $U$ and the three modular multiplications take $B\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N,\log B)$ bit work using fast integer products; alternatively apply the prime powers in sequence. The remaining operations cost $\mathrm{poly}(\log N)$. Thus $B=\exp(O((\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}))$ fits the one-third budget. This uses a large numerical scalar with short enough computation. Supplying suitable scalars on every input, or finding the one-sided halves quickly when a large odd part survives, remains open.

For a small example, the primes 149 and 131 both lack a root of the original sextic. For the separate doubling target $R_3$, the point $R_1+2R_2-R_3$ halves $R_3$ at one of the primes, and the modular comparison splits $19519$. [Fixed sextic and square unit](cogentic-square-mark-cycle.md)

## Including the two known torsion points

Adjoin $T_1=(-1496,0)$ and $T_2=(-264,0)$ as generators four and five, with the same odd multiplier. They retain their orders and leave the odd exponent condition unchanged. For the five directions the local character table is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 38&238&19&7\cdot11\cdot17\cdot19&7\cdot11\\
 -30&-70&-1&-7\cdot11&-3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
At the zero of a coordinate use the derivative of the Weierstrass cubic there, as in the chord proof.

The generated group contains all the rational two-torsion after good odd reduction. Its quotient by doubles therefore has dimension exactly two. The binary space of attained local parity relations has dimension three in $\mathbb F_2^5$, including after any odd power. If the stacked character matrix has rank at least three, its kernel has dimension at most two. At least four local parity classes at either prime are therefore impossible at the opposite prime. Shared local relation lattices would contradict this dimension bound. The saturation algorithm now uses 31 subset tests per round and at most $5k$ enlargements. Integer coefficient sizes stay $O(k)$ by the same argument with $5/2$ in place of $3/2$.

The square classes $-1,2,3,5,7,11,17,19$ are independent. Encode one prime by their eight Legendre bits; each of the 256 possibilities has the same limiting frequency. For each of the $256^2$ ordered pairs form the four rows by adding the indicated character bits in each table entry. Exact binary elimination gives:

| Rank of the four rows | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Number of ordered sign patterns | 1 | 69 | 2,298 | 23,328 | 39,840 |

Thus **$987/1024$** of balanced prime pairs asymptotically satisfy the separation condition with these five fixed generators. The same enumeration can fix the first prime: if its matrix has rank one, at least 212 of the 256 partner patterns give separation, and if its matrix has rank two, at least 248 do. This gives a partner density conditional on any particular first-prime character pattern, without assuming independence between smooth group order and the characters of that first prime. The remaining requirement for the one-third runtime guarantee is that the odd local exponent be covered by the public multiplier at some prime. The torsion-augmented search splits $62471=179\cdot349$ during the mixed-relation tests. On $17711=89\cdot199$ with bound 5, all three original powered points have order two at both primes, and a mixed relation splits the input. The finite suite also compares the two actual relation lattices for 378 small prime pairs. A bounded miss remains possible on other inputs.
