# Cubic signed square kernels using the different

For an explicit cubic field with a supplied maximal-order basis, ideal parity of arbitrary explicit elements can be computed without naming or factoring any rational prime. The different separates the ramified ideal factors. The Smith invariants then recover parity of the remaining factors, including unramified companions of ramified primes. Combining this step with exact cubic square tests gives a rank-sensitive signed square-kernel generator. It finishes in the one-third budget on a density-one family of height-ordered monogenized cubics. [GP replay and API](../experiments/cogentic-cubic-different-kernel.gp)

## Separate the ramified factors by the different

Let $K$ be cubic, let $B=\mathcal O_K$ be given by its explicit integral basis, and let $I$ be a nonzero integral ideal given by an integer lattice in that basis. Compute the different $\mathfrak D$ as the reciprocal of the trace dual $B^\vee=\{x:\operatorname{Tr}(xB)\subseteq\mathbb Z\}$. Invert the trace matrix to get the dual basis, then compute the lattice colon $B:B^\vee$. The trace form is nondegenerate, and the dual ideal is invertible in the maximal order. For a maximal monogenic basis the same different is generated by the polynomial derivative.

Take an integer $j\ge\operatorname{bitlength}(\mathrm N I)$ and put

$$R=I+\mathfrak D^j,\qquad U=I/R.$$

For every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, $v_{\mathfrak p}(R)=\min(v_{\mathfrak p}(I),jv_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak D))$. The different has positive exponent exactly at a ramified prime ideal. This holds for wild primes as well. Since $v_{\mathfrak p}(I)\le\log_2\mathrm N I$, the ideal $R$ consists of precisely the ramified factors of $I$, with their full exponents. The ideal $U$ consists of precisely the factors at unramified prime ideals, including any unramified factors above a rational prime which ramifies in another branch.

In a cubic, a rational prime has at most one ramified prime ideal above it. Any ramified prime ideal has residue degree one, since $e f\le3$ and $e\ge2$. It follows that $\mathrm N R$ is a square integer exactly when all its prime ideal exponents are even. For $U$, compute the integer Smith invariants of $B/U$. At every rational prime $p$, its completed additive quotient is the product of the quotients at prime ideals above $p$, with the ramified factors zero. At an unramified prime ideal of residue degree $f$, valuation $a$ gives an additive summand $\bigl(\mathbb Z_p/p^a\mathbb Z_p\bigr)^f$ because $p$ is a uniformizer. Thus the $p$-exponents of the Smith invariants, with zeros included, are the multiset of the $a$ values repeated by their residue degrees. Every prime ideal exponent in $U$ is even exactly when each Smith invariant is a square. The two integer square criteria together decide whether $I$ is an ideal square.

This handles wild ramification at 2 or 3 without the tame formula for the exponent of the different. In the decomposition pattern with one $e=2$ branch and an unramified companion, the two valuations are separated before either square test. Their contributions cannot cancel as they might in the rational norm.

## Exact arithmetic and signed relations

The ideal $R$ can be computed by repeated Hermite normal forms without first constructing the expanded lattice $\mathfrak D^j$. Start at $R_1=I+\mathfrak D$ and use $R_{2k}=I+R_k^2$. Indeed $R_k^2=\mathfrak D^{2k}+I\mathfrak D^k+I^2$, and the last two summands are contained in $I$. Stop once the exponent reaches the threshold or an iteration stabilizes. At most $O(\log(2+\log\mathrm N I))$ doublings suffice. Every retained lattice lies between $I$ and $B$. Products multiply basis matrices using the supplied order's multiplication table, and the output HNF has determinant at most $\mathrm N I$. Computing the trace matrix, HNF, Smith invariants, and exact colon lattices has polynomial bit complexity in the explicit inputs. The raw product matrices also have bounded bit size since the ambient degree is fixed and the operand HNFs have entries bounded by their determinants.

For a list $z_i\in K^\times$, first clear its coordinate denominators by rational squares and take its principal integral ideals. Split them into coprime ideal atoms by sums and exact quotients as in the [fixed cubic argument](cogentic-cubic-square-resolvent.md#fixed-field-algorithm-using-ideal-lattices). The sum of log norms of the distinct atoms drops at each proper split. Test the atoms with the different and Smith criterion. The parity of the exponent of each nonsquare atom is a row for the ideal-square condition. Add the sign of the rational norm as another row, since the absolute rational norm of an even-ideal product is already a square. Input $-1$ explicitly for a free sign coordinate. This first kernel is exact in polynomial bit time for the ideal and norm conditions.

The remaining squareclass dimension is at most

$$ r_1+r_2-1+\dim_{\mathbb F_2}\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]. $$

On the even-ideal group modulo squares, the half-ideal map has image in the class group two-torsion and kernel given by unit classes. Rational norm sign removes a unit dimension since $-1$ has negative cubic norm. Run the [adaptive basis search](cogentic-adaptive-square-kernel.md) using the [quartic resolvent](cogentic-cubic-square-resolvent.md#a-quartic-for-a-single-square-norm-class) for each candidate product. It returns exact roots together with every basis vector. Its total cost is bounded by a polynomial in the explicit input size times $2^{r_1+r_2-1}|\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]|$. A cap on square queries reports whether the full decision finished and leaves every returned root certified if it did not. No class group or unit generators are computed.

The maximal basis is an input promise. Checking that the supplied basis spans a unital order checks only closure and the identity. Finding or certifying the maximal order from an arbitrary polynomial is separate. For example, a supplied complete prime decomposition of an order discriminant permits a check of local maximality at every possible index prime. A certified monogenizer supplies the basis directly as $1,\alpha,\alpha^2$. The square-kernel cost does not include producing either kind of certificate.

## An unconditional varying family and a conditional uniform bound

Bhargava, Hanke and Shankar prove that $|\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]|$ has limiting mean $3/2$ for totally real and $2$ for complex monogenized cubic fields ordered by height, counted as pairs $(K,\alpha)$ modulo integral translations of the monogenizer. Their height equals $\max(|I|^3,J^2/4)$ in the monogenic case. Its logarithm bounds the coefficient bit sizes after normalizing the quadratic coefficient modulo three, and it bounds $\log(2+|\Delta_K|)$ from above up to an absolute constant. The kernel algorithm therefore has polynomial mean bit cost in the uniform explicit size bound on the field presentations and element lists in this population. In particular, at least $1/2-o(1)$ of the totally real pairs have odd class number. At least $2/3-o(1)$ of the complex pairs have class-group 2-rank at most one. The exact capped generator completes with polynomial bit work on each of these varying low-rank families. [Bhargava–Hanke–Shankar, Theorem 4 and equations (1)–(2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.15744#page=4)

More generally, let $L=\log N$, $V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}$, and $W=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}$. In the population with monogenized height at most $\exp(O(V))$ and lists of uniform explicit size $\exp(O(W))$, Markov's inequality gives a fraction $O(2^{-cW})$ with class-group 2-rank greater than $cW$, for any fixed $c>0$. The signed kernel is recovered in $\exp(O(W))$ on the complementary family. This density concerns monogenized pairs in the specified height ordering and does not transfer to the cubic selected by a shared-root rule as the factoring input varies.

For a pointwise uniform statement with a supplied maximal order, the existing [character cutoff under Dedekind GRH](cogentic-even-squareclass-cutoff.md) can replace the rank-sensitive search after the first kernel. The needed hypotheses are Dedekind GRH for the base field and for the quadratic extensions defined by its nonsquare even-ideal products. They are stronger than the Dirichlet critical-line assumption alone. For arbitrary explicit columns, scale each into its integral power basis by a rational square. A simple-root probe away from the defining discriminant is then computed by Hensel evaluation modulo $p^{v_p(\mathrm N z)+1}$. This evaluates each leading-unit character in polynomial bit work and leaves the squareclass unchanged. Under the stated hypotheses the cutoff yields the complete signed kernel in $\exp(O(W))$ whenever the defining polynomial and supplied basis have polynomial bit size in $L$ and the explicit list has size $\exp(O(W))$. The unconditional pointwise obstacle for arbitrary varying cubics is the residual class-rank or character-detection step, in addition to maximal-order input preparation where it is needed.
