# What modular power tests can detect in a prescribed progression

The [ring class polynomial criterion](cm-isogeny-prime-filters.md) detects exactly $p\equiv11\bmod12$ among primes $3<p\le X$. One possible arithmetic shortcut would express this support through divisibilities of the form $p\mid A^M\pm B^M$ for public rational bases $A/B$. Small catalogs of such tests miss a positive proportion of **every** fixed reduced progression in each sufficiently large dyad. The same conclusion holds for growing moduli $h=X^{o(1)}$ using the proved Dirichlet GRH. Catalogs and bases can change with $X$.

Let $X<p\le2X$, fix $1/2<\delta<1-\tfrac12e^{-1/4}$ and write $\eta_\delta=1/2-2\log(1/[2(1-\delta)])>0$. In a reduced class $a\bmod h$, at least $(\eta_\delta-o(1))X/(\varphi(h)\log X)$ primes have a prime $s>X^\delta$ dividing $p-1$. The classical argument is Goldfeld's weighted count for shifted primes, here applied in the specified progression and dyad. Stronger exponents are known for unrestricted shifted primes. [Goldfeld's original proof](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/PrimeShifts.pdf) · [Kurlberg–Pomerance, Lemmas 19–20 and Baker–Harman](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/par11.pdf#page=13) · [Related bound for exponents depending linearly on factor pairs](round-2.md)

## Counting the primes with a large divisor of their predecessor

The total of $\log(p-1)$ on this dyad and class is $(1+o(1))X/\varphi(h)$. This follows for $h=X^{o(1)}$ from the prime number theorem in progressions with its Dirichlet GRH error. The weight from primes $s\mid h$ is negligible, including their higher powers. Up to $v_s(h)$ copies contribute at most $\log h=o(\log X)$ per $p$. For an extra copy at depth $j\ge1$, when the congruences are compatible the combined modulus is $hs^j$. For $s^j\le X^{1/10}$ the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality on an interval of length $X$ gives total $O(X\log(2h)/(\varphi(h)\log X))$, using $\varphi(hs^j)=\varphi(h)s^j$. For $s^j>X^{1/10}$ use $X/(hs^j)+1$. The geometric tails contribute $O(X^{9/10}\log(2h)/h)$ and the rounding errors contribute $O(\log(2h)\log X)$. These estimates are $o(X/\varphi(h))$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, sharp large sieve and Brun–Titchmarsh](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300004708) · [Dirichlet GRH verification and exact scope](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

For $s\nmid h$, powers $s^j$ with $j\ge2$ also cost $o(X/\varphi(h))$. Below $s^j\le\log X$ apply Brun–Titchmarsh with modulus $hs^j$ and use convergence of $\sum_{s,j\ge2}\log s/\varphi(s^j)$, where $s$ ranges over primes and $j\ge2$. Above this cutoff the bound $X/(hs^j)+1$ costs $o(X/h)+O(\sqrt X\log^3 X)$, by convergence of the corresponding series and the count of nontrivial prime powers through $2X$. It remains to count each prime $s\nmid h$ just once.

Take $U=\sqrt X/(h\log^8 X)$. For $s\le U$, the conditions $p\equiv a\bmod h$ and $p\equiv1\bmod s$ give one reduced class modulo $hs$. The dyadic prime count differs from $(\operatorname{Li}(2X)-\operatorname{Li}(X))/(\varphi(h)(s-1))$ by $O(\sqrt X\log^2(2Xhs))$. After weighting by $\log s$ the total error is $O(\sqrt X U\log^2 X)=o(X/\varphi(h))$. Mertens' estimate $\sum_{s\le U}\log s/(s-1)=\log U+O(1)$ now gives at most $(1/2+o(1))X/\varphi(h)$ from the small primes. For fixed $h$ the older Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem gives the same conclusion with an appropriately large fixed logarithmic power in $U$. [Goldfeld's weighted application of Bombieri–Vinogradov](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/PrimeShifts.pdf#page=2)

For $U<s\le X^\delta$ the dyadic Brun–Titchmarsh upper bound is $2X/(\varphi(h)(s-1)\log(X/(hs)))$. Summing with weight $\log s$ and partial summation gives at most
$$
 \frac{X}{\varphi(h)}\left(2\int_{1/2}^{\delta}\frac{dt}{1-t}+o(1)\right).
$$
Here $\log h/\log X=o(1)$, and the denominator stays a fixed distance from zero after division by $\log X$. At least $(\eta_\delta-o(1))X/\varphi(h)$ weight comes from primes $s>X^\delta$. Each such $p$ contributes at most $\log(2X)$, proving the count. Taking $\delta=3/5$ gives $\eta_\delta=1/2-2\log(5/4)>0.0537$.

## The size of a detecting catalog

Consider any list $(A_i,B_i,M_i)$ with coprime nonzero integers $A_i,B_i$, $|A_i|\ne|B_i|$, and positive integer $M_i$. Define $H=\sum_i\log(2\max(|A_i|,|B_i|))$ and $J=\sum_i\log(2M_i)$. On the primes with a large divisor $s$ just counted, the number satisfying **any** of the divisibilities $p\mid A_i^{M_i}-B_i^{M_i}$ or $p\mid A_i^{M_i}+B_i^{M_i}$ is at most
$$
       O\!\left(\frac{H X^{2-2\delta}+J X^{1-\delta}}{\log X}\right).               \tag{1}
$$
Indeed write $p-1=ks$, $k<2X^{1-\delta}$. If $s\mid2M_i$ for any $i$, there are at most $J/(\delta\log X)$ such distinct $s$, and each permits at most $2X^{1-\delta}$ choices of $k$. Otherwise, at a prime with either divisibility the denominator $B_i$ is a unit and the order of $A_i/B_i$ divides both $ks$ and $2M_i$. For large $X$ we have $s^2>2X$, so that order divides $k$. Hence $p\mid A_i^k-B_i^k$. Over every admissible $k$ and $i$ these nonzero integers have total logarithmic absolute size $O(HX^{2-2\delta})$. Each distinct prime divisor above $X$ costs $\log X$. This proves (1) and includes bases of either sign.

In particular $H,J=X^{o(1)}$ miss proportion at least $\eta_\delta-o(1)$ in **each** reduced class modulo $h=X^{o(1)}$. At $h=12$ the all-negative test transcript occurs for many primes both inside and outside the required class. An arbitrary Boolean function of those divisibility results therefore cannot implement the exact CM criterion. The same argument applies to adaptive binary tests whose all-negative path has the indicated budgets. For a catalog covering the entire class $11\bmod12$ at $\delta=3/5$, either $H\gg X^{1/5}$ or $J\gg X^{3/5}$. Bases depending rationally on an input integer $N$ and regular at zero give the same test modulo $p\mid N$ after specializing at zero, away from denominator primes. The bound concerns public exponents and the binary divisibility outcomes. General modular arithmetic circuits for evaluating the CM scalar remain open.

## Exponents that are polynomials of the input

The same obstruction covers a larger class of input-dependent tests on balanced products $N=pq$. For each $X$ choose a catalog of rational bases as above and nonzero integer polynomials $P_i(T)$ in place of the public exponents. Let $D_*=\sum_i\deg P_i$, let $H$ be the base-height sum above, and let $J_0=\sum_i\log(2|c_i|)$ where $c_i$ is any one nonzero coefficient of $P_i$. Assume $D_*,H,J_0=X^{o(1)}$. Take the exponent $|P_i(N)|$ or skip its zero values. The coefficients other than $c_i$ need no height restriction for this counting statement.

Prescribe independently any reduced classes for $p,q$ at moduli $h_1,h_2=X^{o(1)}$. A proportion at least $\eta_\delta^2-o(1)$ of their ordered distinct prime pairs in $(X,2X]$ have **neither** prime dividing any nonzero tested difference or sum. To prove it, keep the primes with a large marker and remove those where any rational base has order dividing $(p-1)/s$, or where $s$ divides any selected $c_i$. The previous size proof removes only $o(X/(\varphi(h_j)\log X))$ in either class. Primes dividing the base numerators or denominators can also be discarded at the same cost. At each remaining $p$, all the base orders are multiples of its odd marker $s$. Since $p\equiv1\bmod s$, any local hit requires $P_i(q)\equiv0\bmod s$. The reduction of $P_i$ is nonzero, so it has at most $\deg P_i$ roots. The total number of partner integers $q$ permitting a hit at this $p$ is at most $(1+X^{1-\delta})\sum_i\deg P_i$. This is $o(X/(\varphi(h_2)\log X))$. The symmetric estimate with $q$ as the tested prime rules out hits there as well. Subtracting these exceptional pairs from the product of the two marker populations proves the assertion. Rational functions of $N$ as bases also qualify when their specializations at zero form the indicated catalog and their exceptional denominator primes have the same small total logarithmic weight.

In particular both primes can have this common all-negative transcript even when exactly one is $11\bmod12$. Using the complete input to select from the catalog does not create a local power hit on these pairs. This treats bounded total polynomial degree in the exponent, while (1) gives a stronger quantitative size tradeoff for constant exponents.

The finite replay checked 15,492 weighted predecessor factorizations and 2,442 primes with a large divisor of $p-1$. It identified nine constant-exponent hits through the exponent and two through a short order. All four reduced classes modulo 12 included all-negative transcripts at each of four tested dyads. In the input-polynomial test, all 53 local hits satisfied the marker congruence and 2,949 mixed-class prime pairs missed simultaneously.
