# A trace bound with more than one factor-base-size of relations

Use the [cofactor-variable polynomial lattice](cogentic-variable-multiple-lattice.md) and its [finite-order Gaussian bounds and narrow band](cogentic-narrow-band-moments.md). Write $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log A\sim cH$, $d\sim xD$ and $S=xc+1/x$. Let $B^{1-\delta}<p\le B$ be the main band with $\delta=(\log u)^{-1/2}$. Its primes have size $B^{1+o(1)}$ uniformly, where the $o(1)$ may be negative. Choose each generator $\ell$ in $(E,2E]$, $\log E\sim z^3$, append just this prime to the eligible set, and keep the target CRT polynomial exactly as in Cogentic. The prime $\ell$ exceeds $d$ and the value $e^{Cz^2}$ for every fixed $C$. The GRH prime character estimate gives the same fraction $1/2-o(1)$ of separating generators for each pair of prime divisors of $N$ as in the [single-exponent argument](cogentic-dense-exponents.md), since $\sqrt E$ dominates every fixed power of $L$. Its possible removal or forced occurrence in the smooth saddle causes only the previous deletion factors; $\ell^{-\alpha_s}=e^{-(1+o(1))z^3}$. The active supports of all other large primes have the same law as before. The small-prime singular series excludes $\ell$.

Let $n=\exp((2c-2S/(3b))H)$, the exponential scale of the number of joint relations. Assume $c>2b$ and $n>B^{1+\eta}$ for some fixed $\eta>0$ and all sufficiently large $N$. Choose a fixed even $k>2/\eta$. Choose the degree slack and the Gaussian width from the finite-order lemma to handle $k$ marks and at least $2k+2$ prime-row prescriptions. This changes the lower-order terms in the logarithmic norm and preserves $S$. Constants in every $O_k$ and in $L^{O_k(1)}$ below can depend on this fixed $k$ and the fixed parameters.

## Joint smoothness at distinct marks

In one field prescribe up to $2k$ root residues outside $\ell$, allowing distinct residues of the same prime. Distinct prescribed roots modulo a common prime give independent linear equations since $d>2k+2$, by interpolation. On any compatible prefix of marked values, multiplying free forms by the product of the earlier vanishing linear factors permits all remaining missed row values to be adjusted simultaneously modulo each prime. CRT across primes gives the full joint image. Writing $\Delta_{ij}=a_i b_j-a_j b_i$, the $j$th coefficient-form output therefore has step $\prod_{i<j}|\Delta_{ij}|$ multiplied by every prescribed prime whose row the new mark hits without a previous mark at that same slope modulo that prime. Each such multiplier occurs once. The unconditional probability of the entire prescribed root event is $(1+O(e^{-L^3}))\prod_{p}p^{-s_p}$ for $s_p$ distinct residues. The separate fields are independent.

For $h\le k$ distinct marks, exclude $p^2\mid\Delta_{ij}$ whenever $p$ is prescribed in either field or $p=\ell$. Let $\rho_i(v)$ be the one-field reference probability, and $\Phi_i(v;R_i)$ its actual one-point extra row factor with this set of prescriptions. These are the one-point functions from the preceding moment proof. Uniformly on the tuples left, the conditional joint one-field smoothness probability is
$$
 (1+O_k(e^{-50z^2}))\prod_{j=1}^h\rho_i(v_j)\Phi_i(v_j;R_i)
          \prod_{d<p\le B^{1-\delta},\ p\ne\ell}
                  (1-1/p)^{-(h-\nu_p)},                         \tag{1}
$$
where $\nu_p$ is the number of distinct projective reductions of the $h$ marks modulo $p$. The full two-field factor squares the displayed product. Here and below the uniform smooth-window parameters may use a larger fixed constant in the exponent tolerances than $50$ when needed. The two-point proof supplies the case $h=2$. The details for higher multiplicities follow.

Expose in order the Gaussian innovations of the exact projected coefficient lattice. Every residual attached to a new mark occupies a single class after division by all preassigned prime factors. Its modulus is $\exp(O_k(H))$ and its center-to-width ratio is $\exp(O_k(H))$ on the $L^3$ innovation cutoff. The omitted tails contribute $e^{-\Omega(L^6)}$. On coprime classes the smooth-window estimate applies with the actual conditional center; its replacement by the nominal one causes the same exponentially small relative variation used in the two-point lemma. The ratio of coprime local factors to its one-point counterparts is computed prime by prime. A small $p\le B^{1-\delta}$ outside $P_0\ell$ enters the new modulus exactly when a slope repeats; a smooth previous value forbids the zero residue, and the normalizer contributes $p/(p-1)$ once. The denominator primes are coprime to every polynomial value there. Primes above $B$ contribute $1+O_k(B^{-1})$ altogether, since at most $O_k(1)$ divide all the differences or denominator primes. An unprescribed eligible prime dividing an overlap has a previous zero residue with relative probability $O_k(L^{O_k(1)}p^{-\alpha_s})$. For this bound select the first mark in its slope block and expose it first. Imposing $p\mid F_i(v)$ there leaves a free residual progression with its center divided by $p$, whose smooth mass gives the stated probability after the $1/p$ sampling factor. Apply the uniform rough $h$-point estimates after this extra condition to bound the joint contribution of the other marks. There are $O_k(1)$ eligible primes dividing the difference products. Their contribution on excluding these events is $O_k(B^{-1+o(1)})$ relative. The same argument applies for a prescribed prime when the specific overlapping slope is outside its prescribed roots. The finite interpolation argument leaves that slope free.

For a prescribed $p$ and a common slope where the root is forced, a preassigned $p$ is removed from the value at every such mark. If two marks share the slope and $p^2\nmid\Delta$, the second residual has no $p$ in its step after this removal, except at the fixed target treated below. If more share the slope, take the first two in the block first. Dehomogenize by the unit $b_j$. Their lifts modulo $p^2$ are $r+pt_j$ with distinct $t_j\bmod p$ throughout the block. For the actual field polynomial the reduced values modulo $p$ obey
$$
             f_i(r+pt_j)/p\equiv f_i(r)/p+t_jf'_i(r)\pmod p.
$$
The first two determine all these residues. Each later zero excludes one residue of the second reduced value given the first, with a nonzero coefficient since its $t_j$ is distinct from the first. On the second smooth progression the specified class costs $O_k(L^{O_k(1)}/p)$ relatively by the same smooth-window theorem modulo an extra $p$; zero classes can instead be bounded by dividing out $p$. The latter are already impossible if the first two residual values were required to be squarefree at this preassigned prime. Other preassigned factors and $b_j^d$ are units at $p$ and rescale the tests. For $\ell$, the derivative at its prescribed roots is fixed modulo $\ell$ by the base CRT, so each later zero excludes one class at the first reduced value. This costs $O_k(L^{O_k(1)}/\ell)$ relatively. After all exclusions every remaining $p$ in a higher residual modulus gives the harmless factor $1+O_k(B^{-1+o(1)}+\ell^{-1+o(1)})$ relative to its independent one-point normalizer, including deletion of an eligible Euler factor if required. The finite rough bound on all further marks absorbs the excluded cases. The uniformly random missed root conditions, sequential progression step and these prime factors prove (1). ## Expansion of the centered trace

Use the source's weighted matrix difference $\widetilde A=\widetilde H-\widehat C\,\widetilde U^{\rm ex}$, and let $A_0$ contain its entries between main-band prime rows with different rational primes, with zeros in the other positions. The matrix is real symmetric. To bound its operator norm use $\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(A_0^k)$. In a cyclic row tuple $(q_1,\ldots,q_k)$ an individual label consists of field, rational prime and root residue. Consecutive labels have different rational primes. Put $e_j=(q_j,q_{j+1})$ with cyclic subscripts. Its mark coefficient is $g_j(v)=z_{q_j}(v)z_{q_{j+1}}(v)-(\widetilde U^{\rm ex})_{q_jq_{j+1}}$. Its large-row scale is $M_j=(p_j p_{j+1})^{-\alpha_s/2}$. Conditional on the event that all labels are roots, expand the $k$ powers as sums at marked positions. Every position supplies the factor $\pi_0(v)g_j(v)$ and the joint smooth indicator divided by its two single-mark reference probabilities. We first consider $k$ distinct positions.

For each $j$, the hit part is supported on one projective slope modulo $Q_j=p_jp_{j+1}\le B^2$, and its absolute value multiplied by both fields' one-point row factors is bounded by $L^{O_k(1)}M_j[1+Q_j1_{a\equiv r_j b_v(Q_j)}]$. This is (2) of the variable-multiple note, since at most $2k$ deletion-factor gains $p^{1-\alpha_s}$ remain polynomial. The extra root prescriptions at the other row labels may be suppressed when computing the centered class average at mark $j$. On the support of the hit, any extra prescribed slope at a different prime costs $O(1/p+Q_j t/(Aw))$ on a further fixed small modulus $t$ coprime to the main band. Extra prescribed slopes at a prime dividing $Q_j$ are disjoint from the hit unless they are the required slope, already retained. Away from the full hit support the coefficient is $O(L^{O_k(1)}M_j)$, and the union of at most $2k$ extra slopes and $d$ target slopes costs the same with $p$ replaced by $\ell$ if needed. If an opposite field's forced root has the same slope and rational prime as an endpoint, its extra deletion factor would hold throughout the hit. Tuples with any physical prime repeated among different row labels are instead bounded separately below, using their saving of one prime choice.

For tuples with no repeated physical prime among distinct row labels, on every slope class $r_t\bmod t$ with $t\le T=e^{100 z^2}$ supported on the small-prime band excluding $\ell$, the singleton centered estimate becomes
$$
 \left|\sum_{a\equiv r_t b_v(t)}\pi_0(v)g_j(v)\prod_i\Phi_i(v;R_i)\right|
    \le \frac{L^{O_k(1)}M_j}{t}\left[\ell^{-1+o(1)}+t B^2/(Aw)\right].       \tag{2}
$$
It is proved by the same numerator partial summation and the exact incidence identity in the variable-multiple note after preserving the two edge endpoints. The real and saddle reference weights may differ among denominators and are integrated with their actual global average. The numerator interval is of length $\asymp Aw$ for every denominator.

For each $d<p\le B^{1-\delta}$, $p\ne\ell$, expand $(1-1/p)^{-2(k-\nu_p)}$ on the finite partition lattice of the $k$ positions as a linear combination of indicators that specified subgroups of slopes agree modulo $p$. The top term imposing no equalities has coefficient $1$. All other coefficients are $O_k(1/p)$, since the function is $1+O_k(1/p)$ on every partition and the finite incidence inversion depends only on $k$. Across primes the sum of absolute coefficients is at most $\prod_p(1+C_k/p)=L^{O_k(1)}$. For a term with prime product $t>T$, retain one required pair of equal slopes at each prime and expose marks in their fixed index order. Each condition imposes one congruence on its later mark, of aggregate modulus $t_j$ there, $\prod_jt_j=t$. At mark $j$ the mass weighted by $1+Q_j1_{\mathrm{hit}}$ of these conditions is $O(1/t_j+Q_j/(Aw))$, for every modulus, since $(t_j,Q_j)=1$. The product is $O_k(1/t+B^2/(Aw))$. Thus (1) times all the coefficients from $t>T$ costs $L^{O_k(1)}\prod M_j[1/T+B^2/(Aw)]$. For $t\le T$, enumerate residues of every marked slope modulo $t$. The remaining constraints depend only on those residues and each independent marked sum satisfies (2). The $t^k$ possible choices absorb the $t^{-k}$ factors. The exceptional tuples $p^2\mid\Delta_{ij}$ have weighted mass $O_k(1/p+B^2/(Aw))$, uniformly at each fixed first mark, by the single-class bound modulo $\operatorname{lcm}(p^2,Q_j)$. The rough bound handles the actual moments there. Exact coincident marks can be restored to the expanded main sum at cost $L^{O_k(1)}B^2/A^{2-o(1)}$, since at fixed first mark the atom of the second has that weighted bound, and the full small-prime multiplier is at most $L^{O_k(1)}$ even there. Hence for each such root tuple the distinct-position contribution is at most $e^{-40z^2}\prod_j M_j$ in absolute value for large $N$.

The sum of these products times their root probabilities over all row tuples is $L^{O_k(1)}$. Indeed each distinct row of multiplicity $m$ contributes $p^{-m\alpha_s}$, and summing its potential residues against the root probability costs at most a constant. Summing over its prime costs at most $\sum_{p\in J_*}p^{-\alpha_s}=L^{O(1)}$. For the exception of distinct labels sharing a physical prime there is an additional $B^{-1+o(1)}$: combine their two prime sums, one of whose factors then contributes at most $B^{-1+o(1)}$ pointwise. Use the unrestricted rough bound and the independent weighted mark bracket estimate for these exceptions, without a centered estimate. This proves that all distinct-position terms together give $e^{-30z^2}$ for large $N$.

## Repeated marked positions

Partition the $k$ cyclic marked positions into $h<k$ distinct marks. Write $q_1,\ldots,q_k$ for the row sequence with $v$ distinct labels. Every extra copy of a mark costs $n^{-1}B^{o(1)}$ after identifying its repeated smooth indicator: its maximum weight is $\exp((-2c+2S/(3b)+o(1))H)$. What remains is an average under $h$ copies of $\pi_0$, times the product of the root probabilities and a uniform $L^{O_k(1)}$ from the rough $h$-mark estimate. In this upper bound the distinctness restriction may be dropped after the probabilities were bounded, since the resulting geometric integrands are nonnegative. All estimates below tolerate a factor $B^{o(1)}$ because $k$ is fixed and $n/B$ grows by a fixed power of $B$.

Expand the absolute bound for the product of the $k$ centered coefficients by choosing either the hit or the mean term at each position. Suppose $m_0$ positions choose their mean. A hit contributes at most $B^{1+o(1)}$, and a mean contributes at most $B^{-1+o(1)}$. Thus their combined magnitude is at most $B^{k-2m_0+o(1)}$ apart from hit restrictions. Form the undirected bipartite graph with the $v$ distinct rows and the $h$ distinct marks as vertices. Connect a mark to both endpoints of each of its original appearances, omitting duplicate edges. The full graph is connected and the cyclic word traverses a closed walk of length $2k$. Remove its hit restrictions at the $m_0$ mean positions to obtain a subgraph $G$; only distinct edges that are no longer hit anywhere are removed. Write $C$ for its number of components including isolated vertices, and $v_0$ for its isolated rows. Then $C\le1+2m_0$.

There are at most $B^v$ choices of physical primes, times a constant depending on $k$ for the choices of fields and fixed equality pattern. Every active row's residue is determined by any one of its incident marks. Each isolated row has up to $B$ residue choices. For the fixed residues the event that they are roots has probability at most $B^{-v+o(1)}$, including distinct residues of a common physical prime. Since this bound and the conditional rough probability are uniform, the prime and residue choices can be summed before or after the marked congruences. In a component containing marks, use a spanning tree rooted at a mark. Each subsequent mark must match a previously exposed mark modulo the physical prime for its tree parent row. This has mass $O(B^{-1+o(1)}+1/(Aw))=B^{-1+o(1)}$ for each denominator. The forest supplies $h-C+v_0$ such constraints. Hence the total contribution for the pattern is at most
$$
       n^{-(k-h)}B^{k-h+C-2m_0+o(1)}.                           \tag{3}
$$
If two distinct row labels use the same physical prime, there are at most $B^{v-1}$ prime choices, yielding another $B^{-1}$. Otherwise, whenever $G$ contains a cycle, choose one simple alternating cycle in one component. Root it at one mark and expose it in an order that leaves the last mark on the cycle attached to two already fixed row residues. For example, choose as the reference mark for each of these two rows its other mark on this cycle. Their primes are distinct, so the final mark has probability $O(B^{-2+o(1)}+1/(Aw))=B^{-2+o(1)}$. Complete all trees incident to the cycle in tree order. This saves one further factor $B^{-1+o(1)}$ over (3). A 4-cycle, with two marks sharing two distinct row labels, obeys the same two-prime count.

Unless $C=1+2m_0$, the exponent in (3) is already at most $k-h+o(1)$. In the equality case every one of the $2m_0$ removals deleted a different bridge at the time of removal, so any cycle in the full graph remains in $G$. If the full graph was a tree, every distinct edge is traversed at least twice by its original closed walk. The tree therefore has at most $k$ edges. Each mark has at least two distinct row neighbors, since consecutive labels in the large-prime off-diagonal trace have different physical primes. Thus $2h\le k$ whenever this tree case occurs. There are only $O_k(1)$ patterns of vertex equalities and mean choices. Their sum is bounded by
$$
 B^{o(1)}\sum_{h<k}(B/n)^{k-h}
        +B^{1+o(1)}\sum_{h\le k/2}(B/n)^{k-h}=B^{-\Omega_{k,\eta}(1)}.
$$
Together with the distinct terms this gives $\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(A_0^k)\le e^{-20z^2}$ eventually. For example, Markov's inequality gives $\|A_0\|_{\rm op}\le e^{-10z^2/k}$ with probability $1-o(1)$. Positivity of the trace of an even power justifies using an absolute-value expansion for its pieces.

## The target and the diagonal

Ordinary two-point analysis suffices for the remaining entries. For an individual centered entry with one or zero main-band prime rows, its support modulus is at most $B E^2=B^{1+o(1)}$, including its target. For a diagonal main-band row the modulus is its one prime, also $B^{1+o(1)}$. At a duplicated marked position its conditional absolute square is bounded by $L^{O(1)} B^{1+o(1)} M_{\alpha\beta}^2/n$, uniformly. Indeed $|g|\Pi_R\le L^{O(1)}M(1+Q1_{\rm hit})$, and squaring then averaging the bracket costs $O(Q+Q^2/(Aw))=O(Q)$, while the Gaussian rough factor and extra copies of the reciprocal one-point density cost $n^{-1}B^{o(1)}$. Off this diagonal the ordinary centered proof with one or two rows gives $e^{-C z^2}M^2$ for any desired fixed $C$, on taking its small-prime cutoff $T=e^{C'z^2}$ for sufficiently large $C'$. If a target row is involved use the source local suppression of extra target residues, including the matching row exactly. The small cost $E^{-1+o(1)}$ absorbs every such tolerance. The same-prime distinct-root entries have zero hit: their centered values have magnitude $O(\widehat C p^{-\alpha_s})$, confined to blocks of size $\le2d$.

In particular $D_A=\sum_q p_q^{-\alpha_s}\widetilde A_{qq}^2$ has expectation $\le L^{O(1)}e^{-Cz^2}$. The protected target sum $F_\ell^2=\sum_{j,q:\ p_q\ne\ell}\widetilde A_{l_j q}^2$ has expectation at most $\ell^{-\alpha_s}L^{O(1)}e^{-Cz^2}$, by summing the entry scale $M_{l_jq}^2=\ell^{-\alpha_s}p_q^{-\alpha_s}$ against the root probabilities. The archimedean, constant and target rows against all the others have a Frobenius bound $e^{-C'z^2}$ with probability $1-o(1)$, for any desired fixed $C'$ by increasing $C$. The total mass has $\widehat C\in[7/8,9/8]$ with probability $1-o(1)$. In the weight-normalizer comparison use relative tolerance $e^{-Cz^2}$ for any fixed $C$. The deterministic target contributes at most $d\ell^{-\alpha_s}(\log\ell)^2=e^{-(1+o(1))z^3}$ difference from the common saddle contribution, and the variance of the independent other row counts is $B^{-1+o(1)}$. This provides the same polynomial spectral margin as the source's stronger $B^{-0.2}$ tolerance. The narrow band has $C_{\rm bad}=0$ with probability $1-o(1)$. Thus outside an event of probability $o(1)$ the full off-diagonal operator norm, allowing the same-prime blocks, is at most $t=e^{-c_k z^2}$ for some $c_k>0$, the weighted diagonal error $D_A$ is as small a fixed power of $t$ as needed, and $\ell^{\alpha_s/2}F_\ell$ is also as small a fixed power as needed.

For precision about the source's peeling argument, take for example a diagonal tolerance $t^{1/4}$, deletion threshold $\delta_p=t^{1/8}$ and mass threshold $\mu_*=t^{1/4}$. These symbols concern the empirical Gram and are independent of the prime-band width. The initial set $D_0$ of bad diagonal rows has weighted mass $\le t^{-1/2}D_A=o(\mu_*)$ and misses the at most $d$ target roots with probability $1-o(1)$ by their individual diagonal estimates. There are no multiple, shared or medium-conductor rows on the chosen event. Suppose that the growing deletion set $D_s$ avoids the target and has $\mu(D_s)\le\mu_*$. As in the source, a target row loses at most $2\mu(D_s)+\ell^{\alpha_s/2}\sqrt{\mu(D_s)}F_\ell<\delta_p$ of its normalized diagonal. Take all rows $S=D_{s+1}\setminus D_0$ that have been added up to the next step. In their deletion tests enlarge every earlier blocking set to $D_s$ using the nonnegativity of the empirical incidence products. Pairs consisting of the same row are excluded: for a row added earlier, its own row was absent when it was tested. Distinct rows of the same physical prime cannot hit together on this clean event. The contribution from the reference mean to the weighted sum of these tests is $O(\mu(S)\mu(D_s))$, using the exact off-diagonal principal model bound. The regular main-band incidence covariance includes the off-diagonal rank-one constraints already in $\widetilde U^{\rm ex}$; its entries have the source bound $O((p p')^{-\alpha_s/2})$. For the centered part use
$$
  \left|\sum_{q\in S,q'\in D_s}p_q^{-\alpha_s/2}p_{q'}^{-\alpha_s/2}
                          (\widetilde A_{\rm off})_{q,q'}\right|
          \le t\sqrt{\mu(S)\mu(D_s)}.
$$
This bilinear bound is valid even when the sets overlap, because the diagonal is set to zero. It applies after adding the empirical nonnegative pairs first and subtracting the model on the enlarged off-diagonal rectangle. Consequently $\delta_p\mu(S)\le O(\mu(S)\mu_*)+t\sqrt{\mu(S)\mu_*}$, and $\mu(S)\le O((t/\delta_p)^2)\mu_*=o(\mu_*)$. Induction preserves the threshold and protects every target root. The source's Cauchy–Schwarz estimate for columns hitting the final deleted set uses only their mass, $D_A$, and the remaining per-row lost-diagonal bound. It bounds the spectral loss by $L^{O(1)}(\mu_*+\delta_p)$, smaller than any fixed inverse polynomial. The result for the full unprojected Gram on retained rows then follows from $\|\widetilde A_{\rm off}\|\le t$ and the retained diagonal bounds. The Helmert projection and real-unit proof use the same polynomial spectral margin as in the previous Farey construction. ## Randomized coefficient

The joint smooth bound, exact sieve and integer lift give the same expected bit cost coefficient $\max\{2c,4b,2c+2b-2S/(3b)\}$. In the new rank conditions one may take $x\to1/\sqrt c$, $b\to c/2$, and $2c>b+2S/(3b)$ with a fixed strict margin for each schedule. The limiting value of $c$ obeys $c^{3/2}=16/9$. The largest cost coefficient tends to $2(16/9)^{2/3}$. The schedule can be chosen rational with $k$ fixed for each strictly positive margin, and the previous interleaving over strict schedules gives this limiting coefficient for a single Las Vegas algorithm on every input. Gaussian widths, moment dimension and the hidden finite thresholds vary across these schedules. This is a manuscript-level consequence of the local estimates and Cogentic extraction described above.
