# Cubic square kernels from an explicit nonmaximal order

The ideal-and-norm stage of the [cubic different kernel](cogentic-cubic-different-kernel.md) can be computed in deterministic polynomial bit time from a monic irreducible cubic polynomial and an explicit list of nonzero field elements. The algorithm can enlarge the power order when a particular ideal sum is noninvertible. It computes ideal parity through invertible atoms, exact quotient indices and local tests at named small primes or composite supports. It needs no maximal-order basis or factorization of a discriminant or norm. The remaining global square-class step has the same rank-sensitive bound as before. [Exact implementation and replay](../experiments/cogentic-cubic-order-kernel.py)

## Invertible atoms and order enlargements

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic irreducible cubic, \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for a root \(\alpha\), \(B_0=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), and \(\mathcal O\) the ring of integers. For each nonzero explicit input \(z_i\in K\), take \(h_i\) to be a common positive denominator of its three power-basis coordinates. Replace it in the ideal stage by \(w_i=h_i^2z_i\in B_0\). Multiplication by this rational square preserves its square class, ideal parity and rational norm sign.

Start in \(B=B_0\). The ideals \(w_iB\) are invertible. Maintain a list of distinct proper invertible ideals, initially the distinct proper principal ones. For two entries \(I,J\), compute \(C=I+J\). If \(C=B\), the entries are comaximal. Otherwise check whether \(C\) is invertible by computing
\[
 B'=(B:C):(B:C).
\]
For a finite-rank order over \(\mathbb Z\) and a full fractional ideal, this is an overorder of \(B\), and \(B'=B\) if and only if the ideal is invertible. Here \(C\) is nonzero and of full rank because it contains \(I\). If \(B'\ne B\), restart with the principal ideals \(w_iB'\). This is a strict integral enlargement, so the absolute order discriminant drops by the square of the index. [Buchmann–Lenstra, Proposition 2.5 and Section 5](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/buchlen/to.pdf)

When \(C\) is invertible, replace \(I,J\) by \(C,I:C,J:C\), omitting the unit ideal and duplicates. These are integral invertible ideals. An invertible ideal is principal over \(B\otimes\mathbb Z_p\) for every rational prime \(p\), since that ring is semilocal. Multiplication of integral invertible ideals therefore multiplies their lattice indices. Each proper split decreases the sum of log indices of the distinct entries by at least \(\log 2\). At termination there are pairwise comaximal invertible ideals \(A_j\), and every \(w_iB\) is a product of their nonnegative powers. Repeated containment and exact colon division recover the exponents. Extending to \(\mathcal O\) preserves their products and comaximality, so \(A_i\mathcal O\) and \(A_j\mathcal O\) have disjoint prime-ideal supports for \(i\ne j\). The parity matrix needs one row per \(A_j\mathcal O\) that fails the ideal-square test.

## An invertible atom on composite support

Fix an atom \(A\) with lattice index \(m=[B:A]\). Its extension \(A\mathcal O\) has the same norm \(m\). Indeed, at each rational \(p\) write \(A\otimes\mathbb Z_p=\gamma(B\otimes\mathbb Z_p)\) for a local generator in \(K\otimes\mathbb Q_p\). The \(p\)-valuation of both relevant indices equals the valuation of the determinant of multiplication by \(\gamma\), which is independent of the supporting lattice. Only primes dividing \(m\) can matter.

At the three named primes 2, 3 and 5, compute a \(p\)-maximal overorder of \(B_0\) and its prime ideals using the radical iteration and finite-field linear algebra. Test the three original lattice basis vectors of \(A\) for membership in powers of each prime ideal, after localization at \(p\). Their minimum prime-ideal valuation is the valuation of the extended atom. In the computed prime-power basis, localization means allowing rational coordinates with denominators coprime to \(p\). This matters if the current order \(B\) has denominators elsewhere. The norm exponent \(v_p(m)\) bounds all powers to be tested, and their residue-degree weighted sum must equal \(v_p(m)\). Remove these three rational prime powers from \(m\). [Exact computation at named primes](cogentic-monic-singular-ideals.md#known-rational-primes)

Let \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) be a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of \(A\), and set
\[
 g_t=a_0+t a_1+t^2a_2,\qquad 0\le t\le6.
\]
Each \(g_t\) is nonzero by linear independence. For a rational \(p>6\), the ring \(B\otimes\mathbb Z_p\) has at most three maximal ideals. At any such maximal ideal, the rank-one invertible module becomes one-dimensional after reduction to the residue field. Its three basis vectors generate it, so the reduction of \(g_t\) is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two in \(t\), after expressing it in any local generator. That residue field contains \(\mathbb F_p\), so the seven public choices of \(t\) are distinct and at most two fail at each maximal ideal. Some \(g_t\) generates \(A\) over the full semilocal ring at \(p\).

For each \(t\), compute the exact positive integer
\[
 q_t=[A:g_tB]=\frac{|\mathrm N_{K/\mathbb Q}g_t|}{[B:A]}.
\]
The denominator divides the numerator because \(g_t\in A\) and \(A\) is a \(B\)-ideal. A prime \(p\) is coprime to \(q_t\) exactly when the two lattices agree over \(\mathbb Z_p\). Take the largest divisor \(m_t\) of the current remaining \(m\) coprime to \(q_t\), using repeated gcd removal, and remove \(m_t\) from the remaining modulus. This never splits the exponent of an individual rational prime between choices. Each prime in \(m_t\) sees the same local ideal from \(g_tB\) and \(A\), and all remaining prime factors are covered by the end of the seven choices. Each \(m_t\) divides \([B:A]\) and \(|\mathrm N g_t|\).

Decide whether \(g_t\) has even prime-ideal valuations above the composite support \(m_t\). If \(g_t\in\mathbb Q\), it is an integer since it belongs to an order. At each rational prime \(p\), at least one ramification index above \(p\) is odd because \(\sum e f=3\). Its ideal valuations above \(p\) are therefore all even exactly when its rational valuation at \(p\) is even. Use saturation to isolate the portion of \(|g_t|\) supported on the primes dividing \(m_t\), then test it for an integer square.

Otherwise \(g_t\) generates \(K\), since the degree is three. Its monic minimal polynomial \(Q\) is the characteristic polynomial of multiplication by \(g_t\); its coefficients are integral. If \(Q(T^2)\) is reducible, \(g_t\) is already a square: any root of \(Q(T^2)\) generates a field containing a conjugate cubic and has degree either three or six, with the degree-three case equivalent to a square in the cubic. If \(Q(T^2)\) is irreducible it defines the quadratic extension \(E=K(\sqrt{g_t})\). All support primes exceed six, so apply the [composite tame-profile comparison](cogentic-monic-singular-ideals.md#computing-the-weighted-sums-over-composite-inputs) in degrees three and six to decide whether \(E/K\) is unramified over all primes dividing \(m_t\). At odd residue characteristic this is equivalent to even valuation of \(g_t\) at every contributing prime ideal. The comparison uses gcd refinement and perfect powers of composite integers. The split and scalar cases bypass the degree-six test for different reasons.

## Bit cost and the signed square kernel

Let \(S\) be the total explicit bit length of \(P\) and all rational input coordinates. The common denominators, the scaled elements and their rational norms have bit length polynomial in \(S\). The number of restarts is at most \(\log_4|\operatorname{disc}(B_0)|\). Every intermediate order lies between \(B_0\) and the trace dual of \(B_0\), so it has a rational lattice presentation of polynomial bit length. Atom indices at each restart divide a product of the original scaled principal indices. More precisely, the sum of their log indices starts below the sum for the input principals and decreases at every refinement. The lattices are represented in Hermite normal form in the current order. Colon lattices are intersections of linear membership constraints from a fixed number of multiplication operators, with polynomial-sized rational entries. The number of exact divisions by a proper atom when recovering an exponent from one column is at most the logarithm of that column's original index, since every division decreases the index by at least two.

Named prime computations take polynomial time because the only named primes are fixed. Each composite test uses at most seven candidate generators of polynomial bit length and integer moduli dividing the atom index. The tame-profile construction uses only fixed degrees and polynomially many lattice operations and gcd refinements in the explicit bit lengths. Polynomial irreducibility and rational quartic root tests can use deterministic rational polynomial factorization. The first kernel thus costs a polynomial in \(S\). If the list is given instead in compact powers of explicit field elements, reduce its binary exponents modulo two for the square-class decision and carry the omitted half-powers in compact form for the resulting square roots. Expanding those roots as rational coordinate vectors can cost more than polynomial time in the compact input.

Add the sign of the rational norm as a row, with \(-1\) among the input elements if a sign coordinate is desired. An even ideal has square absolute rational norm, so every vector in the first kernel has square positive norm. The remaining exact cubic square tests use the quartic resolvent and the adaptive basis search. Products of at most the length of the input list have polynomial coefficient bit length, by multiplying and reducing fixed-degree polynomials in the explicit basis. The work is bounded by a polynomial in \(S\) times
\[
 2^{r_1+r_2-1}|\operatorname{Cl}(K)[2]|.
\]
This bound follows from the even-ideal quotient by field squares and unit classes. It gives \(\exp(O(W))\) for lists of size \(\exp(O(W))\) under the same rank condition as before, without an order input promise. The existing effective character cutoff can replace the adaptive search under the stated Dedekind GRH hypotheses. An unconditional pointwise bound on the final residual square-class search in arbitrary varying cubics remains open. ## Verification

Run `python3 -B experiments/cogentic-cubic-order-kernel.py` with SageMath available. The replay compares the first kernel against exact prime ideal valuations and the signed square kernel against 3,072 maximal-order oracle decisions across three small cubics. Only these oracle checks factor norms. For \(q=(2^{61}-1)(2^{89}-1)\) and \(P=T^3-2q^3\), the first kernel runs on a single marked generator without changing the initial order. Adding a second principal ideal triggers a lattice enlargement of index \(q^3\). The replay checks scalar and split-cover branches, fractional coordinates, and a square rational norm with odd ideal parity. For the latter take \(\eta=\sqrt[3]2-a\) with \(a\equiv7\pmod{11^2}\) and \(g=\eta\mathrm N\eta\). The norm is a fourth power, while the residue-degree-two ideal above 11 has valuation one in \(g\) because \(7^3-2=341\) has \(11\)-valuation one. The cubic has one linear factor and one irreducible quadratic factor there.
