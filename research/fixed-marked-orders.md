# Large prime factors of the order of a fixed rational point

Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be an elliptic curve, $R\in E(\mathbb Q)$ of infinite order, and $p$ uniformly distributed among the good primes of $(X,2X]$. For $Y=Y(X)\ge2$ with $\log Y=o(\log X)$ write $\mu=\log(\log X/\log Y)$. Then the number of distinct primes $\ell>Y$ dividing $\operatorname{ord}(R\bmod p)$ has a Gaussian limit with centering $\mu$ and scaling $\sqrt\mu$. The proof applies to ordinary non-CM curves and to rational CM models. Its analytic input is Cureton–Thorner's fixed half-plane for Dedekind zeta functions of arbitrary number fields. Liu previously proved the elliptic Erdős–Kac law for the whole group order and studied its exponent. Lombardo–Perucca studied divisibility of the order of individual reduced points at a prescribed prime. Here the point is fixed before $p$ varies and the lower cutoff $Y$ may grow with $X$. [Liu, *Prime analogues of the Erdős–Kac theorem for elliptic curves*](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/fe7d2ca0-71f7-4fa9-aa84-7e7aedf88714) · [Lombardo–Perucca on reduced point orders](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02847) · [Cureton–Thorner, Theorem 1.1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3)

For each fixed $\varepsilon>0$, a proportion $1-o(1)$ of the balanced prime products $N=pq$, $X<p<q\le2X$, simultaneously have the following property on **every** member of any fixed finite library of rational curves and infinite-order rational marked points. No scalar supported on primes at most $Y$ and at most $(1-\varepsilon)\mu$ larger primes kills the marked point at either prime factor. This includes the ordinary local identity test of a one-large-prime second stage, allowing the large primes to be chosen after seeing the input. Fixed torsion marks have the same order at both primes outside a finite set. A library with coefficients and coordinates rational functions of $N$, regular at zero with good specialized models, reduces to this fixed-library case because $N=0$ in each residue field. The obstruction concerns the indicated local scalar tests. Moving models selected by bit extraction fall outside its hypotheses.

## Two finite sets for the local order

Write $V_\ell=E[\ell]$ and $D_\ell=[\ell]^{-1}R$. Both have $\ell^2$ points and $D_\ell$ is a torsor under $V_\ell$. At a good unramified $p\ne\ell$ let $u$ and $v$ be their respective Frobenius fixed-point counts. Thus $u\in\{1,\ell,\ell^2\}$ and $v\in\{0,u\}$. Set
$$ A_\ell=\frac{(u-1)(\ell^2-u)}{(\ell-1)(\ell^2-\ell)}(1-v/\ell),\qquad B_\ell=1-\frac{(u-\ell)(u-\ell^2)}{(1-\ell)(1-\ell^2)}. $$
The first quantity indicates $u=\ell,v=0$, and the second indicates $u>1$. If $A_\ell=1$ there is no rational $\ell$-division of $R$, so $\ell$ divides its order. If $B_\ell=0$, the whole finite group has order prime to $\ell$. Hence $A_\ell\le [\ell\mid\operatorname{ord}(R\bmod p)]\le B_\ell$.

For a non-CM curve the adelic open-image theorem and elliptic Kummer openness give a finite excluded set of primes, depending only on $(E,R)$, outside which the combined affine images for every squarefree product are the full product of $\mathbb F_\ell^2\rtimes\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_\ell)$. Indeed the open linear image contains a principal congruence subgroup and the Kummer index on the combined torsion field is bounded uniformly in the product. All indices greater than one in the translation part are divisible by one of its primes. The infinite-order point generates a Zariski-dense subgroup of $E$. [Serre's adelic theorem, recalled in Zywina's Theorem 1.1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~zywina/papers/ExplicitOpenImage.pdf) · [Corvaja–Demeio–Javanpeykar–Lombardo–Zannier, Theorem 6.9 and Corollary 6.11](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/compositio-mathematica/article/on-the-distribution-of-rational-points-on-ramified-covers-of-abelian-varieties/775198D2DDCD37DC7E16DF3406EA70CB)

Under the uniform affine action, the translation is uniform. When $u=\ell$, the linear map $1-g$ has a one-dimensional image, so $v=0$ with probability $1-1/\ell$. The proportion of linear matrices with $u=\ell$ is $(\ell-2)/(\ell-1)^2+1/(\ell(\ell-1))$: the first term comes from the separate eigenvalues $1,\lambda\ne1$ and the second from the nonidentity unipotent class. Consequently
$$ \mathbb E A_\ell=\frac1\ell-\frac1{\ell^2(\ell-1)},\qquad \mathbb E(B_\ell-A_\ell)\ll\ell^{-2}. $$

For CM with field $K$, condition separately on $p$ split or inert in $K$. Over $K$ the adelic linear image is open in the units of the completed CM order. Away from finitely many primes its projection onto any collection of local Cartans is the full product. The other coset acts by a uniform Cartan element times complex conjugation. The same Kummer index argument gives independent uniform translations in both cosets. [CM open-image theorem and the two Galois cosets](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/opposing-average-congruence-class-biases-in-the-cyclicity-and-koblitz-conjectures-for-elliptic-curves/43DEB36546504B3F9D79902F7226D679)

For split $p$ and split $\ell$, a diagonal matrix has fixed dimension one when exactly one entry is 1, so $\mathbb E A_\ell=2(\ell-2)/(\ell(\ell-1))$. For split $p$ and inert $\ell$, a field scalar on $\mathbb F_{\ell^2}$ has no one-dimensional fixed space, hence $\mathbb E A_\ell=0$. For inert $p$ and split $\ell$, the off-diagonal matrix has fixed dimension one when the product of its two nonzero entries is 1. For inert $p$ and inert $\ell$ the map $x\mapsto c x^\ell$ has fixed dimension one when $N(c)=1$. Both inert-$p$ cases give $\mathbb E A_\ell=1/\ell$. In all four cases $\mathbb E(B_\ell-A_\ell)\ll\ell^{-2}$, including the probability of $u=\ell^2$. These identities apply for odd $\ell$ outside the finite excluded set.

## Passing from local actions to primes

Here is a direct quantitative estimate for the moment comparison. For a fixed smooth nonnegative test function $w$ compactly supported in $(0,\infty)$, the prime-ideal explicit formula and the uniform Dedekind strip give, for any number field $F$ of degree $d_F$ and discriminant $D_F$,
$$ \sum_{\substack{\mathfrak p\text{ degree one}}} \log(N\mathfrak p)\,w(N\mathfrak p/X)
 =X\!\int_0^\infty w(t)\,dt+O_w\big(X^{1-\delta}(\log(2D_F)+d_F\log(2X))^2\big) $$
with any fixed $0<\delta<1/32$. A unit-height zero count is $O(\log(2D_F)+d_F\log(2+|t|))$ and the Mellin transform of $w$ decays faster than any fixed inverse power. Prime powers, higher residue degrees and the archimedean terms are absorbed by the displayed bound. This use of Dedekind zeta functions includes non-Galois fixed fields. [Uniform nonvanishing](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3)

For any finite Galois set $S$, $\#\operatorname{Fix}(\mathrm{Frob}_p,S)$ sums the degree-one primes over $p$ in its orbit fields, excluding ramified primes. Products of fixed counts correspond to Cartesian products of the sets. Thus every fixed moment involving the $A_\ell,B_\ell$ is a linear combination of prime averages over such sets by their displayed polynomial formulas. For $s$ distinct indices of product $m$, their joint splitting field has degree $\le m^6$ since each affine image embeds in its full two-dimensional affine group. Appending the fixed CM field costs a factor of at most two; a split-prime indicator there is half its two-element action's fixed count. Ramification is confined to the fixed bad primes and the primes of $m$, since multiplication by $m$ and its fiber on an abelian scheme of good reduction are finite étale away from $m$. In an orbit field of degree $n$ this implies $\log D_F\ll_{s,E} n\log(2mn)$: the local different exponent is at most $e-1+e v_t(e)$ at ramified residue characteristic $t$. For fixed moment order, the number and sizes of the sets and all coefficients from the indicator expansions are $m^{O_s(1)}$. The frozen $m$ can be reduced to its set of distinct primes even for repeated indices.

It follows that the weighted prime expectation of any given fixed product of indicators, conditional on a CM coset if needed, differs from its finite-group expectation by $O_{w,s,E,R}(X^{-\delta}m^{O_s(1)}(\log X)^{O(1)})$. For the main term use that the mean of the fixed count is the number of orbits. Conditional normalizations in the CM cases have limiting density $1/2$ and the same power-saving error. No Artin holomorphy estimate is needed.

Put $z=X^{1/\mu^{1/4}}$. For large $X$ this exceeds $Y$, and the number of prime factors $>z$ of any reduced order is $O(\mu^{1/4})$ by Hasse's bound $\#E(\mathbb F_p)\ll X$. For each fixed moment order the number of tuples from $Y<\ell\le z$ is $X^{o(1)}$ and their products are $X^{o(1)}$. Summing the errors in the moment comparison, including the centering factors, gives $o(1)$. On each of the applicable Galois cosets the $A_\ell$ follow an independent Bernoulli model. The sum of its means and the sum of its variances are both
$$ \log(\log z/\log Y)+O_{E,R}(1)=\mu-\tfrac14\log\mu+O_{E,R}(1). $$
For the split CM coset this uses the fixed-field Mertens formula for split primes in a quadratic field. Removing the finitely many excluded indices changes the count by a bounded amount. Each Bernoulli cumulant of fixed order at least three is $O_s(\mathbb E A_\ell)$. The normalized fixed moments therefore converge to the Gaussian moments. The sum of $B_\ell-A_\ell$ has bounded expectation, so it is $o(\sqrt\mu)$ in probability after adding back the omitted large primes and finite excluded set. The moment method gives the claimed weak limit under each fixed smooth prime weight. Approximating the dyad indicator from above and below by fixed smooth weights, and using $\log p=\log X+O(1)$ there, gives the uniform prime law. The balanced pair conclusion follows by a union over the fixed library at each factor.

## Simultaneous polynomial-sized prime factors in any fixed dimension

Let $A_i/\mathbb Q$ be any finite list of abelian varieties with infinite-order marks $R_i\in A_i(\mathbb Q)$. For any fixed integer $s\ge1$ there is $c>0$, depending on the list and $s$, and infinitely many balanced products $pq$ for which **each** of these marks has the same $s$ distinct primes $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_s\ge p^c$ dividing its order at both $p$ and $q$. Different varieties and their marks may satisfy algebraic dependencies. This statement uses the same Dedekind strip and the adelic Kummer theorem above. The normal-limit argument used for elliptic curves is unnecessary here.

In the product of the $A_i$, take $B$ to be the identity component of the Zariski closure of the subgroup generated by $R=(R_i)$. Multiplication by the component-group order $a$ gives a point $aR$ dense in $B$. Each image $B_i$ under projection has positive dimension. Outside a finite set of primes the projection $B[\ell]\to B_i[\ell]$ is surjective: kill the finite component group of the projection kernel and use surjectivity of multiplication by $\ell$ on its identity component. Take $s$ primes $\ell_j\in(T,2T]$ outside this finite set and beyond the Kummer index bound, and put $m=\prod_j\ell_j$, $F=\mathbb Q(B[m])$. The translation group over $F$ for $[m]^{-1}(aR)$ is the full $B[m]$ by Theorem 6.9. For each $i,j$ let $F_{ij}$ adjoin to $F$ all the $\ell_j$-divisions of $aR_i$ inside $B_i$. These are Galois fields over $\mathbb Q$. Projection of the Kummer translations gives $[F_{ij}:F]\ge\ell_j$.

An unramified prime splitting in $F$ and failing to split in every $F_{ij}$ fixes the torsion, but in each division torsor acts by a nonzero translation. Consequently $aR_i$ is not divisible by $\ell_j$ in $B_i(\mathbb F_p)$, so the order of $R_i$ in its original variety is divisible by $\ell_j$. The local action on $B_i$ suffices for this conclusion. The degree of every field just used is $T^{O(1)}$, from the affine action in fixed dimension, and the respective logarithmic discriminants are $T^{O(1)}$ by the same ramification bound. Count split primes in each Galois field by its degree-one prime-ideal sum divided by its degree. The union bound on the undesired split subfields leaves main coefficient at least $(1-\sum_{ij}1/\ell_j)/[F:\mathbb Q]$. For $X=T^{C}$ with a sufficiently large constant $C$ the explicit formula at a fixed nonnegative weight inside $(X,2X]$ makes its total error $o(X/[F:\mathbb Q])$. The dyad therefore supplies at least two primes with the desired property for each large $T$. The common $\ell_j$ are at least $p^{1/(2C)}$. This result establishes a worst-case obstruction for the fixed library's smooth and finitely semismooth scalar tests in every fixed dimension.

## Exact finite checks

The replay exhausted 121,664 affine maps, covering the general linear group and all four split/nonsplit Cartan cosets at $\ell=3,5,7$. Both indicator polynomials and all 15 finite-group probabilities matched their direct fixed-point counts. On each of a non-CM example and a CM example it computed actual marked orders at 2,095 good primes. It found 633 and 418 orders respectively with at least two distinct prime factors exceeding seven. The same two primes 11 and 13 divided 25 and nine orders, respectively. These counts illustrate finite instances of the divisibility test. [Exact GP replay](../experiments/fixed-marked-orders.gp)
