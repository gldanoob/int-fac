# A prime cutoff independent of the number of parity relations

Let \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) be monic and irreducible of degree \(d\ge2\), let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\) for \(P(\alpha)=0\), and write \(B=2|\operatorname{disc}(P)|\). On a space of marked products with even valuations at every finite prime ideal of \(K\), degree-one quadratic character probes at rational primes up to

\[
 M=10+2d+\operatorname{bitlength}(B),\qquad X=(1024M)^2
\]

recover the exact square-class kernel under Dedekind GRH for \(K\) and its quadratic extensions generated by the nonsquare products in that space. This cutoff does not use the number of columns or the sizes of their products. The raw and planted one-third polynomials have \(\log B=O(\log N)\), so \(X=O((\log N)^2)\). For two fields, apply the result to each and stack their rows on the joint ideal-parity space. The Dirichlet critical-line statement used in the two-thirds proof has a different scope. ## Discriminant and inert primes

If \(\beta\in K^\times\) is a nonsquare with even finite valuations, put \(E=K(\sqrt\beta)\). Locally at each finite prime, choose a uniformizer \(\pi\) and write \(\beta=\pi^{2k}u\) with \(u\) a unit. The local quadratic algebra is generated by \(\sqrt u\); the order with basis \(1,\sqrt u\) has discriminant \(4u\). The discriminant of the maximal local order divides this ideal, also in the split case. Consequently the relative discriminant divides \((4)\) and

\[
 |\Delta_E|\le |\Delta_K|^2\,4^d .
\]

Every odd prime is unramified in \(E/K\). The exclusions \(p\mid B\) let us represent the degree-one primes in \(K\) by the simple roots of \(P\bmod p\). No mark norms occur in these exclusions.

For a number field \(J\) let \(\Psi_J(x)=\sum_{(\mathrm N\mathfrak p)^j\le x}(x-(\mathrm N\mathfrak p)^j)\log\mathrm N\mathfrak p\), summed over prime ideals and integers \(j\ge1\). For \(x\ge3\) the smoothed prime ideal theorem gives

\[
 |\Psi_J(x)-x^2/2|\le20(1+\log|\Delta_J|+[J:\mathbb Q])x^{3/2}.
\]

This follows from Theorem 1.1 of Grenié and Molteni by discarding its negative terms and using \(\log x\le\sqrt x\). Here the degrees of both fields are at least two. [Grenié–Molteni, *Explicit smoothed prime ideals theorems under GRH*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4465)

In degree \(n\), all terms at norm powers \(p^k\) with rational exponent \(k\ge2\) contribute at most \(12n x^{3/2}\). At fixed \(p,k\) the sum of contributing residue degrees is at most \(n\). Also \(\vartheta(y)=\sum_{p\le y}\log p\le 4(\log2)y\): sum \(\vartheta(2^j)-\vartheta(2^{j-1})\le2^j\log2\) from the central binomial coefficient and round up to a power of two. Hence \(\sum_{k\ge2}\vartheta(x^{1/k})\), restricted to \(k\le\log_2 x\), is at most \(4(\log2)(\sqrt x+x^{1/3}\log_2x)\le12\sqrt x\), using \(x^{-1/6}\log x\le6/e\). The remaining terms above \(p\mid B\) contribute at most \(n x\log B\).

Delete these two types from \(2\Psi_K(x)-\Psi_E(x)\). A remaining degree-one prime of \(K\) over \(p\) gives zero if split and \(2(x-p)\log p\) if inert. The prime ideal estimates use \(1+\log|\Delta_K|+d\le M\) and \(1+\log|\Delta_E|+2d\le2M\). It follows that

\[
 2\sum_{\mathfrak p\ {\rm good,\ deg=1,\ inert}} (x-\mathrm N\mathfrak p)\log\mathrm N\mathfrak p
 \ge x^2/2-128M x^{3/2}-4M^2x ,
\]

where the sum runs through norm \(x\). At \(x=X\) the right hand side exceeds \(X^2/4\), so at least \(X/(8\log X)\) prime ideals detect this nonsquare. The same cutoff works for all products simultaneously.

## Computing each column at all the probe primes

For \(p\nmid B\) and \(P(r)=0\bmod p\), evaluation at the simple Hensel lift \(\alpha_r\) identifies the completion with \(\mathbb Q_p\). Use the quadratic character of the leading unit \(p^{-v_p(z)}z\bmod p\) for any \(z\ne0\). It is multiplicative and equals \(+1\) on squares. When \(v_p(\beta)\) is even, its value is \(-1\) precisely when the local quadratic extension is inert. This defines a single binary row on every column, even when a mark itself is divisible by \(p\).

For a primitive linear mark \(z=a-b\alpha\), evaluate \(a-br\) when it is nonzero modulo \(p\). Otherwise \(p\nmid b\). Write \(H=b^dP(a/b)\ne0\) and \(v=v_p(H)\). Factoring \(P(T)=(T-\alpha_r)g(T)\) over \(\mathbb Z_p\) gives \(g(a/b)\equiv P'(r)\ne0\bmod p\). The leading unit of the mark is therefore

\[
 (H/p^v)\, b^{1-d}/P'(r)\pmod p .
\]

This uses the signed norm and requires no explicit high-precision lift. A shared minus-sign column uses the character of \(-1\). Every square product passes every row unconditionally. Under the stated GRH assumption, the inert-prime bound proves the converse on the ideal-parity space. A smaller finite collection is also sound as a filter: if exact field arithmetic certifies squares for a basis of its survivors, the resulting kernel is complete without the analytic assumption.

For example, in \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})\), the nonsquare \(\beta=2\) has even finite valuations. Its extension \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5},\sqrt2)\) has absolute discriminant \(6400=20^2\cdot4^2\), attaining the local-to-global bound. In \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), at the root \(r=3\bmod7\) the mark \(3-\sqrt2\) has norm 7 and leading unit \(6\bmod7\). Its probe is defined even though its ordinary residue vanishes. Detecting and extracting all square dependencies still leaves the separate requirement that some dependency split the input. [Basis splitting criterion](cogentic-square-kernel-splitting.md)
