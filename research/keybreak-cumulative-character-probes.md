# Polynomial-bit character probes using the cumulative Chebotarev bound

A single KeyBreak epoch in an odd-degree field can keep the original unconditional analytic inputs of Proposition E.3 while changing where it spends the factorial cost. Make $(2d)!\operatorname{poly}(n)$ inexpensive proposals at polynomial bit length, then compress their signs into polynomially many columns. At $d\sim\delta(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$ and retained occurrence coefficient $b$, setup costs $L_N[1/3,2\delta/3]$ and retained work costs $L_N[1/3,b+2\delta/3]$. All random choices and character data are generated after receiving the integer. This also gives an alternative to the [smaller probe list obtained using a signed prime-count estimate](cogentic-single-field-square-certification.md). [KeyBreak, Proposition E.3 and §H.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=89)

## A lower bound for each nonrational class

Keep E.12's polynomial integer $T$ bounding the input bit length, $d$, the logarithms of the absolute discriminants of all the required quadratic extensions, the atom-norm bound, and the log cardinality $h^*$ of the allowed square-class group. Increase $T$ to cover the log of the power-order discriminant and the excluded primes. Put $S=T^6$, $x=2^S$, $F=(2d)!$. For a nonrational class of $H_D$, the field $E=K(\sqrt\gamma)$ has absolute degree $2d$ and contains no rational quadratic subfield, by the odd-degree argument of E.3. For the quadratic extension $E/K$ the nonidentity conjugacy class has relative size **one half**, also the coefficient of the possible exceptional term in the absolute Chebotarev error. In particular the *cumulative* inert count at $x$ is at least
$$
 \tfrac12 [\operatorname{Li}(x)-\operatorname{Li}(x^{\beta_0})]
       - Cx\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x/(2d)}).
$$
Omit the subtracted logarithmic integral in the absence of an exceptional zero. Stark's bound gives $1-\beta_0\ge (4F\log|\Delta_E|)^{-1}$. On $[x^{\beta_0},x]$ the integrand is at least $1/\log x$, so the bracket is $\gg x\min\{1/\log x,1/(F T)\}$. In particular it is $\gg x/(F T^6)$. The error is negligible here: $\sqrt{\log x/(2d)}\gg T^{5/2}$, whereas $\log F=O(T\log T)$. The theorem's range $\log x\gg d(\log|\Delta_E|)^2$ also holds. No sign comparison between two nearby Chebotarev counts is involved. [Lagarias and Odlyzko, Theorem 1.3](https://aareyanmanzoor.github.io/assets/articles/lagarias-odlyzko.pdf) · [Stark's bound as used in KeyBreak E.3](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=89)

Discard ideals of norm at most $\sqrt x$ and all ideals of absolute residue degree greater than one. The former have at most $d\sqrt x$ possibilities, since at most $d$ prime ideals lie over a rational prime; for the latter the underlying rational prime is also at most $\sqrt x$. This loss is negligible uniformly. All remaining prime norms exceed the excluded primes, field/order discriminants and atom norms. Draw an $S$-bit block for a uniform integer in $[1,x]$, rejecting integers at most $\sqrt{x}$ and composites. On an admitted prime, find all linear roots in a fixed order, select a uniform index among $d$ positions, and reject an empty position. Each eligible degree-one ideal is selected with probability $1/(dx)$ per trial. The unconditional probability of detecting the specified nonrational class on a trial is therefore $\gg 1/(dFT^6)$. An integer budget $R=FT^{10}$ makes the probability of missing *any* nonrational class $o(1)$ by independence and the union bound over at most $2^{h^*}$ classes. The constants need only apply above a fixed input-size threshold.

## Compressing the results before building the matrix

Use $m=h^*+\lceil\log_2(128)\rceil$ independent binary output coordinates (round $h^*$ upward if necessary). For each successful proposal store its prime, root, and an independent uniformly random $m$-bit mask. The compressed character of an atom is the XOR of masks from probes on which its sign is negative. Failed proposals represent the trivial character. Every retained atom is a unit at all actual probe primes, and the construction respects multiplication on these atoms. The sign vector of a class with even valuations at the probes is well defined by its local unit part, independently of its representative modulo squares.

Condition on the uncompressed probes having detected every nonrational class. For any one of those classes at least one probe has negative sign. Its mask makes the compressed vector uniform in $\mathbf F_2^m$, even conditional on all the other masks. The union bound gives simultaneous failure probability at most $2^{h^*-m}\le1/128$. This is a statement about the full allowed group before the rows are chosen. If a dependency has zero compressed characters and passes the original ideal-parity columns, its class is rational with the same implication used by E.4. The checked norm and the other rational columns, as in E.4, then give the square. The rest of the epoch uses public data through the exact square verification; the final private signs retain their original law.

Primality of each proposed integer may be certified in polynomial bit time in $S$. The H.6 finite splitting and exact root checks apply with polynomial-bit coefficients and primes. A polynomial-in-$T$ cap with per-trial coupling error $\exp(-\Omega(T^2))$ suffices because $\log R=O(T\log T)$. Uniform scalar and root-index draw timeouts fit the same allowance. Store only valid probes and masks. The public random bits, sequential writes and setup computation cost $R\operatorname{poly}(T)$, including failures. Only completed occurrences scan that list to form their compressed signs, for $R\operatorname{poly}(T)$ each. Failed trees use the original local records. The matrix still has polynomially many character columns; its factor-base dimension and all its other columns are as in the original construction. Every returned integer factor and the lifted square undergo the original exact checks. [KeyBreak (E.14), Theorem E.4 and (H.27)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=90)
