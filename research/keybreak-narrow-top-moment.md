# Ordinary top moments for the narrower coefficient Gaussian

Fix a top band with prime mark denominators of size \(V\), dimension \(d\to\infty\), and \(X=V^d\). Suppose \(\log V\asymp\log M\), \(H=o(\log V)\), \(d=o(\log V)\), and the smoothness bound has the form \(y=V^{c+o(1)}\) for fixed \(c>0\). The indicated degree bound holds in the fixed top bands and ensures that \(2^d\) and powers of \(\log X\) are \(V^{o(1)}\). Use the original mark slope range \(1/6\le a_i/b_i\le 11/12\), with \(G_i\ne0\), \(b_i\nmid 2G_i\), and \((N,G_1G_2)=1\) on the continuing paths used for the ideal estimate. Take the public coefficient Gaussian with \(\sigma\ge V^{37/20-o(1)}\), \(\sigma=V^{O(1)}\). For an ordinary parent use division \(q=1\). For an incoming child use its single admissible root event, with \(q\) prime and both points on that root lattice, \(q\nmid b_1b_2\). Assume the ordinary eligible-point counts, density lower bounds and separate-period bounds for the band. Use Dirichlet GRH in the progression estimate (G.8). Under these hypotheses the original ideal-Gaussian ordinary second-moment upper bound transfers to the actual coefficient Gaussian on a rational-good region. This statement only uses the product of ordinary smoothness tests at the top. ## One nonthin pair on its exact support

For distinct prime denominators put \(\vartheta=b_1b_2/|\Delta|\). Assume \(\vartheta\le V^{1/1000}\). Write \(z_i=F(P_i)/q\) for the divided integer in the predicates and \(T_i=2\sigma |G(P_i)|\|v(P_i)\|/q\) for its marginal Gaussian scale. The support is a union of Cartesian progressions with separate periods \(m_i=2|G(P_i)\Delta|/q\). Use marginal cutoff \((\log X)^2\) and endpoint \(X_i=|\mu_i|+(\log X)^2T_i\), enlarged by a factor at most two for rounding, with \(\mu_i=F_0(P_i)/q\). The original digit bound on \(F_0\) and slope bound give \(|\mu_i|/T_i\ll \mathrm{poly}(d)2^d/\sigma=o(1)\). In particular \(X_i=V^{d+o(d)}\), \(m_i=V^{O(1)}\), and
\[
 \frac{X_i}{m_i b_i^{d-2}}\gg\frac{\sigma b_i}{|\Delta|}
 \gg V^{17/20-o(1)}.
\]
Using these endpoints in Fourier bounds does not change the source's reference smoothness density, which is defined with its own padded endpoint in (G.25). The ideal pair probability below is always under the full ideal Gaussian on the exact support.

In the Poisson fiber formula the integer kernel stays \(\mathbb Z^d\cap\ker E\), including under the incoming root. Both marks satisfy \(F(P_i)\equiv b_i^dF(s,1)\pmod q\), so the single root event is equivalent to divisibility of either raw value and leaves the difference kernel of each fiber unchanged. The raw periods \(2|G_i\Delta|\) are divisible by \(q\), since the marks belong to the same root lattice. Thus the restriction retains whole raw phases. On the restricted image divide the values, centers, periods and cap endpoints by \(q\), taking the raw cap to be \(qX_i\). The periodic frequency coefficient is multiplied by \(q\), so the step frequency on each divided phase is exactly the raw step frequency, and \(X_i/m_i\) is also unchanged. The affine Gaussian retains its original center after restriction and then scales by \(1/q\). The mass normalizations in the Poisson quotient use that restricted ideal image for both numerator and denominator. They require no estimate of the root-event probability. Root-event probability and finite-sampler conditioning costs are charged separately when needed. Further congruence restrictions that are independent of the two values can change the kernel, and this calculation assumes only the specified root event. [Poisson fiber formula](keybreak-narrow-smooth-probes.md) · [Restricted phase structure](keybreak-thin-top-pairs.md#conditioning-on-the-public-root-and-dividing)

Let \(s=A^{\mathsf T}w\), with \(w\) in the dual kernel. Truncate at \(\sigma\|w\|\le(\log X)^2\). The sum of discarded Fourier weights is bounded by \(\exp(-\Omega((\log X)^4))\) times \(X^{3/20+o(1)}\). In each retained mode \(\|s\|\ll V^2(\log X)^2/\sigma\), so that \(\|s\|\le V^{3/20+o(1)}\). The triangular lift \(v_s\) has norm at most \(144\|s\|/(b_1b_2)\), hence \(\sigma\|v_s\|\ll(\log X)^2\) uniformly. The periodic part of the phase is controlled by the short-digit frequencies. The remaining factor \(e(-\langle v_s,x_z\rangle)\) is affine, with frequency in Mahalanobis coordinates bounded by \(\sigma\|v_s\|\), since the minimum-norm lift divided by \(\sigma\) is an isometry from centered Mahalanobis coordinates into the Euclidean row space. Translation of the phase affects only its constant.

On each Cartesian phase use the absolute interval mode sum for \(\epsilon=3/20\). The Gaussian has normalized correlation determinant with square root \(\asymp1/\vartheta\) by its last two evaluation coordinates. The normalized mixed-variation lemma therefore costs at most \(V^{2/1000+o(1)}\), including both marginal endpoint-to-width ratios. Its mesh condition holds since \(m_i/T_i=X^{-1+o(1)}\). Its truncation error remains negligible upon multiplication by the number of modes. Therefore the numerator of the Poisson quotient is \(\nu(f)+O(V^{-1/2})\) and the denominator is \(1+O(V^{-1/2})\), uniformly after mixing the Cartesian phases by their Gaussian masses. Here \(\nu\) is the normalized ideal image on the specified restricted or unrestricted support and \(f\) is the ordinary pair test. The exponent \(1/2\) uses \(229/400-2/1000>1/2\) and absorbs endpoint losses. Since \(0\le f\le1\), the actual pair probability is \(\nu(f)+O(V^{-1/2})\). An individual ideal probability need not have a lower bound for this argument. [Uniform interval mode sum](keybreak-low-height-sum.md) · [Gaussian variation](keybreak-gaussian-variation.md)

## From pairs to the moment

On a rational-good box with geometric modulus \(r\), suppose the number of eligible points is at least \(V^2 e^{-O(H)}/r\), with \(r/V^2\le V^{-c_0}\) for a fixed \(c_0>0\). A fixed point has \(O(1+V^2/(r V^{1/1000}))\) partners with small determinant and \(O(1+V/r)\) with the same denominator. These include the diagonal. Their union is a fraction \(V^{-\min(c_0,1/1000)+o(1)}\). The ordinary diagonal or pair test is bounded by one. Elsewhere the actual probability differs from the ideal by at most \(O(V^{-1/2})\). Bounded-ratio incidence weights give the same fractional estimates. The reference densities are at least \(e^{-O(H)}\). The combined error is therefore negligible even after division by the product of the reference densities. [Rational normalization and collision count](keybreak-thin-top-pairs.md)

The ideal part of G.9 uses its box sizes and smooth-value progression estimates, independently of the coefficient-space two-point smoothing inequality. Its upper bound is \(D^{O(1)}c_1c_2\) once the actual heights satisfy (G.23), the reference scales (G.25) and (G.26), and the separate-period error bound. Write \(\ell_i=RT_i/(C_0\vartheta)\) for the grid length in the proof of G.9. Its retained radii satisfy \(y^{-C_R}\le R\le n\), so \(\ell_i=X^{1+o(1)}\) throughout at the stated top scales. For each contributing forced gcd \(g\le m_i=V^{O(1)}\), the length \(\ell_i/g\) exceeds \(2y_i\); the padded endpoint of G.9 is at least \(2\ell_i\). The progression error \(O_\tau(X_i^{1/2+\tau}m_i^\tau)\) divided by \(\ell_i/m_i\) is \(X^{-1/2+\tau+o(1)}\), negligible relative to \(c_i\ge e^{-O(H)}\). These comparisons use the endpoints and periods of the divided support. The reference saddle-point estimates must also hold for the actual \(\sigma\); invariance of the progression height \(X_i/m_i\) under division does not make the reference smoothness density invariant. [KeyBreak, ideal proof of Lemma G.9](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=115)

This yields the ordinary second moment on the parent-good and incoming-good regions whenever those reference conditions hold. Each one-point coefficient fiber has a basis of lengths \(O(V)\), so a width \(\sigma\gg V\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N)\) still gives its pointwise comparison. Interior two-point comparisons use the original kernel estimate if \(\sigma\gg V_1^2\mathrm{poly}(\log N)\). The source's congruence probability comparisons also use \(\sigma\gg Y_1^3\mathrm{poly}(\log N)\). For different fixed top stages take \(V\) to be the local box. If \(V\le V_{\max}^{9/10+o(1)}\), the original two-point smoothing bound already follows from width \(V_{\max}^{37/20+o(1)}\). In larger boxes the inequality \(\sigma\ge V^{37/20-o(1)}\) holds and all preceding Fourier estimates apply. Their size and density margins are needed only for the finitely many fixed stages.
