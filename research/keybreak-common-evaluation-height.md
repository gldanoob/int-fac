# Height and shared evaluation maps in several number fields

Suppose several small defining polynomials share one modular root on a single input $N$. A way to save algebraic columns would be to realize the polynomials in a common field, or in extensions of a common high-degree field, while keeping a common rational evaluation. There is an arithmetic obstruction to both constructions at the current KeyBreak heights. It concerns distinct defining polynomials with compatible maps on the shared field.

## One field

Let $K$ have degree $d$ and let $\psi:\mathcal O_K\to\mathbb Z/N$ be a unital evaluation map. It is surjective on the integers, so its kernel $I$ has norm $N$. Extend evaluation to elements with denominator coprime to $I$. For $z\ne0$ write $(z)=A/B$ in coprime integral ideals. The multiplicative absolute height raised to degree $d$, denoted $H_K(z)$, equals $\mathrm N(B)\prod_{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C}\max(1,|\sigma z|)$. The product formula gives $\mathrm N(A)\le H_K(z)$. If $\psi(z)=0$, then $I$ divides $A$. Consequently distinct elements with the same evaluation satisfy
$$
 N\le H_K(\alpha-\beta)\le 2^d H_K(\alpha)H_K(\beta).
$$
The upper bound follows from the ordinary triangle inequality at archimedean places and the ultrametric inequality elsewhere. For a primitive generator $\alpha$ the height $H_K(\alpha)$ equals the Mahler measure of its primitive integral minimal polynomial, including its leading coefficient. In particular the argument allows a nonmonic polynomial when its leading coefficient is a unit at $N$. Working with the integralized generator can overstate its height by a leading-order amount. The rational representative $r/Q$ of the shared modular value also has height roughly $M^d$ in $K$ and need never enter this difference bound. [KeyBreak field and evaluation setup](keybreak-public-fiber-order.md)

When $2^d H_K(\alpha)H_K(\beta)<N$, a fixed evaluation therefore has at most one such small generator for the prescribed modular value. If several maps are known, compare their values on a common separating set, for example an integral basis. Distinct maps agreeing modulo any prime $p\mid N$ give a proper gcd from a difference that is nonzero modulo $N$. In the absence of such a split their reductions at one fixed $p$ are pairwise distinct. There are at most $d$ maps $\mathcal O_K\to\mathbb F_p$: the kernels are distinct primes of residue degree one and their number is bounded by $d$. In a monogenic presentation it is enough to compare the generator if it separates maps at $N$, as it does in the source's unit-index or simple-root situation. At prime powers the comparison uses the supplied map on the full modulus. [Lean catalog bound and proper gcd](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedEvaluationMaps.lean)

## A common subfield

More generally take $K_1,K_2$ with degrees $d_1,d_2$, containing an identified field $E$ of degree $e$. Suppose their evaluation maps agree on $\mathcal O_E$, and small primitive generators $\alpha_i$ evaluate to the same $m\bmod N$. Invert the two native leading coefficients, assumed coprime to $N$. Then the monic relative minimal polynomials $g_i$ of $\alpha_i$ over $E$ have coefficients integral in this localization, since the generators are integral there. Both $g_i$ vanish at $m$ after reduction, so their resultant $R\in E$ evaluates to zero by the polynomial Bézout identity. For distinct absolute minimal polynomials the relative polynomials are distinct irreducibles and $R\ne0$. Applying the preceding numerator-ideal bound in $E$ gives $N\le H_E(R)$.

The resultant is a product of differences over the $(d_1/e)(d_2/e)$ pairs of relative roots. Applying the same placewise triangle estimates to these pairs, with multiplicities at each place of $E$, yields
$$
 e\log N\le d_1d_2\log 2+d_2\log H_{K_1}(\alpha_1)+d_1\log H_{K_2}(\alpha_2).
$$
This proof uses relative resultants, so it needs no common choice of a compositum place across the different primes dividing $N$. If many maps on $E$ are used, the gcd comparison partitions the no-split case into at most $e$ classes with identical restrictions on $E$.

At the ordinary single-input schedule $d_i\sim\delta D$, $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, $d_i\log M\sim\log N$, and both field coefficient heights are at most $M^{1+2v_0+o(1)}$ where $v_0=\alpha/(2\kappa)$. The factor $\sqrt{d_i+1}$ converting coefficient maximum to a Mahler upper bound costs $o(\log M)$. Thus equal-map pairs satisfy $e\le 2(1+2v_0)+o(1)$. The strict guard $2v_0<1$ gives **$e\le3$ for all sufficiently large inputs**. Since the native degrees are odd, the tower law leaves only a cubic nontrivial common subfield, and only when three divides both degrees. In particular an identified common subfield of degree at least four allows at most $e$ distinct native polynomials across all restriction classes on a continuing input. With the same asymptotic degree and any fixed constants in the field height, the general inequality gives $e=O(1)$. On the rough-input branch one can bound the number of fields even when comparing maps across presentations is expensive. Let $p_i^{a_i}$ be the rational prime-power factors of $N$, sorted largest first, and let $w=\omega(N)$. A map $\mathcal O_E\to\mathbb Z/p_i^{a_i}$ has at most $e$ possibilities: its kernel must be the $a_i$-th power of a prime of residue degree one, and a surjective unital map with that kernel is unique since the integers already surject. Choose a fixed $c<4$ above the asymptotic constant $2(1+2v_0)$. For $e\ge4$ the first $k=\lfloor cw/e\rfloor+1$ factors have product at least $N^{k/w}>N^{c/e}$ by their geometric mean. Two fields with the same restrictions on these factors have a common subfield evaluation modulo this product. Its size would violate the same resultant bound. There are at most $e^k$ tuples of restrictions. Because $e=O(D)$ and $w=o(H)$ on this branch, $\log(e^k)=O(\log e+w\log(e)/e)=o(H)$. Thus even without an explicit gcd comparison, this construction cannot supply $\exp(fH)$ distinct polynomials for fixed $f>0$. This is a counting argument and uses no prior knowledge of the prime factors in the algorithm.

Small public Möbius corrections can be handled by normalizing the generators first: a rational transform of coefficient maximum $T$ raises $H_K$ by at most $(2T)^d$ when its local denominator is a unit. Corrections with $\log T=O(H)$ preserve the bounded-common-degree conclusion for distinct normalized minimal polynomials, with the new constants substituted in the inequality. Two native presentations can have the same normalized polynomial. Genuinely different evaluation targets, incompatible subfields, and changes of chart that only reindex marks in an existing field require separate cost analyses. Independent fields with only the rational numbers in common remain possible.

## Arithmetic check

For $N=119$, evaluate $\sqrt 2\mapsto11$ and take $m=4$. Over $E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ use $X^2-(-6+2\sqrt2)$ and $X^2-(5+\sqrt2)$. Their absolute polynomials are $X^4+12X^2+28$ and $X^4-10X^2+23$. Each is irreducible because the radicand has nonsquare norm (28 and 23) and generates $E$. Their values at 4 are $4N$ and $N$, and both derivatives there are units modulo $N$. Their relative resultant is $(\sqrt2-11)^2$ of rational norm $N^2$. As a fractional-element check, $(\sqrt2-11)/2$ has norm $119/4$, evaluates to zero and has numerator ideal of norm 119. These examples illustrate the divisibility statements at bounded degree.

For draws from the ordinary Gaussian law, reduction at auxiliary primes further shows that the Galois closure is typically symmetric and small batches of closures are linearly disjoint. [Sampling bound and finite checks](keybreak-native-splitting-fields.md)
