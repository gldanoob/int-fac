# Subfields lower the character setup cost

The degree-one sampler for one odd number field uses polynomial-bit rational primes and spends $d!\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$ in its general bound. This factorial comes from a possible real zero of the base field's Dedekind zeta function. A known small normal closure or a tower of odd extensions with solvable relative normal closures lowers that term. The finite-field operations, number of character rows, and exact square verification use the same algorithm. [Single-field sampler](cogentic-single-field-square-certification.md)

## Smaller zero-gap parameters

If an effective bound $1-\beta_K\gg(G_0\log\Delta_K)^{-1}$ is available for the possible real exceptional zero of $K$, the dyadic degree-one prime-ideal count in the sampler gives an acceptance probability $\gg 1/(G_0\operatorname{poly}(\log N))$. Its conditional detecting fraction stays at least $1/3$. Each trial has polynomial bit cost, so unlimited rejection sampling has expected setup $G_0\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$, and an inflated cap gives the same leading high-probability cost. A numerical upper bound on $G_0$ suffices for the cap. The sampler can run without any group computation and its expected time still reflects the actual acceptance rate. [Signed relative prime count, Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 5.1](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf)

**A small normal closure.** Write $L$ for the rational normal closure and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbf Q)$ with its transitive action on the $d$ roots. Stark's discriminant inequality bounds $\log\Delta_L\le |G|\log\Delta_K$: the compositum takes at most $d$ conjugates of $K$ and each has index $|G|/d$ in $L$. The Aramata–Brauer theorem transfers a zero of $\zeta_K$ to $\zeta_L$. If $1-\beta\le(4|G|\log\Delta_K)^{-1}$, Stark's simple-zero region in $L$ and his descent theorem force a quadratic subfield of $K$ with the same real zero. The degree of $K$ is odd. Thus $G_0=|G|$ works. This reads the proof of Lemma 8 with the actual normal-closure degree. [Stark, Lemmas 3, 7, 8 and Theorem 3](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0023/PPN356556735_0023.pdf) · [Compositum discriminant, Lemma 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01023#page=9)

If the root action is primitive and $G$ does not contain $A_d$, Maróti bounds $|G|<50d^{\sqrt d}$. Hence for $d=O((\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3})$, the probe cost is $\exp(o((\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}))$. These promises concern the root action. For example a prime-degree field always has a primitive root action, and the conclusion covers a prime-degree group of order $d(d-1)$ as well as nonsolvable primitive groups satisfying the same exclusion. More generally, an intermediate field of degree $n$ with $[K:F]=r$ gives an action on $n$ blocks of $r$ roots, and $|G|\le(r!)^n n!$. This can furnish a usable cap even with a nonsolvable relative normal closure. [Maróti, Corollary 1.1(ii)](https://users.renyi.hu/~maroti/primitive.pdf#page=2)

**An odd solvable relative extension.** For an odd extension $L'/F'$ of relative degree $r$ with solvable normal closure over $F'$, Murty's Theorem 4.1 descends a zero of $\zeta_{L'}$ to $\zeta_{F'}$ if its real gap is at most $c/(\delta(r)\log\Delta_{L'})$, with effective absolute $c>0$ and

$$\delta(r)=(e(r)+1)^2 3^{1/3}12^{e(r)-1}=O(r^4),\qquad e(r)=\max_{p\mid r}v_p(r).$$

Consider a tower $F=K_0\subset K_1\subset\cdots\subset K_j=K$, with $K$ odd and these hypotheses at every step. The discriminant tower formula gives $\log\Delta_{K_i}\le\log\Delta_K$. A gap smaller than $c/(\max_i\delta(r_i)\log\Delta_K)$ therefore descends through the whole tower. Apply Stark's bound in $F$ if $[F:\mathbf Q]>1$, or use the absence of real zeros of $\zeta_{\mathbf Q}$ in $(0,1)$ if $F=\mathbf Q$. Thus an effective setup bound is

$$\max\left\{[F:\mathbf Q]!,\ \max_i r_i^4\right\}\operatorname{poly}(\log N).$$

In particular it is polynomial in $\log N$ for a whole odd tower of solvable-relative steps from $\mathbf Q$, including a growing prime-degree solvable field. For a single fixed odd solvable-relative step of degree $r$ over an unrestricted base of degree $d/r$, the leading setup coefficient falls from $\delta/3$ to $\delta/(3r)$ when $d\sim\delta(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$. A relative extension obtained by adjoining a cubic radical qualifies even with a nonsolvable rational normal closure. The factorization of the modulus, discriminant, and class number play no role. [Murty, §4, Theorem 4.1 and Proposition 5.1](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1806606820053512193-1806606820053512193-eb3f44ff2e1e6eddc6b52afa83a90046.pdf#page=7)

## A common-root field from one input

Fix $r=3^j$ with $j\ge1$ and an odd $n\ge3$, and put $d=rn$. For a composite $N$, first divide out 3 and 7 if present, and put $m=\lfloor N^{1/d}\rfloor+1$. A nonunit $m$ gives a proper gcd since $1<m<N$. We construct an irreducible monic $g(X)=f(X^r)$ of degree $d$ with $g(m)=0\pmod N$ and unit derivative there, or find a proper divisor on the way. The base field $F=\mathbf Q(\theta^r)$ for a root $\theta$ of $g$ has degree $n$. The extension $K/F$ is radical of degree $r$. Its relative normal closure is contained in $F(\theta,\zeta_r)$, which is solvable over $F$ because the radical conjugates over $F(\zeta_r)$ form a cyclic group and the cyclotomic quotient is abelian.

Choose a monic irreducible $\bar f$ of degree $n$ over $\mathbf F_7$ whose root $\eta$ is a noncube in $\mathbf F_{7^n}$. A primitive element provides one. Testing a candidate uses modular polynomial irreducibility and $\eta^{(7^n-1)/3}\ne1$, without factoring the multiplicative group order. Enumerating all $7^n$ candidates costs $7^n\operatorname{poly}(n)$ deterministically. Uniform random polynomial proposals give expected $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ work: $2/3$ of the nonzero field elements are noncubes, and at most $n7^{n/2}$ field elements lie in proper subfields. Each irreducible polynomial accounts for $n$ conjugate roots. The $r$th-root polynomial $X^r-\eta$ is irreducible over $\mathbf F_{7^n}$. To see this, put $q=7^n$ and $a=v_3(q-1)$. Every $r$th root $\xi$ of $\eta$ has $v_3(\operatorname{ord}\xi)=a+j$ because a noncube $\eta$ has order divisible by $3^a$. If $\xi$ lies in $\mathbf F_{q^s}$, then $a+j\le v_3(q^s-1)=a+v_3(s)$. Thus its degree is at least $r$ and is at most $r$ by its equation. Its generated field contains $\eta$, proving that $\bar f(X^r)$ is irreducible over $\mathbf F_7$.

Lift $\bar f$ to a monic integer polynomial $f_0$ with lower coefficients in $[0,6]$, and take the degree-$<n$ base-$m^r$ digit polynomial $R$ of the least nonnegative residue of $-7^{-1}f_0(m^r)$ modulo $N$. This degree bound follows from $(m^r)^n>N$. The polynomial $f=f_0+7R$ has $f(m^r)=0\pmod N$ and its reduction is $\bar f$. Its derivative at $m^r$ can be checked by gcd with $N$. A proper gcd gives a divisor. If the gcd is $N$, add $7(X-m^r)$ to $f$. The new derivative is $7$ modulo $N$, a unit. Now $g'(m)=r m^{r-1}f'(m^r)$ is a unit. These steps use only public integer and polynomial arithmetic. Irreducibility modulo 7 certifies irreducibility over $\mathbf Q$.

The coefficient bit heights are $O(1+\log(m^r))$ and $M(g)=M(f)$ for Mahler measure. For $d\sim\delta(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$ and fixed $r$, $\log M(g)\le (r/\delta+o(1))(\log N)^{2/3}(\log\log N)^{1/3}$. Thus the field can be constructed at the one-third digit scale from every input, with a height coefficient growing in $r$. For fixed $r=3$ the valuation-sampler setup drops to the base-degree factorial, giving $\delta/9$. Varying $r$ through fixed powers of three decreases the probe coefficient further and increases the digit-height coefficient. The selected field has sparse defining form $g(X)=f(X^r)$. Its homogeneous norms come from the substitution $(a,b)\mapsto(a^r,b^r)$ into the base form, so ordinary random-polynomial smoothness and incidence estimates do not automatically transfer. The construction supplies the modular evaluation, while a bound on productive relations is still required. The tower scarcity result for maximum relative degrees $o((\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3})$ does not cover the unrestricted base of degree $d/r$ at fixed $r$. [Existing tower scarcity](cogentic-tower-resultant-coverage.md)

An exact Sage replay tried $n=3,5,7$ and $r=3,9,27$ on 405 combinations with composite or prime test moduli coprime to 21. It checked the finite-field irreducibility, coefficient size, common root and derivative identity. There were 395 simple roots without a shift, two simple roots after the derivative shift, and eight proper gcds. This checks the construction and its exceptional paths.
