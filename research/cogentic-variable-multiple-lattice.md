# Varying the multiple of the input at the common root

Fix an integer $N\ge2$ and $m\ge2$. Write $R(T)$ for the base-$m$ digit polynomial of $N$. The full lattice of degree-$e$ coefficient vectors giving an integer multiple of $N$ at $m$ is
$$
 \mathcal L_e=\{H\in\mathbb Z[T]_{\le e}:N\mid H(m)\}.
$$
When $e\ge\deg R$, it has basis $R,(T-m),T(T-m),\ldots,T^{e-1}(T-m)$. Indeed subtracting $(H(m)/N)R$ from $H$ leaves an integral polynomial divisible by $T-m$. Its determinant is $N$, as the evaluation map onto $\mathbb Z/N$ is surjective on constants. Its basis operator norm in the coefficient Euclidean norm is at most $m\sqrt{e+1}+m+1$, by the triangle inequality and the $\ell^1$ norm of $T-m$. Identify these polynomials with binary forms of degree $e$ by homogenization.

Let $v_i=(a_i,b_i)$ be $h$ primitive and pairwise nonproportional projective marks, $\max(|a_i|,|b_i|)\le A$, and $\gcd(a_i-b_im,N)=1$. Put $P=\prod_i(b_iX-a_iY)$. At these marks the image of $\mathcal L_e$ equals the image of the entire coefficient lattice provided $e\ge h$. For an arbitrary form $F$, add $tP Y^{e-h}$ with $t\equiv-F(m,1)P(m,1)^{-1}\pmod N$. The marks retain their values and the result lies in $\mathcal L_e$. Its exact evaluation kernel in $\mathcal L_e$ is $P\mathcal L_{e-h}$ because $P(m,1)$ is a unit. If $e-h\ge\deg R$, the displayed basis therefore gives this kernel operator norm at most
$$
                 (2A)^h\,[m\sqrt{e+1}+m+1].                    \tag{1}
$$
Every extraneous row condition modulo a squarefree $Q$ with $\gcd(Q,N)=1$ has the same conditional image as in the unrestricted coefficient lattice. For example, after specifying any marked outputs, a finite root row missing all the marks is adjustable through $P\mathcal L_{e-h}$ because $N$ times every form belongs to $\mathcal L_{e-h}$. The conditional Poisson lemma hence holds with $s=W/(Q(2A)^h[m\sqrt{e+1}+m+1])$ for an isotropic coefficient Gaussian of width $W$ on $\mathcal L_e$. The factor from a controlled kernel is uniform in the marked values and prescribed congruences. [Integral projective evaluation and its Gaussian law](cogentic-projective-kernel.md)

This lattice Gaussian has a direct polynomial-bit sampler for the sizes used below. Orthogonalize the preceding integral basis using rational Gram matrices, and sample basis coordinates from last to first. If $b_j^*$ is the $j$th orthogonal vector, the conditional factor at that step is a one-dimensional Gaussian centered at the negative projection of the already chosen higher-coordinate combination onto $b_j^*$, with width $W/\|b_j^*\|$. The product of the unnormalized conditional factors is exactly the target radial Gaussian. Each one-dimensional normalizer divided by its width differs from $1$ by $O(e^{-\pi (W/\|b_j^*\|)^2})$, uniformly in its center, by Poisson summation. The joint law thus differs from the target by $O(e\exp(-\pi(W/[m\sqrt{e+1}+m+1])^2))$ in total variation, where the factor $e$ denotes the degree. To implement a shifted one-dimensional sampler, use rejection on an interval of radius $L^2$ times its width. The lost tails are $e^{-\Omega(L^4)}$, and rounding each acceptance probability to $O(L^2)$ binary places costs $e^{-\Omega(L^2)}$ in total variation. Integer intervals and rational Gram matrix arguments have polynomial bit length by the determinant bound on the integral basis. Truncate with radius rounded outward and discrete Gaussian width rounded to relative error $e^{-2L^2}$; relative changes on the retained interval are $O(L^4e^{-2L^2})$. Rational Gram data can in particular be kept exact for the centers. Each rejection loop uses $O(L^2)$ proposals in expectation. This is the usual sequential discrete Gaussian construction, and the preceding normalizer estimate gives the accuracy needed here.

## The two polynomial fields

Use $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$ and $V=DH$. Use the real targets at scale one and the projective Farey marks of the [fixed-multiple construction](cogentic-homogeneous-marks.md), with $d\sim xD$, $\log A\sim cH$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log K_1\sim H$ and $\log(1/w)\sim D\sqrt z$. Set $m=\lceil N^{1/(d-3)}\rceil$, $e=d-1$. After the perfect-power sweep, $N<m^{d-3}$, so $\deg R\le d-4\le e-2$. Choose an integer width $W$ above $m\exp(sH)$ with $s>2c+2b$, and with $\log(W/m)\sim sH$. Take $c_d$ to be the first admissible multiple $P_{\rm odd}3^k$ above $100d K_1 L_0^2P_0BW$. Construct each $f_i^{(0)}$ by the coefficientwise CRT rounding in Lemma 8.1, then subtract $P_0\ell$ times the digit polynomial of $(P_0\ell)^{-1}f_i^{\rm pre}(m)\bmod N$. The rounded coefficient error is bounded by $P_0\ell m$. Although $m$ here is a $(d-3)$rd root, the correction still fits in degree $d-4$ and the integer $f_i^{(0)}(m)/N$ only needs to exist. Each field uses, independently,
$$
            f_i=f_i^{(0)}+P_0\ell H_i,\qquad H_i\sim D_{\mathcal L_e,W}.
$$
Thus $N\mid f_i(m)$, both fields have unchanged leading coefficient and CRT properties, and reduction modulo 2 preserves irreducibility. Primitivity follows because any content prime divides $c_d$, whose prime factors divide $P_{\rm odd}$, and $f_i(0)\equiv1\pmod {P_{\rm odd}}$. The typical coefficient cutoff $\|H_i\|_\infty\le L_0^2 W$ holds with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(L^4)}$. One direct bound is to compare an exponential moment coordinatewise at width $\sqrt2 W$ with the normalizer at $W$. Their ratio is at most $2^{(e+1)/2}(1+o(1))$ by Poisson and (1) at $h=0$. The real-root sign brackets, grid separations and coefficient heights now follow exactly as in the scale-one construction. [Cogentic, base-polynomial CRT and extraction](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=37)

Keep every numerator in the grid at each denominator $b_v$, a prime in $A(1-e^{-30z^2})\le b_v\le A$. Initial gcds with $N$ of $a-b_vm$ split immediately if proper. Every such tested absolute value is $<N$ and nonzero. Every $p\le B$ is coprime to $N$ after the initial factor sweep. Set $J_*=\{p:\sqrt B<p\le B\}$, with no exclusions for the varying field cofactors. Permit the row $r=m\bmod p$ among the possible roots for each $p\ne\ell$. All coefficient residue vectors modulo any $Q\mid\prod_{p\in J_*}p$ occur in $\mathcal L_e$, independently and uniformly up to the above Poisson error when $Q\le B^2$. In particular multiple roots and shared roots each have probability $p^{-2}(1+O(e^{-L^3}))$ per residue. Each field has $p$ potential rows at $p$; the sum of their expected $p^{-\alpha_s}$ weights equals $p^{-\alpha_s}(1+O(e^{-L^3}))$. The variance at distinct residues of the same prime uses linear independence of the two evaluations modulo $p$, which holds as $e\ge1$. These replace the factor $p-1$ and omission of $r=m$ in the source. The target rows at $\ell$ still omit $m$ because of the fixed CRT polynomial, exactly as in the source.

In an absolute evaluation the coefficient perturbation is now $P_0\ell b_v H_i(a,b_v)$, of typical width comparable to $P_0\ell W b_v^d$. After dividing preassigned factors its one-point modulus is $P_0\ell^{1-\epsilon_i(v)}b_v$. At two distinct marks the conditional modulus at the second is
$$
      P_0\ell^{1-\epsilon_i(v')}b_{v'}\frac{|a b_{v'}-a'b_v|}{Q_{\rm both}}.
$$
All these moduli are $\exp(O(H))$. The ratio of center to width is $K_1^{1+o(1)}$ in one coordinate and at most $K_1^{1+o(1)}A^2$ in two. On the $L_0^3$-width first-coordinate cutoff all conditional center shifts are relatively $K_1^{-1+o(1)}$. The factor $b_v$ is coprime to every attainable absolute field value, because $F_i(a,b_v)\equiv c_da^d\pmod {b_v}$. All factors of the two-point determinant are treated by homogeneity as in the fixed-multiple proof. In particular the small-prime multiplier becomes
$$
   \gamma(v,v')=\prod_{d<p\le\sqrt B,\ p\mid (a b_{v'}-a'b_v)} p/(p-1),
$$
without any common-root exception. The same smooth-window and finite-difference saddle estimates give all the conditional one- and two-point formulas with their relative $O(e^{-50z^2})$ and polynomial rough upper bounds. They hold at arbitrary fixed positive $x,b,c$, since the modulus and the width-to-center exponent are $O(H)$ and the norm logarithm is $(S+o(1))V$ with $S=xc+1/x$. The two-field smooth probability at a mark is $\exp((-2S/(3b)+o(1))H)$. ## The cell estimate with two prescribed rows

We give the revised cell bound for Proposition 10.1. Write $Q=Q_{\rm pair}\le B^2$ for the squarefree modulus of the at most two root indicators in $f_*(v)=r_\alpha^\circ(v)r_\beta^\circ(v)$. Its support requires a single projective slope $r\bmod Q$ when $Q>1$. The cases $Q=1$, diagonal row $\alpha=\beta$ and mixed archimedean indices use the same convention. Let $g_*=f_*-\widetilde U_{\alpha\beta}^{\rm ex}$, let $M=M_{\alpha\beta}$ be the paper's entry scale, and let $\Pi_R$ be the product of the two one-point row factors. Uniformly,
$$
  |g_*(v)|\Pi_R(v)\le L^{C}M[1+Q\,1_{a\equiv r b_v\pmod Q}].     \tag{2}
$$
Indeed every deletion-factor gain is $p^{1-\alpha_s}L^{O(1)}=L^{O(1)}$ for $p\le B$, with at most three factors per mark. The normalized $f_*$ on its support is bounded by $L^{O(1)}Q$: two distinct row factors give $q^{\alpha_s}q'^{\alpha_s}\le qq'$ after division by $M$, and a diagonal row gives $q^{\alpha_s}\le q$. The real-coordinate factors and the reference mean divided by $M$ are polynomially bounded. This estimate permits the unprescribed $\ell$ to be omitted from the modulus in the large term. It was also derived independently in the local audit of the fixed-multiple construction.

For each denominator the numerator interval has length $\asymp Aw$, endpoints rounded to integers. For any residue class modulo $k$ its probability under the uniform numerator law is $1/k+O((Aw)^{-1})$, with absolute implied constant even if $k>Aw$. For a function varying by at most $L^{O(1)}M/(Aw)$ per numerator, partial summation gives the corresponding absolute weighted error $L^{O(1)}M/(Aw)$ after inclusion of the supremum. These bounds pass to the mixture of the actual denominators and to equal masses across grid intervals. In applying the weighted estimate the saddle weights themselves may depend on the denominator, since each centered calculation estimates its actual global average. Fix $T=e^{100z^2}$ and assume $c>2b$. Every occurrence of $L^{O(1)}Q T/(Aw)$ below is $e^{-\Omega(H)}$.

For the singular-series square write $\gamma^2=\sum_{h\mid\Delta}\eta(h)$ with $\eta$ multiplicative on squarefree products of $d<p\le\sqrt B$ and $\eta(p)=(2p-1)/(p-1)^2$. Then $\sum_h\eta(h)=\prod(1+\eta(p))\le (\log B)^{O(1)}$. At fixed first mark and second denominator, for any such $h$, the second numerator condition $h\mid\Delta$ is a single class modulo $h$, since $b_v$ is invertible there. Also $(h,Q)=1$. Consequently the mass of this class weighted by the square bracket in (2) is $O(1/h+Q/(Aw))$, uniformly for every $h$, regardless of its size. The first-mark bracket has bounded total mass. Thus the total absolute contribution of all $h>T$ is directly bounded by
$$
    L^{O(1)}M^2\sum_{h>T}\eta(h)[1/h+Q/(Aw)]
       \ll L^{O(1)}M^2[1/T+Q/(Aw)].                               \tag{3}
$$
There is no need to impose a size restriction on the full modulus $h$ in this estimate. For an off-diagonal pair also considered 2-unclean when $p^2\mid\Delta$ for some $p\mid Q\ell$, the same integer count gives weighted mass $O(1/p+Q/(Aw))$ at the second mark: the second intersection with the $Q$-class has modulus $\operatorname{lcm}(Q,p^2)\ge Qp$. Summing over at most three primes and multiplying the polynomial rough factor gives $e^{-\Omega(H)}M^2$. Restoring these pairs to the main sum costs the same, since $\gamma^2\le\sum_h\eta(h)$. The residual rare-prime exclusions in the clean two-point smoothness formula also cost $e^{-\Omega(H)}M^2$ using (2).

For each $h\le T$ and slope $r_h\bmod h$, compute the centered sum $J(h,r_h)=\sum_{a\equiv r_h b_v(h)}\pi_0(v)g_*(v)\Pi_R(v)$. First suppress extra target $\ell$ factors as in the source. If $\ell\nmid Q$, the discrepancy is supported on the $d$ fixed target slopes, and (2) with the integer-class bound gives absolute error at most $L^{O(1)}M h^{-1}[\ell^{-1}+h Q/(Aw)]$, after summing these slopes. If $\ell\mid Q$, the discrepancy vanishes on the unique full $Q$ support, where $f_*\ne0$ and the target factor is kept. Elsewhere $|g_*|\ll L^{O(1)}M$ and the discrepancy is confined to the $d$ target slopes, including the possible matching target slope with a failed second required row. This gives the same upper bound. Here $h$ is coprime to $Q\ell$. Denote by $\Pi_R^\circ$ the remaining factors. The source's local identity is $f_*\Pi_R^\circ=Q\,1_{a\equiv r b_v(Q)}(\widetilde U^{\rm ex}(v))_{\alpha\beta}$. Its right-hand atomic mean and first derivative have bounds $L^{O(1)}M$ and $L^{O(1)}M/(Aw)$; these follow from the saddle finite differences and the grid separation. Averaging at each denominator then summing gives the global reference term divided by $h$, with absolute error $L^{O(1)}M Q/(Aw)$. Inclusion-exclusion on the at most two row hits gives $\sum_{a\equiv r_h b_v(h)}\pi_0\Pi_R^\circ=h^{-1}(1+O(L^{O(1)}[B^{-0.49}+hQ/(Aw)]))$, since the extra local row gains are polynomial and their hit densities are inverse primes. The two main terms therefore cancel, and
$$
 |J(h,r_h)|\le L^{O(1)}M h^{-1}[B^{-0.49}+h Q/(Aw)].
$$
Summing its square over $h\le T$ and $h$ possible classes with weight $\eta(h)$ is negligible on the source's $e^{-7z^2}M^2$ scale. For the variance of total mass, the nonidentity part of the singular series is bounded by $O(\sum_{p>d}p^{-2})+e^{-\Omega(H)}$ using the same class count with $Q=1$, together with the polynomially small target corrections. The diagonal contribution to each centered entry is as in the two-dimensional mark calculation, $\exp((-2c+2b+2S/(3b)+o(1))H)M^2$. The estimates recover Proposition 10.1 whenever
$$
                   c>2b,\qquad 2c>2b+\frac{2S}{3b}.               \tag{4}
$$
The projected support and root-mass estimates in the remainder of the paper use only the stated entry bounds, the root probabilities above, and the smooth first marginal. The projected Helmert coordinates and their norm-direction shifts are the same as for fixed-multiple Farey marks. [Cogentic, centered Gram concentration and peeling](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=52)

The sieve and exact extraction from the two fields again cost in expectation
$$
 \exp\!\left((\max\{2c,4b,2c+2b-2S/(3b)\}+o(1))H\right).
$$
The Gaussian sampler and all coefficient constructions have smaller cost. Choosing $x=1/\sqrt c$, $S=2\sqrt c$, equations (4) admit strict rational schedules approaching $b=c/2$ and $c=(8/3)^{2/3}$ from the feasible side. The three cost coefficients coincide in the limit. This yields a manuscript-level per-input randomized complete-factorization bound with coefficient $2(8/3)^{2/3}=3.845998\ldots$ at the $L_N[1/3]$ scale, using the [exact randomized extractor cost](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md), the cited Cogentic algebraic lemmas and the [uniform smooth-window result](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md). Each fixed strict rational schedule has a uniform asymptotic analysis. The existing interleaving and exponent verification enable one algorithm to achieve the limiting bound.
