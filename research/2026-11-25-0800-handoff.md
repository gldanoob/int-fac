## Strongest reductions

Given one exact ordinary divisor sum $M=\sigma(N)$, a deterministic algorithm now completely factors every input with **unconditional polynomial average bit cost** over uniform integers of each bit length. This average is over the integers, with no random choices in the algorithm. At small-factor cutoff $L$, the unresolved fraction is $O(L^{-3/4})$ and a stage costs $O(\sqrt L\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N))$. Doubling the cutoff makes the mean series converge, including the fallback that finishes every input. The proof combines full generation in quadratic residue algebras, a primitive-character sieve for prime pairs and fast small-factor extraction. The exact replay factored an input with 11 distinct primes, including four repeated prime powers and seven residual primes. The divisor sum is supplied; computing it from $N$ is still open at the desired cost. [Algorithm, scope and proof](average-single-divisor-sum.md) · [Finite replay](../experiments/average-single-sigma.gp)

Complete factoring of arbitrary integers is deterministically polynomial-time equivalent to approximating one real number per odd modulus, $\log|W_m(1)|$, to error $2^{-20}/(1+\log_2m)^2$. Here $W_m$ is the normalized Salié sum. The approximation needs $O(\log\log(3m))$ output bits. A CRT descent replaces any unit frequency by $O(\log m)$ frequency-one queries on moduli at most $m^3$. The replay checked 13,722 transfers, including 195 independent literal root sums. A single fixed-threshold bit at frequency one also suffices with Las Vegas expected polynomial cost, using two auxiliary prime blocks to calibrate the magnitude. [Frequency-one reductions](fixed-frequency-salie.md)

A second reduction eliminates padding altogether. At the **original modulus** $N$, return one bit comparing $|W_N(t)|$ to one at each requested unit frequency. Adversarial replies are allowed within $1\pm2^{-12}$. This gives complete factoring in Las Vegas expected polynomial time for every input. It gives deterministic polynomial time for inputs with $O(\log b/\log\log\log(16b))$ distinct prime factors, where $b$ is the input bit length. The same proof applies to the normalized ordinary Kloosterman sum at the original square modulus. Ten numerical runs recovered all blocks of five inputs with 3–12 prime factors, including repeated powers. The 12-block input used 662,936 unit queries with 46 in-band replies. [Unpadded theorem, assumptions and replay](unpadded-magnitude-threshold.md)

For two odd prime-power blocks, deterministic polynomial recovery uses only $O(\log^2 N)$ of these unpadded threshold queries, all at unit frequencies $|t|\le\sqrt N/12$. On one fixture 4,700 short queries, including 22 ambiguous answers, led to ten translated lattice calls and the verified factor 1,000,033. [Short-frequency proof and replay](unpadded-magnitude-threshold.md#short-frequencies-for-a-two-block-input)

The quadratic floor-sum evaluator now has a complete table of the bits forced by a supplied congruence $q\equiv\pm H\bmod p$. At center $c$ the constant cases are exactly $H/\gcd(H,2c)\in\{1,2,3,6\}$ under the stated size bounds. At the other denominators both answers persist even after fixing $N$ to any reduced class modulo a fixed multiple of $H$. For $H=2^s$ or $3\cdot2^s$, three centers proportional to $H$ need only trial division through 16. [Direct evaluator and limitations](floor-sum-and-log-parity.md#which-remainders-determine-the-bit)

## Cubic search

For any supplied multiple $M$ of $\Phi_3(p)$ on balanced prime pairs, a deterministic algorithm now chooses cyclic cubic fields from the public integer $N$. Its limiting worst-multiplier mean is **two field tests**, optimal in the stated cubic-character model. A field test uses a polylogarithmic number of modular powers. The algorithm works simultaneously for arbitrary pair-dependent multipliers outside a fraction $\exp(-\Omega(\log X/\log\log X))$ through its growing cutoff. The proof shows that small elements generate full residue unit groups at almost all rational primes, including split primes with noncyclic unit groups. Across 10,768 field/prime combinations the measured radius-two scalar rule always held. A separate replay checked 5,292 literal batch gcds over 819 character-state representatives. For the original coefficient scan, at height five the exact single-order miss densities on the two residue branches are approximately $2.39\times10^{-40}$ and $2.40\times10^{-37}$. This fixed-box law holds at pair-dependent multipliers $a\le X^{1/2-\delta}$, or $X^{2/3-\delta}$ on rationally irreducible probes. ## Remaining gap

The divisor-sum algorithm requires $\sigma(N)$. The cubic search requires a useful order multiple. The Salié reductions require a magnitude approximation or comparison. Producing any of these efficiently from unrestricted $N$ remains open. The reductions and finite certificates do not establish an improvement over the all-input deterministic benchmark.
