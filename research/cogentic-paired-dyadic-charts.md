# A productive pair of dyadic charts with growing degrees

Two distinct fields of growing degree can both have trivial dyadic class-group quotients and give a productive match from one fixed signed linear mark per field. The construction below uses different evaluation bases and gives an explicit composite family. Its factorization is already determined by the same parameters. For completely iterated quadratic compositions of one-third height, equal exact values at large arguments force the longer composition to have the shorter as its outer polynomial, up to the change of argument. ## Two charts from a pure tower

Take a power of two \(d\ge4\), put \(e=d/2\), and let \(\theta^d=2\). In the fields \(K_1=\mathbb Q(\theta)\) and \(K_2=\mathbb Q(\theta^2)\), write
\[
 \beta_1=1+\theta,\qquad \beta_2=1+\theta^2,\qquad
 \alpha_i=2\beta_i^2.
\]
For \(k\in\{d,e\}\), put \(P_k(T)=(T-1)^k-2=A_k(T^2)+T B_k(T^2)\) and define the monic integral polynomial \(F_k\) by
\[
 F_k(2T^2)=2^k P_k(T)P_k(-T).
\]
Indeed the product is even and has integer coefficients, so substituting \(T^2=X/2\) and multiplying by \(2^k\) gives the required coefficients. The minpoly of \(\beta_i\) is \(P_k\) by Eisenstein after translating. The trace of \(\beta_i\) is \(k\), so \(-\beta_i\) cannot be its conjugate. If \(\mathbb Q(\beta_i)/\mathbb Q(\beta_i^2)\) were quadratic, its nonidentity automorphism would send \(\beta_i\) to \(-\beta_i\). Thus \(\alpha_i\) generates \(K_i\), and \(F_k\) is its minimal polynomial. Both fields are unramified outside 2, their normal closures have 2-power degree, and they each have a unique prime over 2. The dyadic bound gives \(h_{\{2\}}=0\) in both fields.

Choose a large even integer \(t\) and put
\[
 N=P_d(t)P_d(-t),\qquad m_1=2t^2.
\]
Require \(N\) to be coprime to the discriminants of \(F_d,F_e\) and to \(\operatorname{Res}(P_k(T),P_k(-T))\) for \(k=d,e\). These integers are nonzero by separability and the trace argument. A sufficient condition is that \(t\) is divisible by their product, since \(P_d(0)=-1\). The two factors of \(N\) exceed one and are coprime, by the resultant condition. Also \(t\) is a unit modulo \(N\). The denominator \(B_d(t^2)\) is a unit since a prime dividing it and \(N\) would force both \(P_d(t)\) and \(P_d(-t)\) to vanish. Define
\[
 \tau=-A_d(t^2)/B_d(t^2)\pmod N,
 \qquad n\equiv(\tau-1)^2+1\pmod N,
 \qquad m_2=2n^2,
\]
using the least nonnegative representative \(n\). The formula for \(\tau\) selects \(t\) modulo \(P_d(t)\) and \(-t\) modulo \(P_d(-t)\). Consequently \((\tau-1)^d=2\pmod N\) and \(P_e(n)=0\pmod N\). We have \(F_d(m_1)=2^dN\) and \(F_e(m_2)=0\pmod N\). The discriminant conditions make both evaluation roots simple. If a prime divisor of \(N\) divided \(n\), the identity \((\tau-1)^2\equiv-1\) there would imply \((\tau-1)^d\equiv1\), contradicting \((\tau-1)^d\equiv2\). Thus both bases are units.

In each field take the signed linear mark \(\alpha_i\), a square of \(\sqrt2\,\beta_i\) with \(\sqrt2=\theta^{d/2}\) in both fields. Its integer labels in the two charts are \(2t^2\) and \(2n^2\), both in the nontrivial rational square class of 2. The compatible modular root of 2 in both charts is \(c=(\tau-1)^{d/2}\). In the second chart, \(\beta_2\) reduces to \(n\) since \(P_e(n)=0\) and the rational formula \(-A_e(n^2)/B_e(n^2)\) is defined by the resultant exclusion. The two mark roots therefore reduce to \(c\tau\) and \(cn\), respectively. Their product normalized by the rational square root \(2tn\) is \(\tau/t\) modulo \(N\), giving the two proper gcds. The pair is necessary for this match on the two supplied marks since neither label is a rational square by itself.

For example, \(d=4,t=4\) gives \(N=49217=79\cdot623\), with \(F_d=X^4-8X^3+8X^2-224X+16\) and \(F_e=X^2-12X+4\). Here \(n=44882\), both derivative gcds and the denominator gcds are one, and the reduced roots are \(12360\) and \(44883\). Normalizing gives the difference gcd 79. The [chart adapter](../experiments/cogentic-rational-root-matching.py) reproduces the split with residual square-class rank zero in both fields after testing 2 and the real places.

All conjugates of \(\alpha_i\) have bounded absolute value. Thus \(\log\|F_k\|_\infty=O(k)\), the logarithms of the absolute discriminants and resultants above are \(O(d^2)\), and one can take \(\log t\asymp d^2\log d\) while imposing the divisibilities. Then \(L=\log N\asymp d^3\log d\), so both degrees have the one-third order \((L/\log L)^{1/3}\). Their homogeneous norms on \(|a|,|b|\le\exp(O(W))\) are bounded by \(\exp(O(dW))=\exp(O(L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}))\). The second base can be chosen at most \(2N^2\) and the construction uses rational matching across bases. The factors \(P_d(t)\) and \(P_d(-t)\) are explicit. On this family one can recover the parameters from \(N\) in polynomial bit time by enumerating powers of two \(d\) through the input bit length and binary searching the strictly increasing product \(P_d(t)P_d(-t)\) for \(t\ge4\).

### Inputs above any fixed one-third trial threshold

Fix an integer \(C\ge1\), take \(T=d^{C d^2}\) and \(Z=d^{C d/32768}\), and restrict \(t\in[T,2T]\) to multiples of the required discriminants and resultants and of the primes through \(d^2\). This progression has step with logarithm \(O(d^2)\) by Chebyshev's bound. The candidate input polynomial \(P_d(t)P_d(-t)\) has degree \(2d\) and value one at \(0\), so it avoids every prime dividing the step. Each remaining prime \(p\in(d^2,Z]\) excludes at most \(2d\) classes of progression indices, with density at most \(2d/p\).

Apply the [dyadic-band Bonferroni count for the Pell family](cogentic-growing-pell-pair.md#surviving-the-one-third-trial-division-bound) with its parameter replaced by \(d\). Its conditions hold with at most \(4d\) forbidden residues per prime and a progression of \(\exp((C-o(1))d^2\log d)\) indices. In each band the sum of forbidden densities is at most \(44d\) and the truncation depth is \(1024d\). The product-law lower bound is \(\exp(-O(d\log d))\), with logarithmic rounding error at most \(2048d\log Z+O(d\log^2 d)=(C/16+o(1))d^2\log d\). Hence some \(t\) avoids all primes through \(Z\). Here \(W=((18C)^{1/3}+o(1))d\log d\), so for any fixed threshold \(\exp(cW)\) one can choose a fixed \(C\) with \(Z>\exp(cW)\) for large \(d\).

## Rigidity of complete quadratic compositions

Suppose \(f,g\in\mathbb Z[X]\) are monic of respective degrees \(d=2^r\le D=2^s\), with \(d\to\infty\), and each is a composition of rational quadratics. Let \(m,n\) be positive integer arguments, \(B=\min(m,n)\to\infty\), and assume their coefficient heights have logarithms \(O(\log B)\) and \(\log D=o(\log B)\). If \(f(m)=g(n)\), then \(g=f\circ h\) for a monic integral \(h\) of degree \(D/d\) with \(h(n)=m\). In equal degrees this is \(g(X)=f(X+m-n)\). The assumptions include coefficients and arguments on the same one-third scale. In equal degrees at a shared argument \(m\), both values can instead be multiples of a modulus \(N\) with \(m^d=O(N)\).

Normalize the right-hand partial compositions of \(f\) to be monic with zero constant. Then \(f=h_r+b\), where \(h_0=X\) and \(h_i=h_{i-1}^2+a_i h_{i-1}\). Every normalized \(h_i\) is integral since \(h_i-\eta\) is a monic factor of \(f\) over the algebraic numbers for an outer root \(\eta\). Its nonconstant coefficients are rational algebraic integers. The coefficient of \(X^{2^{i-1}}\) in \(h_i-h_{i-1}^2\) gives \(a_i\in\mathbb Z\). For \(s=2^i\), multiplicativity of Mahler measure gives a factor \(h_i-\eta\) of measure at most \(M(f)^{s/d}\). Bounding each coefficient by \(2^s\) times the measure shows that the coefficients of positive degree of \(h_i\) are at most \(2^s M(f)^{s/d}\) in absolute value. At the least positive degree of \(h_{i-1}\), its square contributes zero. This bounds \(|a_i|\) by \(2^s M(f)^{s/d}\) as well. The argument applies to \(h_r=f-b\) using \(f\) itself as the factor.

Complete the squares by putting \(s_0=X+a_1/2\), \(s_i=h_i+a_{i+1}/2\) for \(1\le i<r\), and \(s_r=f\). Then \(s_i=s_{i-1}^2+c_i\) with \(c_i\in\tfrac14\mathbb Z\). For \(i<r\) one has \(c_i=a_{i+1}/2-a_i^2/4\), and \(c_r=b-a_r^2/4\). The coefficient bounds and \(\log M(f)=O(\log B)\) give \(|c_i|\le B^{\epsilon 2^i}\) for a common \(\epsilon=o(1)\), and likewise for \(g\). At the chosen argument each \(s_i(m)/m^{2^i}=1+o(1)\) uniformly. In fact the initial logarithmic error is \(O(B^{-3/4})\) for large \(d\). If the earlier ratios stay between \(1/2\) and 2, a squaring doubles the log error and the added constant changes it by \(O(B^{-3/4})\), so the errors are \(O(D B^{-3/4})=o(1)\). The same bounds hold at \(n\) for \(g\).

Put \(q=s-r\). Equality of the final values and the uniform ratio estimates give \(m/n^{2^q}=1+o(1)\). Compare \(f\) starting at its initial centered argument with \(g\) starting at its centered level \(q\), so both chains have \(r\) remaining steps. Put \(U=\min(m,n^{2^q})\). At aligned step \(i\) the preceding values are each at least \(U^{2^{i-1}}/2\) and the two added constants are \(o(U^{2^{i-1}})\), since their bounds are \(B^{\epsilon 2^i}\) and \(B^{\epsilon 2^{q+i}}\). The aligned starting values are half-integers. If they differ their gap is at least \(1/2\); otherwise the first unequal constant introduces a gap of at least \(1/4\). At any subsequent step a gap of \(1/4\) or more is multiplied by the sum of two positive preceding values, and cannot be canceled by the difference of the added constants. The gap persists to the final value. Thus the aligned starting values and all remaining constants agree. Writing the centered level \(q\) of \(g\) as its monic integral partial composition plus a half-integer, this gives \(g=f\circ h\) with \(h(n)=m\). The constant shift in \(h\) is integral because the partial composition, \(m\), and \(n\) are integral.

In equal degrees at a shared argument, both values are \((1+o(1))m^d\) and their difference is \(o(N)\). If it is divisible by \(N\), the values are equal. For irreducible \(f,g\) the exact-value identity embeds the smaller field in the larger and makes the evaluation maps agree on the transported power order. The argument applies to complete polynomial compositions. A primitive polynomial for a quadratic tower need not have that shape.
