# One reusable advice string for the finite-coin factorer

For integers \(N<2^b\), let \(k=k_b=O(b^{2/3}(\log b)^{1/3})\) be a computable common upper bound for the number of random bits in one [complete factoring trial](cogentic-one-intercept.md). Use a monotone envelope of the explicit integer schedule. **One advice string of \(b+k+O(\log b)\) bits** generates polynomially many trials that completely factor every such \(N\) in \(L_{2^b}[1/3,O(1)]\) bit operations per input. With a larger list of up to \(2^s\) trials the exact advice tradeoff, up to an absolute additive constant, is
\[
                       b+k-s+O(1)
\]
whenever \(C(\log(b+2)+\log^2(k+2)(\log\log(k+4))^2)\le s\le k-O(1)\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(C\). Optimality here is for generic tests of density at least one half. If \(s=O(b^{1/3}(\log b)^{2/3})\) the upper bound preserves the preceding factoring time. The decoder is uniform. The advice choice is nonuniform. The earlier [finite-coin argument](cogentic-finite-coins.md) uses a list of independent seeds.

The uniform statement behind the advice concerns a fixed input \(N\). For any auxiliary length \(n\le b^{O(1)}\) with \(n\ge K=\lceil(1+\eta)k_b\rceil\), a deterministic decoder succeeds for **all but fewer than \(2^K\)** among the \(2^n\) possible auxiliary strings. Consequently, if an auxiliary string conditioned on this input has point masses at most \(2^{-h}\), its failure probability is less than \(2^{K-h}\). No random bits are needed by the decoder. In any set of \(J\) distinct auxiliary strings, at most \(\min(J,2^K-1)\) fail. This includes shifted integer intervals whose entries all have an injective \(n\)-bit encoding. The auxiliary strings are additional inputs in this statement.

## Expanding the advice

For a target \(2\le N<2^b\) undergoing the extractor, let \(G_N\subseteq\{0,1\}^{k_b}\) be the intercept strings at which the complete, verified algorithm succeeds within its single-trial clock. Ignore surplus bits on shorter inputs. Its proved uniform success probability is \(1-o(1)\) as the input length grows. To make the statement uniform even for small targets, use trial division for inputs of at most \(\lceil\log_2(b+2)\rceil\) bits at a cost polynomial in \(b\). For the remaining targets \(|G_N|/2^{k_b}\ge 1/2\) for all sufficiently large \(b\). For finitely many \(b\) outside this asymptotic range a verified trial-division fallback has bounded cost. The complete-trial property ensures a single string suffices for each target, including its prime powers.

For the weak-source assertion with \(K=\lceil(1+\eta)k_b\rceil\), use Guruswami, Umans and Vadhan's explicit \((K,1/4)\) extractor
\[
       E:\{0,1\}^n\times\{0,1\}^{d_0}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}^{m_0}.
\]
Choose their loss constant \(\alpha=\eta/(1+\eta)\). Theorem 1.5 gives \(m_0\ge(1-\alpha)K\ge k_b\) and \(d_0\le\log_2 n+O_\eta(\log(K+2))\). Extractor evaluation takes polynomial time. Truncate its output to \(k_b\) bits to obtain \(E'\), and enumerate every \(z\in\{0,1\}^{d_0}\). For each \(z\) run the complete verified trial on \(E'(a,z)\). The total number of trials is polynomial in \(b\). The ordinary seeded-extractor guarantee suffices because we average over the seed in the proof and enumerate it in execution. [Guruswami–Umans–Vadhan, Theorem 1.5](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/salil/files/acm2009.pdf#page=4)

If at least \(2^K\) strings \(a\) fail this list for the same \(N\), take \(X\) uniform on any \(2^K\) of them. This source has min-entropy \(K\), yet \(\Pr(E'(X,U_{d_0})\in G_N)=0\). Uniform \(k_b\)-bit strings land there with probability at least \(1/2\). This contradicts statistical distance at most \(1/4\), also valid after truncation. This proves the exceptional count and its assertion for arbitrary conditional point masses.

For any threshold \(K\) whose decoder misses fewer than \(2^K\) masters per target, take \(n=b+K\). The fewer than \(2^b\) targets exclude fewer than \(2^{b+K}\) master strings in total. Fix one of the survivors. With \(n=b+K+t\) for an integer \(t\ge1\), choosing one uniformly random master string instead succeeds **simultaneously** on every target with probability exceeding \(1-2^{-t}\). Either length includes all smaller input lengths in the same choice.

## Near-optimal advice widths

For a polynomial number of trials use the constant-error disperser of Ta-Shma, Umans and Zuckerman, Theorem 1.4. For any \(n,K\) its left side consists of \(n\)-bit strings, its degree is polynomial in \(n\), and every \(2^K\) left strings collectively reach at least three quarters of its right side. Its right side has at least \(c2^K/n^3\) vertices, with a fixed \(c>0\). Take \(K=k+\lceil 4\log_2(b+2)\rceil+O(1)\), \(n=b+K\). For large \(b\) the right side has at least \(2^k\) elements. Map its consecutive integer labels cyclically to the \(2^k\) trial points. The preimage of any set of density \(\ge1/2\) occupies more than a quarter of the right side, since \(\lfloor M/2^k\rfloor 2^k/M>1/2\) for every \(M\ge2^k\). Thus fewer than \(2^K\) masters can miss any \(G_N\), proving the first headline bound with polynomially many decoded points. [Ta-Shma–Umans–Zuckerman, Theorem 1.4](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~umans/papers/TUZ01-final.pdf#page=5)

For a budget of \(2^s\) trials use Corollary 5.8(2) of the same paper at error \(1/4\). For threshold \(K\) its seed length \(d\) is \(O(\log(n+2)+\log^2(K+2)(\log\log(K+4))^2)\) and its output length \(m\) is at least \(K+d-c_0\) for an absolute constant \(c_0\). Set \(K=k-s+c_0\), \(n=b+K\). The lower bound on \(s\) ensures \(d\le s\). When \(m\ge k\), retain the first \(k\) output bits. When \(m<k\), append all \(2^{k-m}\) suffixes to every output. The total number of trials is at most
\[
              2^{d+\max(0,k-m)}\le2^s.
\]
In the second case the relevant target is the projection of \(G_N\) onto its \(m\)-bit prefixes, of density at least \(1/2\). The same flat-source argument proves the exceptional bound \(<2^K\) and therefore the stated advice width. The new factoring time follows by multiplying the one-trial bit bound by \(2^s\), including the polynomial cost to compute each extractor output. [Ta-Shma–Umans–Zuckerman, Corollary 5.8(2)](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~umans/papers/TUZ01-final.pdf#page=17)

## What density alone implies

Consider arbitrary dense tests indexed by \(b\) bits, on a trial space of \(2^k\) points. Fix any proposed advice decoder that tests at most \(2^s\) points on each pair of a label and an advice string, with \(s\le k-1\). If its advice width is at most \(b+k-s-1\), partition all advice strings among the \(2^b\) labels, at most \(2^{k-s-1}\) per label. For a label, take all the points the decoder tests for its assigned strings, following each all-failure transcript if its choices are adaptive. Their union contains at most \(2^{k-1}\) points. Make the complement that label's success set. Each label is at least half successful, yet every advice is defeated by its assigned label. This gives the matching \(b+k-s\) lower bound for generic tests of density at least one half. For stipulated density \(1-\delta\), the construction allows \(\lfloor\delta\,2^{k-s}\rfloor\) advice strings per label. The arithmetic structure of the factoring tests could admit shorter universal advice or uniform generation.

The [integer replay](../experiments/cogentic-extractor-advice.py) computes the exact largest statistical-distance error over all flat sources of 32 strings in a toy 256-point multiplier family, by sorting the hit counts for all 256 output events. The largest error is \(173/512\). All 163 events of uniform mass at least one half have fewer than 32 exceptional masters, all 163 tested unions of eight such exceptional sets have a common survivor, and an eight-label example verifies the list obstruction.
