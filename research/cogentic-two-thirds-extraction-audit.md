# Audit of the conservative four-coordinate extraction and worst-case bit bound

**Verdict.** Given the retuned statement that a strictly positive surviving true Gram matrix exists for each generator, the four-coordinate deterministic search in the [fixed-schedule argument](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) yields complete factorization in $\exp((400000+o(1))V)$ bit operations, where $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$, and $V=DH$. This audit checks the parameter and algorithm interfaces of Lemmas 7.3–7.5, Lemma 8.1, Lemma 11.1, and Theorem 1.2(a) in the pinned paper. The separate analytic review covers the smooth-window and centered moment arguments which establish that Gram input. Google's stated worst-case bound for its original algorithm is $O(\exp(3L^{3/4}))$ for a verified proper divisor. ## Saturation, a fully integer guard, and repeated primes

Write $b=\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor+1$, $\beta=\log_2B\in\mathbb Z$, and $J=\lceil\log_2(6b)\rceil$. A direct implementation of upward rounding is
$$
 K=B2^{\lceil bJ/\beta\rceil}.
$$
It satisfies $K\ge B\exp((L/\log B)\log(6L))$ since $b\ge L/\log2$ and $J\ge\log(6L)/\log2$. Its natural logarithm has leading term $V/1000$, by $b=L/\log2+O(1)$ and $J=z/\log2+O(1)$. Check divisibility by every $2\le t\le\min(K,N-1)$ before the polynomial construction. All surviving primes dividing $N$ then exceed $K$.

Let $M_*=\operatorname{lcm}(2,(E_\ell)_{\ell\in P_{\rm ok}})$ using verified exponents only. A simpler integer acceptance guard is $3\beta |P_{\rm ok}|\ge B$. It implies the required $6(\log B)|P_{\rm ok}|\ge B$ because $1/2<\log2$. It eventually accepts all successful-prime lists of the fixed construction, since $|P_{\rm ok}|\sim B/(2\log B)$ and $3/(2\log2)>1$. For any prime $p\mid N$, the subgroup generated by the verified primes modulo $p$ has order dividing $M_*$. For $k=\lfloor\log(p-1)/\log B\rfloor$, products of $k$ of those primes, with repetition, are distinct as multisets modulo $p$ because they are positive integers less than $p$. The subgroup therefore has at least $(B/(6L))^k$ elements. Its index is less than $B(6L)^k\le K$. Thus $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)$ is a multiple of the index and
$$
 M=M_*\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)
$$
is an even multiple of every $p-1$. Multiplication here is essential: subgroup order and subgroup index can share prime factors. Theorem 1.2(a) accepts precisely this common divisibility condition for a composite non-perfect-power input. It also applies to numbers divisible by two distinct primes when one or both multiplicities exceed one. [Cogentic, Theorem 1.2(a) and §11.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=55)

## Bit cost and finite failures

The number of calls is at most $16BL_0^8U^4A^2$. Each has $D_{\rm row}\le2dB+1$ and precision parameter $P_{\rm bit}=64d^3D_{\rm row}L_0$. Lemma 7.5 gives $\mathrm{poly}(A,B,d,D_{\rm row},P_{\rm bit})=\exp(O(H))$ bit operations, including trial division of the marked values, exact pivoting, certified real approximations and the final modular exponentiation. The integers $U$ and $c_d$ each have $O(V)$ bits. Generating a tuple and evaluating its polynomial coefficients costs $\mathrm{poly}(L)$ extra per trial. None of these quantities is an array of $U$ bits inside a single call. Preliminary grid gcds, generator sieving and field constructions take $\exp(O(H))$ besides trial division through $K$.

The running $M_*$ has $O(BP_{\rm bit})$ bits. The final product has $O(BP_{\rm bit}+K)$ bits by Lemma 11.1, so the printed bound $\log M\le\exp(L^{0.65})$ for the *original* schedule must be replaced when using this saturation step. Omitting its output cap entirely is valid with the verified count guard. Even the coarse bound $\log_2\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K)\le K\log_2K$ is sufficient for the new exponent. Serial trial divisions, prime powers and schoolbook arithmetic in this final step cost at most $O(K^4\mathrm{poly}(L))$ for large $N$. Theorem 1.2(a) then costs $\exp(O(\sqrt L\log L))(\log M)L^2=\exp((1/1000+o(1))V)$. The search cost $\exp((400000+o(1))V)$ dominates.

For positive integers, take 1 as the empty product and run deterministic polynomial-time primality recognition at each other node. For a composite, handle an even input or an exact perfect power before the analytic search. A failed integer precondition, exhausted search with too few accepted primes, or full-input preliminary gcd can trigger direct trial division through the first proper divisor. The validated asymptotic interfaces exclude these branches at all sufficiently large *surviving* moduli, uniformly in $\ell$. The threshold need not be an input to the algorithm. Trial division on bounded moduli contributes a fixed constant to the global worst-case bound. Recurse only on verified divisors and complementary cofactors. Counting repeated prime leaves gives at most $2\lfloor\log_2 N\rfloor-1$ nodes. This contributes $O(\log L)$ to the logarithm of the running time.
