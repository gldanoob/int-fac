# Sealed square roots in the Cogentic randomized factoring theorem

Theorem 1.1 in the November 25, 2026 Cogentic paper states that a uniform Las Vegas algorithm completely factors each $N\ge2$ in expected time $L_N[1/3,105]$ over its internal fair coins, with deterministic workspace $L_N[1/3,40]$. This is a per-input time expectation. Definition 6.1 interleaves deterministic trial division with a randomized phase. The latter samples the base pair, two discrete-Laplace polynomial forms on a lattice, descent choices, auxiliary quadratic characters, and sealed random units. Proposition 2.6 lists classical effective prime number, character-sum, large-sieve and Stark–Chebotarev inputs. The pinned README reports a completed Mathlib-only Lean 4.35.0 formalization to be released separately; the pinned directory contains the paper and README. [Primary theorem and Algorithm 6.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf) · [Pinned README and release statement](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization)

Lemma 5.3 keeps independent uniform units $z_i\in(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times$ sealed, exposing only their squares $w_i$ during the construction of a tested dependency. For odd $N$ with at least two different prime divisors, a given unit square has $2^{\omega(N)}$ roots, each equiprobable after revealing the square. Conditional on the accepted transcript and the nonempty chosen index set, the product of the sealed roots is therefore uniform on the full fiber over its square. Exactly two roots equal the constructed comparison root up to global sign, giving successful probability $1-2^{1-\omega(N)}$. The deterministic square-root computation in the companion number fields supplies the comparison root. [Cogentic, Lemmas 5.2–5.3, printed pp. 42–44](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf)

Here is the exact cost of retaining this form of hidden choice in a publicly enumerable finite support. Let $A$ be any list of $K$ residues modulo $N$. Test $\gcd(z,N)$ for every $z$, returning a proper divisor on any nontrivial result and discarding a zero residue. Sort the remaining pairs $(z^2\bmod N,z)$ by their first component. A scan of each equal-square group finds two roots $z,z'$ with $z'\ne\pm z\bmod N$, or certifies that the whole fiber in $A$ has only one global-sign class. In the former case
$$
                 1<\gcd(z-z',N)<N.
$$
Indeed $(z-z')(z+z')=0\bmod N$. If $z-z'$ were a unit then $z'=-z$, and if its gcd were $N$ then $z'=z$. Sorting, unit checks and scanning take $K\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N,\log(2K))$ bit operations in addition to generating the list. Checking each member against the first root of its group is sufficient, even when a fiber has many duplicates.

In the latter case, revealing $w=z^2$ specifies a single global-sign class of the sealed $z$ in that support, whatever the draw probabilities. If this is true of every individual support, the complete square transcript specifies the product of any chosen subcollection up to global sign. This removes the local CRT sign randomness used in Lemma 5.3. For the specific support $1\le z<\sqrt N$, all integer squares are smaller than $N$ and distinct even before quotienting by sign. A deterministic choice could still split on a particular transcript. Proving that it does requires further information about the comparison root. Enumerating a small support that demonstrably retains a nontrivial hidden CRT choice gives a deterministic splitting algorithm at the enumeration cost through the sorting argument itself.

Fixing long random tapes can give a nonuniform consequence by Markov's inequality and amplification over all inputs of a bit length. The resulting choice of tapes is advice. Lemma 5.3 supplies no uniform method for generating them. The [deterministic elliptic mean bound](sharp-elliptic-mean.md) uses the distribution of the integer cofactor and explicit local parity instead.

The [finite replay](../experiments/cogentic-sealed-roots.py) checks all 86 unit-square fibers across seven odd composite moduli, including repeated prime factors and up to four distinct primes. Sorting each two-root support produced a proper divisor in all 4,064 cases where the two roots belonged to different global-sign classes. Each initial support $1\le z<\sqrt N$ passed the injectivity check.
