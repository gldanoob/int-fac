# Exact finite selection of four coefficient words

The [runnable selector](../experiments/cogentic-coefficient-selection.py) accepts two rectangular integer boxes with power-of-two side lengths and a signed sum of products of field indicators. Each field indicator can include up to two affine smooth-mark tests and any conjunction of affine congruence hits or misses. Weights are exact integers or rationals. A smooth test can omit band primes, and the translated affine value can change sign or be constant.

For each nonconstant smooth mark of slope $a$, set $u=x+ay$ and substitute $x=u-ay$ into the modular conditions. For each inclusion–exclusion subset of hits, choose the mark whose projected coset has the fewest admissible $u$ in the box's projected interval. A constant smooth test takes one divisibility check for the whole box. For each admissible $u=u_0+Pi$, apply the chosen smooth test once. Clip the $y$ interval by the original bounds on $x=u-ay$, then intersect it with $y\equiv y_0+Ri\pmod Q$. With one mark, an accepted section contributes this progression's length. A second mark of slope $b$ has ordinate $u+(b-a)y$, so a single signed progression suffices to count its accepted values. Omitted primes are removed from each mark's band product. [Progression-index sieve](cogentic-scalar-progression-sieve.md)

The two fields are independent in every prefix box, so a term's conditional mean is the product of its field fractions times its signed weight. The selector completes the first field and then the second, always bisecting the longer side and keeping the child with the smaller exact rational mean. Since the children have equal volume, their mean averages to the parent mean, even when terms have negative weights. There are exactly as many decisions as the sum of the binary logarithms of the four side lengths. The returned leaf score is evaluated directly and checked against the projected score.

Run `python3 experiments/cogentic-coefficient-selection.py` to verify both child means at every visited split against exhaustive four-tuple evaluation. The four finite trials have 13, 12, 10 and 0 decisions. The three nontrivial trials reduce mean scores of $443/512$, $221/256$ and $27/32$ to a leaf score of $5/8$. Selection itself counts scalar progressions and never enumerates the four-tuple box.

A factor with only modular conditions uses a separate exact lattice count. The hit constraints intersect a coset of points $(x_0+Pi,y_0+Ri+Qj)$. A new hit first restricts $i$ by a congruence obtained from the gcd of the $j$ coefficient and the modulus. Substituting that progression for $i$ then determines $j$ by a modular inverse in the reduced modulus. Clipping the resulting coset to the box gives two signed floor sums over the admissible $i$. Euclidean division evaluates both without iterating over the box or residue period. Each miss adds an inclusion–exclusion choice. The bit cost is polynomial in the binary input length per subset of misses, since the lattice index $PQ$ grows by at most a factor of the modulus at each hit. A wholly modular score also skips constructing the prime band. The replay checks 304 small modular factors by brute force and hit and miss counts of $x≡y$ on a box with 2049-bit side lengths against the closed form from the sizes of the residue classes.

For each inclusion–exclusion subset with a nonempty coset, the mixed counter inspects at most $\min_a\{1+\lfloor (p-1+a(q-1))/P_a\rfloor\}$ first-mark positions on a box of side lengths $p,q$. Here $P_a$ is the spacing of the projection for mark $a$. Each position costs binary arithmetic and one divisibility test against the eligible band product. With two marks, each surviving nonempty section supplies one scalar sieve of length $T\le 1+\lfloor(q-1)/Q\rfloor$. The sieve makes linear passes in $T$, tests each eligible prime's congruence, and visits the indices in its progression. The work has no direct dependence on the value of the modular period or mark determinant. It may still be large when both projections are dense or second-mark sections are long. The replay compares 300 small smooth-and-modular counts against direct evaluation, and checks six closed forms on boxes of 1025-bit side lengths. In one case the larger mark satisfies $x+y\equiv5$ modulo $2^{2048}-1$. Its projection meets the box only at $5$, leaving six candidates and two accepted points. Starting with the smaller mark $x$ would admit all $2^{1024}$ projected positions. The fast scalar counting question for the one-third schedule remains open.

## From selected polynomials to a factor

The finite replay takes $m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and two monic imaginary quadratics with common root $m$ modulo $N$. It selects $u_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $v_i\in\{0,1\}$ in the perturbation $f_i(X)+N(u_i+v_iX)$. The score counts marks $1\le a\le100$ with $\gcd(a-m,N)=1$ for which both evaluations are squarefree products of primes between $\sqrt{300}$ and $300$. The selector minimizes the negative of that count. The extractor uses the selected polynomial pair with homogeneous relation height 250 and smoothness bound 300. It can also use relations outside the selector's mark set. Python independently checks each exponent, the modular squaring chain and the returned divisor. The one-third proof still needs control of the full rank score and a uniform fast smoothness count.
