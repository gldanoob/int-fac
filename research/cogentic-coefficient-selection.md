# Exact finite selection of four coefficient words

The [runnable selector](../experiments/cogentic-coefficient-selection.py) accepts two rectangular integer boxes with power-of-two side lengths and a signed sum of products of field indicators. Each field indicator can include up to two affine smooth-mark tests and any conjunction of affine congruence hits or misses. Weights are exact integers or rationals. A smooth test can omit band primes, and the translated affine value can change sign or be constant.

For each nonconstant smooth mark of slope $a$, set $u=x+ay$ and substitute $x=u-ay$ into the modular conditions. For each inclusion–exclusion subset of hits, estimate the candidate scan for each projected coset by the divisor windows below and choose the least expensive mark. A constant smooth test takes one divisibility check for the whole box. Recover each admissible smooth $u=u_0+Pi$ from the chosen scan. Clip the $y$ interval by the original bounds on $x=u-ay$, then intersect it with $y\equiv y_0+Ri\pmod Q$. With one mark, an accepted section contributes this progression's length. A second mark of slope $b$ has ordinate $u+(b-a)y$, so a single signed progression suffices to count its accepted values. Omitted primes are removed from each mark's band product. [Progression-index sieve](cogentic-scalar-progression-sieve.md)

The two fields are independent in every prefix box, so a term's conditional mean is the product of its field fractions times its signed weight. The selector completes the first field and then the second, always bisecting the longer side and keeping the child with the smaller exact rational mean. Since the children have equal volume, their mean averages to the parent mean, even when terms have negative weights. There are exactly as many decisions as the sum of the binary logarithms of the four side lengths. The returned leaf score is evaluated directly and checked against the projected score.

Run `python3 experiments/cogentic-coefficient-selection.py` to verify both child means at every visited split against exhaustive four-tuple evaluation. The four finite trials have 13, 12, 10 and 0 decisions. The three nontrivial trials reduce mean scores of $443/512$, $221/256$ and $27/32$ to a leaf score of $5/8$. Selection itself counts scalar progressions and never enumerates the four-tuple box.

A factor with only modular conditions uses a separate exact lattice count. The hit constraints intersect a coset of points $(x_0+Pi,y_0+Ri+Qj)$. A new hit first restricts $i$ by a congruence obtained from the gcd of the $j$ coefficient and the modulus. Substituting that progression for $i$ then determines $j$ by a modular inverse in the reduced modulus. Clipping the resulting coset to the box gives two signed floor sums over the admissible $i$. Euclidean division evaluates both without iterating over the box or residue period. Each miss adds an inclusion–exclusion choice. The bit cost is polynomial in the binary input length per subset of misses, since the lattice index $PQ$ grows by at most a factor of the modulus at each hit. A wholly modular score also skips constructing the prime band. The replay checks 304 small modular factors by brute force and hit and miss counts of $x≡y$ on a box with 2049-bit side lengths against the closed form from the sizes of the residue classes.

For each inclusion–exclusion subset with a nonempty coset, the mixed counter inspects at most $\min_a\{1+\lfloor (p-1+a(q-1))/P_a\rfloor\}$ first-mark positions on a box of side lengths $p,q$. Here $P_a$ is the spacing of the projection for mark $a$. Each position costs binary arithmetic and one divisibility test against the eligible band product. With two marks, each surviving nonempty section supplies one scalar count on a progression of length $T\le 1+\lfloor(q-1)/Q\rfloor$. When the complementary window bound below gives no improvement, the fallback sieve makes linear passes in $T$, tests each eligible prime's congruence, and visits the indices in its progression. The work has no direct dependence on the value of the modular period or mark determinant. It may still be large when both projections are dense or second-mark sections are long. The replay compares 300 small smooth-and-modular counts against direct evaluation, and checks six closed forms on boxes of 1025-bit side lengths. In one case the larger mark satisfies $x+y\equiv5$ modulo $2^{2048}-1$. Its projection meets the box only at $5$, leaving six candidates and two accepted points. Starting with the smaller mark $x$ would admit all $2^{1024}$ projected positions. The fast scalar counting question for the one-third schedule remains open.

## Exact counts by complementary divisor windows

For a second-mark section $c+dj$ of length $T$ with $d\ne0$, put $g=\gcd(c,d)$. If $g$ does not divide the eligible band product, the section contributes zero. Otherwise divide the section by $g$, remove its prime factors from the band, and remove any eligible prime dividing the new step. The latter primes cannot divide any reduced member because the reduced progression has coprime start and step. These are the same gcd and unit-class reductions used in the scalar approximation. [Reduction to unit classes](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md#a-sharp-count-for-long-progressions-at-arbitrary-moduli)

Let $P$ be the reduced band product. Clip the reduced progression to $[-P,P]$ by integer division, leaving $T'$ members. Reverse a negative step and write $D>0$ for the resulting step and $c'$ for the clipped start. For each sign $s\in\{-1,1\}$, use integer division to find the least and greatest magnitudes $l_s,h_s$ in that sign, skipping an empty sign. Every accepted magnitude $v\mid P$ corresponds bijectively to a complementary divisor $e=P/v$ in $[\lceil P/h_s\rceil,\lfloor P/l_s\rfloor]$. Since $c'$ and $P$ are units modulo $D$, the original condition $v\equiv s c'\pmod D$ becomes $e\equiv sP(c')^{-1}\pmod D$. Thus for each sign we test $e\mid P$ only at the members of its complementary progression. The two signs are counted separately even when their residue classes coincide. For $D=1$ the residue condition is vacuous.

Writing $W$ for the sum of the lengths of the two complementary progressions, the exact counter chooses between $W$ division tests and a sieve on $T'$ members. Its bit cost is $\operatorname{poly}(w,\pi(B))(1+\min(W,T'))$, where $w$ includes the input word lengths and the bit length of the eligible band product. All arithmetic in the window uses integer endpoints. Each nonempty sign gives at most $1+P(h_s-l_s)/(D l_s h_s)$ complementary candidates. Thus even a wide integer window can be cheap when the reduced step is large.

The replay fixes the first mark by $x\equiv0\pmod {2^{256}}$ and leaves $2^{256}$ candidates for the second mark. With band $(\sqrt{997},997]$, step $2^{512}$, and last positive value $P/997$, the complementary window is exactly $\{997\}$. The step is coprime to the start and exceeds the range of small-modulus counting, while the section is shorter than the square root of its largest absolute value. The counter returns one for each sign of the progression. Another section with the same length and step has an unfiltered complementary window wider than $2^{400}$, but its width is less than one step and the complementary residue leaves exactly one candidate. A further construction forces $P/997$ and leaves a single candidate after clipping. The final one forces $5^2$ and certifies zero even though its section length exceeds $2^{253}$.

The same windows can produce the accepted values of the outer projection. On a projected coset $u=u_0+Pi$ take $i=i_0+j$, where $i_0$ is the first index in the projected box, and write the test value as $c+dj$ with $d=P\,\mathrm{step}$. A complementary hit of sign $s$ gives the original value $v=sgP'/e$, where $g$ is the forced gcd and $P'$ is the reduced band product. Thus $i=i_0+(v-c)/d$ even when the planner reversed or clipped the progression. If the clipped ordinary scan is cheaper, it yields the same values by testing each one against $P'$. This reconstructs the original $y$-section using the actual lattice index. The counter plans both marks separately per modular coset and chooses the one minimizing $\min(T'_a,W_a)$, rather than the shortest projected interval. If either projection has no possible accepted value, the entire coset contributes zero.

The outer scan therefore costs $\operatorname{poly}(w,\pi(B))(1+\min_a\min(T'_a,W_a))$ per modular coset before the second-mark counts. The replay places two marks on a box with one $y$ value and $2^{256}$ values of $x$. Both projected intervals have length $2^{256}$. The first test's clipped cost is still $2^{256}$, and the second uses the wide complementary window above with one admissible candidate, recovering $x=2^{256}-1$. Both signs return one, in addition to 1,800 projected value lists checked against direct enumeration.

The cost can still equal the full projected length. With a one-row box, both mark tests can have the value $1+x$. If $x$ runs from $0$ to $T-1$ and the band product $P'$ exceeds $2T$, the clipped ordinary count is $T$ and the complementary window contains $P'-\lceil P'/T\rceil+1\ge T$ unrestricted candidates for $T\ge2$. This certifies that the short-window criterion alone supplies no uniform one-third count, even when the two projections agree.

## Limits of a complete-period modular prefilter

Consider the nonproportional tests $F=1+x$ and $G=1+x+y$ on $0\le x,y<T$. Before evaluating smoothness, filter each test by any selected forbidden-prime exclusions $p\nmid F,G$ or square exclusions $p^2\nmid F,G$. Let $M$ be the common modulus of these tests, with $2M\le T$. For each mark $H\in\{F,G\}$ let $R_H$ be its number of accepted residues modulo $M$. The Chinese remainder theorem gives
\[
\frac{R_H}{M}\ \ge\ \prod_{p\mid M}(1-1/p)\ \ge\ \frac{1}{1+\log_2 T}.
\]
For the first inequality each prime removes at most a fraction $1/p$ from a mark's residues. For the second, if $p_i$ are the $k$ distinct primes in increasing order, then $p_i\ge i+1$, so the product is at least $1/(k+1)$, and $2^k\le M\le T$.

The two tests are independent on a complete residue box because the map $(x,y)\mapsto(F,G)$ is invertible modulo $M$. When $M\mid T$, the number of locally passing pairs is exactly $(T/M)^2R_FR_G$. For every admissible first value $F$, the $y$ interval spans a complete period, so some $y$ makes $G$ pass. This leaves at least $\lfloor T/M\rfloor R_F$ first-mark positions. On the $G$ projection, the diagonal $x+y=u$ has at least $M$ values of $x$ for $M-1\le u\le 2T-1-M$. For each allowed $G$ in this middle interval, some $x$ makes $F$ pass. The interval contains at least $T$ consecutive ordinates, hence at least $\lfloor T/M\rfloor R_G$ projected positions survive.

Thus on these boxes a counter that computes complete-period wheel feasibility, even exactly by floor sums, and then visits every surviving projected position must still visit at least $T/(2(1+\log_2 T))$ positions whichever mark it chooses. This applies to dyadic boxes as well. If both band products exceed $4T$, the clipped and complementary windows also choose the ordinary scans for these unit-step tests. For $T=\exp(\Theta(V))$ the wheel bound rules out a one-third running time for this visit-every-survivor strategy. Algorithms that aggregate smooth counts across surviving positions or use moduli longer than the box fall outside this bound.

## From selected polynomials to a factor

The finite replay takes $m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$ and two monic imaginary quadratics with common root $m$ modulo $N$. It selects $u_i\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $v_i\in\{0,1\}$ in the perturbation $f_i(X)+N(u_i+v_iX)$. The score counts marks $1\le a\le100$ with $\gcd(a-m,N)=1$ for which both evaluations are squarefree products of primes between $\sqrt{300}$ and $300$. The selector minimizes the negative of that count. The extractor uses the selected polynomial pair with homogeneous relation height 250 and smoothness bound 300. It can also use relations outside the selector's mark set. Python independently checks each exponent, the modular squaring chain and the returned divisor. For 77 the chosen quadratics are $X^2-39X+787$ and $X^2+41X+763$. The extractor finds $31^{267480}\equiv1\pmod{77}$ and $\gcd(31^{33435}-1,77)=11$. The unperturbed pairs split three inputs, and the selected pairs split one. Counting jointly smooth marks alone can favor a pair whose relations fail to give a split under these limits. The one-third proof still needs control of the full rank score and a uniform fast smoothness count.
