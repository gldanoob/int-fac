# Two threshold layers suffice for the elementary factoring tests

A generator that fools depth-two integer threshold circuits on the four coefficient words to error $\exp(-CH)$ gives a deterministic one-third search. Here $L=\log N$, $H=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}$, and $V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}$. The circuits have size $\exp(O(H))$ even though the coefficient words have $O(V)$ bits and the affine norms can have magnitude $\exp(\Theta(V))$. A sufficient complexity assumption is an explicit function with exponential average-case hardness for depth-three threshold circuits. The arithmetic representation below is unconditional. The factoring application uses the fixed-schedule moment and extractor claims of the branch. ## One threshold for all the smoothness and residue requirements

Write $x\in\{0,1\}^k$ for the coefficient bits in a trial. Every marked value is an integer affine form $n(x)=c+\sum_i d_ix_i$, since the marks are fixed in each summand. Choose $W\ge1$ with $|n(x)|<2^W$ for all the forms. In the application $k,W=O(V)$. A summand requires at most $h=4$ values to be nonzero squarefree products of their own allowed band primes $J\subseteq(\sqrt B,B]$, along with a bounded number of affine modular predicates. Allow the product $1$ and either sign. For any integer modulus $p\ge2$, reduce the affine coefficients and constant to least nonnegative residues, giving a new form $v_p(x)\in[0,(k+1)(p-1)]$. Then
$$
  1_{p\mid n(x)}
  =\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\big(1_{v_p(x)\ge jp}-1_{v_p(x)\ge jp+1}\big).
$$
A shift of the constant handles any prescribed residue. Each summand on the right uses two ordinary threshold gates, independently of $p$.

Put $Q=1+h(W+1)$ and $w_p=\lfloor Q\log_2 p\rfloor$. For a single smoothness test use the integer
$$
 E_J(n)=\lceil Q\log_2\max(1,|n|)\rceil-\sum_{p\in J}w_p 1_{p\mid n}
           +\left(Q+\sum_{p\in J}w_p\right)1_{n=0}.
$$
At zero it equals $Q$. For nonzero $n$, put $r=\prod_{p\in J,p\mid n}p$. The integer $r$ divides $|n|$, and $E_J(n)\ge Q\log_2(|n|/r)\ge0$. On an accepted norm $|n|=r$, and rounding costs at most $W+1$, since at most $W$ distinct primes divide $|n|$. On a rejected nonzero norm the integer quotient $|n|/r$ is at least two, giving $E_J(n)\ge Q$. This handles both a forbidden prime and a repeated prime.

The rounded logarithm is exactly a sum of $QW$ indicators $1_{|n|>2^{j/Q}}$, $0\le j<QW$. Each is a sum of two threshold gates on signed affine forms at the integer cut $1+\lfloor 2^{j/Q}\rfloor$. Compute the cut by integer root comparison against $2^j$. Compute $w_p$ by taking the binary length of $p^Q$ and subtracting one. Express $1_{n=0}$ as $1_{n\ge0}-1_{n\ge1}$. Consequently the sum of the $h$ defects and $Q$ times each modular failure indicator is an affine combination of bottom-layer threshold gates. A single top threshold accepts precisely when this sum is less than $Q$. Arbitrary Boolean combinations of a bounded number of the affine modular predicates can also be expanded into disjoint conjunctions before applying this result, at constant cost. This includes complementary residues. The output on every trial word is exact.

There are $O(h(Bk+QW)+k)$ bottom gates for a fixed bound on the number of modular requirements. The integer thresholds, coefficients, wires and the cost of producing them have total binary size $\operatorname{poly}(B,k,W)$ for the fixed schedule, by sieving to $B$ and testing the exclusion gcds. A real or signed score coefficient multiplies the result outside the circuit.

## A sufficient average-case hardness assumption

Suppose there is a fixed $\gamma>0$ and a Boolean function $f$ decidable in deterministic time $2^{O(n)}$ on $n$ input bits, such that for every sufficiently large $n$ every depth-at-most-three integer threshold circuit of binary description length at most $2^{\gamma n}$ agrees with $f$ on a uniform $n$-bit word with probability at most $1/2+2^{-\gamma n}$. The extra input layer used in the argument consists only of ANDs of literals. This particular exponential average-case hypothesis is an additional assumption.

For any fixed $C>0$ the Nisan–Wigderson construction then gives $k$ pseudorandom coefficient bits on $O_C(H)$ seed bits. Here are the parameters and the depth count. Take $n=\lceil C' H\rceil$ with a sufficiently large fixed $C'$ and $t=\lfloor\min(\gamma/4,1/4)n\rfloor$. Construct $k=O(V)$ sets of size $n$ in a universe of $m=Dn$ positions with pairwise overlaps at most $t$, for a sufficiently large fixed integer $D$. For a fixed earlier set and random next set the probability of overlap at least $t+1$ is at most $\binom{n}{t+1}D^{-(t+1)}$ by a union bound on the chosen positions. Choosing $D$ large makes this exponentially small in $n$, while $k$ is polynomial in $n$. Greedily scanning the subsets constructs the design in $2^{O(n)}$ time. On an $m$-bit seed output $f$ applied to each of its $k$ ordered projections. [Nisan and Wigderson, *Hardness vs. Randomness*](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/NOAM/HARDNESS/final.pdf)

If an elementary test distinguishes this list distribution from uniform bits by $\epsilon$, adjacent hybrids differ by at least $\epsilon/k$ in absolute value. In the hybrid replacing position $i$, fix the seed outside the $i$th projection and the independent uniform suffix while preserving that signed gap. For an $n$-bit input $y$ to $f$, the preceding output bits become functions of at most $t$ bits of $y$. Evaluate the test on those bits, a guessed bit $b$, and the fixed suffix. If the signed gap is positive, predict $b$ on acceptance and $1-b$ on rejection. With uniform $b$ the prediction advantage is exactly the hybrid gap. For a negative gap flip the prediction. Some constant choice of $b$ preserves this advantage.

Every preceding bit can be represented as a sum of disjoint satisfying minterms on its intersection, with at most $2^t$ ANDs. Substitute these sums into the bottom affine inequalities of the test. This inserts one threshold layer, and complementing a final answer only reverses its integer threshold. The binary description length is at most $2^t\exp(O(H))\operatorname{poly}(n)$, allowing even a fresh copy of the minterms at every use. Choose $C'$ large enough that this is at most $2^{\gamma n}$ and $\exp(-CH)/k>2^{-\gamma n}$. The hypothesized hardness forces $\epsilon<\exp(-CH)$.

Finally the uniform mean of the nonnegative sufficient score tends to zero, and the sum of absolute coefficients in its expansion is at most $\exp(C_0H)$. Take $C>C_0$. On the new support its mean is below one at every target covered by that moment argument. Trying all the seeds thus produces a verified order at every such target, without evaluating either the score coefficients or any prefix expectations. Constructing the design, computing $f$, enumerating all $2^{Dn}$ seed words and running all extractor calls cost $\exp(O(H))$ in total. The branch's prime-base splitting argument, primality and perfect powers, recursion and finite fallback then give the conditional deterministic complete factoring bound.
