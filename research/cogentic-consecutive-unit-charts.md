# Pooling unit corrections over consecutive parameters

Three consecutive eligible charts can split a modulus even when the entire ratio group of each chart individually has only global involutions. Moreover, the corrections from the integer charts \(2,\ldots,Q\) generate exactly the same subgroup as the bases \(2,\ldots,Q+1\). Under Dirichlet RH the known small-generator bound gives a polynomial-size pool whose correction group is the whole unit group. Finding a nonglobal involution in that group still requires a modular relation. ## Products of corrected roots

In a chart at \(t\), let \(r_t,s_t\) be the two base root evaluations and let \(a_t,b_t\) be the two unit evaluations. Thus \(r_t/s_t=-t\), \(a_t=t^2b_t\), and \(b_t=(t-1)/(t(t+1))\). All four are invertible for an eligible chart. Compare products of roots by recording the chart and field order for each factor and reducing each factor with that order's evaluation map.

For a correction \(a_t^i b_t^j\), pair two occurrences of the chart with opposite orientations. The ratio \((r_t a_t^i)/(s_t b_t^{-j})\) on the first occurrence and \(s_t/r_t\) on the second give the desired correction. This lets any product of the chart units multiply an original marked ratio \(r_v/s_v\). The two products of evaluated roots \(R,S\) give a split when \((R/S)^2=1\) and \(R/S\) is a nonglobal sign. Every correction here is a power of a unit in the corresponding field order.

At \(N=2929=29\cdot101\), the three charts \(t=2,3,4\) are eligible and the pooled ratio
\[
(-3)a_2^{-3}b_2^{-1}b_4^{-1}\equiv2524\pmod {2929}
\]
is an involution. Its difference gcd is 29. Each individual group \(\langle -t,a_t,b_t\rangle\) has only global involutions. To verify this in the fixture, project the three generators by raising them to the odd part \(175\) of \((29-1)(101-1)\). This fixes all involutions. The projected generators have orders dividing four, and \((-t)^2=a_t/b_t\), so testing the two projected unit exponents modulo four and the projected marked-ratio exponent modulo two covers the projected group. ## The shared rational parameter

Write \(x_i=T+i\) for \(0\le i\le m+1\) with \(m\ge2\), and use the charts at \(x_1,\ldots,x_m\). The formal multiplicative group \(A=\langle x_0,\ldots,x_{m+1}\rangle\subset\mathbb Q(T)^\times\) is free on these generators by the distinct linear factors. Let \(H\) be the correction subgroup generated by \(x_i^2\) and \(b_{x_i}=x_{i-1}/(x_i x_{i+1})\) for \(1\le i\le m\). Both endpoint squares also belong to \(H\), by squaring the expressions for \(b_{x_1},b_{x_m}\) and rearranging. Thus \(A/H\) has exponent two. Its relations are \(e_{i-1}+e_i+e_{i+1}=0\), where \(e_i\) is the class of \(x_i\). These \(m\) relations are independent by their distinct leftmost coordinates. Hence \(A/H\cong\mathbb F_2^2\), with the \(e_i\) cycling through its three nonzero elements.

For \(m\ge3\), any three consecutive charts realize the three nonidentity formal cosets by taking one marked ratio with pooled corrections, up to the global minus sign. Arbitrary products of marked ratios and corrections realize the full signed group \(\langle-1,A\rangle\). In fact, multiplying three consecutive marked ratios by \(b_{x_i}x_{i-1}^{-2}\), with \(i\) the middle index, gives \(-1\). This yields each interior \(x_i\) from its marked ratio, and the end chart corrections then give the two endpoint generators.

On specializing \(T\) to an eligible value modulo \(N\), write \(L=\mathbb{Z}^{m+2}\) for the exponent lattice of \(A\), and let \(K\) consist of the exponents whose word evaluates to \(1\) modulo \(N\). If \(q:L\to\mathbb F_2^2\) is the formal quotient above, the modular quotient by the image of all corrections is exactly \(\mathbb F_2^2/q(K)\). The group of involutions, including the identity, in the image of \(A\) is
\[
(L/K)[2]\cong (K\cap 2L)/2K,\qquad [2v]\longmapsto[v].
\]
Adding the global sign preserves whether a word is a nonglobal involution. These formulas isolate the required modular information. An identity in the shared rational parameter has zero exponent vector in \(L\). If a signed rational word has square identically one, its value is the constant \(+1\) or \(-1\), and every eligible specialization gives the same global sign. The same argument applies to identities among the rational numbers after an integer specialization, including the identities below. To produce a split by pooled alignment requires a modular relation whose half-word yields a nonglobal sign.

## Specialization to a small integer window

For every \(Q\ge5\), use the integer charts at \(t=2,\ldots,Q\). Over \(\mathbb Q\), the group generated by the corrections is exactly \(\langle 2,\ldots,Q+1\rangle\). One inclusion follows from the chart formulas. For the other, start with
\[
  2=(a_5b_4)^{-1},\qquad 3=(2b_2)^{-1}.
\]
Then use the identity
\[
  (t+1)^2 b_t b_{t+1}=\frac{t-1}{t+2},\qquad 2\le t\le Q-1.
\]
The step from \(t-1\) to \(t+2\) starts from one of \(1,2,3\) and adds a bounded number of unit factors at each step. Since \((t+1)^2=a_{t+1}/b_{t+1}\), each resulting word has \(O(Q)\) factors before collecting powers. The same identities therefore give explicit polynomial-size words modulo \(N\) whenever the charts are eligible and \(Q\) is polynomial in \(\log N\).

Under Dirichlet RH, take \(Q=\max(5,3\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil^2)\). If \(N\le Q^2+1\), trial division costs polynomial bit time in \(\log N\). Otherwise, the four eligibility tests at each parameter involve positive integers below \(N\), so any failure gives a proper divisor. When all charts are eligible, the small-generator theorem gives \(\langle 2,\ldots,Q+1\rangle=(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^\times\) after reduction. In particular, under this hypothesis every mixed sign can be represented using pooled corrections with one marked ratio, for example the ratio at \(t=2\). The representation has polynomial bit length because each small-base exponent can be reduced modulo its order, which is less than \(N\). The rational identities construct the words for the small bases. They give neither the modular exponents for a mixed sign nor an odd-order certificate for any base.
