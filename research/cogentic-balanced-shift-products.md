# Two coprime predecessor parts on balanced semiprimes

There are balanced prime pairs on which two short monic shifts jointly supply a full predecessor. An unconditional prime-cluster argument gives a polynomial-time split on at least $X/\operatorname{polylog}X$ distinct products with both prime factors in $(X,2X]$ for every sufficiently large $X$. Thus the inputs have size $\asymp X^2$. A larger marker range follows from an explicit exceptional-modulus version of Bombieri–Vinogradov and gives the corresponding one-third scale. On retained inputs, the earlier power pool leaves large orders, and one small base's local order needs both shifts at the prescribed smooth cutoff. The pair also satisfies a short affine relation between the two hidden primes. ## Constructing the two shifts

Fix a sufficiently large tuple size $h$ and put $C=\prod_{r\le h,\ r\text{ prime}}r$. Choose distinct primes $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_h$ in intervals of fixed positive relative length inside $(L_0,1.1L_0)$, separated by gaps $\gg_h L_0$. Set
\[
  M=\prod_i\ell_i,\qquad a_i=M^2/\ell_i,\qquad b=1+CM,\qquad L_i(n)=a_in+b.
\]
The tuple is admissible. At primes at most $h$, $n=0$ works. At larger primes each form excludes at most one class because $\gcd(a_i,b)=1$. Use $n\in[x,1.1x]$ with $x=\lceil X/\min_i a_i\rceil$. For $L_0=X^{o(1)}$, all values lie in $(X,2X]$ at sufficiently large $X$.

Suppose $p=L_i(n)$ and $q=L_j(n)$ are prime at distinct indices. Put $m=(p-1)/\ell_j$ and
\[
   s=b-\ell_i(b-1)/\ell_j.
\]
Then $q=\ell_i m+s$ exactly, $p-1=\ell_j m$, and $\ell_j\nmid m$ because $v_{\ell_j}(a_i)=2$ and $v_{\ell_j}(b-1)=1$. As $N=pq\equiv q\bmod(p-1)$, the shift $N-s$ is divisible by $m$ and misses $\ell_j$ since $s\not\equiv1\bmod\ell_j$. The shift $N-1$ is divisible by $\ell_j$, and its gcd with $m$ divides $s-1$. Thus $p-1\mid E=(N-1)(N-s)$. The separated marker intervals give $|s|\asymp_h M$, and this can be used as a short signed shift.

At the other prime, $q-1$ shares with $N-1$ a divisor of $\ell_j(s-1)$. Indeed $N\equiv p\bmod(q-1)$ and $\ell_j(q-1)-\ell_i(p-1)=\ell_j(s-1)$. It shares with $N-s$ a divisor of $(\ell_i+\ell_j)(s-1)$, by $\ell_i(p-s)-\ell_j(q-1)=-(\ell_i+\ell_j)(s-1)$. Both bounds are $X^{o(1)}$. Hence the kernel of the map $z\mapsto z^E$ modulo $q$ has $X^{o(1)}$ elements. Products of distinct primes through $B=\operatorname{bitlength}(N)^2$, using $\lfloor\log(q-1)/\log B\rfloor$ primes per product, supply $q^{1/2+o(1)}$ distinct units below $q$. At least one prime through $B$ lies outside the kernel. The algorithm tests $\gcd(z^E-1,N)$ over those small integers and all signed shifts through a public bound for $|s|=O_h(L_0^h)$. Since $X^2<N\le4X^2$, the bound can be set as a function of $N$ alone. This takes polynomial bit time for $L_0=(\log X)^D$ with fixed $D$, or $\exp(O(W(X)))$ bit operations for $L_0=\exp(O(W(X)))$, where $W(X)=(\log X)^{1/3}(\log\log X)^{2/3}$.

These same inputs satisfy $\ell_j q-\ell_i p=(\ell_j-\ell_i)b$. An independent splitter can enumerate short affine coefficients and test the corresponding quadratic discriminants. The single affine exponent $\ell_j(N-s)$ also covers $p-1$ when the marker is supplied or enumerated. The comparison below concerns short monic shifts with the specified smooth padding and the earlier bounded power pool.

## Prime-cluster supply at the two marker ranges

For each fixed $D>0$ take $L_0=(\log X)^D$. Maynard's Theorem 3.4 with $m=2$ gives $\gg x/(\log x)^h$ parameters with at least two prime values. The slopes are at most $(\log X)^{D(2h-1)+o(1)}$ and the intercept is $(\log X)^{Dh+o(1)}$, so choose the theorem's short-interval parameter small enough for these slopes. The interval of parameters has length comparable to $x$. For each pair of labels, a given prime pair determines $n$, so the resulting products have bounded multiplicity in this count. This gives $\gg_D X/(\log X)^{h+D(2h-1)}$ products. [Maynard, Theorem 3.4](https://compositio.nl/Content/prize2018_maynard.pdf#page=5)

The larger-marker argument uses the following distribution input. At scale $Z$, exclude one prime $B_*$, or take $B_*=1$. Require a total prime progression error $O(Z\exp(-c\sqrt{\log Z}))$ up to moduli $Z^{1/2-c}$ coprime to $B_*$, for endpoints up to $Z$, with the main term $\pi(t)/\varphi(d)$. This is the exceptional-modulus estimate obtained in Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, Section 7 after (7.3). Choose the marker primes in their disjoint intervals avoiding $B_*$. Each slope is then coprime to $B_*$. For $L_0=\exp(D W(X))$, the slopes are $\exp(o(\sqrt{\log X}))$. Take $Z=2X$, and a fixed small $\theta>0$ such that all $a_id$ for $d\le x^\theta$ are within the distribution range. For a fixed $i$ these moduli are distinct. Restrict the absolute error sum to this set and subtract its two endpoints. To normalize the main terms by the prime count for each individual form as in Maynard's Hypothesis 1, add at most the error for $d=1$ times $\sum_{d\le x^\theta}\varphi(a_i)/\varphi(a_i d)=O(\log x)$. The resulting error is $x\exp(-c'\sqrt{\log x})$. The main prime count for form $i$ is asymptotic to $a_i\#\mathcal A(x)/(\varphi(a_i)\log X)$, where $\mathcal A(x)=[x,1.1x]\cap\mathbb Z$. This and $\varphi(B_*)/B_*\ge1/2$ give the required weighted lower bound. The integer distribution follows from the parameter interval. With fixed $h$ sufficiently large for two primes, the theorem gives $\gg x/(\log x)^h=X^{1-o(1)}$ products with both prime factors in $(X,2X]$. The splitting algorithm enumerates the shift values directly. [Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, equations (7.2)–(7.3)](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A4f92b379-4935-4df4-ab12-ad6e8e85c965/files/m82130bfb3e8ac4562cce865fd741d525) · [Maynard, Hypothesis 1 and Theorem 3.1](https://compositio.nl/Content/prize2018_maynard.pdf#page=2)

## Comparing the older pool and individual shifts

Fix maximum base, smooth and power-index cutoffs $A,Y,R=\exp(O(W(X)))$ and let $T$ be the maximum of $Y$, $2L_0$, $C$ and $(R+1)^2\log(2L_0)(\log X)^{h+3}$. Enlarge the base list to contain integers through $(\log X)^4$. For a prime $r>T$ dividing $p-1$, the coefficient $a_i$ is a unit and $a_i q\equiv c_{ij}=a_i b-a_j(b-1)\pmod r$. If $r$ divides an earlier tested $N^k\pm1$, it divides the fixed integer
\[
           \prod_{1\le k\le R}(c_{ij}^{2k}-a_i^{2k}).
\]
The rational number $c_{ij}/a_i$ differs from both signs for large $X$ because the relative gaps between slopes are fixed and $b$ grows. The product has log size $O_h(R^2\log(2L_0))$. Every candidate prime $r>T$ restricts $n$ to one class modulo $r$, so the total number of lost parameters is $O_h(R^2\log(2L_0)(x/T+1))=o(x/(\log x)^h)$. This applies to both orientations of all pairs of labels.

The full $T$-smooth part of each predecessor is at most $x^{1/12}$ outside another $o(x/(\log x)^h)$ parameters. To see this, divide $a_i n+b-1$ by $\gcd(a_i,b-1)\le b-1=X^{o(1)}$ to get a primitive linear form. If the original smooth part exceeds $x^{1/12}$, the primitive form has a $T$-smooth divisor in $[x^{1/24},Tx^{1/24}]$ by taking prime factors successively. Any such divisor $d$ restricts $n$ to one class modulo $d$ and contributes $O(x/d)$ values. Rankin's bound with $t=1/(2\log(2T))$ gives
\[
   \sum_{P^+(d)\le T,\ d\ge x^{1/24}}\frac1d
   \le x^{-t/24}\prod_{r\le T,\ r\text{ prime}}(1-r^{-1+t})^{-1}
   \ll x^{-t/24}(\log(2T))^{O(1)}=o((\log x)^{-h}).
\]
Here $\log T=O(W(X))$. The few primes at which any tested base has order at most $\lfloor x^{1/3}\rfloor$ divide the product of the corresponding $a^j-1$, of log size $O(A x^{2/3}\log(2A))$. Their number is $X^{2/3+o(1)}$ and each form gives at most one parameter per prime. Thus the earlier odd pool of all $N^k\pm1$, even multiplied by the full smooth scalar through $Y$ and the power of two, leaves residual orders exceeding $x^{1/4}$ at both primes for every retained base. A bounded residual search of one-third size misses all these pairs. [Earlier residual-order test](cogentic-partial-odd-orders.md#search-from-an-odd-partial-scalar)

If the chosen marker exceeds $Y$, there is also an actual small-base order at $p$ that needs both shifts. Outside $X^{1/2+o(1)}$ primes in the dyad, the integers through $(\log X)^4$ generate the full multiplicative group. Indeed a proper subgroup containing them would give a nonprincipal character modulo $p$ taking value one on all of them. The set $S$ of products of $\lfloor2\log X/\log((\log X)^4)\rfloor$ distinct primes through $(\log X)^4$ lies below $X^2$ and has size $X^{3/2+o(1)}$. That character has sum $|S|$ on $S$. The multiplicative large sieve for primitive characters through modulus $2X$ bounds the sum of squared character sums by $O(X^2|S|)$, giving the asserted exception count. Remove these primes from the tuple supplies as well. Some base through $(\log X)^4$ now has order divisible by $\ell_j$. Its order part supported on primes above $T$ exceeds $x^{1/4}$ by the same low-order and smooth-part exclusions. This part divides $m$. For any signed $t$ of subpolynomial size, a single exponent $N-t$ with smooth padding through $Y$ and the power of two can annihilate the order only if that large part divides $s-t$, forcing $t=s$. But $N-s$ misses $\ell_j>Y$, so the padded exponent fails at that same base. [Montgomery–Vaughan, large sieve](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/large_sieve.pdf)

## Why a predecessor smoothness count does not provide partners

When $p\asymp q\asymp X$, a fixed class modulo $p-1\asymp X$ has only $O(1)$ integer candidates in the dyad. A count of primes $p$ whose largest predecessor prime is at most a specified level gives no prime count among those candidates. For a fixed pair of distinct shifts $1,s$, even the number of candidate classes can be bounded. For $r^e\parallel p-1$ and $v=v_r(s-1)$, the roots of $(q-1)(q-s)$ modulo $r^e$ number at most $2r^v$. If $e\le2v$, a root must satisfy $q\equiv1\bmod r^{\lceil e/2\rceil}$, giving at most $r^v$ candidates. If $e>2v$, one factor must have valuation at least $e-v$, giving at most $2r^v$. The CRT bound is $2^{\omega(p-1)}|s-1|$. Summing over all $s\ne1$ in a subpolynomial shift range gives at most $X^{1+o(1)}$ balanced products with full predecessor coverage, matching the exponent of the cluster count.

If all primes of $p-1$ already lie below the smooth scalar's cutoff, the scalar with its power of two covers $p-1$ for every choice of $q$. Otherwise the remaining congruence modulus is the part $d(p)$ of the predecessor supported on primes above that cutoff. For each such modulus, let $w(d)$ count prescribed reduced partner classes over first primes with $d(p)=d$, with multiplicity. An averaged progression estimate suffices when its modulus range covers the used $d$ and $\sum_d w(d)E_X(d)=o((X/\log X)\sum_d w(d)/\varphi(d))$, where $E_X(d)$ is the maximum dyadic error over reduced classes. A largest-predecessor-prime bound at a larger level than the scalar's cutoff can leave $d(p)$ as large as nearly all of $p-1$. The prime-cluster construction supplies the partners by a simultaneous prime statement.
