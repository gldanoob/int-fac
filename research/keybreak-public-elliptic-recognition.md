# Using the almost-all-prime elliptic bound in public descent

Under the hypotheses of the [private elliptic recognition argument](keybreak-private-elliptic-recognition.md), the public descent can use the same $1.4143$ recognition coefficient. This requires changing the proof of **which candidates are good**. A public cofactor can contain arbitrary primes, including ones for which the elliptic estimate is weak. We remove such candidates from the analytic lower count and charge their incidence mass to the actual parent–child bounds in KeyBreak. All numerical acceptances still require complete verified factor lists. [KeyBreak, Theorem G.1 and Propositions G.11–G.12](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)

Write $B$ for the factor base, $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, $\Lambda=D\log\log N$, and $T_Q=\sqrt{\log Q\log\log Q}$. At each dyadic interval $(P,2P]$ call a prime exceptional if it fails the Hasse smooth-count lower bound used in the private argument. Fix the tolerances there once. The exceptional fraction of the primes in any complete band above $B$ is at most $\eta=\exp(-cT_B)$ eventually, for an absolute $c>0$ at the chosen elliptic parameters. Indeed in the Fourier proof the low-frequency term is $\exp(-(2c_0/3+o(1))T_P)$ and the middle-frequency sinc term is $\exp(-(1/c_0+o(1))T_P)$ for stage-one constant $c_0=1/\sqrt2$. The other bounds save a power of $P$. The density normalization, disjoint integer neighborhoods, and division by the number of primes in the dyad lose at most powers of $\log P$. Deleting the primes of $N$ from a dyad does not change this bound after reducing $c$: there are at most $O(\log N)$ of them and $B$ exceeds every fixed power of $\log N$. Since $T_B\gg (\log D)^m$ for every fixed $m$, the saving survives the descent recursion.

### Counting deleted candidates

Use the original numerical smoothness indicators, means $\tau$, point counts $Z$, and weighted incidence surrogates. A candidate counted in the new good count $D_\ell$ must satisfy the previous recursive conditions with the new child-good sets and have no exceptional numerical prime $q>B$ in the quotient being tested. The new condition concerns *all* distinct prime factors of that quotient, including even valuations and primes terminal for the sampled number field. It is only a proof predicate. Pass-throughs keep their ordinary identity edges. A proper gcd exit remains an absorbing success.

Every factor $q>B$ of a smooth quotient is within the request's local child ceiling. At an internal active label the parent exceeds the child ceiling, so $q$ is distinct from the parent. At the top the quotient is the undivided algebraic value. A child lies below the prime denominator interval and below the environment modulus $Q$, and the rational environment excludes its distinguished root: on eligible points the only possible rational prime above $B$ at the top is its own larger parent, and internally there are none. Primes dividing $N$ are covered by the original exact gcd exit on the nonzero algebraic value, whose magnitude is less than $N$. Thus the formal incidence surrogate counts each required deletion. It already retains even valuations, raw-content incidences and terminal primes. The source's incoming parent-denominator mask stays in place. No lower bound for an unmasked parent incidence is being used. [KeyBreak (G.46)–(G.51) and §H.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=120)

For clarity, charge this deletion separately from the recursive bad-child term in (G.47). For an internal level, (G.51), summed over roots by $\Pr(R_{q,s})\ll1/q$ and Cauchy–Schwarz with (G.14), gives
$$
 \sum_s\mathbb E H_i^{\rm det}(q,s)\ll D^{O(1)}B_{i+1}^{\rm act}(q).
$$
On the incoming-rational good part the same estimate follows from (G.50). Here $B_{i+1}^{\rm act}\le B_{i+1}$ and all weights are deterministic. In each complete band $B_{i+1}(q)$ varies by at most a factor of two, since it is a positive sum of powers with exponents in $[-1,0]$. Consequently $\sum_{q\;\mathrm{exceptional}} B_{i+1}(q)\ll\eta W_{i+1}$. This is a first-moment charge and needs no independence between children or between the marks of a field. Dividing by $W_i$ and using (G.41) adds at most $D^{O(1)}\eta$ to the right side of (G.44). Identity edges need no additional numerical test. At a top band (G.55) and the aggregate (G.14) give the corresponding summed expectation $\ll D^{O(1)}B_1(q)$ on $q$ below the local ceiling, with the same local-to-global comparison as in the [tiered descent](keybreak-tiered-descent.md). This adds $D^{O(1)}\eta$ to (G.53), alongside the ordinary $D^{O(1)}\sqrt{\delta_1}$ term. All cutoffs are integer dyadic exponents, so there are no partial prime bands at an endpoint.

The internal recursion has depth $O(\log D)$ and multiplies an error by at most $D^{O(1)}$ per level. Even after the top square root and the polynomial union over initial bands, the extra error is $o(1)$. In particular it is smaller than $D^{-4A_{\rm tail}}$ even after the internal propagation, for $A_{\rm tail}=(j_{\rm depth}+10)^3$. This gives the same simultaneous positive fraction of good top parents in one field, with the smaller $D_\ell$ and the original absolute/relative density lower bounds (H.16).

### Executing the capped test

At each algebraic recognition call, first recover factors at most $B$ using the bounded genus-two **partial** factor routine with cutoff $B$. Its proper-split guarantee on a composite containing a small prime applies also when other prime factors are large; primality and perfect powers are direct cases. This costs $\exp(o(\Lambda))$ since $\log B=O(\Lambda)$. Then run the bounded elliptic splitter on each unresolved composite with the local child cutoff $y$, recursively splitting verified divisors. For a counted good smooth candidate, every remaining prime above $B$ is elliptic-eligible, so the least-prime hypothesis of Lenstra–Pomerance applies at every required split when the preliminary tests recovered all small factors. Couple these capped small-factor failures only on the required good paths, or charge their uniform error directly. The elliptic confidence factor can be polynomially enlarged to cover all calls. For other candidates any failure returns a local rejection. Full numerical factor lists and the original gcd, unit and sign checks are still mandatory on acceptance. [Lenstra–Pomerance, Theorem 7.1](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper85.pdf#page=19)

The H.4 induction requires a direct opportunity bound because a smooth candidate with an exceptional prime may be rejected. Condition on the public history and good field. At a good state, a point in $D_\ell$ passes the revised factor tests with probability $1-o(1)$ uniformly; take this at least $3/4$. Any accepted point belongs to the original $Z$ unless there is a proper-factor exit. Thus the fraction of good opportunities among verified candidates is at least $(3/4)D_\ell/Z$, the good opportunities per rational acceptance are at least $(3/4)D_\ell/|R|$, and each raw trial has mass at least $(3/8)D_\ell/|\Omega|$ including the rank draw. The exact rational test can equivalently be coupled with the original bounded implementation using its global confidence allowance. These three estimates with (H.16) and the original child success bound are fixed fractions of the values used to prove H.4. Multiplying all three finite caps by any sufficiently large fixed constant preserves its failure bounds and every leading coefficient. No coupling to a recognizer that must accept *every* smooth point is assumed. The recorded rows and all acceptance decisions stay public before the final extraction. The input-level prepass continues to handle any small prime of the input itself, and charges its original genus-two cost.
