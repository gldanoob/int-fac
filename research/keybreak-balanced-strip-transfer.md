# Transferring a balanced top strip through the descent proof

The balanced top predicate can use its own ordinary and child-forced densities in the weighted proof of G.12. The interior G.11 recurrence can be applied at each fixed target multiplier, while the top pair bound is required only in the mixture conditional on the incoming child root. The prime multiplier changes the affine phases and the anchor height. The row identity and square certificate continue to be identities for the current input \(N\). [KeyBreak, Propositions G.11–G.12 and Theorem E.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)

## Strip means and the ideal pair contribution

Fix a complete private rational dyad \(J\) inside one top box. Use the denominator interval of relative width \(1/d\), so the original marginal Gaussian scales are all comparable to a deterministic \(X_*\). Let \(\mathcal S\subset[X_*,2X_*]\) be the disjoint prime-bin product strip and set
\[
\mu=|\mathcal S|/X_*,\qquad
\mathcal S_q=\{z/q:z\in\mathcal S,\ q\mid z\},\qquad
\mu_q=|\mathcal S_q|/(X_*/q).
\]
Use \(q=1\) for the ordinary test. A nonempty \(\mathcal S_q\) consists of the patterns occupying the bin of \(q\), with that one prime fixed and removed. For each such pattern, the fraction of the original products selecting \(q\) from its bin is \(1/n_q\), where \(n_q\gg q/(d\log y)\) at bin width \(\asymp1/d\). Consequently
\[
0\le \frac{\mu_q}{\mu}=q\frac{|\mathcal S_q|}{|\mathcal S|}\le D^{O(1)}.
\tag{1}
\]
The ratio can vanish at a prime in an empty bin or outside the allowed bins. The upper estimate suffices. All quantified tests use the same predetermined \(\mathcal S\) for the whole dyad. [Prime-bin strip and density](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md) · [Divided strip and conditioned moments](keybreak-mixed-strip-moment.md)

For the ordinary one-point lower bound, remove the \(O(1)\) possible bin primes dividing the progression step \(2|G(P)|\). A complete prime-bin pattern loses a vanishing fraction of its products. The pointwise nonprincipal character estimate of the prime-bin argument makes the full-strip count in each unit residue modulo this step comparable to the strip mass divided by \(\phi(2|G(P)|)\). Indeed, the absolute error is at most the full strip mass times \(X_*^{-1/2}V^{o(1)}\), while the step is \(V^{O(1)}\). The Gaussian density on the positive strip is comparable to \(1/X_*\) after the usual progression normalization, by bounded marginal-width ratios and \(|F_{0,k}(P)|/X_*=o(1)\) uniformly in \(k\). The one-point kernel comparison then gives an ordinary probability \(\gg\mu\) for every eligible mark. The analogous *upper* bound in the divided single-root law is \(D^{O(1)}\mu_q\), uniformly even on incoming-rational exceptional environments. Here one can use the elementary bound \(m/\phi(m)\ll1+\log m=D^{O(1)}\). A nonunit at an actual step prime is excluded by the support. A prescribed child \(q\) is removed exactly once because the strip is squarefree. [One-point kernel and periods, KeyBreak G.4–G.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=109) · [Prime-bin character estimate](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md)

The ideal zero-mode upper bound also follows from the prime-bin argument, without assuming a pointwise majorant for arbitrary friable subsets. Put \(x=X_*/q\). On a nonthin pair use \(\vartheta\le V^\zeta\) and discard Mahalanobis layer radii below \(V^{-a}\), for fixed small \(a,\zeta>0\). The discarded layer mass is \(O(V^{-2a})\) up to negligible mesh error. Each grid side in a retained layer is \(\gg xV^{-(a+\zeta)}\). Let \(I\) be a side of length \(L\) and suppose the smallest free prime in any pattern is at least \(V^{c+o(1)}\). Fix all but one free prime in a pattern. The remaining prime lies in an interval of length \(O(PL/x)\) at its bin scale \(P\). If \(a+\zeta<c/2\), the RH square-root prime-counting error is dominated by this interval-length term. Its fraction of the whole bin's primes is \(O(dL/x)\); when this is above one the trivial bound suffices. The same conclusion holds after dividing by \(q\) because there is still a free slot. Summing the patterns shows that the principal term on \(I\) uses at most \(O(dL/x)|\mathcal S_q|\) products.

For the Cartesian step \(m_i=2|G_i\Delta|/q\), remove the \(O(1)\) allowed primes dividing the two periods before applying this argument. The pruned shape is supported on unit phases and still has the same prime-bin prefix bounds. Orthogonality and the nonprincipal bound cost \(|\mathcal S_q|x^{-1/2}V^{o(1)}\) on each phase. Dividing by \(L/m_i\) gives \(\mu_q x^{-1/2+o(1)}\). The principal term gives at most \(D^{O(1)}\mu_q\) normalized by that same period count. In the ideal pair distribution, each removed prime has divisibility probability \(O(1/p)\) or zero in either coordinate. A base-step prime is excluded by unit support. A determinant prime away from the base step divides the value in a fraction \(1/p\) of the phases. A prime coprime to the entire coordinate period refines that coordinate progression by a factor \(p\), which preserves negligible Mahalanobis mesh since \(p=V^{O(1)}\). All three cases use the same translated-phase mass comparison. The removed prime events are therefore negligible against \(\mu^2\). The two coordinate counts multiply on each phase in the enclosing grid of a retained ellipse. The G.24 area comparison and layer-cake summation give ideal pair probability at most \(D^{O(1)}\mu_q^2+o(\mu^2)\), for both root and no-root cases, uniformly in \(k\). Thus the multiplier-averaged mixed-mode estimate can be joined to a polynomial ideal main term. The conditions on \(a,\zeta\) are compatible with a sufficiently small positive determinant cutoff for every fixed Fourier width excess. ## Weighted incoming incidence and elliptic deletions

Set \(T'_0=V^2/(d\log V)\). The rational box proof gives ordinary rational-good size \(\asymp T'_0\rho_p/p\) at top parent \(p\), and incoming-good size \(\asymp m_J(q)=T'_0\rho_J/q\). Shrinking the denominator interval changes the squared relative rational error by a factor \(O(d)\), which the fixed power gaps absorb. It preserves the distinct roles of the parent and denominator primes in the product convolution. Use the deterministic top reference
\[
\bar\tau_p=\mu T'_0\rho_p/p.
\]
The strip mean supplies the lower bound \(\mathbb E Z_p\gg\bar\tau_p\) and the averaged ordinary pair estimate supplies \(\mathbb E Z_p^2\ll D^{O(1)}\bar\tau_p^2\) on rational-good environments. This uses the polynomial moment comparison, with no concentration around an individual field mean required. [Shrunk-denominator rational estimate](keybreak-balanced-ideal-pair-audit.md#denominator-restriction-in-the-rational-argument) · [Top moment amplification](keybreak-polynomial-top-field-pool.md)

In (G.54) replace the top value indicator by the strip test and use \(\bar\tau_p\) above. The same incidence reordering holds exactly, with the squarefree test \(F(P)/q\in\mathcal S_q\) on the common root. The comparable within-dyad parent weights and the cancellation \(|J|\rho_p/p\asymp\rho_J\) imply
\[
\mathbb E[H^{\rm det}_{0,J}\mid g,R_{q,s}]
\ll \frac{D^{O(1)}}{q}\frac{\mu_q}{\mu}\frac{R_J(g,q,s)}{m_J(q)}.
\tag{2}
\]
This is a comparison with the tested strip's own mean. The upper bound holds without a lower bound on \(R_J\), so it handles the incoming-rational exceptional part via (G.14) and \(\Pr(R_{q,s}\mid g)\ll1/q\). On the incoming-good part the conditioned pair estimate gives \(\mathbb E[(H^{\rm det}_{0,J}/B_1(q))^2\mid g,R_{q,s}]\ll D^{O(1)}\) with the original positive profile \(B_1(q)\ge1/q\), using (1). The diagonal and discarded geometric pairs have a negative-power top geometry saving after division by \(\mu^2\). None of these bounds divides by \(\mu_q\). [KeyBreak, (G.14) and (G.53)–(G.55)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=121)

Apply the interior G.11 proof at fixed \(k\), using the single-root means of that fixed-multiplier field experiment. The digit height, rational counts, and unit checks used in that proof hold uniformly. In particular a prime dividing \(G(P)\) is smaller than \(k\), and the coprimality with \(N\) gives the same unit support. The deterministic comparison profile and weighting measures can use the same parameters for every \(k\). Averaging the resulting normalized \(\delta_1(k)\) gives the child-bad mass in the enlarged joint law. Cauchy–Schwarz in G.12 now takes place in the law of \((k,F)\) conditioned on \(g,R_{q,s}\), allowing the child-bad indicator to depend arbitrarily on the drawn field and multiplier. A per-multiplier top pair bound and concentration around a pooled interior mean are unnecessary. [KeyBreak, (G.42) and (G.44)–(G.52)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)

Keep the original positive profile, or a polynomial multiple of \(1/q\), when counting elliptic-exceptional child primes. The exact ratio \(\mu_q/\mu\) can have gaps inside a dyad and is used only as the upper bound (1). Equation (2) and (G.14) bound the root-averaged first moment by the deterministic profile with a polynomial factor. Thus the exceptional fraction in each complete dyad still bounds the added bad mass, using bounded within-dyad profile ratios. This deletion counts every numerical child prime above the base cutoff needed by the recognizer, including even valuations and terminal ideals. Use the nominal strip predicate in the moment proof and charge exceptions after counting incidences. The prime-bin character estimate then only removes finitely many pair-modulus primes, and needs no equidistribution within an arbitrary elliptic-exception set. At the private rational side, discard exceptional top primes along with uncovered parents before the staged split-prime genus count. [Elliptic incidence deletion](keybreak-public-elliptic-recognition.md) · [Staged genus count after dyad deletions](keybreak-reduced-form-smooth-supply.md#field-good-prime-bands-and-staged-prefixes)

The top pool draws full independent triples \((g,k,F)\). Its hit probability is for this full law, so the same Paley–Zygmund and Markov argument, polynomial amplification, and union over parents apply after subtracting the negligible unconditional setup rejection. A pool holding the multiplier fixed would require a lower hit probability at that fixed multiplier. The successful field supplies the entire subtree of its top prime. ## The input and the square certificate

The equality \(F(r,Q)=kN\) gives \(F(m_N,1)\equiv0\pmod N\) for \(m_N=rQ^{-1}\bmod N\). This is exactly the root condition in the public data of E.1. With an odd irreducible degree and unit leading coefficient \(A\), the monic coordinate \(\beta=A\alpha\) is sent to \(Am_N\) in (E.2). Its unit denominators are the derivative and the checked atom values modulo \(N\). The rational correction uses \(G(a,b)\equiv Q(a-bm_N)\pmod N\), independently of the exact integer anchor value. Equation (E.7) uses the actual primitive form values and the signed occurrence sum, including the leading factor \(A^{-E}\), to get the correct norm square class. The exact field roots, derivative clearing, and the product of their evaluated values in (E.19) therefore use just the root congruence modulo \(N\). All corrected row identities and the final square are verified modulo \(N\). [KeyBreak, (E.1)–(E.7), Theorem E.4, and §E.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=85)

With the full coefficient cap below \(k\), the content divides \(N\), as in the prime-multiplier calculation. The setup screen takes gcds of the content, leading coefficient and derivative against \(N\), and the relation and extraction screens also use \(N\). The derivative rejection without a factor has the same one-coordinate bound. Throughout the descent the allowed child primes and numerical recognition cutoffs are below \(k\). A raw value divisible by \(k\) cannot pass its tested smooth or strip predicate. A possible factor \(k\) of a polynomial discriminant is merely a bad prime of that field and does not appear as an allowed child label. In the unlikely case that the sampled prime actually divides \(N\), the separate gcd with \(N\) detects a genuine input divisor. The incidence profiles, elliptic exceptional-prime counts, and norm and sign certificate use the numerical form factors at marks or the given modulus \(N\), with no factorization of the prescribed integer \(kN\).
