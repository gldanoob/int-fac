# Factoring from either twist of a fixed elliptic curve

Fix an elliptic curve over the rationals with three rational roots in its Weierstrass equation. After a rational change of variables it has the form
\[
                 E:\ y^2=(x-e_1)(x-e_2)(x-e_3)
\]
with distinct fixed integers \(e_i\). Assume Dirichlet RH. Given an odd \(N\) with at least two distinct prime factors, the following deterministic search returns a proper divisor if, at **some** good prime \(p\mid N\), **either** quadratic twist of \(E\) has \(B\)-smooth odd group order. The two order candidates are \(p+1-t_p\) and \(p+1+t_p\), where \(t_p\) is the trace of \(E\). The cost is \(B\,\mathrm{poly}_E(\log N)\) for \(2\le B\le 4N\). The search does not use \(p\), its trace, or a supplied nonresidue. The constant in the polynomial can depend on the fixed roots and the character estimates. Bad reduction at another factor is caught by the initial trial division for large inputs.

More generally, let \(U\) be the product of the largest odd prime powers \(\ell^a\le4N\) for primes \(\ell\le B\). The search with a second stage of width \(D\) suffices if the odd part of the **group exponent**, after division by its gcd with \(U\), is at most \(D\) for one of the two twists at \(p\). The work is \(\widetilde O((B+\sqrt D)\,\mathrm{poly}_E(\log N))\). In particular one can set \(B=L_N[1/3,O(1)]\) and \(D=B^2\). [Residual-order search](cogentic-elliptic-residual-order.md)

## Fixing a twist and a point coset at the same time

Use a rational parameter \(c=u/v\) and put \(d=\prod_i(c-e_i)\). The twist \(E_d\) has roots \(de_i\) and its marked point is \(R=(dc,d^2)\). Substitution verifies the equation. For good odd primes at which \(v\) and the three differences are units, let \(A_i\) be the quadratic-character bit of \(c-e_i\) and let \(\omega=\sum_i A_i\) in \(\mathbb F_2\). The twist sign is \(\omega\). The first two usual descent coordinates of \(R\) are the square classes of \(dc-de_i\) for \(i=1,2\), hence the bits \((\omega+A_1,\omega+A_2)\). Solving these three linear equations shows that every twist sign and every two-bit point coset specify exactly one triple of bits \(A_i\).

The descent map gives an isomorphism \(E_d(\mathbb F_p)/2E_d(\mathbb F_p)\simeq\mathbb F_2^2\). Indeed its kernel consists of doubles by full two-descent, and a finite group with four elements in its two-torsion has index four over its doubles. For two parameters of the same local twist sign, scaling by an isomorphism changes \(X-de_i\) by a square. Thus a chosen coset, together with its order properties, can be identified before the parameter is selected. [Full two-descent](cogentic-elliptic-local-halves.md#including-the-two-known-torsion-points)

Take any other prime divisor \(q\) and choose either twist there. At each of \(p,q\), let \(2^{a_r}\) be the exponent of the two-primary subgroup of the selected twist. Both exponents are at least two. At the prime with larger \(a_r\), breaking ties arbitrarily, select the coset of an element of maximal two-primary order. Every representative of that coset has the same maximal two-primary order. In a cyclic factor of maximal exponent its coefficient is odd and adding a double preserves that parity. At the other prime select the zero coset. Every representative there has two-primary order at most \(2^{a_r-1}\). The two selected heights therefore differ for every pair of representatives, even if the torsion points themselves yield identical descent coordinates.

For these two specified triples of signs apply the short linear-forms estimate to the four forms \(v,u-e_1v,u-e_2v,u-e_3v\). Require square signs for \(v\) at both primes. The other three signs then prescribe the signs of the rational differences. The forms are pairwise nonproportional and their coefficients are fixed. For clarity about the real ranges, take \(u\) between \(T/2\) and \(3T/4\), and take positive \(v\) smaller than \(T/(8(1+\max_i|e_i|))\). This rectangle has area \(\gg_E T^2\) and puts all four forms in \([1,T]\). On a cyclic prime of size \(C_E T\) with \(C_E>4(1+\max_i|e_i|)\), truncating the four forms to \([1,T]\) gives no spurious modular wraps for \(u,v\) in the enumerated ranges after restricting \(u\) to \([1,T]\). An equivalent proof weights \(u,v\) by compactly supported interval autocorrelations in this rectangle. Their normalized Fourier \(\ell^1\) norms are bounded. The generalized von Neumann bound controls every nonprincipal term by a short-character \(U^3\) norm, since the determinants of the four distinct directions are nonzero modulo the cyclic prime. Trial division first catches \(p\) or \(q\) at or below a fixed multiple of \(T\). Under Dirichlet RH the short character estimate gives a suitable \(T=(\log(2N))^{C_E'}\) with a sufficiently large fixed exponent. The explicit scale \(T=\lceil\exp((\log\log(3N))^2)\rceil\) also suffices asymptotically and still costs \(L_N[1/3,o(1)]\) to enumerate. [Short-character argument and its sources](cogentic-cm-linear-forms.md)

## Recovering the divisor

Enumerate the fractions of height at most \(T\). Removing a common factor of \(u,v\) preserves \(c\) and its three rational differences. Gcd-test the denominators, root differences, and numerical differences \(u-e_iv\) individually. Use the integral positions \((ve_1,ve_2,ve_3,u)\) to build the marked twist. Scaling all positions by \(v\) multiplies the abscissa by \(v^4\) and the ordinate by \(v^6\), giving the same local points up to isomorphism.

Multiply the marked point by \(U\) and double until it reaches the identity or the public Hasse bound on the number of doublings is exhausted. At the promised prime \(p\), \(U\) kills the odd order. If the odd order at \(q\) survives, only \(p\) can reach the identity. If both odd orders die, the two prescribed heights differ, since odd multiplication preserves the two-primary orders. In either case the checked modular group law exposes a proper divisor by the first one-sided identity. This argument compares reductions at distinct primes and works when \(N\) has repeated prime factors.

For the residual-order variant, remove the two-primary orders by public doubling and look for a local annihilator up to \(D\) on the resulting point. The second stage either exposes a proper gcd or verifies a positive global annihilator. In the latter case restoring the odd part of this annihilator in the first-stage multiplier kills both odd orders and preserves the unequal two-heights. Sieving and forming the smooth multiplier take \(B\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N)\) bit operations, for example with a product tree. The \(O(T^2)\) charts add a polynomial in \(\log N\) at the stated fixed-power height.

## A non-CM example

The base with roots \(0,1,3\) has \(j=21952/9\). At \(p=131\) its two group orders are 136 and 128. At \(q=223\) they are 220 and 228. For \(B=5\) only the twist of order 128 satisfies the smoothness condition. At both primes the Gaussian curve with roots \(0,1,-1\) has trace zero. Every number \(p-1,p+1,q-1,q+1\) also has a prime divisor larger than five.

Running the public search below gives \(29213=131\cdot223\) at \(c=-5\). The executable additionally checks the order counts using Sage. This gives an example outside the earlier guarantees for the Gaussian curve and for the two torus orders. General coverage still requires a suitable local order on at least one public base.
