# A degree ladder for shorter coefficient intervals

Translated polynomial powers can distribute the random coefficient ranges over a sequence of exact evaluation quotients. With the original cofactors fixed, this preserves the fixed-schedule score and permits descent to a box in which every remaining interval has one-third size. The product of their lengths and the width of a universal one-mark sampler remain on the two-thirds scale. ## Parameters and the evaluation quotients

Write $\log m\sim\mu V$, $\log X_{\rm sc}\sim\lambda V$, and assume the existing schedule gives the fixed-cofactor moment and extractor hypotheses with $\lambda>2\mu$. The original Gaussian low coefficient scales have leading logarithm $(\lambda-\mu)V$. Choose a physical width exponent $S$ with
\[
                  \mu+\lambda/2<S<\lambda.
\]
Keep $f(m)=k_0N$ unchanged by adding only $g(m-X)$ times integer polynomials of degree at most $d-2$. Choose an integer translation $Z\ge A$ with $\log Z=O(H)$, and write $b_i=Z+a_i$ at a pair of distinct marks. Let $r\le d-3$ and $\rho_j=j\log Z/V$. For each $0\le j\le r$, define $B_j$ to be the evaluation lattice generated by the columns with entries $n_i b_i^j$ and $n_i b_i^{j+1}$, where $n_i=m-a_i$. After dividing coordinate $i$ by $n_i b_i^j$, this is the lattice of pairs congruent modulo $\Delta=a_2-a_1$. The matrix of the two columns has both singular values within $e^{O(H)}$ of $mZ^j$: use its determinant $n_1n_2b_1^jb_2^j\Delta$, the upper norm bound, and $(1+A/Z)^d=e^{O(H)}$. These bounds include every mark in $[1,A]$.

The lattices satisfy $B_{j+1}\subseteq B_j$. The quotient is cyclic, generated by the degree-$j$ column and of order $b_1b_2$. The common degree-$(j+1)$ column vanishes in the quotient, while
\[
 b_i^{j+2}=(b_1+b_2)b_i^{j+1}-b_1b_2 b_i^j
\]
and the determinant ratio is $b_1b_2$. Thus **one** coefficient per degree covers each successive quotient. For a gap of $h$ degrees, two coefficient directions at the lower end of the gap suffice with lengths $Z^h e^{O(H)}$. To see the bound, divide the values by $n_i b_i^j$, floor in the two-column basis for $B_{j+h}$, and interpolate the remainder by $x+b_i y$. The remainder entries are bounded by $(Z+A)^h(1+Z+A)$, and the interpolation denominators are at least one. This gives representatives with coefficients at most $Z^h e^{O(H)}$, uniformly over the gaps.

Use two wide directions of degrees $r,r+1$ with integer coefficient lengths $T$ satisfying $\log T=(S-\mu-\rho_r)V+O(H)$ and $S-\mu-\rho_r\ge0$. The $O(H)$ guard can be positive when equality holds. In the consecutive ladder use one correcting coefficient of width $(Z+A)^2e^{C_1H}$ per lower degree. The guards are chosen after fixing the required precision and the small congruence moduli. Each correction mixes its cyclic quotient to $O(e^{-C_1H})$ by the interval residue bound. For grouped gaps the two-coefficient translation argument gives the same estimate. First replace the higher correcting sum by uniform on $B_{j+1}/B_r$, then convolve with the degree-$j$ correcting law, and induct towards $B_s$. Total variation adds over at most $O(d)$ levels. After a coefficient congruence class modulo $M=e^{O(H)}$, divide each available length by $M$ and apply the same proof to the scaled lattices. For a consecutive digit of width $C_j$ this bounds the conditional error by $O(Mb_1b_2/C_j)$, which the guard absorbs along with the number of levels.

The physical size of the sum of all corrections is at most $mZ^r e^{O(H)}$ before reinstating $g$. The guard on $T$ can make this sum an arbitrarily small $e^{-CH}$ fraction of the smaller top singular width. Within a fixed $B_r$ coset, an integer translation in top basis coordinates changes the uniform top box by at most the ratio of the coordinate displacement to its shorter side. The half-open top parallelogram contains the same number of points in each coset. Combining this coupling with the quotient laws gives uniform on a translate of $B_s$ in that parallelogram to $e^{-CH}$ error, if the correcting blocks below $s$ are fixed and the blocks from $s$ to $r$ are averaged. The power-basis coefficient 1-norm and the sup norm on $|X|\le A$ of the added terms are $\exp((S+o(1))V)$, using $(1+Z)^{j+1}$ and $(Z+A)^{j+1}$, respectively. This is $o(X_{\rm sc}/K_1)$ and stays below the extractor coefficient-height envelope. The degree, leading coefficient, primitive congruences, protected roots, real-root brackets and original cofactors are preserved. ## Computing the true fixed-cofactor score on the ladder

The scalar estimate in the $B_s$ law has the condition
\[
                     S-\mu-\rho_s>\lambda/2.                 \tag{1}
\]
Divide the two physical coordinates by $n_i b_i^s$ before making the square panels. The normalized lattice then has just the $\Delta$ congruence, the side lengths have leading logarithm $(S-\mu-\rho_s)V$, and the condition number costs $e^{O(H)}$. Alignment with the normalized stride $M\Delta$ gives Cartesian residue panels with this same leading length. In physical coordinates each progression has stride divisible by $n_i b_i^s$. Under Dirichlet RH the sharp scalar count works at arbitrary moduli of $O(V)$ bit length once the number of terms exceeds the square-root threshold. All admitted marks are already units modulo $n_i$ because the cofactors are fixed and the grid includes its original coprimality check. After a protected or prescribed division, the corresponding primes have been omitted from the band. To reduce any other nonunit progression, only the factors of $b_i$, $g$ and the small stride $M\Delta$ are needed. They can be found by bounded trial division in $e^{O(H)}$ time. A forced square or a forced excluded prime kills the class. Otherwise divide by the forced squarefree product and delete its primes. This uses the actual progression, including all previously fixed coefficients. A one-mark term can insert a distinct dummy mark with its test identically one. A term with no smoothness mark uses just the finite residue state below. [Sharp scalar count](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md#a-sharp-count-for-long-progressions-at-arbitrary-moduli)

The fixed-cofactor score is the same separated expression as for the old affine family. Every term has at most two smooth marks per field and a bounded number of evaluations and derivatives modulo a common $M=e^{O(H)}$. The original root and band references use the small-prime totient truncation for their $q_a/\varphi(q_a)$ factors. Listing all residue tuples of a growing number of coefficients would cost too much. Instead form one additive state modulo $M$ for each of the term's required evaluations and derivatives. Add two coordinates for the residue of the evaluation shift in $B_s/MB_s$. In the normalized lattice these are $h_1$ and $(h_2-h_1)/\Delta$ for a shift $(h_1,h_2)$. All the state maps are integer linear functions of the active coefficient coordinates. Convolve the exact integer residue counts for the actual intervals, then divide by the product of interval lengths. The table has $M^{O(1)}$ entries and the state dimension is bounded independently of $d$. The integer counts have bit lengths at most the sum of the logarithmic interval sizes, $O(V)$ here. Fixed offsets simply translate the state. [Score weight computation](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md#computing-the-score-weights-without-auxiliary-factorizations)

To use one scalar panel calculation per state, subtract the interval origins and fix one half-open top parallelogram spanning $\lfloor T/M\rfloor$ periods in each top direction of $MB_r$. Conditional on all individual residues, replacing the top side lengths by these common floors changes total variation by $O(M/T)$ per side. Restoring the individual residue offsets moves the physical top box by at most $e^{O(H)}mZ^r$, including all lower residue offsets. For any realized correcting shift, reduce its top-basis coordinates into the first fundamental cell modulo $MB_r$. The comparison box is exactly the shifted coset inside the fixed parallelogram. Endpoint coupling bounds the incurred error by the normalized physical shift, which the top guard absorbs. Every $MB_r$ coset in the given affine $MB_s$ class contributes the same number of points. Quotient mixing therefore gives the conditional uniform law on that class inside the common parallelogram, determined by the state alone. The term's root predicates have their original state values and are never evaluated on a changed coefficient tuple. Consequently the score can be estimated to $e^{-CH}$ with $e^{O(H)}$ bit work. [Separated score and reference coefficients](cogentic-conditional-score.md#fixing-the-two-fields)

Start with the original broad affine $x,y$ boxes in addition to the ladder. Conditional on the entire ladder, the old moment proof applies because the fixed added polynomial has size $o(X_{\rm sc}/K_1)$ and the original Gaussian widths are unchanged. The conditional two-mark Gaussian centers move by linear interpolation combinations of the fixed shifts with coefficients at most $e^{O(H)}$, keeping the changes at most $\exp((S+o(1))V)$. This gives the initial $o(1)$ mean for the actual score. Bisect the longer affine side in each field, so the width ratio stays within $e^{O(H)}$ at the crossover. Above the crossover $Z^rT$ in affine units, partition the two top axes into $e^{O(H)}$ balanced panels. At two marks the fixed high contribution has integral affine interpolation. Its change within a panel, and the entire change of the correcting ladder, are small relative to the affine widths. After the residue split freeze these evaluation shifts by interval translation and use the old affine scalar estimate. The guarded top widths can absorb both the paneled class split and the lower correction drift. Below crossover, partition the two affine axes to make their physical contribution small relative to the top parallelogram. Their shifts lie in $B_0$, and two coefficient pairs in the same affine residue class differ by a shift in $MB_0$. The ladder already fills every required quotient and the affine axes need only be averaged as small translations. Enumerate the two affine residue sets explicitly even if an axis has length less than $M$. Condition (1) with $s=0$ gives strict scalar margin throughout the crossover. This permits both original affine axes to reach singletons without a lower affine correction floor. [Broad and narrow boxes](cogentic-high-degree-score-descent.md#two-estimates-along-one-descent)

For the consecutive ladder, next fix the degree-$s$ coefficient by enumerating its $e^{O(H)}$ possible values and estimating the score conditional on each, using the remaining $B_{s+1}$ law. Condition (1) with $s+1$ suffices for this step. There are $O(V+d)$ decisions overall. The selected conditional mean rises by at most twice the chosen estimation error at each decision. It stays below one for large inputs, and the original score-to-extraction implication applies to every terminal tuple with score below one. This gives a guarantee for exhaustive enumeration of the remaining tuple, with the original relation and order checks. The argument uses the original analytic moment and extraction hypotheses.

## Width tradeoffs and the remaining scan

For a fixed number $q$ of grouped gaps from zero to $r$, use approximately equal gaps and set $\rho_r=q(S-\mu)/(q+1)$. One can realize any such positive $\rho_r$ by choosing $r$ a sufficiently small fixed fraction of $d$ and adjusting the exponent in $Z=e^{O(H)}$. This gives $2q+2$ averaged axes per field after the affine descent. The maximum side has leading logarithm $(S-\mu)V/(q+1)$, while their product has leading logarithm $2(S-\mu)V$. In particular one gap and fixed cofactors give four axes with maximum exponent arbitrarily close to $\lambda/4$ on the conservative schedule, versus $40000$ in the fixed $Z=A$ five-axis construction. The product remains above exponent $\lambda$ per field.

For consecutive levels take $0\le (S-\mu)V-r\log Z=O(H)$ and a sufficiently large one-third top guard. For example choose $\log Z\asymp H$ with a large enough constant and round $r$ down from $(S-\mu)V/\log Z$. Then all axes individually have length $e^{O(H)}$. Fixing successive low levels can reach any fixed $\rho_s<S-\mu-\lambda/2$. For $S$ close to $\lambda$ this freezes a fraction approaching $(\lambda/2-\mu)/(\lambda-\mu)$ of the consecutive ladder. The raw remaining product has logarithm $2(S-\mu-\rho_s)V$ plus guard costs $O((r-s)H+H)$ and a contribution $O((r-s)\log(1+A/Z))$. The per-degree guard costs are on the $V$ scale when $r\asymp d$. Their leading coefficient can be made arbitrarily small by decreasing the fixed ratio $r/(V/H)$ and increasing $\log Z/H$ to keep the same magnification. The correction mixing guard can be chosen independently of $Z$ in the consecutive case, since $(Z+A)^2$ already dominates the quotient order and the score term modulus depends only on the fixed moment predicates. The top guard may depend on $Z$ and occurs in only two axes. Thus this construction can approach the same raw exponent $\lambda$ per field, with more and shorter coordinates.

The small maximum side does not itself imply a smaller one-intercept sweep. On the terminal $B_s$ parallelogram, each value of a single normalized mark has probability at most $\exp(-(S-\mu-\rho_s+o(1))V)$ under the comparison law. One slice contains at most the longer projected width divided by $\Delta$, up to one, whereas the total lattice count is the parallelogram area divided by $\Delta$. The aspect ratio is $e^{O(H)}$. If a sampler must fool every unary mark test to fixed error below $1/2$, test membership in the mark projection of its own support. The total-variation error in the comparison law gives the same required support exponent $S-\mu-\rho_s$, which exceeds $\lambda/2$ by (1). The old one-intercept sampler uses the affine coefficient pairing to attain the $\lambda/2$ exponent. The ladder supplies neither that pairing in its short coordinates nor a lower support bound for the specific arithmetic smoothness tests. ## Exact modular selection on all remaining axes

For a score term let the additive state space be $G=(\mathbb Z/M)^k$. Encode the states in base $M$. For an axis with interval $[a,a+L)$ and column $v$, partition $G$ into orbits of translation by $v$. Each orbit has length $t=M/\gcd(M,v_1,\ldots,v_k)$. Write $L=qt+b$. If $c_i$ are the old counts in cyclic orbit order, the new count at position $i$ is
\[
q\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}c_j+\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}c_{i-a-j}.
\]
Advancing $i$ changes the second sum by one addition and one subtraction. An entire interval therefore costs $O(k M^k)$ arithmetic operations, including the orbit enumeration, irrespective of its length. The counts have at most $1+\log_2\prod_j L_j$ bits. Use integer divided differences for the two-mark quotient coordinate before reducing modulo $M$, so the formula also works when the mark difference and $M$ share a prime.

For any sum of signed rational tables on such states, evaluate each term at its own modulus and add the resulting box totals. Bisect one coefficient interval and compute the left total. Subtraction gives the right total. The child with smaller mean has mean at most its parent's, so after at most $\sum_j\lceil\log_2 L_j\rceil$ decisions the singleton's modular score is at most the original average. All divisions in the comparisons can be cleared exactly. This statement concerns terms whose values are determined by their modular state. The actual score also tests squarefree smoothness of the integer values.

The replay counts the 3,360 tuples above at moduli 6 and 7, compares every state to direct enumeration, and selects an actual tuple for a signed two-modulus score. It also checks an interval longer than $10^{50}$ along a nonprimitive direction.
