# Common cofactors and sparse coefficient steps

The integer-mark Cogentic construction can fix the same cofactor for every target prime in a nominal real-root grid, with no cofactor exclusions at any mark or prime in the band. A separate change spaces the coefficient trials by a large power of two. Under the uniform Dirichlet-RH window estimate this reduces the conservative one-intercept search from $\exp((100000+o(1))V)$ to $\exp((50000.5+\epsilon+o(1))V)$ for any fixed $0<\epsilon<49999.5$, where $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$ and $V=(\log N)^{2/3}(\log\log N)^{1/3}$. The sparse step preserves the physical Gaussian width and the local row model. [Fixed schedule](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) · [One-intercept search](cogentic-one-intercept.md)

## One exact cofactor per real-root grid

Let $Q(X)$ be a nominal rational polynomial of degree $d$ with integral leading coefficient $c_d$, as in Lemma 8.1, and $q_*=Q(m)/N$. Use the source's $P_0$ and assume $\gcd(P_0,N)=1$ and $\gcd(N,m-a)=1$ at every grid mark $a$. Form the positive integer
\[
 R=\prod_{p\le B}p\;\prod_{a\in\mathcal A}|m-a|.
\]
Its bit length is $\exp(O(H))$ in the fixed schedule. Among the integers $k\equiv N^{-1}\pmod{P_0}$, start immediately above $q_*$ and take the first with $\gcd(k,R)=1$. This takes $\exp(O(H))$ bit work without factoring $R$. Indeed, for each prime $p\mid R$ coprime to $P_0$, the progression index excludes one residue modulo $p$. By CRT a common translation of the index turns these residues into zero. Iwaniec's Jacobsthal bound then gives a valid index after at most $O((1+\omega(R))^2\log^2(2+\omega(R)))$ trials. Primes dividing $P_0$ are already avoided by the selected residue. Each trial uses one integer gcd. In particular $|k-q_*|/P_0=\exp(O(H))$, and the cofactor is positive for sufficiently large inputs in the fixed schedule. The construction is independent of the target prime. [Iwaniec, *On the problem of Jacobsthal*](https://doi.org/10.1515/dema-1978-0121)

For a target prime $\ell>2d+3$ coprime to $P_0Nc_d$, prescribe a polynomial modulo $\ell$ of leading coefficient $c_d$ and value $kN$ at $m$. A completely split polynomial with simple roots is obtained by fixing $d-2$ distinct nonzero offsets from $m$ and making the product of the final two offsets equal the required nonzero residue. At most $2(d-2)+2$ nonzero choices for the first of those two offsets are forbidden by the earlier offsets and the equality of the final two. A root-free polynomial is obtained by multiplying irreducible degree-two and degree-three factors in $X-m$, with one factor's constant term chosen to give the specified value. For $j=2$ or $3$ every nonzero constant term occurs in a monic irreducible degree-$j$ polynomial. The norm fiber in $\mathbb F_{\ell^j}^\times$ has more than $j$ elements and at most $j$ come from $\mathbb F_\ell$. Since $j$ is prime, an element outside $\mathbb F_\ell$ gives the requisite degree and its norm determines the constant term. Brute force over the at-most-cubic coefficients and roots costs $\operatorname{poly}(\ell,d)$.

Round the lower coefficients of $Q$ to the nearest allowed residues modulo $M=P_0\ell$, combining the prescribed reduction with the original binary irreducible and constant-one odd-prime reductions. The rounded polynomial $F$ has $F(m)\equiv kN\bmod M$. Correct $F$ by $M$ times the signed radix-$m$ expansion of $(kN-F(m))/M$ with its last digit unrestricted. This yields a polynomial with value exactly $kN$, the same leading coefficient and all prescribed reductions. Since $N<m^d$ and $|F(m)-Q(m)|=O(Mm^{d-1})$, the lower $d-1$ digits have magnitude below $m$ and the last has magnitude at most $O(1+m|k-q_*|/M)$. The total coefficient error is $O(m(M+|k-q_*|))=m\exp(O(H))$. It is negligible in the fixed schedule's real perturbation bounds, since $dA^{d-1}m\exp(O(H))$ has leading logarithm $10001V$ and the marked values have leading logarithm $100001V$. The same construction installs any finite batch of compatible target reductions by replacing $M$ by the product modulus and retaining the explicit error bound above. For a simultaneous batch, the larger progression modulus must still be checked in the analytic estimates.

This preprocessing removes cofactor-dependent mark filtering and band omissions when applying the fixed-cofactor moment proof. It does not provide the cofactor averaging used in the shortened-window calculations. In fact the following exact divisibility obstructs transporting one known smoothness table between small cofactor intervals. If two integer polynomials agree at distinct marks $a_1,\ldots,a_j$ and their evaluations at $m$ differ by $N\Delta k$, then
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^j(m-a_i)\mid\Delta k.
\]
The polynomial difference is divisible over $\mathbb Z[X]$ by the monic product of the corresponding linear factors, and the value of that product at $m$ is coprime to $N$. In a cofactor interval of width $\exp((\kappa+o(1))V)$ with $\kappa<1$, no nonzero change of cofactor can preserve even one of these marked values, since $m-a=\exp((1+o(1))V)$. At fixed cofactor, same-leading-coefficient degree-$d$ polynomials agreeing at $d-1$ marks must coincide, since their difference also vanishes at $m$.

## Spacing the coefficient trials by a power of two

Retain the conservative schedule's $U=2^{\lfloor(100000+o(1))V/\log 2\rfloor}$, nominal height, field construction and $K_1$. Use the original two cofactors or the common ones above. Take $D_2=2^J$ with $J\log 2=(t+o(1))V$, $0<t<49999.5$, and write $U'=U/D_2$. Replace the perturbation step $g=P_0\ell$ by $g'=gD_2$ and take Gaussian widths $S_1'=U'$, $S_0'=U'A$ and centers $L_0^2S_j'$. The product of the step with each center and each width agrees exactly with the old product. The entire new coefficient search is a sublattice of the original coefficient search, so the same height and real-root guards apply. The perturbation evaluations and their first- and second-point Gaussian widths are in the same physical units as before.

Every marked polynomial value is odd by its irreducible binary reduction. The only added prime power in a progression modulus is therefore always coprime to its residue. Also $q/\phi(q)$ is unchanged because $2$ already divides the original step. No new prime is deleted from the smooth band. All auxiliary root and derivative restrictions are at odd primes coprime to $D_2$, so their ranks and CRT probabilities are unchanged. The exact Poisson projection estimates in Lemma 9.4 have error $O(e^{-U'})$ since $U'\ge4B^4$ for large inputs. The integer moduli in the one- and two-point windows and the auxiliary congruence splits are at most $g' m A B^{O(1)}$. This follows from the first-step modulus $g'|m-a|$ and the second-step modulus $g'|m-a'|\,|a-a'|Q_{\rm free}/Q_{\rm both}$ before cancellation of any prescribed factors. In divided classes the gcd excludes $2$ and is bounded by the original $AB^3$.

The window heights still satisfy $\log Y=(100001+o(1))V$ and $\log(Y/S)=O(H)$, including the second point and divided classes. The nonprincipal term in the [uniform Gaussian estimate](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md#a-gaussian-distribution-in-progressions) is therefore at most
\[
 q(Y/S)Y^{-1/2+\delta}
 \le \exp\bigl((-49999.5+t+o(1))V\bigr).
\]
The far-principal term has the original margin. The saddle, its normalization and all local case factors are unchanged. For arbitrary distinct pairs use this same Gaussian estimate after the gcd division instead of the source's rough Rankin bound in Lemma 9.4, which was written in terms of the old coordinate scale. The bound $G\le AB^3$ and the saddle-ratio comparison at $Y/G$ are unchanged, so this gives the polynomial loss in the [retuned arbitrary-pair bounds](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md). The rest of the centered moment and peeling proof uses the projected one- and two-point laws, the unchanged row-event probabilities and the same geometric grid. These inputs give the same sufficient rank probability $1-o(1)$. [Source Lemmas 9.4–9.5 and Proposition 10.1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=48)

Under the Dirichlet RH used by the Gaussian and dense-prime estimates, the one-intercept sampler applies with torus size proportional to $L_0U'A$ and with $U'A/U'=A$ as before. Its three short lists and the extraction per tuple still cost $\exp(O(H))$. The random draw is only over the first intercept. Enumeration replaces this draw entirely, and the short lists remain explicitly deterministic. The resulting worst-case cost is $U'\exp(O(H))$, including the sweep of target primes and verified splitting. Use the dense-prime individual order chains and trial division through $B$, so no index least common multiple is needed. For bounded sizes or failed guards interleave a verified trial division fallback. Complete recursion increases the bound by at most a logarithmic factor. For the generic schedule with $\log U\sim vV$, $\log m\sim V/x$, the same argument allows $0<t<(v-1/x)/2$ and leaves width exponent just above $(v+1/x)/2$. Optimizing the previous integer-grid ledger already takes $v$ arbitrarily close to $1/x$, so this does not give a better limiting constant there. Nor does it bring the final width down to $\exp(O(H))$.

The [PARI/GP replay](../experiments/cogentic-common-cofactors-sparse-steps.gp) checks the first admissible cofactor and all four constructed fields at two target primes, the binary irreducibility and exact value at $m$, and the widened-step identities on 80 marked classes and 760 two-point scales. Run `gp -fq < experiments/cogentic-common-cofactors-sparse-steps.gp` from the repository root. These are arithmetic consistency checks for the finite setup, independent of the asymptotic window bounds.
