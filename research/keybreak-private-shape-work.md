# Charging descents only after a private shape passes both tests

Under the hypotheses of KeyBreak's Appendix H, its single-input GRH schedule admits a bit-time bound $L_N[1/3,2.813]$ after a small-prime pass, a cap on the number of *launched* shapes, and fast extraction. Keeping its original extraction gives coefficient $2.85+o(1)$ in the exponent instead. This is a manuscript-level consequence of its relation and smooth-order theorems. It uses the published factor-base, private distributions and descent parameters. The calculation below refers to the November 26 preprint with SHA-256 `2b534eaf77fb2b9712018879059d8d4667c40c0a4adf2fff90efc22fb356cfc9`. [KeyBreak, Appendix H](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=128)

## Counting the launches

Write $L=\log N$, $D=(L/\log L)^{1/3}$, $H=D\log L$, $V=D H$, $d\sim3D/4$ and $B=\exp((19/20+o(1))H)$. A fresh private block is a uniform $n$-bit integer, where $n$ is the bit length of $N$. An out-of-range draw is rejected; a gcd may already split $N$. For a unit $h$, the public square $R=h^2\bmod N$ determines the shortest-vector computation, including its tie rule. A launch needs nonzero, unit coordinates $(u,v)$ with $u=Rv\pmod N$ and $|u|,|v|\le X=\lceil\sqrt{2N}\rceil$ after normalization. In particular both coordinates must be $Y_0$-smooth. Every pair determines at most one residue $R=u/v$. For odd $N$, each unit square has exactly $2^{\omega(N)}$ unit roots, including prime powers. Hence, before any descent,
$$
 \Pr(\text{launch})\le\frac{4\,2^{\omega(N)}\Psi(X,Y_0)^2}{2^n}.
$$
This upper bound holds for every fixed field and every prior public history at the start of a fresh draw. It permits all smooth coordinate pairs and hence covers the narrower squarefree band and cardinality checks in (H.18). KeyBreak's Lemma H.5 supplies a separate *lower* bound for shapes with good parents. [Shape selection and root multiplicities, §H.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=135)

Here $\log Y_0\sim(5/9)\log M\sim(20/27)V$ by Proposition G.13. Put $w=\log X/\log Y_0\sim27D/40$. The elementary Rankin bound gives $\log(\Psi(X,Y_0)/X)\le-w\log w+o(H)=-(9/40+o(1))H$. In detail, take $s=1-\log(w)/\log Y_0$; $\Psi(X,Y_0)\le X^s\prod_{p\le Y_0}(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$. Higher prime powers cost $O(1)$ in the logarithm since $s\to1$. For the first powers, partial summation with the Chebyshev upper bound costs $O(\log\log Y_0+w)$: in $\int e^{(1-s)t}\,dt/t$ split at $t=1/(1-s)$ and bound the remaining integral by $O(w)$. This is $o(H)$. For $\omega(N)=o(H)$ the resulting launch probability is at most $\exp(-(9/20+o(1))H)$.

## Ensuring the count suffices on every input

After elementary preprocessing, add a bounded first-split pass on an odd composite which is not a prime power. Use the trial division and curve stages in the **proof** of Proposition H.3 with $y=2^{d^2\lceil\sqrt{m_b}\rceil}$, where $m_b$ is the bit length of $n$. Stop on the first verified proper factor. Its worst-case bit work is $\exp(o(H))$ since $\sqrt{\log y\log\log y}=O(D(\log L)^{3/4})$. If any prime $p\mid N$ is at most $y$, the pass succeeds with probability $1-o(1)$ under RH: trial division covers $p\le z_y$ and the H.3 proof bounds success in each curve trial for a chosen remaining $p\le y$ independently of the size of the other prime divisors. The applicable statements are the CRT split argument and trial cap (H.13)–(H.14); the weaker smooth-input guarantee in the proposition's statement alone would not justify this pass. If every prime exceeds $y$, then $\omega(N)\le L/\log y=O(D\sqrt{\log L})=o(H)$ uniformly. The algorithm does not need to decide which case holds. [Useful-prime argument and trial cap](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=132)

The program still allows all $R_{\rm shape}=\exp((7/5+o(1))H)$ raw attempts. End the epoch on the first proposed launch beyond $(C+1)2^{\lceil t/10\rceil}$, with $C$ its column bound and $t=dm_b$. This cap has coefficient $19/20+3/40$. In the large-prime case the expected number of launches in all raw slots, even with virtual draws after stopping, is at most $\exp((19/20+o(1))H)$. Markov bounds the chance that the new cap changes the epoch by $\exp(-(3/40+o(1))H)$. In the other case the preliminary pass already succeeds with probability $1-o(1)$. The cap examines only public shape acceptances, hence the hidden-sign argument is preserved. One-sided predicate errors can use the original confidence caps. Consequently the per-epoch conditional success for sufficiently large nonterminal inputs is at least $1/512-o(1)$, uniformly after previous failed epochs. Trial division between failed epochs and recursion on verified factors give termination on every tape as in Theorem H.9.

For clarity, the cubic number-field root and sequential-product estimates in Table 2 must also be replaced. All selected product and coefficient bit lengths have a common cap $L_0'=\exp((19/20+o(1))H)$ from (H.30), as does the occurrence count. With fast integer multiplication and exact polynomial reduction in the monic degree-$d$ order, sequential expansion costs at most $(L_0')^{2+o(1)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$ even if every occurrence is charged a full expanded multiplication. The same estimate covers signed rational factors, verification and modular reduction. Keep KeyBreak's integral target $\Gamma$ with its derivative clearing factor and its explicit root coefficient bound (E.18).

For nonzero $\Gamma$, find an odd prime $p$ dividing neither $\operatorname{disc}(g)$ nor $|\operatorname{Nm}\Gamma|$. Their logarithms total at most $L_0'\operatorname{poly}(n)$ by the determinant height bound. Chebyshev's lower bound for the prime product gives a suitable $p=O(L_0'\operatorname{poly}(n))$. Scan and certify primes, testing coprimality modulo $p$ by polynomial gcd with $g$. Even a full read of $\Gamma$ per candidate fits $(L_0')^{2+o(1)}\operatorname{poly}(n)$. The zero target is a direct case. Factor $g\bmod p$ and find both square roots of the unit target in each irreducible component using finite-field splitting with output verification. Standard independent capped factor splits at odd $p$ give negligible abort probability with polynomial overhead in $n,d,\log p$; nonsquares are recognized by the residue-field quadratic character. Recombine the at most $2^d$ residue roots. Each lifts uniquely by Newton iteration in the finite free unramified algebra because twice the root is a unit. Lift past twice the power-basis coefficient bound from (E.18), center and verify each candidate exactly. The clearing factor guarantees an integral power-basis root on successful square transcripts. All branches, even with quadratic bit arithmetic in the expanded precision, cost $(L_0')^{2+o(1)}2^d\operatorname{poly}(n)$ and $\log(2^d)=o(H)$. Thus this part has coefficient at most $1.90$. The largest remaining coefficient is that of the private coordinate tests.
