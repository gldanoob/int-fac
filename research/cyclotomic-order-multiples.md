# Deterministic splitting from one cyclotomic multiple

Fix an integer $k\ge3$. Let $N$ have at least two distinct prime divisors, and let $M>0$ be divisible by $\Phi_k(p)$ for one unknown prime $p\mid N$. Under the uniform zero-free hypothesis below, the following deterministic search returns a proper divisor of $N$ in polynomial time in $\log N+\log M$. The exponent of the time bound may depend on $k$. Repeated prime divisors and arbitrary extra factors in $M$ are allowed.

For $k=3$ this removes both congruence restrictions in the [conductor-seven proof](cubic-order-multiples.md). For example, that field is inert at both primes of $85=5\cdot17$, and its projective group at each prime is killed by $31\cdot307$. The new search can split 85 even with that common exponent. The theorem gives a split on the supplied promise. Producing a suitable $M$ from an arbitrary $N$ remains open.

For a balanced semiprime and a polynomially bounded multiple, a different argument removes the analytic hypothesis. In particular, an unfactored multiple of $p^2+p+1$ up to $N^2$ suffices for polynomial-time recovery on balanced pairs. [Intersection lattice and exact replay](hidden-divisor-lattices.md)

## The search uses integer polynomials

Write $d=M/2^{v_2(M)}$. At heights $T=1,2,4,\ldots$, run both steps.

1. Try all integer divisors from $2$ through $\min(T,N-1)$, returning any proper divisor.
2. For every monic $f\in\mathbb Z[X]$ of degree $2\le s\le k$ with its other coefficients in $[-T,T]$, compute $X^d\bmod(f,N)$. Return the gcd of $N$ and its $s-1$ nonconstant coefficients whenever this gcd is strictly between $1$ and $N$.

At fixed $k$ a round costs $O_k((2T+1)^k\,\mathrm{poly}(\log N+\log M+\log(2T)))$ bit operations. There is no irreducibility test. Every returned gcd is valid. We prove that some successful round has $T\le(2+\log N)^{O_k(1)}$. In the proof, call any quantity bounded this way *small*. If a prime divisor of $N$ divides a nonzero small integer, trial division already succeeds at a small height. This observation will cover all exceptional denominators and indices.

## Choosing a field in which the primes behave differently

The analytic input is a fixed $\delta>0$, independent of the fields, such that their Dedekind zeta functions have no zeros in $\Re(s)>1-\delta$. Frei, Sofos and Widmer, Corollary 1.2, then gives prime ideals with prescribed Frobenius in an interval $(x/2,x]$ once $x\gg_\delta(\log |D_L|)^{1/\delta}(\log\log |D_L|)^{2/\delta}$ for a Galois extension $L/K$. Its lower count is $|C|x/(4|G|\log x)$ for a conjugacy class $C$. Their bound is uniform in the degree. Cureton and Thorner, Theorem 1.1, reports the required strip with $\delta=1/32$ for all number fields. The proof here uses that number-field statement, which is stronger in scope than a result for rational Dirichlet characters alone. [Frei–Sofos–Widmer, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18491v2#S1.SS1) · [Cureton–Thorner, version 2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3)

Choose a second prime $q\ne p$ dividing $N$. Trial division covers primes dividing $2k$. Over $F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2k})$, the classes of $p,q$ generate a copy of $(\mathbb Z/k)^2$ in $F^\times/F^{\times k}$, by valuations at primes over $p$ and $q$, both unramified. In $L=F(p^{1/k},q^{1/k})$ choose the automorphism over $F$ multiplying $p^{1/k}$ by a primitive $k$th root and fixing $q^{1/k}$. Kummer theory gives this automorphism. The extension is Galois over $\mathbb Q$. Its degree is bounded in terms of $k$, and $\log|D_L|=O_k(1+\log N)$ by the discriminant of the order with product basis from $F$, $X^k-p$, and $X^k-q$.

Chebotarev gives a small rational prime $\ell$ in the chosen conjugacy class. Thus $\ell\equiv1\pmod{2k}$, the class of $p$ generates $\mathbb F_\ell^\times/\mathbb F_\ell^{\times k}$, and $q$ is a $k$th power modulo $\ell$. These conditions hold for every conjugate of the chosen automorphism. In the degree-$k$ subfield $K$ of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_\ell)$, $p$ is inert and $q$ splits completely. This field is totally real since $-1$ is a $k$th power modulo $\ell$. Its discriminant is $\ell^{k-1}$ by the conductor–discriminant formula. This use of a varying cyclotomic field follows the conductor selection in Bach and Shallit. The search above never constructs $K$. [Bach–Shallit, §§6–8](https://www.ams.org/journals/mcom/1989-52-185/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1.pdf)

## A bounded algebraic integer with prescribed quadratic signs

Put $\mathfrak m=pq\mathcal O_K$. A sign character here is a homomorphism $(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m)^\times\to\{\pm1\}$ trivial on rational residue units. For every nontrivial such character $\chi$ there is an algebraic integer with sign $-1$ whose conjugates all have small absolute value, unless trial division already succeeds.

First obtain an integral $z$ of small absolute norm with $\chi(z)=-1$. A global unit with this sign suffices. Otherwise the ray exact sequence induces a nontrivial character on $H=\ker(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\to\mathrm{Cl}(K))$ because $H$ is the quotient of the residue units by the image of global units. The kernel of this character, regarded as a subgroup of the full ray group, defines an abelian extension $E/K$ of degree $2h_K$, with $h_K$ the ordinary class number. Minkowski's bound represents each ideal class by an integral ideal of norm $O_k(\sqrt{|D_K|})$. There are polynomially many sublattices of bounded index in fixed rank, as one sees from Hermite normal form. Thus $h_K$ is small. The extension has finite conductor dividing $\mathfrak m$, so
$$
\log |D_E|\le 2h_K(\log |D_K|+k\log(pq)).
$$
This is small even though $|D_E|$ itself may be large. [Milne, Chapter V, ray exact sequence](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=159) · [Existence theorem](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=167) · [Conductor–discriminant formula](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CFT.pdf#page=174)

Apply the same Chebotarev bound over $K$ to the nonidentity element of $H/\ker\chi$. Increase $x$ within a polynomial in $2+\log |D_E|+h_K$ so its lower count exceeds $2k$. A prime ideal with this Frobenius then exists away from $pq$. Its ordinary ideal class is trivial and it has small norm. Any integral generator $z$ has the prescribed sign, since the character was trivial on global units.

Small norm alone allows large conjugates. Write $b=|\mathrm{Nm}(z)|$, choose $C\ge1$ with $C^k>\sqrt{|D_K|}$ and $C^k\ge b$, with $C$ small. In the $k$ real embeddings put
$$
w_i(t)=|z_i|^t/b^{t/k},\quad 0\le t\le1,\qquad \prod_i w_i(t)=1.
$$
Subdivide the path finely enough that every adjacent ratio $w_i(t')/w_i(t)\le2$. At each point Minkowski gives a nonzero integral $a_t$ with $|(a_t)_i|\le Cw_i(t)$, since the box has volume $(2C)^k$ and the lattice covolume is $\sqrt{|D_K|}$. Use $a_0=1$ and $a_1=z$, both inside their boxes. The integer norm has magnitude between $1$ and $C^k$, so $|(a_t)_i|\ge w_i(t)/C^{k-1}$. For each adjacent pair
$$
y_t=|\mathrm{Nm}(a_t)|\,a_{t'}/a_t
$$
is integral and has all conjugates bounded by $2C^{2k}$. The factors telescope to $z$ times a rational integer with prime factors at most $C^k$. Trial division covers all failures of invertibility at $p,q$. The rational multipliers have trivial character, so some $y_t$ has sign $-1$. Only existence enters the running time. The algorithm enumerates neither the path nor units.

For two independent sign characters, the joint images of bounded elements span $\mathbb F_2^2$. Otherwise a nontrivial product of the characters would vanish on every bounded element, contrary to the preceding paragraph applied to each of the three products. Multiplying at most two such elements gives any specified sign pair, still with small conjugates.

## Projection to the cyclotomic order

Let $\sigma$ be Frobenius at $p$, a generator of $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)$, and set
$$
U(X)=(X^k-1)/\Phi_k(X)=\sum_i u_i X^i,\qquad
\beta=\prod_i \sigma^i(\alpha)^{u_i}.
$$
Here $\alpha$ will be a bounded integral unit modulo $pq$. At $p$ the image of $\beta$ in $\mathbb F_{p^k}$ is $\alpha^{U(p)}$, so its order divides $\Phi_k(p)$. If $k$ is not a power of two this order is odd, since $\Phi_k(p)\equiv\Phi_k(1)\equiv1\bmod2$. If $k=2^j$ with $j\ge2$, then $\Phi_k(p)=p^{2^{j-1}}+1\equiv2\pmod8$. Requiring $\alpha$ to be a square at $p$ makes $\beta$ a square in the image of the power map $u\mapsto u^{U(p)}$, hence of odd order there. The quadratic character imposing this requirement is trivial on $\mathbb F_p^\times$ because $k$ is even.

At $q$, label the $k$ components cyclically under $\sigma$. Take the product of the quadratic signs in two adjacent components of $\beta$. As a character of $\alpha$ its exponent vector is $(1+X)U(X)$ in $\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^k-1)$, up to cyclic relabeling. This vector is nonzero because its polynomial is monic of degree $k-\varphi(k)+1<k$ for $k\ge3$. The character is trivial on diagonal scalars and can take either sign by choosing the components separately. For power-of-two indices it is independent of the sign at $p$ by CRT. The bounded-element argument therefore gives $\alpha$ with the product of the two signs at $q$ equal to $-1$ and, when required, positive sign at $p$.

The odd part $d$ of $M$ now kills $\beta$ at $p$, whereas $\beta^d$ has mixed signs at $q$ and consequently is nonscalar. A rational integer $A=|\mathrm{Nm}(\alpha)|^{\sum_{u_i<0}|u_i|}$ clears the denominators of $\beta$. All conjugates of $\gamma=A\beta$ are small because $k$ is fixed, all conjugates of $\alpha$ are small, and their product is a nonzero integer. Thus $\gamma^d$ is scalar at $p$ and nonscalar at $q$, with small prime divisors of $A$ covered by trial division.

## Why the polynomial search finds it

Let $f$ be the minimal polynomial of $\gamma$. Its degree $s$ is in $[2,k]$ by the nonscalar reduction. Its coefficients and its nonzero discriminant are small by the bounds on its roots. Outside primes dividing that discriminant, the power basis of $\mathbb Z[\gamma]$ gives the full integer ring of $\mathbb Q(\gamma)$ locally. Reduction of this subfield's integer ring embeds into $\mathcal O_K/t\mathcal O_K$ at each rational prime $t$, since $t\mathcal O_K\cap\mathbb Q(\gamma)=t\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\gamma)}$. Hence for this $f$, every nonconstant coefficient of $X^d\bmod f$ vanishes modulo $p$, and at least one survives modulo $q$. If either prime divides the discriminant, the trial step already splits $N$ within the bound. In all cases a small height suffices, including when other prime divisors or repeated powers occur in $N$.

For index three, the norm-one projection above allows the coefficient search to enumerate only cubics of positive square discriminant with negative-cube constant. There is a deterministic $H^{1+o(1)}$ enumeration at height $H$. [Filtered search, running-time proof and implementation](cyclic-cubic-enumeration.md)

## One divisor sum for bounded multiplicities

Fix $B\ge2$. Suppose each original exponent $e=v_p(N)>0$ has $e+1$ divisible by some prime $s\le B$. One value $M=\sigma(N)$ suffices for complete deterministic polynomial-time factorization under the same analytic input. Indeed, $\Phi_s(p)\mid (p^{e+1}-1)/(p-1)\mid M$. At every composite cofactor with two distinct primes, run the searches for the finitely many indices in parallel, reusing the original $M$. For $s=2$ use the [one-prime $p+1$ splitter](p-plus-one-multiples.md), whose Dirichlet prime-coset input also follows from a fixed zero-free strip by Chebotarev in the corresponding bounded-index abelian extension. Handle primes, powers and factors of two directly. Every prime in a cofactor retains its original promise regardless of how previous gcds divided its multiplicity. In particular this covers all inputs of bounded prime multiplicity, including cubefree integers with an arbitrary mix of first and second powers.

Bach–Miller–Shallit give randomized complete factorization from a single divisor sum for arbitrary multiplicities. Bach–Shallit give randomized splitting from a cyclotomic multiple. Źrałek's deterministic cyclotomic theorem uses a smooth value and a suitable fixed field. Publication priority for the deterministic result above has not been established. [Bach–Miller–Shallit, Theorem 2](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/BMS86.pdf) · [Bach–Shallit](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1) · [Źrałek, Theorem 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=11)

## How often a fixed box succeeds

Fix $k\ge3$. Choose an ordered pair of distinct primes $p,q$ uniformly from $[X,2X]$. Let $B_k(T,X)$ be the fraction of pairs for which **some** positive multiple $M$ of $\Phi_k(p)$ gives no proper gcd in the polynomial box of height $T$ on $N=pq$. Unconditionally there are $a_k,b_k>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large fixed $T$,

$$
\limsup_{X\to\infty} B_k(T,X)\ \le\ \exp(-a_k T^{b_k}).
$$

Consequently any height $T(X)\to\infty$, however slowly, succeeds for all supplied multiples on a fraction tending to one. This last assertion follows by monotonicity, taking $X\to\infty$ first for each fixed height. It specifies no convergence rate in $X$. The multiple is still an input.

**Independent fields of bounded height.** For an integer $m$ choose distinct primes $\ell_i\equiv1\bmod 2k$ for $1\le i\le m$, all of size $m^{O_k(1)}$ by the prime number theorem in this fixed progression. Write $K_i$ for the cyclic degree-$k$ subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{\ell_i})$. The cyclotomic Chinese remainder theorem makes their compositum $K$ of degree $k^m$. A Gaussian period $\eta_i$ generates $K_i$ and all its conjugates have absolute value at most $\ell_i$. Its power-basis discriminant therefore has absolute value $m^{O_k(1)}$. Bach and Shallit use these fields, periods and group-ring projection in their search over conductors for a given input. Here the fields and elements are fixed before the two input primes vary. [Bach–Shallit, §§3–4 and 7](https://www.ams.org/journals/mcom/1989-52-185/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1.pdf)

At step $i$ choose an odd prime $r_i\equiv1\bmod\ell_i$ avoiding all the $\ell_j$, the discriminant of $\eta_i$, and the norms of the previously chosen elements. Such an $r_i$ has size $m^{O_k(1)}$ with an exponent independent of $i$. Indeed, choose an auxiliary prime $u\asymp m^3$ distinct from $\ell_i$. For each nonzero residue modulo $u$, Linnik's theorem gives a prime $\equiv1\bmod\ell_i$ in that residue of size $O((u\ell_i)^L)$ for an absolute $L$. These $u-1$ candidates are distinct. The construction in the next paragraph bounds each norm by a fixed power of $m$ whenever $r_j\ll(u\ell_j)^L$. Thus only $O_k(m\log(2m))$ candidate primes are excluded at any step, proving the bound by induction for sufficiently large $m$. [Xylouris, introduction and Theorem 1.1](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)

The prime $r_i$ splits completely in $K_i$. Modulo the square of one prime above $r_i$, prescribe $\alpha_i\equiv r_i$; modulo the squares of the other primes above it prescribe $\alpha_i\equiv1$. The ideal Chinese remainder theorem gives these residues modulo $r_i^2\mathcal O_{K_i}$. Since $r_i$ misses the power-basis index, take $\alpha_i$ in $\mathbb Z[\eta_i]$ with all $k$ coefficients in $[0,r_i^2)$. Its conjugates and its absolute norm have size $m^{O_k(1)}$.

Let $L_i$ be obtained from $K_i$ by adjoining square roots of the $k$ conjugates of $\alpha_i$. Each $L_i$ is Galois over $\mathbb Q$. Over $K L_1\cdots L_{i-1}$ the conjugates of $\alpha_i$ still have independent square classes. Indeed, $r_i$ is unramified in $K$ since it misses all conductors. The earlier square-root extensions are also unramified there since $r_i$ is odd and misses all earlier norms. Valuations at primes above the $k$ distinct primes of $K_i$ over $r_i$ therefore give the $k$ coordinate vectors modulo two. Each step has relative degree $2^k$ by Kummer theory. Hence the full normal compositum has degree $(k2^k)^m$ and its Galois group is the direct product of the $m$ groups $\operatorname{Gal}(L_i/\mathbb Q)$, each of order $k2^k$.

**Success in one field.** Put $\epsilon=1$ when $k$ is a power of two and $\epsilon=0$ otherwise. For $p$ inert in $K_i$, use its Frobenius generator $\sigma$ and the projected element $\gamma=\alpha_i^{U(\sigma)}$, where $U=(X^k-1)/\Phi_k$. As above, a rational integer clears denominators. Include the finitely many choices of $\sigma$ so the list is independent of $p$. Before clearing denominators, the multiplicative order of the projection at an inert prime divides $\Phi_k(p)$. For $\epsilon=0$ this is odd for odd $p$. For $\epsilon=1$ it has exactly one factor of two, and requiring $\alpha_i$ to be a square modulo $p$ makes the projected order odd. This square condition holds precisely when $\operatorname{Nm}(\alpha_i)$ is a square modulo $p$. On each Frobenius coset above a generator of $\operatorname{Gal}(K_i/\mathbb Q)$ exactly half the elements fix $\prod_j\sqrt{\alpha_{i,j}}$, a square root of that norm, because the $k$ signs vary independently. Thus the covered condition at $p$ has density $(\varphi(k)/k)2^{-\epsilon}$.

At a split $q$ the signs of the $k$ conjugates of $\alpha_i$ form a uniform vector in $A=\mathbb F_2[X]/(X^k-1)$, up to cyclic permutation. Projection multiplies it by $U$. The image has dimension $\varphi(k)$, since representatives $UV$ with $\deg V<\varphi(k)$ are distinct modulo $X^k-1$. Its intersection with the constant sign vectors has dimension $\epsilon$: the all-ones vector $(X^k-1)/(X-1)$ belongs to the image exactly when $X-1$ divides $\Phi_k$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Thus the conditional probability of mixed signs is $1-2^{-(\varphi(k)-\epsilon)}$. Clearing denominators multiplies all components by the same scalar and preserves mixedness. The odd exponent $d$ from the search gives a scalar at $p$ and a nonscalar at $q$. All these implications hold away from finitely many primes dividing the norms and power-basis discriminants for this fixed list of elements. Their minimal polynomials have height $m^{O_k(1)}$, since denominator clearing uses bounded powers of their original integral norms; mixed signs force degree at least two.

Chebotarev in the fixed compositum makes the Frobenius classes of a prime in $[X,2X]$ equidistribute with their class-size weights. Sampling the two primes gives the product measure in the limit since the excluded diagonal has vanishing relative size. The sufficient success events in the $m$ fields are consequently independent, each of probability

$$
c_k=\frac{\varphi(k)}{k^2}\, 2^{-\epsilon}\bigl(1-2^{-(\varphi(k)-\epsilon)}\bigr)>0.
$$

The list therefore succeeds for every permitted $M$ on a limiting lower fraction $1-(1-c_k)^m$. Choosing $m$ proportional to a sufficiently small fixed power of $T$ proves the bound. For $k=3$, $c_3=1/6$. An explicit conductor-seven element gives the mixed-sign fraction $3/4$ among the split primes. The regression enumerates every binary sign vector for $3\le k\le10$ and checks the number with constant projection. [Cubic witness](cubic-order-multiples.md) · [PARI/GP regression](../experiments/cyclotomic-multiples.gp)

**Uniform bound.** There are constants $a,b>0$ depending only on $k$ such that, for large $X$ and $T\ge1$,

$$
B_k(T,X)\ll_k \exp\bigl(-a\min(T^b,\log\log X)\bigr).
$$

Let $F=L_1\cdots L_m$, of degree $n=(k2^k)^m$. In a tower generating $F$ by the periods and the square roots above, there are $O_k(m)$ integral generators of relative degree at most $k$, each having all conjugates of size $m^{O_k(1)}$. The relative field discriminant divides the relative power-basis discriminant. Bounding all the conjugates of the latter by the product of squared root differences gives $\log |D_F|\ll_k n m\log(2m)$ by the tower formula. All possible ramified primes divide $2$, a conductor $\ell_i$ or an original norm. The extra primes excluded by the power-basis tests divide nonzero polynomial discriminants of size $m^{O_k(1)}$. All these primes lie below $X$ in the range used next.

Thorner and Zaman prove the unconditional classwise bound $\pi_C(2X,F/\mathbb Q)\ll(|C|/|G|)X/\log X$ when $\log X\gg(\log |D_F|)\log\log |D_F|$. Choose $m$ at most a sufficiently small constant times $\log\log X$ to meet this condition. The prime number theorem counts $\asymp X/\log X$ primes in $[X,2X]$. Thus each pair of conjugacy classes has probability at most an absolute constant times its product Haar probability, also after conditioning on the primes being distinct. Summing over all pairs failing the sufficient tests in every field bounds the miss probability by $O((1-c_k)^m)$. Take $m$ proportional to $\min(T^b,\log\log X)$ with a sufficiently small $b>0$ and proportionality constant. The implied constant covers bounded $T$. [Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 1.1 and the discriminant range immediately following it](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.09238#page=3)

Stopping the doubling search at height $(\log\log X)^{1/b}$, up to a constant factor, consequently misses at most $O_k((\log X)^{-a'})$ of the ordered pairs for some $a'>0$. Its mean number of polynomial tests is $O_k(1)$, even when $M$ is chosen separately for every pair. Indeed, reaching a box of height $2^j$ requires the preceding box to miss, and summing $O_k(2^{jk})\exp(-a2^{(j-1)b})$ gives a constant. This counts modular polynomial powers, each of which still incurs bit cost depending on $\log N+\log M$.

Under the uniform Dedekind zero-free strip used above, Frei–Sofos–Widmer's classwise interval upper bound holds already when $X\gg_\delta(\log |D_F|)^{1/\delta}(\log\log |D_F|)^{2/\delta}$. The same proof then replaces $\log\log X$ by $\log X$ in the uniform bound. Beyond height $O_k((\log X)^{1/b})$, the fraction reaching any further box is $O_{k,\delta}(X^{-a''})$ for some $a''>0$. The worst-case height proved above bounds the remaining number of tests by a polynomial in $\log X$, giving $O_{k,\delta}(1)$ mean polynomial tests for the full search including its trial-division step. [Frei–Sofos–Widmer, Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18491v2#S1.SS1)

### An unconditional bound using higher moments

An unconditional cutoff of height $(\log\log X)^{O_k(1)}$ can in fact make the missed fraction $O_{k,A}((\log X)^{-A})$ for **every** fixed $A>0$, with the same cutoff exponent for all $A$. For any fixed $\varepsilon>0$, a cutoff of height $(\log X)^\varepsilon$ gives the stronger bound $\exp(-c_{k,\varepsilon}(\log\log X)^2)$. The mean number of tests stays $O_{k,C}(1)$ through any height $(\log X)^C$ for fixed $C>0$.

Write $W=\log X$. Use $m\le W^D$ fields for fixed $D>0$, and let $h=\lfloor d_k\log W\rfloor$ with $d_k=1/(16\log(k2^k))$. A compositum $E$ of at most $2h$ of their normal fields has degree $n_E\le(k2^k)^{2h}\le W^{1/8}$ and $\log |D_E|\ll_k n_E h\log(2m)$ by the same tower bound. Thus $\log |D_E|\le W^{1/4}$ for sufficiently large $X$ depending on $k,D$. All primes in $[X,2X]$ also avoid the listed ramification primes and polynomial discriminants, which are bounded by a fixed power of $m$. Put $Q=\exp(W^{1/4})$. If $\zeta_E$ has a real zero $\beta>1-c/\log Q$ for sufficiently small absolute $c\le1/4$, Stark's normal-field threshold $1-1/(4\log |D_E|)$ puts that zero in a quadratic subfield. Its primitive Dirichlet character has conductor at most $|D_E|\le Q$ by the discriminant tower formula. Landau–Page gives at most one such character among *all* these subcomposita. If its field occurs, the corresponding quadratic character on the full direct product is nontrivial on some factor $i$. Every subcompositum omitting $L_i$ is fixed by that factor, so none can contain the exceptional quadratic field. Omit coordinate $i$ in the moment estimate. [Stark, Lemma 8](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01405166) · [Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, Lemma 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029#page=32)

For each nonempty remaining subcompositum, $\log(|D_E|n_E^{n_E})=O(W^{1/4})$, so Thorner–Zaman's effective Chebotarev Theorem 1.1 applies at $x=X,2X$. It gives the principal and possible exceptional terms with relative error

$$
O\!\left(\exp\!\left(-\frac{c'W}{\log(|D_E|n_E^{n_E})}\right)+
         \exp\!\left(-c'\sqrt{W/n_E}\right)\right).
$$

Any remaining exceptional zero satisfies $1-\beta\ge c/\log Q$, so its logarithmic-integral term relative to $\operatorname{Li}(x)$ is $O(\exp(-c'W/\log Q))$. The interval count therefore equals the class's group proportion times $\operatorname{Li}(2X)-\operatorname{Li}(X)$ with relative error $O(\exp(-W^{1/4}))$. The class proportion is at least $1/n_E$, so including the lower endpoint adds relative error $O(n_E W/X)$. Summing the interval counts over classes gives the same relative estimate for the total, which normalizes the class counts to probabilities. Under two independent prime draws, the product class probabilities have relative error of the same order. The diagonal has probability $1/\#\{p\in[X,2X]\}=O(W/X)$, so conditioning on distinct primes changes the expectation of any function bounded in absolute value by one by $O(W/X)$. [Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 1.1](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf#page=3)

Let $Y_i$ indicate success of the sufficient Frobenius test in field $i$, let $r\ge m-1$ count the retained coordinates, and write $Z=r^{-1}\sum_i(Y_i-c_k)$. Every term of the expansion of $Z^{2h}$ uses at most $2h$ coordinates and has magnitude at most one before the common normalization. Summing it against the product class probabilities and conditioning on distinct primes gives its independent Bernoulli expectation with absolute error $O(\exp(-W^{1/4}))$. There are $r^{2h}$ terms and the normalization is $r^{-2h}$, so the whole moment has the same absolute error. In the independent model, integrating the Hoeffding tail $2\exp(-2rt^2)$ for $|Z|\ge t$ bounds the moment by $2\,h!/(2r)^h\le(h/r)^h$. If the box misses, all the retained $Y_i$ vanish and $|Z|=c_k$. Applying Markov's inequality, using $r\ge m/2$ and $c_k^{-2h}=\exp(O_k(\log W))$, gives a missed fraction of at most

$$
\left(\frac{2h}{c_k^2m}\right)^h+\exp(-W^{1/5})\qquad(m\ge 2).
$$

Write the height bound for the family as $O_k(m^{s_k})$ with $s_k>0$. Taking $m=\lceil(\log W)^2\rceil$ gives height $O_k((\log W)^{2s_k})$ and first term $\exp(-\Omega_k(\log W\log\log W))$. For cutoff $W^\varepsilon$, take $m=\lfloor W^{\varepsilon/(2s_k)}\rfloor$. Its witness height is $O_k(W^{\varepsilon/2})$ and the first term is $\exp(-\Omega_{k,\varepsilon}((\log W)^2))$. In the doubling search, round up the first cutoff by at most a factor of two and use the last full box at height at most $W^\varepsilon$ for the second cutoff.

For the mean through height $W^C$, the earlier uniform bound gives $O_k(1)$ mean tests up to the crossover at height $O_k((\log W)^{1/b})$. Rounds between the crossover and the first cutoff cost at most a power of $\log W$ in total and occur on a fraction $O_k(W^{-a'})$. Rounds above the first cutoff cost at most $O_k(W^{Ck})$ and occur on a fraction smaller than every inverse power of $W$. The bounds hold when the supplied multiple is chosen separately for each ordered pair.

### A sharper height bound for cubics

For $k=3$ there are absolute constants $a,c>0$ such that the fraction of balanced ordered prime pairs on which some supplied multiple defeats the whole height-$H$ box satisfies
$$
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}B_3(H,X)\le\exp\!\left(-aH^2/\log(2H)\right),\qquad
 B_3(H,X)\ll\exp\!\left(-c\min\{H^2/\log(2H),\log\log X\}\right).
$$
The first bound holds for every sufficiently large fixed $H$, and the second is uniform for large $X$. For each fixed $A>0$, height $O_A(\sqrt{\log\log X\,\log\log\log X})$ makes the missed fraction $O_A((\log X)^{-A})$. One cutoff $O(\sqrt{\log\log X}\,\log\log\log X)$ works for every fixed $A$. These are estimates for an actual proper gcd, allowing the order multiple to depend on the input pair.

Choose primes $\ell\equiv53\pmod{120}$. The fixed-conductor construction supplies a totally real cubic field $F$ with discriminant $\ell f^2$ for $f\mid10$. At 3 its quadratic resolvent is inert and the field is unramified, so $3\mathcal O_F$ has decomposition type $(1,2)$. In the projection of $\mathcal O_F$ perpendicular to the scalar integers take a shortest nonzero vector and lift it to $\beta$. Its residues $(a,b)\in\mathbb F_3\times\mathbb F_9$ obey $b\ne a$, since otherwise $(\beta-a)/3$ would be integral with a shorter nonzero projection. Among the three translates $\beta-k$ with $k\equiv a\pmod3$ modulo nine, two have valuation exactly one at the linear prime, and all have valuation zero at the quadratic prime. In one of those classes choose $k$ within $9/2$ of a real conjugate. The resulting $\alpha$ has a monic cubic polynomial of height $O(\sqrt\ell)$ by the projected lattice bound and the elementary symmetric functions. Only the existence of this polynomial is needed by the box search. [Fixed-conductor fields and the projected lattice](cubic-resolvent-profiles.md#bounds-as-the-coefficient-height-grows)

For $m$ such primes the cubic splitting fields have joint Galois group $S_3^m$: the quadratic resolvents are independent and their distinct characters separate the order-three coordinates. This compositum is unramified at 3. For each field label the three conjugates of its generator. At a prime over 3 one conjugate has valuation one and the other two have valuation zero. Conjugating that prime by $S_3^m$ realizes every independent choice of which coordinate has valuation one in each block. Thus in any joint multiplicative relation modulo squares the parity exponents must be constant within each block, by changing just one index. Consequently the two-dimensional subspaces of even-weight triples in the blocks are jointly independent modulo squares. Adjoining the square roots of pair products gives a further degree $4^m$. For one field choose the signs of the three square roots so their product is the rational norm of $\alpha$. The group embeds in the even signed permutations of three coordinates, of order 24 and isomorphic to $S_4$. Comparing degrees gives the full product $S_4^m$ for the joint extension.

At an inert prime $p$ in one cubic, $\alpha^{p^2+p+1}$ reduces to a rational scalar by the finite-field norm. This occurs on one third of the Frobenius elements. At a second prime $q$, either of the following conditions makes every odd power nonscalar.

* If the cubic splits and its three roots have mixed quadratic signs, the signs still differ after an odd power. Three of the four lifts over the identity in $S_3$ give this condition.
* If the cubic has type $(1,2)$ and the norm of its quadratic root is a nonsquare modulo $q$, that root is a nonsquare in $\mathbb F_{q^2}$, whereas every rational scalar there is a square. For each transposition, its action on the square root of the corresponding pair product computes the norm's quadratic sign. Exactly two of the four lifts negate it.

These two $q$ conditions occur at $3+6=9$ of the 24 group elements. The odd part of any positive multiple of $p^2+p+1$ is still a multiple, so the modular coefficients give $p$ for every supplied multiple simultaneously. Discard the finitely many primes dividing a generator norm, polynomial discriminant or field discriminant. For a fixed list, the independent prime-pair Frobenius events in each block have probability $(1/3)(9/24)=1/8$ by Chebotarev. Taking all markers up to a sufficiently small constant times $H^2$ gives $m\gg H^2/\log(2H)$ and a limiting missed fraction at most $(7/8)^m$. [Chebotarev and splitting types](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/Lenstra-Chebotarev.pdf)

For uniformity take the first $m$ markers, all $O(m\log(2m))$. All cubic generators and square roots of pair products are integral and have conjugates bounded by $m^{O(1)}$ for $m\ge2$. The tower discriminant argument above gives $\log|D_E|\ll [E:\mathbb Q]h\log(2m)$ for any $h$-block compositum, with degree $24^h$. The same classwise prime bound and fixed-moment argument above apply with 24 in the degree bound and $1/8$ in the success probability. Specifically let $W=\log X$, $h=\lfloor(\log W)/(16\log24)\rfloor$. Taking $m=C_A h$ for a sufficiently large constant gives any fixed power $W^{-A}$ in the displayed moment estimate; taking $m=\lceil h\log(2h)\rceil$ gives every fixed power with one schedule. The required height is $O(\sqrt{m\log(2m)})$. Under the stated uniform Dedekind zero-free strip the uniform classwise bound also permits $\log X$ in place of $\log\log X$ in the exponential estimate. [Classwise prime estimates and higher moments](#an-unconditional-bound-using-higher-moments)

## A lower bound on the search height

For each fixed odd $k\ge3$ and arbitrarily large $N=pq$, the polynomial search needs height at least $c_k(\log N)^{1/k}$, even when $M=\Phi_k(p)$ exactly and $\gcd(M,N)=1$. This concerns the coefficient search above. With a promise of exact equality an integer root search in $\Phi_k$ would itself recover $p$.

Fix an integer height $T$. Take the splitting field $F$ of all monic polynomials in the box of degrees $2$ through $k$, together with the roots of unity of order $k(2^L-1)$, where $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\dots,k)$. Choose an unramified prime $p>\max(T,k,3)$ whose Frobenius is conjugate to complex conjugation. All the polynomials in the box split into factors of degree at most two over $\mathbb F_p$, since the residue fields of $F$ at $p$ have degree at most two. Also $p\equiv-1\pmod{k(2^L-1)}$. For odd $k>1$, $\Phi_k(-1)=1$, and $\Phi_k(1)$ is either 1 or a prime dividing $k$. Hence $M$ is odd and coprime to $p(p^2-1)$: common factors with $p+1$ divide $\Phi_k(-1)$, and a common prime with $p-1$ would divide $k$ and $p-1$, despite $p\equiv-1\pmod k$ and $k$ odd. Also $M\equiv1\pmod{2^L-1}$.

Choose an odd prime $q\equiv2\pmod M$ by also requiring $q\equiv1\pmod4$. Linnik's theorem gives $M<q\ll(4M)^{O(1)}$. We have $\gcd(M,q(q^L-1))=1$. In the algebra $\mathbb F_t[X]/f$, for $t=p,q$, every unit has order coprime to $M$. Indeed reduction modulo the nilradical leaves finite fields of degrees dividing 2 at $p$ and dividing $L$ at $q$. The kernel on units has $t$-power order. If $f(0)\ne0$, then $t>T$ makes $X$ a unit, and raising to $M$ is an automorphism on the quotient of the unit group by the scalar units. As $\deg f\ge2$, $X$ is nonscalar and remains so at both primes. If $f=X^s$, then $X^M=0$ at both primes for sufficiently large $p$. Otherwise $f(0)=0$ gives a nilpotent component and a nonzero unit component, so the power is nonscalar at both. Every tested polynomial therefore misses the split. [Xylouris on Linnik’s theorem](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/83078)

For the quantitative bound, the box has $O_k(T^k)$ polynomials. Adjoining their roots gives degree at most $O_k(1)(k!)^{O_k(T^k)}$. Each root has all conjugates bounded by $1+T$. In a tower adjoining the roots one at a time, each relative field discriminant divides that of the order obtained by adjoining the root. Every conjugate of the relative polynomial discriminant is bounded by $(2+2T)^{k(k-1)}$ from the root differences. The tower discriminant formula consequently gives $\log |D_F|\le [F:\mathbb Q] O_k(1+T^k\log(2T))$. Apply the same uniform Chebotarev interval bound above to choose $p>T$ with $\log p=O_{k,\delta}(T^k)$. Then $\log N=O_{k,\delta}(T^k)$ by the bound for $q$. Taking $T$ to infinity proves the assertion, with the constant depending also on the fixed zero-free strip. As an illustration, the entire height-two quadratic and cubic box misses $N=210133967=14489\cdot14503$ at $M=209945611=\Phi_3(14489)$.

### Balanced lower bounds at every index

For every fixed $k\ge2$ there are arbitrarily large **balanced** semiprimes $N=pq$, $p<q\le2p$, at which the exact input $M=\Phi_k(p)$ requires coefficient height at least $c_k(\log\log N)^{1/k}$. Under the uniform zero-free hypothesis from the search theorem, the bound strengthens to $c_{k,\delta}(\log N)^{1/k}$. These bounds apply to the specified coefficient scan with its trial-division step. They include even indices.

Fix a height $H$ and $L=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)$. Take the compositum of the splitting fields in the box and the cyclotomic field of conductor the lcm of the following integers: all $\ell^2$ for primes $\ell\le2L+1$, and the least power of two exceeding $(1+kH)^k$. Take an unramified completely split prime $p$. All tested polynomials split into linear factors at $p$, including possible repeated factors. Put $D=M/2^{v_2(M)}$ and $g=\Phi_k(1)/2^{v_2(\Phi_k(1))}$. The value $\Phi_k(1)$ is one or a prime dividing $k$. Since $p\equiv1$ at the prescribed prime squares, $\gcd(D,p(p-1))=g$. Among the primes at most $2L+1$, the only possible divisor of $D$ is $g$ when it is prime, with valuation one. The growing conductor ensures $p>(1+kH)^k$. Its degree is polynomial in $H$ at fixed $k$. Together with the box tower bounds above this gives degree $\exp(O_k(H^k))$ and log discriminant $\exp(O_k(H^k))$ for the compositum. Unconditional least-prime Chebotarev gives $\log\log p=O_k(H^k)$. Under the uniform strip use the interval bound above to get $\log p=O_{k,\delta}(H^k)$. [Least-prime Chebotarev bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.00508) · [Conditional interval theorem](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.18491v2#S1.SS1)

There is a prime $q\in(p,2p]$ with $\gcd(D,q(q^L-1))=g$. Here are the details of the sieve needed to keep the two primes balanced. Write $z=(\log p)^4$. Sieve the primes in $(p,2p]$ by all conditions $q^L\not\equiv1\pmod\ell$ for $2L+1<\ell\le z$. Each condition excludes $\rho_\ell\le L$ reduced classes, of density $v_\ell=\rho_\ell/(\ell-1)<1/2$. For $S=\sum v_\ell\le L\log\log z+O_L(1)$ the formal survival product is at least $e^{-2S}\gg_L(\log z)^{-2L}$. Apply the lower Bonferroni bound through an odd number $r$ of terms with $r$ a sufficiently large constant times $1+L\log\log z$. The independent product exceeds its truncation by at most $S^{r+1}/(r+1)!$, which is less than half the product for this choice. All squarefree moduli in the truncation are at most $z^r<p^{1/3}$, and the CRT multiplicity per modulus is at most $L^r=(\log z)^{O_L(1)}$. The Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for primes in progressions therefore gives a total error $O_L(p/(\log p)^3)$ by taking a sufficiently large fixed logarithmic saving before multiplication by $L^r$. The surviving prime count is $\gg_L p/(\log p(\log z)^{2L})$. Above $z$, there are $O_k(\log p)$ prime divisors of $D$. Each excludes at most $1+L(1+p/z)$ integers in the interval, including the possible $q=\ell$. Their total is smaller than the surviving count for all sufficiently large $p$. If $g$ is an odd prime, $g-1\mid L$ ensures $g\mid q^L-1$. This proves the gcd equality. [Bombieri–Vinogradov in residue classes, equation (1.1)](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X2500747X)

For a polynomial with nonzero constant, $X$ is a unit at both primes. Its projective order divides $p(p-1)$ at the completely split prime, and $q(q^L-1)$ at the other, including nilpotent parts since both primes exceed $k$. At both primes $X^D$ is therefore scalar exactly when $X^g$ is scalar. Every coefficient of $X^g\bmod f$ over the integers has absolute value at most $(1+kH)^g<p$. The two tests agree. With zero constant term a pure power of $X$ gives the same scalar outcome on both sides, and a mixed nilpotent and unit algebra gives a nonscalar power at both. Every probe misses and the trial step sees no divisor. Taking $H$ to infinity proves both height bounds.

### Exact scalar criterion when three divides the exponent

Let $p,q$ be distinct primes, $p\equiv1\pmod3$, $M=p^2+p+1$ and $\gcd(M,q(q^6-1))=3$. Suppose $q>\max(3,H^2+H)$. If $p\le H^2+H$ the height-$H$ box already splits $pq$. Indeed the intervals $[a^2-H,a^2+H]$ for $a=0,\ldots,H$ cover the integers from zero to $H^2+H$. Set $b=a^2-p$, so $0<|b|\le H$. The linear coefficient of $X^3$ modulo $X^2+aX+b$ is $a^2-b=p$. Thus $X^M$ is scalar at $p$. At $q$, $X$ is a unit and its order modulo the scalars divides $q(q^6-1)$. Its cube is nonscalar, hence its $M$th power is nonscalar as well.

For $p>H^2+H$ the **only** polynomials returning a proper gcd are nonpure cubics irreducible modulo $p$. Here a pure monic cubic means $X^3+c$. For $f(0)\ne0$, $X$ is a unit at both primes. At $q$ its projective order divides $q(q^6-1)$ since each residual field degree divides six and the nilpotent kernel has exponent dividing $q$. At $p$ a quadratic or reducible cubic instead has projective order dividing $p(p^2-1)$, again of gcd three with $M$. On these components the $M$th power is scalar exactly when the cube is scalar. For quadratics the nonconstant coefficient of $X^3$ is $a^2-b$. For cubics the two coefficients are the negatives of the linear and quadratic coefficients of $f$. Their zero tests agree over both primes and over the integers by the size bounds. An irreducible cubic at $p$ has $X^M$ scalar there by the finite-field norm. The other cases $f(0)=0$ give either a pure nilpotent power or a product with both a nilpotent and a unit component, with the same scalar outcome at both primes.

For $H\ge2$, the conductor-seven cyclic cubic $X^3-X^2-2X+1$ is in the box. A miss on every nonpure cubic forces $p\equiv\pm1\pmod7$ away from seven, and then $7\nmid M$. Arbitrarily large primes $q\equiv2\pmod M$ satisfy the required gcd condition since $2^6-1=63$ and $v_3(M)=1$. In particular any fixed box can miss on the $p\equiv1\pmod3$ branch: take sufficiently large $p$ splitting completely in the compositum of its splitting fields and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$, then choose $q$ in that progression. The exact norm-degree calculation applies to the fields with a nonpure representative. At height five all 213 fields have such a representative; this follows by comparing the two sets of reduced defining polynomials with and without $X^3+c$ in the box enumeration. ### Matching the two primes even when cubics stay irreducible

Write $h(t)=t^2+t+1$. Suppose $p,q>H^2+H+3$ are distinct primes on the same branch modulo three, $h(q)\mid h(p)$, and $\gcd(h(p),q(q^2-1))=g$, where $g=1$ on the branch $2$ and $g=3$ on the branch $1$. At an irreducible cubic the $h(p)$th power of $X$ is scalar at either prime by the norm. At a reducible cubic or quadratic the projective unit exponent divides $t(t^2-1)$ at $t=p,q$, and its gcd with $h(p)$ is $g$ at both. Hence the scalar condition for a unit reduces to $X^g$ being scalar. At $g=1$ this never holds. At $g=3$ it holds for a cubic precisely when the polynomial is pure, and for a quadratic precisely when $a^2-b=0$ in the notation above. Zero constant terms give the same outcomes at both primes. Consequently a full box misses exactly when the two primes have identical irreducibility bits on every cubic at $g=1$, or on every nonpure cubic at $g=3$.

Every sufficiently large prime $q$ can serve as the first member of such a pair. Let $F$ be the compositum of the splitting fields of the cubics in the fixed box and exclude the finitely many polynomial or field discriminant primes, taking the squarefree part of a polynomial with repeated roots. Set $D=h(q)(q^2-1)$. The Frobenius of $q$ in $F(\zeta_D)$ prescribes its cubic types and the residue $q\bmod D$. Since $q$ is unramified in this extension, the totally ramified field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)$ is linearly disjoint from it. Chebotarev therefore supplies a positive density of primes $p$ with those types and residue and with $p\equiv1\pmod q$. They satisfy $h(q)\mid h(p)$, $\gcd(h(p),q^2-1)=\gcd(h(q),q^2-1)=g$ and $q\nmid h(p)$. The construction allows any irreducibility pattern that occurs at the seed $q$. [Chebotarev density theorem](https://websites.math.leidenuniv.nl/algebra/Lenstra-Chebotarev.pdf)

In the explicit pair stored in the replay, $p$ and $q$ are 1259- and 431-bit primes, both one modulo three. Exactly 78 nonpure height-four cubics are irreducible at both, including $X^3+4X^2-3X-4$. All 810 literal quadratic and cubic powers give trivial gcds at height four. The first height-five witness in enumeration order is $X^3-4X^2-5X-5$ and returns $q$. To reproduce both primality proofs, the two full box searches and the independent finite-field irreducibility checks, load the GP file and call `checkPairedCubicBox()`. [Exact primes and replay](../experiments/cyclotomic-multiples.gp)
