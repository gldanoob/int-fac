**Verdict.** I obtain the progression estimate and the error margins needed in the fixed-parameter deduction of $\exp(O((\log N)^{2/3}(\log\log N)^{1/3}))$ worst-case deterministic complete factorization. ## Prime sums, including a pole at the Perron origin

Write $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$, $V=D^2z$. In this schedule all of $\log B,\log(q+2),\log(4+|t|)$ in the prime-sum calculation are $\le L^2$ eventually, even after adjoining its auxiliary Perron height. Set $\delta=256z/\log B$. Eventually $0<\delta<1/8$. A direct logarithmic-derivative proof works for every relevant height. For primitive nonprincipal $\chi$ use $g(w)=L(w,\chi)$. For the principal primitive character use $g(w)=(w-1)\zeta(w)/(w+1)$ with the removable value at $w=1$. On the closed disk centered at $2+i\tau$ of radius $3/2-\delta/4$, GRH and the Euler product give a holomorphic zero-free $g$. The nonprincipal Dirichlet partial sums are bounded by the modulus. Abel summation gives $|g|\ll q(4+|\tau|)$ here. For $\zeta$ use $\zeta(w)=w/(w-1)-w\int_1^\infty\{x\}x^{-w-1}dx$ and the indicated rational factor. The Euler product on the center line gives an absolute positive lower bound at the center, including the rational factor. Apply Borel–Carathéodory to the logarithm taking value zero at the center on dividing by $g(2+i\tau)$; Cauchy's derivative estimate on an intermediate disk gives
$$
 |g'/g(w)|\ll\delta^{-2}\log(q(4+|\tau|))
$$
for $\operatorname{Im}w=\tau$ and $1/2+\delta/2\le\operatorname{Re}w\le2$. This provides a pole-regular estimate even when $t$ tends to zero. The original window note lines 12–22 use the equivalent zero partial-fraction estimate. The source's disk and half-integer arguments are on pp. 45–46, Lemma 9.2(1). [Window lemma, individual characters](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md#an-individual-character-estimate) · [Cogentic, p. 45](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=45)

For half-integer endpoints $x,y$ in the prime band, $s=\sigma+it$, set $E(v)=(y^v-x^v)/v$, with $E(0)=\log(y/x)$. The cut Perron integral of $-L'/L(s+v)E(v)$ starts on $a=1-\sigma+1/\log B$. Choose the larger-separation endpoint of $T_0\in\{B^3,2B^3\}$, so $||t|-T_0|\ge B^3/2$. For either endpoint $b=x,y$, the terms $n\in[b/2,2b]$ in the truncation error cost $O(b^{1-\sigma}\log^2 B/T_0)$ by $|\log(b/n)|\gg |b-n|/b$ and the inverse-distance harmonic sum. The other terms cost $O(B^{1-\sigma}\log B/T_0)$ by absolute convergence on $1+1/\log B$. Moving to $a'=1/2+\delta/2-\sigma\le-\delta/2$ gives a vertical error $O(x^{-\delta/2} L^{12})$ and horizontals $O(B^{1-\sigma}L^{12}/T_0)$. Indeed $\delta^{-2}\le L^4$, the logarithms in the disk bound are $O(L^2)$, and $\int_{-T_0}^{T_0}|a'+iv|^{-1}dv=O(L^3)$. The extra pole term in the restored logarithmic derivative is bounded on these sides by the chosen heights and the distance from $1$ on the vertical. For the principal character the only residue is $E(1-s)=\int_x^y w^{-s}\,dw$ when $|t|<T_0$. This includes $\sigma=1,t=0$ as written. When $|t|>T_0$ that integral is $O(B^{1-\sigma}/T_0)$ by its explicit antiderivative. No $v=0$ residue is ever added independently.

Dyadic intervals and $\sum_{p^k\le t,k\ge2}\log p\ll\sqrt t\log^2(2t)$ remove prime powers at cost $O(x^{1/2-\sigma}L^{12})$. Rounding arbitrary endpoints carries $O(x^{-\sigma}\log B)$, and Abel summation divides the prime main term by $\log w$. Deleting imprimitive and prescribed factors costs $O(L^{O(1)}B^{-1/4})$ in this schedule. The nonlinear Euler logarithm is bounded by $O(\sum_{n>\sqrt B}n^{-1-2\delta})=O(\delta^{-1}B^{-\delta})$. The worst vertical bound is $O(L^{-64+12})$; choosing $D_0=256$ thus leaves ample room for $O(L^{-10})$. These give the entire logarithmic-product statement (1) for the finite range actually used here. The pole cancellation concerns the analytic logarithmic derivative; the finite prime Euler product is entire.

## Mellin inversion and the saddle

Cogentic's exact compact modification $\widetilde W$ uses an ordinary Gaussian in $x-Y$ and a cutoff in $\log(x/Y)$, p. 42. For $q\le S$ the normalized progression truncation error is $O(Re^{-0.15R^2})$, with $R=Y/S$. The compact Mellin transform $M(s)=\int\widetilde W(x)x^{s-1}dx$ satisfies $\int|M(\sigma+it)|dt\ll RY^{\sigma-1}$ and $|M(\sigma+it)|\ll Y^{\sigma-1}\exp[-c(|t|/R)^{2/3}]$ for $|t|\ge R^2$, uniformly in $1/2\le\sigma\le1$. The latter pointwise assertion is visible in the proof on p. 43, following the $k$th derivative estimate. Mellin inversion of each of the finitely many products gives the sign convention $M(\sigma+it)F_\chi(\sigma+it)$. For nonprincipal $\chi$ integrate on $\sigma=1/2+\delta$ through $T=B^4R^2$, giving $O(RY^{-1/2+\delta})$. At higher frequencies $|F_\chi|\le\exp(O(\sqrt B))$, and the Gevrey bound dominates even $q$ times this quantity. Orthogonality has coefficient $q/\phi(q)$ on each character, so the total nonprincipal error is $O(qRY^{-1/2+\delta})$. [Window note, lines 24–46](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md#a-gaussian-distribution-in-progressions) · [Cogentic, Lemma 8.4, pp. 42–43](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=42)

The factor band at the saddle has $\lambda=(1-\alpha)\log B=\log u+\log\log u+O(1)$ and $\log F(\alpha)=(1+o(1))u$, by the PNT prime integral. A polynomial number of removed primes makes an error exponentially small in $\log B$ in each fixed normalized derivative. Uniformly for $|t|\le1/\log B$, $|F(\alpha+it)|\le F(\alpha)e^{-c u(t\log B)^2}$. The Taylor majorant and the positive central integral give $I_{\rm pure}\asymp Y^{\alpha-1}F(\alpha)/(\sqrt{u}\log B)$. Thus the $O(R^{-2})$ low-frequency Mellin error is relative to $I_{\rm pure}$. On $1/\log B\le|t|\le1$ the prime mass in $1-1/\lambda\le\log p/\log B\le1$ loses at least $c e^\lambda/\lambda^3\asymp u/\lambda^2$ in the real logarithm. This applies at exact resonances $t\log B=2\pi j$ as well: integration of $1-\cos$ on the band has quadratic loss in its width when it covers less than one period. The full unit-length integral and the relative factor $\sqrt u\log B$ are absorbed by reducing $c$. The parameter-free saddle lower bound is Lemma 9.1(2), pp. 43–44. The Taylor/Edgeworth and two-prime finite differences in Lemma 9.2(2), pp. 45–46, need $u\to\infty$, $\varphi^{(j)}(\alpha)=O_j(u\log^j B)$, $\varphi''\asymp u\log^2B$, and the stated negligible deletions. All hold here. Both the derivative bound $|d I_{\rm pure}/d\log Y|\ll I_{\rm pure}$ and the $O(u^{-2})$ in the two-prime covariance are provided by that calculation with these bounds. [Cogentic, Lemma 9.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=44) · [Cogentic, Lemma 9.2 proof](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=46)

For $1\le|t|\le T$ deform to $\sigma(t)=\max(1/2+\delta,\alpha-\log |t|/\log B)$. In $w=\log v$, the continuous prime main uses $e^{(1-\sigma)w}/w$, whose variation on $[\log B/2,\log B]$ is at most the sum of its endpoint values. Integration by parts therefore gives $O(B^{1-\sigma}/(|t|\log B))=O(e^\lambda/\log B)=o(u)$ in $\log|F|$. Before the corner, $Y^{\sigma(t)-\alpha}=|t|^{-u}$ exactly. The line element has bounded modulus, so the curved contribution is at most $O(Y^{\alpha-1}e^{o(u)}/(u-1))=O(e^{-c u}I_{\rm pure})$. After the corner use $O(TY^{-1/2+\delta}e^{o(u)})$. The principal orthogonality factor is $O(\log\log(3q))$ and is absorbed in this last exponential. The horizontal joins are negligible by the pointwise Gevrey bound above. In particular an invalid fixed-saddle $R$-length error is avoided. [Window note, lines 46–57](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md#a-gaussian-distribution-in-progressions)

## Divided classes and numerical margin

Write $q$ for the modulus of a conditional value after dividing its prescribed primes, and $G=(r,q)$. If $G$ has a repeated or forbidden prime, that squarefree class has zero mass. Otherwise the exact substitution is
$$
    qW_{Y,S}(Gm)=(q/G)W_{Y/G,S/G}(m),\qquad
    m\equiv r/G\pmod {q/G}.
$$
The remaining eligible band must exclude **every** prime of $G$, also when that prime has disappeared from $q/G$; otherwise squareful values would be counted. The source uses $J'_2=\{p\in J(a'):p\nmid q\}$ in Lemma 9.4, p. 50, which does exactly this. The normalized $q/S$ and $R$ are unchanged. The structural coprimality at $P_0|a'-m|$ follows from the input-unit sweep and $\gcd(a'-m,k_0k'_0)=1$; it leaves $G\mid\ell\Delta Q_{\rm free}\le AB^3$. For arbitrary close pairs the parameter-free rough Lemma 9.1 applies since the second lattice width divided by its step is at least $U/(\sqrt2 B^2)>e^{0.62}B$ (source pp. 49–50). In the rough Rankin factor the powers of $G$ cancel exactly when $Y,q,S$ are divided together; the allowed saddle comparison at $Y/G$ can also cost only $L^{O(1)}$. For a clean pair, a prime dividing just the difference either contributes the small-prime coprime factor, or its rare divisibility in the first smooth value costs $O(p^{-\alpha_s}e^{0.04H})$. At the target or an extra free prescribed prime, the excluded first-value residue is either a nonzero class of relative size $O(1/p)$ or is bounded by the same division estimate. The original local cases, Lemma 9.5 on p. 51, are covered. [Cogentic, pp. 49–51](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=49)

For the fixed schedule in lines 9–19 of the reduction note, $\log B\sim1000H$, $\log Y\sim100001V$ even after division, $u\sim100.001D$, $\lambda\sim z/3$. Every clean class and its auxiliary prime split has $\log q\le(2+o(1))V$ and $\log R=O(H)$. Thus the logarithms of the nonprincipal and far-principal errors are respectively at most $(-49998.5+o(1))V$ and $(-50000.5+o(1))V$. The term $\delta\log Y=O(H)$ for the fixed choice $D_0=256$. The inverse one-point saddle costs $\exp((33.3337+o(1))H)$, below $\exp(34H)$, and $u/(\log u)^2\gg z^2$. Hence the desired relative error $O(e^{-50z^2})$ follows. Rough losses have log at most $(1/3000+o(1))H$ at each application, since $\log X_{\rm sc}-\log U=\log m+O(H)$. With the printed allowances the repeated-mark exponent is at most $(0.05+2000-9997+68+o(1))H=(-7928.95+o(1))H$. For coincident-square-prime discard the inverse prime gives $e^{-(500+o(1))H}$ and close-pair discard gives $e^{-(47+o(1))H}$, each before a rough $e^{0.04H}$ loss. These supply the centered variance thresholds; the rank and exact search are applied as in lines 53–73 of the reduction note. ## Adversarial finite checks

Run `python3 experiments/cogentic-fixed-window-audit.py`. Six contour integrals include a pole exactly at the Perron origin, one within $2\cdot10^{-14}$, and poles outside the contour. The maximum residue discrepancy is $1.43\cdot10^{-13}$. The exact nonunit arithmetic check exhausts 1,542 classes, including 218 reduced moduli still sharing a prime with $G$ and 595 incompatible squarefree classes. Four actual linear-Gaussian comparisons use a principal character and the real nonprincipal character modulo four. Direct enumeration, a vertical inversion and the bent inversion agree to $5.13\cdot10^{-11}$. The transform integral restricts $|\log(x/Y)|\le0.28$ where the paper's compact cutoff equals one; the omitted portion at the tested $R\ge32$ is bounded by its displayed Gaussian tail. The finite Fourier integral uses a cutoff $28R$ and numerical quadrature. These checks test normalization, contour orientation, endpoint collisions and the prime-removal case that an all-coprime replay misses.
