# Rational eligibility in the last descent box

Two ways to reuse the late rational search admit exact cost comparisons for one input. A complete late-box table serves all terminal requests in one field. Its leading cost is slightly higher than the low-search term of the [2.250 schedule](keybreak-tiered-descent.md#polynomial-bit-probes-and-a-2250-schedule). Choosing the smooth numerator first without using enough information about the rational environment has a different obstruction: the chance of fitting any denominator in the short box is exponentially small on the larger radix scale. [KeyBreak §G.1, §H.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105)

## A shared exact table

Let $B=e^{(b+o(1))H}$, $Y_i=e^{(y+o(1))H}$ and $V_i=e^{(v+o(1))H}$ at the last active internal level, where $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. The next cutoff is $B$. Suppose $b,y,v$ are fixed positive constants in the schedule. KeyBreak's eligible point $(a,b')$ has a prime denominator $b'>B$ in $(V_i/2,3V_i/4]$ and $w=rb'-Qa$ positive and $B$-smooth. A valid active parent $p$ satisfies $B<p\le Y_i$, its simple root label is $s=a/b'\bmod p$, and $F(a,b')/p$ is $B$-smooth up to sign. The original admissibility and unit checks still apply. [KeyBreak (G.5), §H.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=129)

Enumerate the whole integer rectangle once, testing the linear value range, the denominator and the rational predicate. On a rationally eligible point use H.3 at cutoff $Y_i$ on $|F(a,b')|$. This succeeds with high probability on each relevant value because the desired product is $Y_i$-smooth. The verified factor list identifies its active parent if there is exactly one prime above $B$ to the first power and it is admissible. Save one such point and its complete factor list per label by sorting. All other primes in the list are terminal. A valid candidate cannot carry two different active parent labels at this level. [KeyBreak Proposition H.3](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=129)

Every good label has an eligible point in the rectangle by the source's lower mean and goodness conditions. Use the H.3 confidence multiplier for the total number of scanned points, rather than for a single lookup. The number of coordinate tests is $e^{(2v+o(1))H}$ and each rational or algebraic smoothness test costs $e^{o(H)}$ at these late cutoffs. The union bound is uniform over the subsequent adaptive requests. The table stores at most $dY_i$ labels, by the degree bound for roots at each good prime. Its records have polynomial bit lengths in $\log N$. Scanning the whole table for each of $e^{(b+o(1))H}$ requests costs $e^{(b+y+o(1))H}$. This fits the sweep budget at the 2.250 schedule, where $b+y<1.630$ and $2v>2.251$. The table depends only on the public field and environment. Selecting the first verified point preserves the private-root law and replacing a good-label search by its saved result preserves its success guarantee. [KeyBreak (G.6), (H.16), Theorem E.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=133)

For the current schedule $d/D\sim1.33226$, with $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$. Use $t=d\,b(n)$, $e_B/t\to\beta=.61135$, the box coefficients $\alpha=.5001,\gamma=.88185$, the height $h_T=.7078$, and the low cutoff ratio $\lambda_L=2.54$. At the last active level

$$e_B<e_i\le \frac{(\lambda_L-\gamma)e_B-T_H/d}{\alpha}.$$

The upper bound is the exact clamp condition on the ceiling defining the next cutoff. The resulting sweep coefficient $2v$ lies between $2.2511333976489$ and $2.25159667108$, up to vanishing terms. The recorded low-search term is $2.249849956\ldots$. More generally, write $s_R,s_A$ for the pointwise rational and algebraic smoothness losses in an originally uniform terminal box. Positive expected supply at a label of size $e^{yH}$ requires $2v-y-s_R-s_A>0$ in the source's mean calculation. Since $y\ge b$, the sweep coefficient $2v$ exceeds the leading coefficient $b+s_R+s_A$ obtained by charging a comparable per-request allowance to the $B$-scale retained search count. This comparison concerns the complete sweep with one test per point. It leaves output-sensitive enumeration and changes to the relation supply law open. [Source mean comparison](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=124)

## The numerator-first congruence cost

Fix the public prime $Q\in(3M/4,M]$ and draw $r$ uniformly from the source's integer interval $(M/2,3M/4]$. For any specified numerator $w$, and a specified denominator $0<b'<Q$, the condition $rb'\equiv w\pmod Q$ selects at most one such $r$. There are $O(V_i)$ allowed denominators, even allowing every integer in the box. Thus every distribution on the proposed numerator that is independent of $r$ conditional on $Q$ has probability $O(V_i/M)$ of producing a valid denominator. This applies to an exact uniform sampler of the $B$-smooth numerators in the required value interval. Finding the matching denominator can be offered for free in this bound. Requiring additionally that $p\mid F(a,b')$ for a next relation only decreases the success probability. [KeyBreak environment and lattice](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105)

The estimate extends to a restricted class of adaptive samplers. Give the sampler at most $A$ possible values of advice depending on $r$, and condition on $Q$ and its independent random coins. For each advice value, follow the proposed numerators on the all-rejection path, of length at most $R$. The first success belongs to one of these $AR$ proposals. A union bound gives probability $O(ARV_i/M)$ even if the advice allocates $r$ to menus arbitrarily. At a late box $\log V_i=O(H)$ and $\log M\asymp DH$. With $\log A=o(DH)$ and $R=e^{O(H)}$, this probability is $e^{-\Omega(DH)}$. Thus a proposal distribution that saves the $e^{s_RH}$ rarity by drawing smooth numerators on its own still has to solve a radix-scale alignment problem. This bound does not constrain a direct sampler whose arithmetic uses the full value of $r$ to build each smooth numerator.
