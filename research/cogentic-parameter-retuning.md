# Shorter perturbation ranges in Cogentic's deterministic factoring algorithm

Cogentic's Theorem 1.1 gives a proper divisor of every composite binary integer \(N\) in \(O(\exp(3(\log N)^{3/4}))\) deterministic bit operations. The proof below changes its parameter schedule and gives **complete deterministic factoring in \(\exp(O((\log N)^{18/25}))\) bit operations**. This deduction uses the paper's classical analytic and algebraic lemmas. The coefficient in the exponent is absolute and can be large. A [schedule with logarithmic factors](cogentic-two-thirds.md) improves the exponent further. Write \(L=\log N,\ L_2=\log L,\ \kappa=\log 2\). Decimal exponents below are exact rationals. [Pinned deterministic paper, §§7–11](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf) · [Authors' theorem statement and release status](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/README.md)

## Integer schedule and polynomial pairs

Use the paper's computable integer \(e(N;a,b)=L^{a/b}+O(1)\) from §8.2. Keep its degree \(d=e(N;3,10)\), base \(m=\lceil N^{1/d}\rceil\), factor-base cutoff \(B=2^{e(N;9,25)}\), root precision \(K_1=2^{e(N;7,20)}\), interval width \(w=2^{-e(N;31,100)}\), and subgroup-index bound. Make just two changes to the algorithm:
\[
             A=2^{4e(N;21,50)},\qquad U=2^{1000e(N;18,25)}.
\]
Retain \(S_1=U,S_0=UA\), the centers \(\bar u_j=L_0^2 S_j\), and the whole finite rectangle \(\mathcal B_U\) of §8.2. In the analysis set \(z_{\rm Br}=\exp(L^{.36})\) for the Brun cutoff on page 40 and use \(.355\) for the exponential decay power denoted \(.38\) in Lemma 9.2(3) and Proposition 9.3(2). This decay power is separate from the zero-free width \(\nu_0=L^{-.355}\) in the same section.

The bounds \(6K_1 B P_0 m\bar u_0\le X_{\rm sc}=c_d A^d\le18K_1 B P_0 m\bar u_0\) give
\[
\begin{split}
 \log X_{\rm sc}&=1000\kappa L^{.72}+(1+o(1))L^{.70},\\
 d\log A&=(4\kappa+o(1))L^{.72},\qquad
 \log c_d=(996\kappa+o(1))L^{.72},\\
 u=\log X_{\rm sc}/\log B&=(1000+o(1))L^{.36}.
\end{split}
\]
The choice of the least nonnegative leading-coefficient exponent in §8.2 attains the stated sandwich for all large inputs because its lower target divided by \(A^dP_{\rm odd}\) tends to infinity. In particular the coefficient guard \(4P_0 B\le c_d\), the height cutoff \(2X_{\rm sc}\le2^{L_0}\), and \(4dA\le m\) hold. The root perturbation argument on page 41 requires \(dA^{d-1}=o(\bar u_0)\), which follows from \(4<1000\) at the common exponent \(.72\). The error remains at most \(K_1^{-1}\) in the normalized target polynomial, the signs at the root brackets persist since \(.35>.30\), and \(|\log|P_i^*(a/A)||=O(L^{.31})\) on the sampling intervals. Thus the local order sizes \(Y\) in §§8–10 still have \(u_Y=(\log Y)/(\log B)\in[u/2,2u]\). The root windows and conditional Gaussian width bounds in Lemma 8.2(3) depend on \(K_1,w\) and the ratios \(X_{\rm sc}/(P_0\ell mUA)\), for which the original inequalities remain valid.

The Brun step in Lemma 8.2(2) uses \(r_0=\lfloor L^{.06}\rfloor\), \(M\le B^4\), and intervals of size at least \(Aw\exp(-O(L^{.06}L_2))L^{-O(1)}\). They have logarithmic size \((4\kappa+o(1))L^{.42}\). Each modulus in the even Bonferroni truncation is at most \(B^4 z_{\rm Br}^{2r_0}=\exp((2+o(1))L^{.42})\). The difference in leading coefficients is positive because \(4\log 2>2\). The error from counting progression endpoints, even after the paper's bound \(\exp(L^{.10})\) on the number of terms, is negligible at its stated relative error. The contribution from primes above \(z_{\rm Br}\) is \(O(z_{\rm Br}^{-1/2})\) at this precision by the same counting argument. Its logarithmic decay power \(.36\) exceeds \(.06\). The reciprocal-prime bound on the divisors of \(k_0 k'_0\) still applies since \(\log c_d\ll L^{.76}\). This proves the three estimates of the modified Lemma 8.2 with the same error tolerances.

## Smooth relations and their two-point estimates

Lemma 8.4 and Lemma 9.1 have scale-independent statements. The auxiliary quantities in §9.2 controlling zero density and Mellin height still use \(B,K_1,m\), so \(Q_{\max}=\exp(3L^{.70})\), \(T_* =\exp((3.5\kappa+o(1))L^{.35})\), and the exceptional conductor counts in Proposition 9.3(1) retain their original bounds. In particular \(\log(Q_{\max}^2B^2)=O(L^{.70})=o(\log Y)\), as required when splitting a smooth product into two initial blocks in Proposition 9.3(2).

In Lemma 9.2(2), \(\lambda_Y=(1-\alpha_Y)\log B=\log u_Y+\log\log u_Y+O(1)=(.36+o(1))L_2\). The saddle expansion retains its error \(O(u_Y^{-2})\). Its finite-difference and derivative estimates use the unchanged range \(B^{u/3}\le Z\) and the \(O(L^{.31})\) variation from Lemma 8.2. The undamped logarithm of the Euler product is \(O(u_Y)=O(L^{.36})\). In Lemma 9.2(3) the low frequencies in the Euler product, including the possible real exceptional character, have damping exponent at least a constant times \(u_Y/\lambda_Y^2\gg L^{.355}\). For larger frequencies the proof gives at least \(u_Y/2\). The relative loss from the inverse Mellin cutoff has logarithm \(O(L^{.35}+\log L)\), strictly below \(L^{.355}\). The zero-free prime sum in that lemma still saves \(B^{-\nu_0/4}\exp(\lambda_Y/2)=\exp(-cL^{.005}+O(L_2))\) when the width is \(\nu_0\). All threshold comparisons with the width \(\nu_1\) at smaller conductors also retain their original bounds since \(\lambda_Y\) has decreased.

For the nonexceptional characters in Proposition 9.3(2), page 48 bounds the Euler product by \(e^{-.04u}\) times the positive full product. Multiplication by \(|J'|^2\le B^2\) costs \((2\kappa+o(1))L^{.36}\) in the logarithm. The progression orthogonality and integration costs have logarithms \(O(L^{.34}L_2+L^{.35}+\log L)\). As \(.01u=(10+o(1))L^{.36}\), their sum and \(2\log B\) are less than \(.01u\) for all large \(N\). This leaves \(e^{-.03u}=o(e^{-cL^{.355}})\). The middle- and high-conductor bounds on page 47 are unchanged. This establishes the clean progression formula with relative error \(O(e^{-cL^{.355}})\).

Lemma 9.4 is stated for arbitrary choices with \(m>2A\) and \(U\ge4B^4\), both satisfied here. The clean one- and two-point asymptotics in Lemma 9.5 use the bounds on \(\mu_i^{\rm ex}(a)/(UA)\), \(\mu_i^{\rm ex}(a')/U\) from Lemma 8.2. Their logarithms, including the fixed products of \(B\) and \(Q_R\), are at most \((1+o(1))L^{.70}\), inherited from \(m\). Hence the greedy-Rankin cost in their logarithms stays \(O(L^{.34}L_2)\) since \(.70-.36=.34\) and \(.36/\kappa<.57\). The bad-residue and Gaussian-tail comparisons on page 51 use \(B^{-.49}\), \(K_1^{-.90}\), and the clean progression estimate. The new progression error still beats any fixed power of \(K_1^{-1}\) and the displayed greedy-Rankin losses, by \(.355>.35>.34\).

## Concentration, order extraction and uniform termination

The diagonal in Proposition 10.1(1) has factor at most
\[
        e^{4L^{.34}L_2}B^2(Aw)^{-1}e^{L^{.39}L_2}.
\]
The last term remains an upper bound for the reciprocal of the product of the two local smooth probabilities because its logarithm is \(O(u\log u)=O(L^{.36}L_2)\). Thus the expression is \(o(K_1^{-.48})\) since \(.42>.39\). For nearby distinct pairs, \(\Delta_{\rm close}/(Aw)=K_1^{-1/2}/w\ll K_1^{-.49}\) as \(.35>.31\). For the remaining pairs, \(Aw\) exceeds every fixed power of \(B\) and \(K_1\). The medium-conductor divisor costs, high-conductor divisor costs, and centered main term on pages 52–53 retain their bounds. They use \(m=\exp((1+o(1))L^{.70})\), the conductor counts with exponents \(.005,.345\), \(B\), the unchanged Brun error \(\exp(-.10L^{.06}L_2)\), and the estimates of Lemma 9.5; none requires the former larger \(A\). The Lemma 8.2 bound \(\omega(|f_i(a)|)\le 2.1u\) and the saddle derivatives continue to bound all rows and weights. These give the same peeling error and protected rows at \(\ell\) on pages 53–54, since \(u\) is polynomial in \(L\).

For the last spectral estimate write \(w_i\) for the vectors on page 54. Now \(\|w_i\|^2\asymp u(\log B)^2\asymp L^{1.08}\). The published lower bounds for the order of the transformed vector in the two cases are still valid, since \(d/\max_i\|w_i\|^2\gg L^{-.81}\) and \(d/(\log X_{\rm sc})^2\gg L^{-1.20}\). Thus \(\|v'\|^2\ge L^{-1.25}\) still suffices there. The centered model loses \(O(u^{-1})\) in its factor-base block and retains the \((128u)^{-1}\) coefficient on the Archimedean squared form. That form is bounded below with the same \(L^{-.30}\) factor, since \(d,w\) are unchanged. Therefore the comparison \(y^T\widetilde U^{\rm ex}y\gg L^{-.69}\) on page 55 still holds, and the final eigenvalue is at least \(L^{-3}\). This proves Proposition 10.1(2) for the stated schedule. In particular, at every good generating prime some pair in the **enumerated** rectangle satisfies the rank condition.

The certified order extractor in Lemma 7.5 continues to cost \(\operatorname{poly}(A,B,d,D_{\rm row},P_{\rm bit})=\exp(O(L^{.42}))\) per pair. Proposition 9.3(1) supplies \((1/2-o(1))B/\log B\) generating primes. The subgroup-index bound and the bit-length guard of §11 depend on \(B\) and \(L\), which are unchanged. They ensure \(\log\widetilde M\le\exp(L^{.65})\), whether \(N\) is squarefree or has repeated primes. Thus the trial-division escape for a failed guard still occurs only below an effective absolute threshold. The number of calls is at most \(16B L_0^8 U^4 A^2\), giving logarithmic cost \((4000\kappa+o(1))L^{.72}\). The cost of §11.2 outside enumeration has logarithm \(O(L^{.64}L_2)\), and Theorem 1.2(a) takes time \(\exp(O(\sqrt L L_2)+L^{.65})\) on its output. This proves the claimed uniform bound for one proper divisor. AKS primality and recursive division through at most \(2\log_2N\) nodes yield every prime factor and its multiplicity within the same big-O bound.
