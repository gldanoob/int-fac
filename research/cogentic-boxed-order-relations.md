# Boxed order relations and relative refinement

For odd $N>1$, unit bases $h_1,\ldots,h_m\bmod N$, a positive scalar $s$, and bounds $B_i\ge0$, write $h^v=\prod_i h_i^{v_i}$. The search exhibits a proper divisor $\gcd((h^v)^s-1,N)$ from the signed box $|v_i|\le B_i$, or counts all global relations in the box and recovers their rational span. The lists have size on the order of the square root of the box volume. Saturation can certify products whose exponents lie outside the box. A nonunit input base already gives a divisor if its gcd with $N$ is proper.

## Rectangles and prime-local collisions

Set $w_i=2B_i+1$, $V=\prod_i w_i$, and $T=\lceil\sqrt V\rceil$. Choose the first pivot $j$ with $\prod_{i\le j}w_i\ge T$, and put $P=\prod_{i<j}w_i$, $Q=\prod_{i>j}w_i$, $b=\lceil T/P\rceil$. Write $w_j=fb+e$ with $0\le e<b$. In the full rectangle, put the earlier coordinates and the pivot values $-B_j,\ldots,-B_j+b-1$ on the left. Put the later coordinates and the pivot values $0,b,\ldots,(f-1)b$ on the right. Omitted coordinates are zero. If $e>0$, add a rectangle with the same earlier and later coordinates, left pivot values $-B_j,\ldots,-B_j+e-1$, and right pivot $fb$. The quotient and remainder of $v_j+B_j$ give a unique pair $v=u+w$ in these rectangles. The left list has size at most $Pb\le T+P$. The right lists have sizes $fQ\le V/T$ and $Q\le V/T$, using $Pw_j\ge T$.

For each rectangle, bucket the left words by $a=h^{su}$ and the right words by $y=h^{-sw}$, with residues reduced modulo $N$. Since $h^{sw}$ is a unit, $\gcd(a-y,N)=\gcd((h^{u+w})^s-1,N)$. Form $F(X)=\prod_y(X-y)$ over $\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ using the distinct right residues. For a left residue $a$ absent on the right, test $\gcd(F(a),N)$. If $a$ occurs on the right, use $F'(a)=\prod_{y\ne a}(a-y)$ to remove the exact equality. Product trees and multipoint remainder evaluations give the values of $F$ and $F'$ at every distinct left residue. The polynomial divisors in the remainder trees are monic, so the computation works over the residue ring. A nonunit test value, including one with gcd $N$, guarantees a nonidentical pair with a proper gcd. Scan its right list once to recover the pair and verify the gcd on the original word. In the absence of such a pair, every box word that is a relation modulo any prime dividing $N$ is a relation modulo $N$.

An exact bucket with left list $A$ and right list $C$ contributes $|A||C|$ distinct box words to the global count. For fixed $u_0\in A,w_0\in C$, retain the star sums $u+w_0$ and $u_0+w$, counting their common edge once. They span every pair sum by
\[
 u+w=(u+w_0)+(u_0+w)-(u_0+w_0).
\]
Rational elimination visits at most $|A|+|C|-1$ candidate rows per bucket. The count uses the full Cartesian multiplicity.

## Saturation and repeated boxes

Let $R\in\mathbb Z^{m\times r}$ have the retained relation vectors as columns. For integral unimodular $U,V_0$, take $URV_0=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r)$ in rectangular Smith form with $0<d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_r$. Write $c_i=U^{-1}e_i$. For $i\le r$, $d_ic_i=RV_0e_i$, so $(h^{c_i})^{sd_i}=1\bmod N$. The first $r$ columns form a basis for the saturation $(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}R)\cap\mathbb Z^m$. Verify each exponent by modular powering. Pooled comparison raises each certified base to the odd part of its exponent, generating its 2-primary cyclic subgroup, and detects any square root of one other than $1,-1$ in the joint subgroup. Its witness gives a proper gcd. [Pooled comparison](cogentic-pooled-order-certificates.md)

For a positive-rank round without a split, replace the active bases by $h^{c_{r+1}},\ldots,h^{c_m}$ and multiply $s$ by $d_r$. Use the explicitly supplied uniform bound from the `--refine-bound` option on all surviving coordinates in subsequent rounds. Keep all previous order certificates for cumulative pooled comparisons. Unimodularity preserves the unpowered subgroup when all dropped bases are included with the active ones. Each dropped base has a verified exponent dividing the current scalar, so the subgroup generated by the active powered bases is exactly $G^s$, where $G$ is generated by the original bases. Positive-rank rounds drop at least one coordinate. A split, a zero-rank round, or exhaustion of the active basis ends the search.

## Rank conditions and cost

For a current prime $p\mid N$, the lattice
\[
 L_p=\{v\in\mathbb Z^m:(h^v)^s=1\bmod p\}
\]
has determinant $D_p=|\langle h_1^s,\ldots,h_m^s\bmod p\rangle|$, which divides $(p-1)/\gcd(s,p-1)$. Let $\lambda_1\le\cdots\le\lambda_m$ be its successive minima for $[-1,1]^m$. Minkowski's second theorem gives $\prod_i\lambda_i\le D_p$. All minima are at least one by integrality, so $\lambda_j\le D_p^{1/(m-j+1)}$. A uniform bound $B\ge1$ with $D_p\le B^{m-j+1}$ for some $p\mid N$ guarantees a local split or at least $j$ independent global relations. If the completed collision search finds no split, each of the independent prime-local words is global. The sufficient condition concerns the current state and box size.

Suppose the initial bases generate the unit group modulo squares and the cumulative recovered rank leaves $k$ formal directions uncertified. Pooled comparisons on every recovered cofactor leave blocks with at most $k+1$ distinct prime divisors. The recovered directions extend to an integral basis of the original exponent lattice and have binary codimension $k$. On a cofactor $D$, the initial list surjects onto the quadratic quotient of dimension $\omega(D)$, so the certified subspace has image of dimension at least $\omega(D)-k$. Failure of pooled comparison implies its 2-primary subgroup is cyclic, whose quadratic image has dimension at most one. For arbitrary bases the pool can only expose signs in the subgroup they generate. [Cofactor threshold proof](cogentic-saturated-product-certificates.md)

For each round, list construction, exact-bucket traversal, and the recovery scan use $O(\sqrt V)$ entries. With fast polynomial multiplication and monic division, the product and remainder trees cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt V)$ ring operations. Word evaluations, gcds, and exact rational elimination contribute factors polynomial in the active dimension, $\log N$, $\log s$, and the bit lengths of the bounds. Smith form with transformations admits polynomial bit complexity on the retained matrix. For $B\ge1$ bounding all retained entries, Hadamard gives $d_r\le\prod_{i=1}^r d_i\le r^{r/2}B^r$, since the product divides every nonzero maximal minor. Across rounds the ranks dropped sum to at most the initial dimension $m_0$. Thus the total increase in $\log s$ is at most $m_0\log B+\tfrac12m_0\log m_0$. Modular powering in the transformed basis uses the bit lengths of the Smith coefficients. The cost bounds charge the boxes visited, with no general guarantee of positive rank at a prescribed volume. [Polynomial Smith form](https://doi.org/10.1137/0208040)

## Exact fixtures

- At $N=91$, use bases $2,17$, uniform bound $2$, and $s=128$. Neither base separately has a local hit for exponents $1,2$. The joint box has nine global matches and rank two. The saturated certificate pool returns $7$.
- At $N=91$, start with bases $(5,9,9)$, bounds $(1,1,2)$, and $s=1$. Direct enumeration finds 40 words with gcd one and five global relations, namely $v_1=0$ and $v_2+v_3\equiv0\bmod3$. The first round has rank two and leaves the pool unsplit. One Smith basis has columns $(0,1,2),(0,0,1),(1,0,0)$ and nonzero invariants $1,3$. At refinement bound $2$, the remaining base $5$ and scalar $3$ detect $5^{-6}\equiv64\bmod91$, giving gcd $7$.
- At $N=481=13\cdot37$, use bases $(31,45)$, initial bounds $(4,1)$, $s=1$, and refinement bound $3$. The rounds certify $(31,4)$ and $(45,12)$ respectively. Each certificate alone stalls pooled comparison. Together they give $45^3/31\equiv38\bmod481$, a square root of one with $\gcd(38-1,481)=37$.
- At $N=26182823=2803\cdot9341$, use bases $(15759937,23615508,3958576,19144294)$, uniform bound $3$, and $s=1$. Both prime-local relation sets are exactly $\{0,\pm v_1,\pm v_2,\pm v_3\}$, with $v_1=(2,2,3,3)$, $v_2=(3,3,-2,-3)$, and $v_3=(3,-3,3,-2)$. All seven matches are global. The saturation contains $w=(v_1+v_2+v_3)/2=(4,1,2,-1)$, outside the box. Its value $h^w=18710024\bmod N$ has square one and gives $\gcd(h^w-1,N)=9341$. The Smith certificate pool finds the split on this box.
