# Known square parts in private coordinates

The private squarefree test can allow an unfactored square part with a verified integer root. Removing it is compatible with the hidden-sign argument. In the ordinary independent private sampling model it gives no leading gain in the staged shape density or in the logarithmic length that the prime bands must cover. Also, in the exact rational-correction parity model a partial row leaving an odd large prime cannot contribute anchored rank through a fixed late public pool. [Private band counts](keybreak-private-band-stages.md) · [Enlarged private boxes](keybreak-enlarged-private-lattice.md)

## The public correction

For a primitive positive representative write
\[
 u=s_u^2 u_0,\qquad v=s_v^2 v_0,\qquad u/v\equiv h^2\pmod N,
\]
with $u_0,v_0$ squarefree. The factor lists can certify the odd kernels and the two integer square identities without factoring $s_u$ or $s_v$. Reject nonunit coordinates or use their proper gcd as a factor. Then
\[
 u_0/v_0\equiv \bigl((s_v/s_u)h\bigr)^2\pmod N.
\]
The known multiplier is fixed by the public transcript and is a unit. Multiplying a uniform conditional root by it gives the uniform root of the stripped target. Independent private blocks retain independent hidden signs when conditioned on their squares and independent public randomness. Thus a verified correction can be carried as a public multiplier in the exact comparison. A modular multiplier supplied without such a bounded rational representative belongs to a different sampling model. [KeyBreak, Theorem E.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=93) · [Known-multiplier sign rank](keybreak-anchor-reuse.md#conditional-sign-law)

## Sharp leading density in the staged model

Let $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$, $D=H/\log\log N$, and $X\asymp\sqrt N\exp(tH)$ for fixed $t\ge0$. On the rough-input branch, a fixed primitive unit pair names at most one unit square and has probability at most $N^{-1}\exp(o(H))$ per independent block. For a fixed prefix $j$ let $T_i$ be the certified product threshold and $Y_i$ the upper prime cutoff at stage $i<j$. Put $S_j=\sum_{i<j}w_i$, where $w_i$ is the two-coordinate inverse-density weight of the original gate. The thresholds can differ from the ordinary thresholds by $O(H)$ in the logarithm.

For one coordinate $u=s^2u_0$, count possible witnesses consisting of $s\ge e^{\tau H}$ and a certified divisor $d_i\mid u_0$ for each earlier stage. The $d_i$ are pairwise coprime before the upper bound drops their support restrictions. Counting multiples of $s^2\prod_i d_i$ gives
\[
 \# u\ \le\ X\sum_{s\ge e^{\tau H}}s^{-2}
      \prod_{i<j}\ \,\sum_{\substack{d_i\ge T_i\\P^+(d_i)\le Y_i}}d_i^{-1}
 \ \le\ X\exp\bigl(-(\tau+S_j/2+o(1))H\bigr).
\]
The reciprocal sums use the original Rankin bounds. Moving a threshold by $O(H)$ in the logarithm changes its coefficient by $o(1)$, since the Rankin parameter is $O(\log D/(DH))$. This count allows all roots $s$, regardless of the cost of finding them. For two coordinates with square-root thresholds $e^{\tau H}$ and $e^{\nu H}$, the expected number of corresponding prefix pairs per block is at most $\exp((2t-S_j-\tau-\nu+o(1))H)$. In particular the full sum over square parts adds no positive leading exponent.

The square-tail penalty is attained in the good full-shape first moment when the two roots have the same exponent $\tau$. For $\tau>0$, pick a prime root in a fixed dyadic interval at $e^{\tau H}$ and use the H.20 good kernel count at coordinate bound $X/(4e^{2\tau H})$. The scale change leaves each band weight fixed to leading order. Unique squarefree decomposition and the prime count give $X\exp(-(S/2+\tau+o(1))H)$ distinct coordinates, where $S=\sum_iw_i$. Use root one when $\tau=0$. These coordinate sets are the same on each side, so the source's quadratic-class argument applies. Sharing one of the newly introduced root primes costs a proportion at most $\exp(-\tau H+o(H))$ before the quadratic-class loss. Shared kernel primes remain negligible as in H.5. Thus the good pair first moment has coefficient $2t-S-2\tau$. No claim about distinct occupied squares follows in an enlarged box. [KeyBreak, (H.18)–(H.20)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=135)

There is also a bound uniform over adaptive representative selection from each ordinary private box. Across $\exp((C+o(1))H)$ fresh blocks, take $K>C+2t$. The number of coordinates up to $X$ divisible by any square with root at least $e^{KH}$ is at most $X\sum_{s\ge e^{KH}}s^{-2}$. The number below $Xe^{-KH}$ is at most $Xe^{-KH}$. Counting the unrestricted second coordinate and summing over the blocks shows that with probability $1-o(1)$ every available coordinate has magnitude at least $Xe^{-KH}$ and maximal square root smaller than $e^{KH}$. On that event $\log u_0=\log X+O(H)$ and likewise for the denominator. Thus defining a gate's length fractions relative to the stripped kernels preserves their leading values. In particular the moment constraint $\sum_i z_i^2 w_i=2\kappa\delta/3+o(1)$ remains in the current private cost ledger. Each permitted prime at the radix scale is already squarefree on every such coordinate with high probability. A square test can save finite recognition work without reducing the required leading odd mass. ## Partial rows and public coverage

Fix a public field and rational environment before a fresh independent draw. Take a collection of late boxes whose total raw coordinate records number $\exp(O(H))$. Include all their rational and algebraic numerical values, and the fixed denominators and leading coefficients, in a set of supports. Each nonzero value has polynomial bit length in $\log N$. Any prime $p\ge Y_{\min}=\exp(\Omega(DH))$ in this set divides a coordinate in a fresh private box with probability at most $O(X^2/(pN))\exp(o(H))$, by counting raw coordinate pairs divisible by $p$. The total support has at most $\exp(O(H))$ labels. The union over all permitted labels and over $\exp(O(H))$ draws has probability $\exp(O(H))/Y_{\min}=o(1)$. The late rows may be chosen after seeing the private coordinates if drawn from this fixed raw support. The same estimate applies to any independently fixed prebuilt pool of polynomial-bit values with $\exp(O(H))$ records. Previously sampled private coordinates also contribute only $\exp(O(H))$ labels, so conditional application at each fresh draw excludes a shared permitted prime between independent blocks. [Private prime sharing](keybreak-enlarged-private-lattice.md#shared-factors-and-sieve-cutoffs)

In the existing exact extractor a skipped odd prime of the private target can be kept provisionally in its rational correction. For every such prime above $Y_{\min}$, the valuation modulo two is one on the corresponding partial row and zero on all other fresh rows and on a public pool supported as above. The ordinary completed paths put only $B$-smooth terms and the common rational coefficient prime in their rational corrections. Thus the skipped prime is a parity row forcing that private label's selection coefficient to zero. This argument applies to an unfactored residual as an integer identity, without needing to index its prime explicitly. A verified square residual has zero parity and is covered by the square count above. A collection in which every private row has an uncovered odd prime produces no usable anchored dependency in this parity model. An observed large prime can instead be discharged by a targeted descent or another certified comparison; charging that work and proving its availability are necessary for a different collection bound.
