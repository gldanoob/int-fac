# Choosing a cubic field from the public integer

Suppose $N=pq$ for distinct primes in $[X,2X]$, and an arbitrary positive multiple $M$ of $\Phi_3(p)=p^2+p+1$ is supplied. A deterministic choice of cyclic cubic fields using $N$ has limiting **worst-multiplier mean two field tests**. If the exponent regime is fixed in advance, the limiting mean is $3/2$ for either regime. The worst-multiplier mean for a prescribed list independent of $N$ is at least $21/8$. These means count batched field tests; each test uses a polylogarithmic number of modular powers. A polylogarithmic cutoff gives failure $\exp(-\Omega(\log X/\log\log X))$ uniformly in the multiplier. No analytic hypothesis is required.

For each prime conductor $\ell_i\equiv1\bmod6$, identify its cubic character values with $\mathbb F_3$ additively by using a primitive root. Let $x$ and $y$ be the vectors of these characters at $p$ and $q$. The algorithm computes **$s=x+y$ directly from $N$**. A nonzero linear form $\lambda$ defines a cyclic cubic field in which the two primes are inert precisely when $\lambda(x)\ne0$ and $\lambda(y)\ne0$, respectively. The preceding generation theorem gives, simultaneously in all the fields used and outside a power-saving exceptional fraction, the following two possible behaviors.

* If $\Phi_3(q)\nmid M$, a field test returns $p$ exactly when $\lambda(x)\ne0$, and returns 1 otherwise.
* If $\Phi_3(q)\mid M$, it returns the product of the primes inert in the field. A proper split occurs exactly when one prime is inert.

Both behaviors can be selected on each balanced pair with $p,q\ge5$ by using $\Phi_3(p)$ or the two-order lcm. In particular the worst-multiplier analysis permits one choice for the entire run on a given pair. ### A basis requiring at most two conductors per field

When $s\ne0$, take its first nonzero coordinate $r$. Set
$$
 \lambda_r=e_r^*/s_r,\qquad \lambda_i=e_i^*+(1-s_i)\lambda_r\quad(i\ne r).
$$
Use $\lambda_r$ first, then the other rows in coordinate order. They form a basis and every row takes value one on $s$. Thus in the first regime a row misses exactly when $\lambda(x)=0$. In the second it misses exactly when $\lambda(x)=2$, or $\lambda(x+s)=0$.

In the uniform character model $x$ is uniform given any $s$. For $j\ge1$ the two prefix miss events are disjoint affine spaces of probability $3^{-j}$ each. The worst-multiplier miss probability is $2\cdot3^{-j}$ conditional on $s\ne0$. The mean number of tests capped at $m$ is $2-3^{1-m}$ for a multiplier chosen to maximize that number, and $\tfrac32(1-3^{-m})$ for either fixed regime. When $s=0$, $y=-x$ and the two primes have the same inertness in *every* field defined by these characters. An adversary covering both orders defeats the entire family in that case. The exact optimum worst-mode failure with all $m$ characters, before conditioning on $s$, is therefore
$$
                        3^{1-m}-2\cdot9^{-m}.
$$
These are exact limiting probabilities at fixed $m$. The last probability is unavoidable even when every cyclic cubic subfield of the chosen compositum can be tried. At $s\ne0$ the cases $x=0$ and $x=-s$ are unavoidable in the respective regimes.

The mean is optimal among deterministic policies whose field choices use $s$ and earlier field gcds. For fixed $s\ne0$, if the first row has $\lambda(s)=0$, covering both orders forces a miss for every $x$ and the capped worst mean is at least two for $m\ge2$. Otherwise follow the path on which every single-order gcd is one. At depth $j$ it contains the common kernel of the $j$ chosen rows and has probability at least $3^{-j}$. In the dual-order regime follow the responses at $x=-s$. A row with $\lambda(s)=0$ then returns 1 and every other row returns $N$. The same responses occur throughout the translate by $-s$ of the common kernel of the rows on that path. This contributes at least $3^{-j}$ and is disjoint from the first path by the first query. Summing gives the claimed bound. This model permits reading all the indicated cubic characters and full failed gcds; an algorithm using other numerical information in $N$ or $M$ is outside the optimality proof.

If the algorithm randomly permutes the same basis and the multiplier is fixed before that order is drawn, the limiting worst expected mean becomes $3/2$. For a pair having $b$ bad coordinates for one regime, the random-order mean is $(m+1)/(m-b+1)$ if $b<m$, or $m$ if $b=m$. Each of the two bad counts has marginal $\operatorname{Binomial}(m,1/3)$ in the uniform conditional model. Both concentrate with exponentially small tails, so the mean of the larger of the two expected costs tends to $3/2$. The known-regime lower bound follows from the single-order kernel path for each deterministic realization.

### The cost of a fixed list

For a fixed list of $j$ cubic fields let $z$ be the probability that $p$ is inert in none, and $C$ the probability that the two inertness patterns agree. The worst-mode miss probability is $z+C-z^2$. The product-root Burnside bound gives $z\ge3^{-j}$ and the odd Walsh coefficient argument gives $C\ge(5/9)^j$. For $z\le1/2$ these imply a lower bound $3^{-j}+(5/9)^j-9^{-j}$. For $z>1/2$ the miss probability is at least $z$ and the same bound follows when $j\ge2$. At $j=1$ a transitive cubic group is $C_3$ or $S_3$, giving $z=1/3$ or $2/3$ and the same $7/9$ miss probability. Independent cyclic fields attain equality at every prefix. Summing the three geometric series gives $3/2+9/4-9/8=21/8$. [Burnside and Walsh bounds](prime-index-miss-density.md#optimal-fixed-probe-order-for-one-covered-prime)

### Uniformity at the growing cutoff

Choose the initial prime conductors up to the largest prefix whose product is at most $N^{1/8}$. Then $m\asymp\log X/\log\log X$, $\ell_m=O(\log X)$ and the conductor product is below $\sqrt X$ for large $X$. Replacing this public stopping rule by fixed lower and upper cutoffs defined with $X^2\le N\le4X^2$ changes it by at most one field. For each such fixed cutoff the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality bounds the actual probability of each pair of full character profiles by an absolute constant times its uniform-model probability. Indeed each profile consists of $\varphi(\prod\ell_i)/3^m$ reduced progressions. Summing over the at most two cutoffs preserves uniform upper bounds.

All possible responding fields have conductor $Q\le\ell_m^2=O((\log X)^2)$. A degree-three defining generator is the Gaussian period summed over the kernel of the primitive cubic character at its conductor. Each conjugate has absolute value at most $Q$. Its nontrivial Fourier coefficient on the three cosets is a nonzero primitive Gauss sum, so it generates the cubic field. Products of periods, the minimal polynomial and the multiplication table can be computed in the cyclotomic polynomial ring in time polynomial in $Q$. The power basis and its inverse embedding matrix have entries bounded by fixed powers of $\log X$, the latter by cofactors and the nonzero integral discriminant.

Write $W=\log X$, $Y=W^B$. Uniformly in these fields there are $\gg Y/\log Y$ split prime markers in $[Y/2,Y]$ for a sufficiently large absolute $B$. Apply Thorner–Zaman with base $\mathbb Q$, abelian group of order three and maximum character conductor at most $Q$. There is no nontrivial real character and the Riemann zeta function has no real zero in the exceptional interval. Their relative error is as small a fixed constant as needed at both endpoints by increasing $B$. [Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02823#page=3)

At each split marker $v$, one evaluation map from the power-basis order to $\mathbb F_v$ has kernel of lattice index $v$. Pigeonholing a cube gives a nonzero element of this kernel with coordinates $O(v^{1/3})$. Its conjugates are $O(W^D v^{1/3})$ for an absolute $D$. Its norm is a nonzero multiple of $v$, at most $O(W^{3D}v)$, so other markers do not divide it when $B>3D+1$. In the smooth-product proof, choose $r=(2/(B/3+D)+o(1))\log X/\log\log X$ factors, allowing a further fixed power of $W$ to convert back to coordinates at the end. The log of the number of distinct products is at least $r((B-1)\log\log X+O(1))$. Thus the exponent of the exceptional *prime count* is at most $6(1+3D)/(B+3D)+o(1)$ by the same additive sieve. Choose $B$ so it is below $1/2$. The order index is polynomial in $W$, so all primes in the interval give the same algebra as the maximal order. A union over $O(m^2)$ possible fields costs only $X^{o(1)}$. Testing the corresponding nonzero elements with coordinates up to a sufficiently large fixed power of $\log N$ is deterministic and polynomial in the bit length of the input and supplied exponent.

The profile and generation estimates give $O(3^{-j})$ worst-mode probability at every prefix $1\le j\le m$, plus a power-saving exceptional fraction. The resulting cutoff failure is $\exp(-\Omega(\log X/\log\log X))$. For convergence of the mean to the exact stated constant, the first $j$ rows depend on only a fixed prefix if the pivot occurs there. The probability of having no pivot in the first $h$ coordinates is $O(3^{-h})$ since the uniform sum vector has independent coordinates. First fix $h$ and use the prime number theorem in fixed reduced progressions, then let $h$ tend to infinity. Summation against the geometric prefix bound proves convergence. For the fixed-list mean the analogous prefix events already depend only on the prescribed fields.
