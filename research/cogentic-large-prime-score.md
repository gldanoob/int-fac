# Large primes in the Cogentic conditional score

Cogentic's order extractor cancels ideal valuations as integers. Above the height of its integer marks, any additional ideal row belongs to one column and forces that column out of the rational relation space. Bounded semismoothness below the mark height can supply new relations through exact cycles. On the fixed schedule, even the band $(B,8B]$ changes the normalized mass enough to exceed the existing score's mean budget. ## Private squares and exact cancellation

Fix one field and let $T$ consist of the additional ideal valuation rows. Use distinct marks $a$ with $|a-a'|\le A$. If an ideal above $p$ divides both marked linear factors, it contains the nonzero integer $a-a'$, so $p\mid a-a'$. For $p>A$ such a row therefore has just one nonzero entry. The shifted valuations used by the extractor agree with ordinary valuations at these primes since the leading coefficient is supported below the cutoff. Even a positive even valuation then forces the entire column coefficient to vanish in $\ker_{\mathbb Q}T$. In particular, if the only enlargement consists of values $s r^2$, where $s$ is an original squarefree band product and $r$ has all prime divisors above $A$, imposing exact cancellation of the added rows recovers the original pool. The target generator lies below this cutoff and has prescribed zero valuations at the added rows. [Valuation and target equations](cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.md#algebraic-interface-and-existence-to-search-step)

There is also a probability bound for these square tails. Fix the slope in a centered original intercept box. At a mark the norm is an affine function of the intercept with step $q_a=P_0\ell |a-m|$. If $p>A$ divides $q_a$, the grid unit condition prevents a hit. The possible protected-prime exception has $p=\ell\le B<A$. Otherwise $p^2$ forces one residue modulo $p^2$, and its fraction in an intercept interval of length $T_0$ is at most $p^{-2}+T_0^{-1}$. For height at most $h$ the probability of a square divisor above $A$ at a given mark is bounded by
\[
 O\left(\frac1{A\log A}+\frac{\sqrt h}{T_0}\right).
\]
The first term follows by partial summation over primes. On the fixed schedule, $h=\exp((100001+o(1))V)$ and the intercept length is at least $U=\exp((100000+o(1))V)$. A union bound over both fields and $O(A)$ marks gives $O(1/\log A)+\exp((-49999.5+o(1))V)$. The full-box probability that a private square tail changes the pool is therefore $o(1)$. The same estimate for individual intervals with shorter $T_0$ loses its small error at the square-root threshold. Exact cancellation still removes those columns at every prefix. ## One bounded extra prime

Keep the original squarefree band and normalization. Independently in each field allow the original product $s$ or $sp$ for one prime $B<p\le8B$. At a nonprotected unit mark, omit primes dividing $q_a$. For any other $p$ in the new band, requiring $p\mid f(a)$ has probability $(1+o(1))/p$ by Gaussian congruence counting. In that class the divided value is in a progression of modulus $q_a$, centered at $Y/p$ with width divided by $p$. The relative width remains the same, so the long Gaussian estimate applies uniformly. The leading saddle in the smooth density ratio contributes $p^{1-\alpha}(1+o(1))$. Indeed $\log p=O(\log B)$, the curvature is $\asymp u(\log B)^2$, and differentiating the log-density with respect to $\log Y$ gives $\alpha-1$ at leading order, with an $o(1)$ error in the log-ratio. The residue factors cancel. Thus the normalized contribution of this extra prime is $p^{-\alpha}(1+o(1))$. Different extra-prime events are disjoint. The $O(L)$ omissions from $(B,8B]$ have total weight $o(1)$. [Gaussian progression and saddle estimates](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) · [Fixed normalization](cogentic-intermediate-score-budget.md#dyadic-subboxes-and-the-linear-mass-term)

Write $\xi=(1-\alpha)\log B$. By the prime number theorem, the saddle equation gives $u\sim e^\xi/\xi$, since the leading sum $\sum_{\sqrt B<p\le B}p^{-\alpha}\log p$ is asymptotic to $B^{1-\alpha}/(1-\alpha)$. Also $\xi\sim z/3$. On the fixed schedule $u\sim(100001/1000)D$ and $\log B\sim1000Dz$. A second application of the prime number theorem gives
\[
 \sum_{B<p\le8B}p^{-\alpha}
 \sim \frac{e^\xi}{\log B}\log 8
 \longrightarrow \kappa=\frac{100001\log 8}{3\cdot1000^2}
 =0.0693154\ldots.
\]
The exceptional target-root classes have grid mass $o(1)$. By field independence and positivity the new mass statistic $C'$ with the original weights satisfies $\mathbf E C'\ge(1+\kappa)^2+o(1)$. Its square penalty alone has expectation at least $64((1+\kappa)^2-1)^2+o(1)=1.3167\ldots+o(1)$ by Jensen's inequality. This exceeds the threshold of one used by the score. Recentered weights and a new rank estimate would require a separate argument. [Original scalar penalty and thresholds](cogentic-conditional-score.md#a-computable-nonnegative-score)

There is also an exact reduction for uniform scalar counters. Let $K_B(n)$ be the Boolean predicate for this one-extra-prime admission, with signs removed and zero rejected. Fix a prime $p\in(B,8B]$ by bounded sieving. Then $K_B(pn)=I_B(n)$ for every integer $n$, where $I_B$ is the squarefree band test. The mandatory factor $p$ uses the full extra-prime allowance and an admitted value has valuation one at $p$. The scalar progression $(pc,pd,T)$ gives precisely the original fraction on $(c,d,T)$, with the same additive accuracy and an $O(\log B)$ increase in input length. The scaled progression can have a nonunit residue. This reduction concerns counters uniform over progressions. [Required scalar counter](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md)

## Exact cycles and the surviving rank

Let $S$ be the matrix of retained rows, including the protected rows, and let $T$ be the matrix of exact added valuations. A target with zero added valuations has its retained vector in $S(\ker_{\mathbb Q} T)$. Over the reals the corresponding Gram matrix is $S\Pi_{\ker T}S^{\mathsf T}$ with $\Pi_{\ker T}$ the orthogonal projection onto the kernel of $T$. The unprojected matrix $SS^{\mathsf T}$ can have full rank while a protected target is missing. Take $S=I_2$ and $T=(1,1)$, for which $S(\ker T)$ is the line spanned by $(1,-1)$ and misses $(1,0)$.

A triangle of unit unsigned incidences has determinant of absolute value two over $\mathbb Q$ and has a binary parity cycle. When a column has exactly one extra prime per field with valuation one, the extra rows form a bipartite multigraph. Negating all the rows of the second field turns its unsigned incidence into oriented incidence. Alternating signs on an even cycle then give exact valuation cancellation. Its contribution to the protected and archimedean ranks is governed by the projected matrix. The old proof estimates at most two mark tests per field. In the independent Boolean edge-presence variables of a chordless $k$-cycle, its completion indicator is the product of all $k$ edge tests and has multilinear degree $k$. The old moment bound therefore supplies neither an estimate of general cycle completion nor a lower bound on the surviving rank.
