# One random intercept for Cogentic's two-polynomial order search

Use the published two-field order extractor and the [verified-RH parameter schedule](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md). Put \(L=\log N\), \(H=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}\), and \(V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}\). A whole factoring trial uses **exactly \(\log_2(4UA)\) random bits** to draw one integer intercept. It enumerates three deterministic lists, each of size \(\exp(O(H))\), and returns the complete prime factorization with probability \(1-o(1)\). All reported prime powers and their product are checked. The cost of a trial is \(\exp(O(H))\). Repetition and a deterministic fallback give a zero-error complete algorithm with the same expected time. The public powers of two \(U,A\) here are for the composite that remains after preprocessing; \(\log U=(100000+o(1))V\) in the cited schedule. Enumerating the intercept as well gives deterministic cost \(U\exp(O(H))\) for this schedule. The original search enumerates \(U^4\exp(O(H))\) perturbations. Its [four-coordinate randomized trial](cogentic-finite-coins.md) uses \(4\log_2 U+2\log_2 A+8\) random bits. [Cogentic, Lemmas 7.5, 9.4–9.5 and Proposition 10.1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf)

## A cyclic distribution and two affine evaluations

Write \(G=\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\), with \(M\) a power of two. There is an explicit probability measure \(\mu\) of support size \(\operatorname{poly}(\log M,1/\epsilon)\) with \(|\mathbb E_\mu e^{2\pi i k y/M}|\le\epsilon\) at each nonzero \(k\in G\). Such cyclic constructions are classical. One elementary version chooses a uniform prime \(r\in(T,2T]\), a uniform \(0\le j<r\), and outputs \(jr^{-1}\bmod M\). Set \(K=\lceil 10/\epsilon\rceil\) and take \(T\) sufficiently large of order \(K^3(1+\log M)^2\). To check the bias, write \(rr^{-1}=1+hM\) with the inverse an integer in \([0,M)\). For \(0<k<M\), the conditional character average is a geometric sum with angle \((kh+k/M)/r\). If the least absolute residue of \(kh\bmod r\) is greater than \(K\), its magnitude is at most \(1/(2(K-1))\). Otherwise \(r\) divides some \(k+iM\), \(|i|\le K\), since \(hM\equiv-1\pmod r\). All these integers are nonzero. At most \((2K+1)\log((K+1)M)/\log T\) primes in the interval divide their product. The prime number theorem leaves an exceptional proportion \(O(K\log((K+1)M)/T)\). Adjusting the constant handles bounded sizes. Deterministically sieving the primes and listing their arithmetic progressions has the claimed polynomial cost. Only the support is required by the algorithm. [Cyclic small-bias constructions and the inverse-prime construction](https://people.csail.mit.edu/akavia/akaviaCOLT10.pdf) · [More general explicit abelian Cayley expanders](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2021.24)

Let \(x\) be uniform on \(G\), independently of \(y\), and take arbitrary integers \(a,b\). For functions \(F,G_1,h_0,h_1\) on the group, replacing uniform \(y\) by \(y\sim\mu\) changes
\[
 \mathbb E\big[h_0(x)h_1(y)F(x+ay)G_1(x+by)\big]
\]
by at most \(\epsilon\|h_0\|_{\rm A}\|h_1\|_{\rm A}\|F\|_2\|G_1\|_2\). Here the A-norm is the sum of absolute normalized Fourier coefficients and the \(L^2\) norm uses uniform probability. In fact after expanding \(h_0,h_1\) at frequencies \(v,w\), averaging over \(x\) pairs the frequency \(k\) of \(F\) with \(-v-k\) of \(G_1\). The change at frequency \(w+ak+b(-v-k)\) is at most \(\epsilon\), and is zero at frequency zero. Cauchy–Schwarz and Parseval bound the sum of \(|\widehat F(k)\widehat G_1(-v-k)|\) by the stated two norms. This works for noninvertible differences \(a-b\), including repeated evaluation points.

For two such fields draw \(x_1\) uniformly, put \(x_2=x_1+s\), and draw \(s,y_1,y_2\) independently from \(\mu\). To compare a product of field observables, first apply the preceding estimate to the two marginal expectations. Next form their slope-averaged functions \(g_i(x_i)\) and use
\[
 \big|\mathbb E_{x,s}g_1(x)g_2(x+s)-\mathbb E g_1\,\mathbb E g_2\big|
       \le\epsilon\|g_1\|_2\|g_2\|_2 .
\]
This follows by Fourier pairing under uniform \(x\) and the cyclic bias bound. The three short draws in this comparison can all be fully enumerated in an actual trial.

## Gaussian weights and local tests

Use deviations from the two paper centers \(\bar u_j=L_0^2S_j\), with \(S_0=UA,S_1=U\). Take \(M\) the least power of two at least \(8L_0UA\), and represent its residues in \([-M/2,M/2)\). For \(S\in\{UA,U\}\), let \(w_S\) equal \(M\) times the probability of the discrete centered Gaussian of width \(S\), restricted and normalized on \([-L_0S,L_0S]\). This restriction changes the Gaussian probability by \(O(e^{-cL_0^2})\). The normalized group mean of \(w_S\) is one. Its supremum is \(O(M/S)\), and its total variation around a cycle is at most twice its supremum by monotonicity on either half. Summation by parts bounds its A-norm by \(O((M/S)\log(2M))\). Also the A-norm of \(z\mapsto e^{2\pi i\theta\operatorname{rep}(z)}\) is \(O(\log(2M))\), uniformly in real \(\theta\), by the geometric sum over one full interval of representatives.

The product weight
\[
 W=w_{UA}(x_1)w_U(y_1)w_{UA}(x_2)w_U(y_2)
\]
is used only in the proof. On its support each \(x_i+a y_i\), \(1\le a\le A\), lies in \([-2L_0UA,2L_0UA]\), where the cyclic representative is the exact integer sum. Thus any one-row squarefree smoothness indicator for field \(i\) is a function of \(x_i+a y_i\), with the deterministic centers and unperturbed polynomial incorporated in the function. A pair of rows gives the two arbitrary bounded functions permitted above.

An auxiliary root condition \(f_i(r)=0\pmod p\), or the condition on \(f'_i(r)\), is an affine congruence in \((x_i,y_i)\) because the perturbation is \(P_0\ell(X-m)(u_0+Xu_1)\). Its indicator is an average of characters \(e^{2\pi i(\theta_0 x_i+\theta_1 y_i+\theta_2)}\). Each is separable, using the integer representatives, and the total absolute weight in that average is one. A bounded number of intersections or complements has bounded coefficient sum. The A-norm bounds above and \(M/U=O(L_0A)\) show that an unnormalized two-field rectangle consisting of Gaussian weights, at most two row indicators per field, and the specified root conditions, differs from its independent-Gaussian expectation by at most \(\epsilon\exp(O(H))+O(e^{-cL_0^2})\). This applies also with only one field or no evaluation rows. In particular \(Z=\mathbb E W=1+O(\epsilon\exp(O(H)))\).

## Which moments the comparison transfers

Use the weighted law \(W/Z\) of the cyclic construction. The moment proof in Cogentic's §10 needs the following bounded-degree expressions before its deterministic peeling and spectral argument.

* For the total relation weight \(\widehat C\), its mean and second moment use one and two evaluation rows. For a fixed pair of row indices, the conditional centered square in Proposition 10.1(1) uses two rows, together with the prescribed root events \(E_{\alpha,\beta}\). The row coefficients, Archimedean reference coordinates, and the model matrix entries given in §10 depend on the index and the unperturbed reference values. Their values for a fixed index do not depend on other random root occurrences.
* The means and variances of the factor-base mass and projected weight norms on page 53 use at most two root conditions per field. The expected mass of multiple or shared roots uses the affine derivative or the two independent field conditions. In the GRH transfer the medium-conductor rows are absent.
* The estimates for the squared diagonal, Frobenius norms, and protected \(\ell\)-rows on pages 53–54 are sums of the same centered squares. The bounds on the probability of bad protected diagonals follow from those squares. Only Markov and Chebyshev are used after these expectations. Once the resulting inequalities hold, all the subsequent deletions and the rank certificate are deterministic consequences of the source's proof.

For clarity about additive precision, each evaluation index ranges over at most \(A\) values; each potential root index ranges over at most \(2B^2\) values. Thus these means, squares, and norm sums expand into \(\exp(O(H))\) rectangles. The same bound applies to the sum of the absolute scalar coefficients in each expansion. Indeed the reciprocal product of the two one-point probabilities entering \(w_{\rm eq}(a)\) is \(\exp(O(u\log u))=\exp(O(H))\), every incidence rescaling is at most \(B\), and all reference log coordinates and remaining dimensions are polynomial in \(L,A,B\). The unconditional centered expectation can be used before division by \(\Pr(E_{\alpha,\beta})\). If this conditional comparison is made explicitly, each allowed nontrivial root event has independent-Gaussian probability \(\gg B^{-4}\) by the source's modular Gaussian calculation; the forced generator roots occur with probability one. The target errors on pages 53–54 are bounded below by \(\exp(-O(H))\), including the \(\ell^{-\alpha_s}\) factors and the negative powers of \(B\). Choose \(\epsilon=(2+ABL_0)^{-C}\) with a sufficiently large absolute effective \(C\), larger than the fixed powers in these finite moment expansions and tolerances. All comparison errors can then be absorbed while retaining the positive spectral margin and success probability \(1-o(1)\), uniformly for each generator prime. This compares the proof's moments directly; it does not require the entire successful-rank event to be a union of few rectangles. [Moment and peeling proof, pp. 51–55](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=51) · [Retuned moment tolerances](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md)

## Extracting all primes with one intercept

First remove all known small prime powers by trial division through \(B\), recognize primes by AKS, and take a perfect-power root until the surviving composite is not a perfect power. Record multiplicities. If the residual size changes, use its schedule; one may initially take a monotone majorant of the trial cutoffs through the original bit length. In each polynomial search remove from the sampling grid any \(a\) with \(\gcd(a-m,N)>1\), by integer gcd. This gives every inversion in Lemma 7.5 on the surviving grid. In the grid-sieve count inside an interval \(I'\) and class modulo \(M'\le B^4\), the additional absolute loss divided by \(|I'|/M'\) is at most
\[
       \sum_{p\mid N}(1/p+M'/|I'|)\le
       O(L)(1/B+B^4/|I'|).
\]
Indeed all remaining primes exceed \(B\) and are coprime to the grid moduli (their prime factors are at most \(B\)). The [grid length bound](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md#the-grid-its-sieve-and-its-real-roots) makes this \(\exp(-\Omega(H))\). The same relative loss is below the source's weighted Brun allowance, by the same partition into \(I'\) used for bounded-variation weights. The analytic proof and all algebraic prerequisites thus apply to this filtered grid without creating separate random splitting jobs at its rejected entries.

For a trial keep the entire remaining original modulus. Use the whole prime interval \((B/2,B]\). On finding a verified integer \(E_\ell\) with \(\ell^{E_\ell}\equiv1\pmod N\), write \(E_\ell=2^s h\), \(h\) odd, and take the gcds with \(N\) of \(\ell^{2^j h}-1\) through \(0\le j\le s\). Each of these gcds contains either the full power \(p^{v_p(N)}\) or none of it for each odd \(p\mid N\). The order of \(\ell\) modulo that prime power differs from its order modulo \(p\) by a power of \(p\). The odd part is killed by \(h\), and the two-adic valuations consequently agree. Successive gcds therefore distinguish two prime powers precisely when the corresponding local orders have different two-adic valuations. Refine the current factors by each such gcd and test the resulting blocks for primality and perfect prime powers. The refinement costs at most a polynomial in the input length per gcd and at most \(\log_2 N\) blocks.

For each pair of distinct prime divisors, the [quadratic character sign argument](cogentic-dense-exponents.md#a-single-successful-prime-can-give-the-divisor-directly) gives at least \((1/2-o(1))\) of the primes in this interval with the required order-valuation difference, uniformly over the pair. Under \(W/Z\) the expected **fraction** of bases failing to return a certificate is \(o(1)\). Markov shows that a single tuple gives certificates for at least three quarters of the bases with probability \(1-o(1)\). Such a tuple separates all pairs simultaneously and completes the factorization.

It remains to generate only the first intercept. Under the weighted joint law its marginal density relative to uniform on the torus is
\[
 Z^{-1} w_{UA}(x)\,\mathbb E_{s\sim\mu}w_{UA}(x+s)\,
                     (\mathbb E_{y\sim\mu}w_U(y))^2
       =w_{UA}(x)(1+o(1))
\]
uniformly on the support. Both expectations are \(1+O(\epsilon\exp(O(H)))\) by cyclic bias and the A-norm bounds. A set of intercepts of Gaussian probability \(1-o(1)\) consequently admits at least one successful tuple above each of its members. In the integer interval \([-2UA,2UA)\) uniform probability is bounded by an absolute multiple of this Gaussian probability. Draw one integer uniformly there, costing exactly \(\log_2(4UA)\) bits. Enumerate \(s,y_1,y_2\) in the cyclic support, discarding deviations outside the Gaussian truncation, and try the resulting polynomial pairs for the generator primes. It suffices to retain and combine all certified gcds across different tuples. The lists and all loops besides the intercept have size \(\exp(O(H))\). Fresh independent trials give mean \((1+o(1))\log_2(4UA)\) random bits at growing residual size. Deterministic dovetailing handles bounded exceptional sizes and every coin tape. The uniform trial guarantee applies to the entire computation after the single deterministic preprocessing stage.
