# Order limits for moving elliptic norm lifts

On balanced products $pq$ with $X<p<q\le2X$, take $L=\log X$, $W=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}$ and $V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}$. With smoothness cutoff $B=\exp(O(W))$ and residual-order cutoff $B^2$, the moving norm-lift menu yields a model gcd or a local residual-order hit on an infinite-order rational mark on at most an $\exp(-\Omega(V))$ fraction of pairs. This uses the uniform Dedekind-zeta strip in the [marked-order tail](smooth-marked-order-tail.md). Rational torsion marks cannot supply a split at good primes $p,q>17$. Applying the smooth multiplier one small prime at a time gives the same bound for checked intermediate additions. The monolithic binary ladder has additional gcd opportunities. ## A fixed catalogue for each prime dyad

The norm generation takes primes $s,\ell\le Q(N)$ where $Q(N)$ is a fixed power of $\log(2N)$. Include all primes up to the maximum of $Q(N)$ in the dyad and all four markings. The rational norm generator depends on $(s,\ell)$ before reduction modulo $N$. Even if one chooses either prime above $\ell$ and any generator of its $h_s$th power, there are at most twelve choices for each pair, since generators of a fixed ideal differ by a unit. The resulting catalogue has $K=L^{O(1)}$ members. Counting the reduced forms with first coefficient at most $\sqrt{s/3}$ gives the elementary bound $h_s=O(s)$. The norm identity then bounds the logarithmic heights of the integral coordinates, the Weierstrass coefficients and the marked point by $O(Q\log(2Q))$. The catalogue size and logarithmic heights remain polynomial in $L$ for the reciprocal marking and for polynomially many fixed rational conic parameters of polynomial logarithmic height. [Fixed marking and conic parameters](cogentic-fixed-marking-twists.md)

For a single model, the nonzero differences of the four coordinates have total logarithmic size $L^{O(1)}$. The fraction of dyadic primes dividing any of them is $O(L^{O(1)}/X)$ by the prime number theorem. Outside these exceptions the Weierstrass model has good reduction and the three rational two-torsion roots remain distinct. If the marked point is rational torsion, reduction is injective on the subgroup generated by it and the two rational torsion directions at every good $p>17$. Its scalar relations, affine equalities up to sign and coincidences with two-torsion are then the same at both primes. Only infinite-order rational marks need the marked-order estimate. [Uniform height estimate and torsion reduction](smooth-marked-order-tail.md)

## The residual-order and intermediate checks

Let $U$ be the odd smooth multiplier, and put $P=[2^k U]R$ after sufficiently many doubles to kill every local two-primary part. A local relation $[j]P=O$ with $1\le j\le B^2$ implies $\operatorname{ord}(R\bmod p)\mid 2^k Uj$. The same implication covers a reported common relation, whether or not the subsequent odd continuation splits. For a search bound $D$, the baby and giant tables use multiples $i$ and $jw$, with $w=\lceil\sqrt D\rceil$ and $j\le w$. A same-abscissa comparison witnesses a nonzero relation with coefficient $|jw\pm i|=O(D)$. A nonunit denominator in generating the tables also witnesses a relation of size $O(D)$, since equality of abscissas over a good field means equality up to sign, and a tangent denominator vanishes at two-torsion. Strip the two-part of any reported common relation before multiplying it into $U$. A one-sided coincidence with a torsion root in the final doubling chain is again a local annihilation after one more doubling. [Checked curve addition](../experiments/cogentic-elliptic-cycle-split.py)

For a factor-by-factor schedule, apply each prime in $U$ separately, with repetitions for prime powers. While multiplying a point already powered by a smooth integer $u_0$ by one prime $r\le B$, all intermediate coefficients relative to $[u_0]R$ are at most $r$. Any checked gcd over good primes gives a nonzero relation $\operatorname{ord}(R\bmod p)\mid u_0 j$ with $|j|\le 2r$. The same argument applies to a separate continuation by the odd part of a residual relation of size $O(D)$. The factor-by-factor schedule uses $B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ group operations to apply the smooth part. In contrast, binary multiplication by the full integer $U$ compares multiples whose coefficients are binary prefixes and their small sums or differences. Smoothness of $U$ provides no applicable cofactor bound on those integers. The marked-order tail thus bounds the designated stage hits, and bounds the entire checked search for the factor-by-factor schedule.

The original tail admits $j\le X^{1/8}$, sufficient for all the coefficients above with $D=B^2$. It extends to $j\le X^a$ for any fixed $a<1/3$. Choose $a<b<1/3$. For an infinite-order mark the sum of the logarithms of the denominators of $x([m]R)$ over $m\le X^b$ is $O(L^{O(1)}X^{3b})$. A reduced order at most $X^b$ contributes a denominator divisible by $p$. Thus such primes have proportion $O(L^{O(1)}X^{3b-1})$. At every other prime any successful relation forces the full $B$-smooth part of the order to exceed $X^{b-a}$. Apply the same truncated positive divisor moment, with truncation exponent reduced to be below $b-a$. For $B$ bounded by $\exp(CW)$, the exponent $u\log(2u)$ of that estimate, using the envelope $u=L/(CW)$, is $(2/(3C)+o(1))V$. Taking the union over $K$ members, at both primes, preserves the asserted $\exp(-\Omega(V))$ bound. Any dyad-wide catalogue of $\exp(O(W))$ members of polynomial logarithmic height also has this property, even if the input selects a member adaptively. A per-input time bound alone does not bound the total dyad-wide catalogue. [Positive divisor moment](smooth-marked-order-tail.md#truncated-smooth-divisors-with-positive-weights)

## Cardinality and arbitrarily many twists at fixed bases

There is a separate uniform bound that needs no count of the marks or twist parameters. Fix a rational Legendre parameter $b$ of polynomial logarithmic height. For a good prime and either quadratic twist of the Legendre curve, if $d$ divides the group exponent, at least $\varphi(d)$ torsion vectors of exact order $d$ are fixed by the twisted Frobenius. For $p\nmid d$ the reference Frobenius acts on them by $+1$ or $-1$. Thus it fixes at least $\varphi(d)/2$ unordered classes $\{v,-v\}$ in the Galois set of exact $d$-torsion modulo sign. The orbit count and total degrees of the quotient are bounded by those of the original torsion set. The same ramification and discriminant estimates apply. The positive divisor moment therefore bounds a large $B$-smooth part of the exponent at either twist simultaneously. The classical CM version also works by allowing both signs in its Frobenius test. [Torsion orbit bounds](smooth-marked-order-tail.md#a-uniform-rational-torsion-sieve)

The finite group of an elliptic curve over a prime field has at most two generators. Its $B$-smooth exponent is therefore at least the square root of the $B$-smooth part of its cardinality. For any fixed $\epsilon>0$ and a catalogue of $\exp(O(W))$ Legendre bases, shrinking the divisor truncation exponent shows that, outside a prime fraction $\exp(-\Omega_\epsilon(V))$, the $B$-smooth part of **both** twist cardinalities for every base is below $X^\epsilon$. This covers all four norm-lift markings by taking the ratios of root differences of their Legendre models. By Hasse, for any fixed $a<1$, taking $\epsilon<1-a$ excludes cardinalities dividing a $B$-smooth integer times any integer $1\le j\le X^a$. Particular marked orders can be much smaller than the cardinality.

On the remaining primes, the kernel of multiplication by $2^kU$ has at most $X^\epsilon$ points in either twist. In its image the kernel of multiplication by $j$ has at most $j^2$ points. Thus at most $2X^\epsilon\sum_{j\le D}j^2$ points across the two twists have residual order at most $D$. For the marking $c$ in the conic construction, identify the curve over the prime field with the appropriate twist $y^2=D_\pm x(x-1)(x-b)$, using a fixed representative $D_\pm$ of its square class. The marked abscissa in this model is $x=c$. So the same bound applies to usable values of $c$, with a further factor of at most four for conic parameter residues where the rational map has degree four. At $D=B^2$ this is $X^{\epsilon+o(1)}$ exceptional residues per prime. Such a full-field bound gives no guarantee for a short interval of conic parameters, or for a particular adaptively selected mark.

## Analytic assumptions

The mixed non-CM and CM bounds above use the uniform fixed-width zero-free strip asserted in Cureton–Thorner's Theorem 1.1 for the Dedekind zeta functions of the varying torsion-orbit fields. The CM argument from Hecke Brun–Titchmarsh needs no such strip. In the original four markings $(0,1,b,-1)$ the three retained roots normalize to Legendre parameters $(b+1)/2,b+1,2,b$, respectively. The norm identity gives square classes $\ell$ and $s$ for $b$ and $b-1$, so $b\ne2,3$ since $\ell,s$ are odd primes. The only rational CM parameter greater than one is 2, by integrality of the CM invariant. The classical CM proof applies to the marking at $b$. The other three markings and the reciprocal and conic variants are non-CM. The $Q=\lceil\log_2(2N)\rceil^6$ separation proof uses Dirichlet RH independently. [Cureton–Thorner, Theorem 1.1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3) · [Classical CM estimate](smooth-marked-order-tail.md#classical-proof-of-the-cm-upper-bound) · [Legendre invariants](cogentic-fixed-marking-twists.md#what-the-twist-can-change)

The same uniform strip can replace Dirichlet RH in separation with a larger polynomial menu. It gives the strip for Dirichlet characters by cyclotomic factorization. For a fixed smooth weight supported in $(1/2,1)$, the explicit formula bounds the weighted von Mangoldt error at $Q$ by $O(Q^{1-1/40}\log^2(4NQ))$ for the conductors in the sign projectors, which are at most $4NQ$. Prime powers and primes dividing a modulus contribute lower-order terms for $Q=(\log(2N))^{120}$. The error is then $o(Q)$, and the independent sign projector still has a positive main term. This enlargement preserves the dyad-wide catalogue and height bounds.
