# Odd powers of imaginary quadratic classes

Odd powering can accelerate factoring on inputs whose quadratic order has small residual odd class order after smooth-prime powering. It preserves the relative CRT sign distribution in each surviving square fiber exactly. The gain is input-dependent. An infinite family from Lenstra–Pomerance gives a polynomial obstruction to this screening route for every multiplier discriminant. [Lenstra–Pomerance, §11](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=29)

## Sign law under powering

Let \(N\) be odd and have \(k\ge2\) distinct prime divisors. Set \(G=\operatorname{Pic}(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-N}])\), \(T=G[2]\), and \(S=\mu_2(\mathbb Z/N)/\{\pm1\}\). The relative sign map \(\sigma:T\to S\) is surjective, with \(|S|=2^{k-1}\). Fix an odd \(E\), set \(G_E=\{g^E:g\in G\}\), and write \(D_E=|\ker(g\mapsto g^E)|\). A uniform \(g\) gives a uniform \(h=g^E\) on \(G_E\). Its square is uniform on \(G_E^2\), which has size

\[
M_E=|G_E^2|=\frac{|G|}{|T|D_E}.
\]

Every square fiber in \(G_E\) is a full \(T\)-torsor since \(\tau^E=\tau\) for \(\tau\in T\). For two original classes in the same square fiber with ratio \(\tau\), the ratio after powering is still \(\tau\), so their relative sign is still \(\sigma(\tau)\). Even when distinct original square fibers merge, each powered fiber has uniform relative signs. At a unit leading coefficient, a separate fair global sign gives uniformity on all roots of that coefficient. The joint law applies after tests on the reduced square class. Deterministic powering and squaring preserve total-variation error bounds on the joint law, and \(r\) independent observations with per-draw error \(\varepsilon\) have joint error at most \(r\varepsilon\). [Reduced square roots and their sign law](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md) · [Direct reduced-class sampler](keybreak-direct-reduced-class-sampler.md)

For \(\Delta=-4mN\) with odd \(m\) coprime to \(N\), the sign law projects from the modulus \(mN\) to \(N\), because every CRT sign pattern at \(N\) extends to one at \(mN\). The arguments on cardinalities hold for any finite abelian class group.

## A saturated odd exponent

Take \(X\ge \max(B,|G|)\), with \(\log X=O(\log|\Delta|)\), and form

\[
E_B=\prod_{\substack{3\le p\le B\\p\text{ prime}}}p^{\lfloor\log_p X\rfloor}.
\]

A class-number upper bound depending only on the discriminant suffices for \(X\). In particular Lenstra–Pomerance give \(h(\Delta)<\sqrt{|\Delta|}\log|\Delta|\). Saturation kills every odd \(p\)-primary component with \(p\le B\), including all repeated prime powers. The odd part of \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,B)\) can leave part of such a component, as with \(C_9\) at \(B=3\). Write \(G_p\) for the \(p\)-primary part and \(Q_B=\prod_{p>B,\ p\text{ odd}}|G_p|\). Powering by \(E_B\) permutes \(G_2\) and each remaining odd component, so

\[
D_{E_B}=\prod_{3\le p\le B}|G_p|,
\qquad
M_{E_B}=\frac{|G_2|}{|T|}\,Q_B .
\]

Prime enumeration and sequential binary powering by the prime-power factors give bit cost \(B\operatorname{poly}(\log X)\) for setup and one powered class. In fact \(\log E_B\le\pi(B)\log X\), and reduced-form composition keeps the class representation bounded by a polynomial in \(\log|\Delta|\) throughout. Integer ideals raised without reduction could have much larger representations. If a sampler supplies words in a fixed list of generators, compute \(E_B\)-th powers of the generators once and evaluate the words in the powered list. This shares the powering cost across samples, provided the generator list and each fresh word can be obtained at the charged cost. The direct reduced-class sampler supplies classes as reduced forms. [Lenstra–Pomerance, §2 and (2.13), and §5 on group words](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=5) · [Direct reduced-class sampler](keybreak-direct-reduced-class-sampler.md)

## Principal tests, collisions and 2-primary screening

The event \(h^2=1\) has probability \(1/M_{E_B}\). Conditional on it, \(h\) is uniform on \(T\), giving a non-global sign with probability \(1-2^{1-k}\). The canonical square has leading coefficient \(1\), and its root with a mixed sign exposes a proper factor by taking a gcd with \(N\). To extract the sign from \(h\) at \(\Delta=-4N\), take its canonical reduced form. Its leading coefficient is below \(N\), so it either gives a proper gcd directly or is a unit and permits the normalized root calculation when squaring \(h\). The class \(h=1\) itself gives only a global sign on this branch. This route has exact mixed-sign success probability \((1-2^{1-k})/M_{E_B}\) under the ideal uniform law, before side gcd tests. [Principal square and CRT signs](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md) · [Lenstra–Pomerance, ambiguous-class extraction in §2](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=5)

With \(O(\sqrt{M_{E_B}})\) independent draws and comparable storage, a collision on the canonical square class has constant probability. If the rule choosing the first pair uses only the square classes, the ratio of its two powered classes is uniform on \(T\), conditional on a collision. It gives a mixed sign with the same probability \(1-2^{1-k}\). For a multiplier, one can extract the coprime factorization of \(mN\) directly from the ambiguous form, then take gcds with \(N\). The equal fibers of that classical extraction give the same probability after projecting to \(N\). Conversely a cap of \(r\) proposals has probability at most \(\binom r2/M_{E_B}\) of any such collision. [Lenstra–Pomerance, Theorem 2.5](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=6)

There is a separate fixed-order test depending only on \(Q_B\). Let \(2^K\ge X\). Test \(h^{2^K}=1\), which accepts with probability exactly \(1/Q_B\) because the odd part of \(h\) is uniform on a group of size \(Q_B\). Accepted classes are uniform on \(G_2\). Write \(d=\dim_{\mathbf F_2}(G_2/G_2^2)\). A list of \(\lceil\log_2 X\rceil+1\) accepted classes generates \(G_2\) with probability at least \(1/2\): the probability that one of the \(2^d-1\) nonzero binary linear forms vanishes on the entire list is at most \((2^d-1)2^{-\lceil\log_2 X\rceil-1}\). Spanning the quotient gives generation by iterating \(G_2=H G_2^2\) for the generated subgroup \(H\).

For \(\Delta=-4N\), the ambiguous-class count gives \(|\ker\sigma|\le2\). Take successive squares of each nonidentity generator until its last nonidentity power, called its top here. If any top has nontrivial sign, extract a factor. Otherwise every top is the same nonidentity \(\kappa\in\ker\sigma\). Keep a generator \(x\) of maximum order \(2^j\). For every other generator \(y\) of order \(2^i\), replace \(y\) by \(y x^{-2^{j-i}}\). This preserves the generated subgroup, and taking its \(2^{i-1}\)-th power gives \(\kappa/\kappa=1\), strictly lowering the order. Repeat for each remaining nonidentity generator with trivial top sign. If all other generators became the identity, the group would be cyclic, in conflict with \(|T|\ge4\) when \(|\ker\sigma|=2\) and \(k\ge2\). Thus a generating list yields a mixed sign after polynomially many operations in \(\log X\). Including repeated lists, the ideal fixed-order test has expected cost \(B Q_B\operatorname{poly}(\log X)\) with a uniform oracle of polynomial bit cost. A large value of \(|G_2|/|T|\) does not enter this bound. [Lenstra–Pomerance, Theorem 2.5](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=6) · [Schnorr–Lenstra, Sylow-2 correction in §4](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1984c/artc.pdf#page=5)

## What the class-order distribution must supply

Every event \(F\) on the canonical square class has powered probability at most \(D_E\) times its unpowered probability. This follows by counting \(F\cap G_E^2\) inside \(F\cap G^2\) and dividing by the smaller support \(M_E=|G^2|/D_E\). In particular, on the rough-input branch under Dirichlet GRH, the width upper bound at \(A\le N^\theta\), for fixed \(\theta<1/2\), implies \(D_E\ge N^{1/2-\theta-o(1)}\) is necessary for a probability \(N^{-o(1)}\) at that width. Even with this much shrinkage, a width or smoothness claim needs a supply bound inside \(G_E^2\). Counts for all square classes alone can give no lower bound within that subgroup. [Fixed and adaptive reduced-class width bounds](keybreak-reduced-class-width-floor.md)

Write \(H_N=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\). If \(B=\exp(bH_N)\) and a law on polynomial-size coprime odd multipliers gives mass at least \(\exp(-\eta H_N)\) to orders with \(M_{E_B}\le\exp(cH_N)\), run \(\max(2,\lceil\exp(cH_N/2)\rceil)\) powered proposals per freshly sampled multiplier. Given a polynomial-cost uniform sampler and polynomial setup apart from powering, the expected work for a sign-blind collision and extraction has exponent at most \(b+c/2+\eta+o(1)\). If words and generators are available as above with polynomial bit costs and list size, shared powering replaces the sum \(b+c/2\) by \(\max(b,c/2)\). Principal testing has \(b+c+\eta+o(1)\). A fixed-order 2-primary screen instead costs \(b+\log Q_B/H_N+o(1)\) in exponent. These sufficient ledger hypotheses involve a residual odd order or a powered square image. Sampling split primes, counting smooth reduced forms, and estimating ranks address other quantities.

## Worst-case obstruction and classical comparison

Lenstra–Pomerance prove that infinitely many primes \(p\) have largest prime factors of both \(p-1\) and \(p+1\) exceeding \(p^{1/3}\). Take a prime \(q\) between \(p\) and \(2p\), and \(N=p^2q\). Their Lemma 11.2 gives a prime divisor \(r>p^{1/3}\) of the class number of **every** negative discriminant divisible by \(N\). It comes from the order class number factor \(p-(\Delta'/p)\) at \(\Delta=p^2\Delta'\), up to a bounded unit correction. In particular the factor survives every saturated cutoff \(B=N^{o(1)}\), giving \(M_{E_B}\ge Q_B\ge r\gg N^{1/9}\) even with an adaptive choice of multiplier. For coprime multipliers, the prime \(r\) divides \(p-1\) or \(p+1\) and differs from \(p,q\), so powering additionally by \(N\) still leaves it. [Lenstra–Pomerance, Lemmas 11.2–11.3](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=29)

For independent uniform proposals in any order chosen before each draw, this forces \(\Omega(N^{1/9})\) proposals for constant probability of a principal-square hit or an accepted 2-primary screen. Testing for repeated canonical square classes within the same order takes \(\Omega(N^{1/18})\) proposals by the union bound, including adaptive choices of order. Even matching just the unit leading coefficients across multipliers \(m=N^{o(1)}\) takes \(N^{1/18-o(1)}\) independent proposals in this family: each particular \(A\) coprime to \(N\) has at most \(O(2^{\omega(A)}\sqrt{\gcd(A,mN)})=N^{o(1)}\) canonical forms in the next order. The remark after Lemma 11.3 permits replacing \(1/3\) in the shifted-prime threshold by any fixed \(c<1/2\), giving corresponding exponents \(c/3\) and \(c/6\) on an infinite family. These bounds concern specified success events for the powered proposal. Factors found by other gcd tests, relations between distinct coefficients, or other ways of processing the class group have separate analyses. The exhibited inputs have a repeated prime factor. [Lenstra–Pomerance, remark after Lemma 11.3](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=30) · [Reduced-form multiplicity bound](keybreak-reduced-class-width-floor.md)

Schnorr–Lenstra’s first stage already raises a random form to an odd product of small prime powers. Its cutoff uses \(p_i^\nu\le p_t^2\) for each odd \(p_i\le p_t\). It then squares toward an ambiguous form. Their second stage searches for a small residual odd order, and their Sylow-2 correction combines classes when one cyclic subgroup gives an unhelpful ambiguous form. The saturated exponent here gives an exact finite description of what the first stage kills. The relative-sign argument explains its compatibility with a reduced square as the shared public record. [Schnorr–Lenstra, §§2–4](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1984c/artc.pdf#page=2)

Lenstra–Pomerance’s rigorous expected \(L_N[1/2,1+o(1)]\) factoring algorithm uses multiplier selection to obtain suitable split-prime populations, finds relations among smooth leading coefficients using generator words, and extracts an ambiguous class from dependencies. Its smoothness bounds concern the sampled leading coefficients. The class-order obstruction above is part of their separate analysis of Schnorr–Lenstra’s heuristic. [Lenstra–Pomerance, §§9–10 and Theorem 10.5](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=23) · [§11](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=29)

## Squarefree inputs and small powers

**A fixed cubing step.** For odd squarefree \(N>3\) with \(N\equiv3\bmod8\), the fundamental discriminant is \(D=-N\), and \(2\) is inert. The class map from discriminant \(-4N\) onto \(D\) has kernel of order \(3\), by the conductor-\(2\) class-number formula and the surjection of ideal classes. Thus a single cubing shrinks both the square image and the odd part of the class order by a factor of at least \(3\). The per-draw principal-square and 2-primary screen probabilities increase by that factor. This holds for balanced semiprimes in those residue classes as well. The cost of cubing must still be charged. For the saturated cutoff \(B\ge3\), the quantities \(Q_B\) and \(M_{E_B}\) on the two orders are equal, since the 2-primary parts and the primary parts at primes \(>B\) are isomorphic. A conductor change in any fixed quadratic field gives a surjection on powered images to the maximal-order image. A multiplier with a new squarefree part can change the field. [Bhargava–Varma, Lemma 33](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~ila/BV.pdf#page=22) · [Lenstra–Pomerance, (2.12)](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=7)

**An unconditional average obstruction for small exponents.** Fix \(A\ge0\) and \(0<\epsilon<2/3\). Let \(L=\lfloor(\log X)^{2/3-\epsilon}\rfloor\). For all but \(X^{o(1)}\) balanced semiprimes \(N=pq\) with \(p,q\in[\sqrt X,2\sqrt X]\), every odd multiplier \(1\le m\le X^A\) coprime to \(N\) and every odd \(1\le E\le L\) satisfy

\[
|\operatorname{Pic}(-4mN)^{2E}|\gg Z/\log Z,
\qquad Z=\tfrac14 X^{1/(4L)}.
\]

Here \(\operatorname{Pic}(\Delta)\) denotes the class group of the order of discriminant \(\Delta\). The underlying fundamental discriminant \(D\) has \(N\mid D\) because \(p,q\) occur to the first power in \(-4mN\). Given distinct split primes \(r,s\in[Z,2Z]\) in that field, choose one ideal over each. If their \(2E\)-th powers represent the same class, their quotient class has order dividing \(2E\). Multiplying the second ideal by the conjugate of the first and raising to \(2E\) makes an integral principal ideal of norm \((rs)^{2E}\). The generator cannot be rational because the ideal uses only one prime above each of the two split primes. Any nonrational integral element has norm at least \(|D|/4\), whereas \(4(rs)^{2E}<X\le|D|\). The powered split-prime classes are therefore distinct, and the order class group maps onto the field class group. [Heath-Brown–Pierce, norm argument in (2.3)–(2.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3177#page=7)

To bound the simultaneous exceptional set, put \(F=16X^{1+A}\), let \(P\) count the primes in the band, and take \(j=\lceil2\log F/\log Z\rceil\). The \(j\)-th power of \(\sum_{Z\le r\le2Z}\chi_D(r)\) has coefficient sum \(P^j\) and each coefficient is at most \(j!\). By the primitive character large sieve, the number of \(D\) with absolute prime sum exceeding \(P/4\) is at most

\[
\ll (F^2+(2Z)^j) j! P^j(4/P)^{2j}
\ll j!(C\log Z)^j=X^{o(1)}
\]

for an absolute constant \(C\). This is the split-prime moment argument of Heath-Brown–Pierce with a growing moment. Outside the exception, the ramified primes removed from the band number \(O_A(\log X/\log Z)=o(P)\), so there are \(\gg P\) split primes. Each exceptional \(D\) is divisible by only \(O_A(1)\) primes in \([\sqrt X,2\sqrt X]\) and can spoil only \(O_A(1)\) balanced inputs. Consequently the principal-square and same-order birthday routes with these powers require \(\exp(\omega(H_N))\) proposals on the resulting family. For a fixed upper bound \(L\), the same proof gives a polynomial image lower bound \(X^{1/(4L)}/O(\log X)\). [Heath-Brown–Pierce, (2.5)–(2.7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.3177#page=8)

**Odd order, odd exponent and prime factors.** For example take balanced primes \(p\equiv5\bmod8,\ q\equiv3\bmod8\) with \((p/q)=-1\). The quadratic large sieve and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the sum of \((p/q)\) over these prime pairs by \(O_\nu(X^{3/4+\nu})\) for every fixed \(\nu>0\), so asymptotically half the pairs in those residue classes qualify. For \(D=-pq\), genus theory gives 2-rank \(1\) and the Rédei criterion gives 4-rank \(0\), hence the 2-primary part has order \(2\). The conductor-\(2\) order has the same class group since \(2\) splits. The odd order is therefore \(N^{1/2+o(1)}\) by Siegel’s class-number bound. The class of a prime above \(2\) has order at least \(\log_2(N/4)\) by the same norm argument with one split prime, so the odd exponent is at least \(\tfrac12\log_2(N/4)\). On this family the image bound for \(m=1\) also bounds the residual odd order after each small power, because the 2-primary square image is trivial. [Heath-Brown, quadratic large sieve](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/acta-arithmetica/all/72/3/108575/a-mean-value-estimate-for-real-character-sums) · [Fouvry–Klüners, Theorem 5](https://math.uni-paderborn.de/fileadmin-eim/mathematik/AG-Computeralgebra/Publications-klueners/ranks.pdf#page=6) · [Lenstra–Pomerance, §2](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=5)

Large odd order, a growing odd exponent, and a large prime divisor are different conclusions. The first two bounds still permit a large smooth primary part, and the small-exponent argument controls exponents of far smaller magnitude than the saturated \(E_B\). The squarefree estimates here leave open the distribution of \(Q_B\) for \(B=\exp(bH_N)\), in particular across multipliers for a fixed balanced semiprime. Two published averages concern related quantities. Soundararajan’s bound for fields with bounded odd class order is \(O(X(\log\log X)^6/\log X)\), which is larger than the size of the balanced-semiprime family. Bhargava–Varma’s mean of \(2\) for the subgroup killed by cubing implies that at least half of imaginary quadratic fields satisfying any fixed finite local conditions have no 3-torsion asymptotically. This average is over fields and gives no corresponding proportion within the balanced-semiprime subfamily. [Soundararajan, Corollary 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0237#page=3) · [Bhargava–Varma, Corollary 4](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~ila/BV.pdf#page=3)
