# Common signs at real embeddings of the two monic fields

Let \(F,G\) be the distinct monic irreducible polynomials of the construction, each of degree at least two. For marks \((a_i,b_i)\) with \(b_i>0\), write \(t_i=a_i/b_i\). Order the real roots of \(H=FG\) as \(x_1<\cdots<x_r\). A cut \(t_i\) is never one of the \(x_j\), since both defining polynomials have degree greater than one. [Monic pair](cogentic-monic-pair.md)

For a binary support \(I\), put \(Q_I(X)=\prod_{i\in I}(a_i-b_iX)\). There is a single sign \(\epsilon\in\{\pm1\}\) for which \(\epsilon Q_I\) is positive at every real embedding of both fields **if and only if each open interval \((x_j,x_{j+1})\) contains an even number of selected cuts, counted with multiplicity**. Indeed, each factor changes sign exactly once, at its cut. Thus consecutive values have the same sign precisely when the interval contains an even number of cuts. The two unbounded intervals can only change all signs together or leave all signs alone. The condition is empty for \(r\le1\).

This gives the exact real-sign constraint in the common-sign square search. Its binary matrix has one row per occupied bounded interval, and each column contains at most one 1. The rows have disjoint nonempty supports and are independent. Its rank is the number of occupied bounded intervals, hence at most \(\deg F+\deg G-1\). A kernel basis before imposing other constraints consists of the unbounded-interval columns individually, and the differences between each column and one reference column in the same bounded interval. These rows can also be appended to the ideal-parity matrix. A relation surviving this filter still has to be a square in both fields. Allowing arbitrary units changes the sign conditions by their own signatures, as the unit-corrected split at 323 illustrates. [Unit correction](cogentic-monic-unit-corrections.md)

## Computing columns on the one-third grid

For each cut, compute the number \(j_i\) of real roots of \(H\) below \(t_i\) using the variation difference between \(-\infty\) and \(t_i\) in a Sturm chain. Its interval row is \(j_i\) if \(0<j_i<r\). Neither approximate roots nor field arithmetic enter this computation. To form each signed remainder, the replay multiplies by the absolute leading coefficient of the divisor before each leading-term cancellation, then divides the negative remainder by a positive content. These steps preserve the signs of the ordinary Sturm chain. [Exact replay](../experiments/cogentic-monic-real-gaps.py)

An elementary bound suffices even for this unreduced cost estimate. Let \(D=\deg H\) and let \(h\) bound the coefficient bit lengths of \(H,H'\), counting a sign bit. In a remainder step with degree drop \(\delta=\deg A-\deg B\ge1\), at most \(\delta+1\) cancellations occur. Each cancellation multiplies coefficient height by at most \(2\|B\|_\infty\). Thus a common logarithmic height bound for \(A,B\) grows by a factor of at most \(\delta+2\), taking logarithmic height to be \(\log_2(2\max(1,\|\cdot\|_\infty))\). The successive \(\delta\)'s sum to at most \(D\) by telescoping, so all coefficient bit lengths are \(O(3^D h)\). Clearing the positive denominator at a cut of height \(A_0\) adds \(O(D\log(2A_0)+\log D)\) bits. The number of integer operations per cut is polynomial in \(D\).

On the monic schedule, \(D=O((\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3})\), \(h=O(V+\log D)\), and \(\log A_0=O(W)\). The chain and the sign checks use \(\exp(o(W))\) bit operations per cut even with schoolbook arithmetic. Processing \(\exp(O(W))\) supplied marks stays within \(\exp(O(W))\). On six manually fixed cubic/quartic pairs the replay checks 768 exact root counts against PARI. For \(N=323\) it places the sole even-ideal singleton's cut \(-1/2\) in a bounded gap, which certifies its common-sign obstruction.
