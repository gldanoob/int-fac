# Screening smooth cofactors in Cogentic's randomized descent

Assume the relation-supply lemmas and stated local cost bounds of the [pinned Cogentic randomized manuscript](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf). The following modification of its algorithm gives complete factorization of every $N$ in zero-error expected time $L_N[1/3,36]$ with deterministic space at most $L_N[1/3,40]$. The manuscript states 105 for time. Here and below the constant is a limsup in natural logarithms as in its Theorem 1.1. The improvement uses the proved ordinary Riemann hypothesis for smooth integers in genus-two order intervals, and the sparse support of the paper's matrix. The bit operation model is a uniform probabilistic multitape machine. These deductions are on paper. [Source statements and release status](cogentic-factoring-papers.md)

## A one-sided test for smoothness

Lenstra, Pila and Pomerance already identified the constant 2 for the genus-two method under a short-interval smoothness hypothesis, and explicitly proposed its use as a smoothness screen. The number-theoretic step here supplies the density in the required $p^{3/2}$ intervals from Younis and proved ordinary RH. The application to Cogentic's descent and the sparse-kernel cost accounting are separate. [Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance I, p. 398](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/hyperI.pdf#page=2)

Put $S(p)=\sqrt{\ln p\,\ln\ln p}$ for sufficiently large primes. In a dyad $P<p\le2P$ choose the cutoff $Y$ with $\ln Y=(1+o(1))S(P)$, rounding up to a power of two. For $x\asymp p^2$, $u=\ln x/\ln Y$ has $u\ln u=(1+o(1))S(p)$. Hildebrand's long-interval estimate gives $\Psi(x,Y)/x=\exp(-(1+o(1))S(p))$. In the central genus-two order interval, write $n=2s$, with $s$ odd. The length in the $s$-coordinate is $\asymp p^{3/2}$, exceeding $x^{2/3}$ on the scale $x\asymp p^2$. Younis's relative short-interval estimate using ordinary RH at $x$ and $x/2$ shows that the number of such $n$ with $s$ smooth is $p^{3/2}\exp(-(1+o(1))S(p))$. Indeed the even part of the $s$ count is $(1/2+o(1))$ of the whole, by $\Psi(x/2,Y)/\Psi(x,Y)=1/2+o(1)$. Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance lose at most $p$ central orders; for every other eligible $n$ their odd-trace class provides $\gg p^{9/2}/\mathrm{polylog}(p)$ quintic models. Dividing by $p-1$ to make the quintic monic yields at least $p^5\exp(-(1+o(1))S(p))$ good monic models, each a product of precisely one monic irreducible quadratic and one monic irreducible cubic. [Short-interval theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05761) · [Long-interval density](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(86)90013-2) · [Lenstra–Pila–Pomerance, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 2.4](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/pila/HyperII.pdf) · [Parity, model counts and pairing](quadratic-cubic-genus-two.md)

For a composite current modulus $d$ containing distinct odd primes $p,q$, choose a monic quadratic $G_2$, a monic cubic $G_3$, and a mark $a$ independently and uniformly modulo $d$. Set $F=G_2 G_3$, $e=F(a)$, and use $(a,e)$ on $y^2=eF(x)$. Given a good factorization at $p$, at least $p/4-O(\sqrt p)$ marks have both $G_i(a)$ nonzero squares, by the Weil bound. The twist is then square, and the quadratic-factor Tate pairing places the marked class in the odd-order part of the Jacobian. At $q$, a positive absolute fraction of the draws have $\chi_q(G_2(a))=-1$, nonzero $G_3(a)$ and unit discriminant of $F$. For fixed $a$ the two evaluations are independent uniform field values; the nonzero discriminant polynomial in the five coefficients has bounded degree and removes $O(1/q)$ of the draws. This second sign forces even marked order. Bounded small primes are found directly. CRT makes the two prime-field samples independent. The usual gcd checks in Cantor arithmetic find a proper split on these events, including when the modulus contains powers or other primes. The odd scalar is applied as successive largest powers of all odd primes up to $Y$ with each power at most $16d^2$. Its sieve and all group operations cost $Y\,\mathrm{poly}(\log d+\log P)$ bits per draw. [Composite arithmetic and exact local checks](three-parameter-genus-two.md) · [Cantor's algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1987-0866101-0)

Thus at the correct dyad the expected cost of a proper split is at most $\mathrm{poly}(b)\exp((2+o(1))S(p))$, where $b$ bounds the input lengths. For a limit $Q$, run the $O(\log Q)$ dyads in parallel and handle prime or power inputs directly. A $Q$-smooth input requires at most $O(b)$ successful splits. Each one has uniform expected cost at most $\mathrm{poly}(b)\exp((2+o(1))S(Q))$. A capped run returns a prime factorization checked by primality, multiplication and the cutoff, or returns failure. The latter is always permitted on a nonsmooth input. For uniform bounds, an integer budget exceeding the expectation by a factor of two makes a run succeed with probability at least $1/2$ on every smooth input; $O(b)$ independent restarts reduce the miss probability below $e^{-b}$. This is a one-sided screen and never certifies a nonsmooth number as smooth. It includes the trivial factorization of 1.

## The calls in Definitions 4.5 and 6.1

Write $\Lambda=(\ln N)^{1/3}(\ln\ln N)^{2/3}$. Cogentic's top prime cutoff has $\ln Q_0=(1/3+o(1))(\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}$. Consequently $2S(Q_0)=(2\sqrt2/3+o(1))\Lambda<\Lambda$. The previous screen with $Q=Q_0$ and budget $\exp(1.5\Lambda)$ per restart has time below $L_N[1/3,2]$ and miss probability below $e^{-b}$ for large $N$. Integer approximations as in the source's Definition 2.7 can round the clock upward within a constant factor. The machine needs no unknown asymptotic threshold. Its ordinary background trial division supplies correctness on all finite inputs.

* In Definition 6.1, Step 3(b), a successful pair requires each of $x_1,y_1$ to be a product of primes at most $Q_0$. Screen both. Failure rejects the current blinded residue.
* At the root of Definition 4.5, the two positive weights require $n_{\rm rat}, n_{\rm alg}$ to be $Q_0$-smooth. Screen both and evaluate the original weights exactly on their returned factorizations. The third integer $n'_{\rm alg}=f_2(a_0,b_0)$ enters the children's history by its **value**. Its prime list from the printed call to `FactorPos` is unused: the history tests take remainders modulo a known prime and gcds with known numbers, including $\gcd(\Delta_t',y^*_{t,1}y^*_{t,2})$. Retain its value and the original range check.
* At an algebraic node the positive acceptance weight requires both $C_\iota,C_{\iota'}$ to be $Q_0$-smooth. Use the same screen. The calls on $\Delta_t'$ are genuinely unrestricted, but Lemma 4.6 bounds $\Delta_t'\le E_{Q_0}^2=L_N[2/3,0.35]$ up to its asymptotic convention. Use the classical Lenstra–Pomerance full factorer on these operands. Its expected cost at $M\le L_N[2/3,0.35]$ is at most $\exp((\sqrt{0.35\cdot2/3}+o(1))\Lambda)$. This upper bound is uniform even for smaller or bounded $M$, by taking the maximum of its eventual bound and a finite constant. The coefficient is below $0.49$. Cap and independently repeat this call with the same $\exp(1.5\Lambda)$ clock and $O(b)$ restarts as the smoothness screen. A verified factorization is returned except with probability below $e^{-b}$ on every such operand. A timeout rejects the current trial. The clock also bounds workspace on every tape. [Lenstra–Pomerance, Theorem 10.5](https://math.dartmouth.edu/~carlp/PDF/paper85.pdf#page=29)

On a nonsmooth value the weight or ratio test necessarily rejects. On a genuinely smooth value the capped screen can differ from the original factorization call with probability at most $e^{-b}$. Couple the modified phase with the paper's phase, independently of its existing coins at each such call. Its capped trial counts and Lemma 4.6 permit at most $L_N[1/3,24]$ of these calls in a phase. The probability of any acceptance decision differing is $o(1)$ by the conditional union bound, without assuming independent past histories. All tests see the sealed units only through their squares as prescribed in Lemma 5.3. Replacing Gaussian elimination below can choose a different nullspace basis; the original three-dimensional argument applies to every verified triple of independent null vectors, with independent private coins for this choice.

The cost of the screens and the capped determinant factorizations together is at most $L_N[1/3,26]$ on every tape. At a current subproblem use its own input length for the clock and probability. Failed verifications restart the phase. All outputs retain the original primality and multiplication checks. [Descent subroutine, pp. 38–39, and complete algorithm, pp. 44–49](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf#page=38)

## Three null vectors from a sparse matrix

For the $m\times n$ binary relation matrix $A$, $n=m+3\le(d+1)^{O(1)}(B+\mathrm{poly}(b))$ with $\ln B=(18+o(1))\Lambda$. Each column contains at most $\exp(O((\ln\ln N)^2))\mathrm{poly}(b)$ nonzero entries. Its valuations come from the factor lists in a single tree collection, and there are just $\mathrm{poly}(b)$ character rows. Write $s$ for the total number of nonzeros. A Monte Carlo call returning either three verified independent vectors of $\ker_{\mathbb F_2} A$ or failure uses $O(n(s+n)\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$ bit operations and succeeds with probability uniformly bounded as close to one as needed. This is the sparse finite-field linear algebra of Wiedemann; the details below handle arbitrary rank, including characteristic two. [Wiedemann, *Solving sparse linear equations over finite fields*](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1986.1057137)

Work over $K=\mathbb F_{2^k}$ with $128(n+1)^2\le |K|<256(n+1)^2$. Sample a monic irreducible degree-$k$ polynomial by the standard Frobenius test, capping proposals at $100k^2$; the fraction irreducible is $\gg1/k$. Draw uniform independent diagonal matrices $D$ of size $m$ and $E$ of size $n$. Put $C=EA^TDA$. If $r=\mathrm{rank}(A)$, the coefficient of $t^{n-r}$ in $\det(tI-C)$ is, up to sign,
$$
 \sum_{|I|=|J|=r}\det(A_{I,J})^2\prod_{i\in I}D_{ii}\prod_{j\in J}E_{jj}.
$$
It is a nonzero polynomial of degree $2r$, by the principal-minor expansion and Cauchy–Binet. With probability at least $1-2r/|K|$ it stays nonzero. Then $\mathrm{rank}(C)=r$, $\ker C=\ker A$, and zero is semisimple: $K^n=\ker C\oplus\operatorname{im}C$. The assertion includes $r=0$.

Draw independent uniform $x,v\in K^n$, put $y=Cx$ and compute $2n$ scalars $v^TC^j y$. Berlekamp–Massey recovers the scalar minimal recurrence $h$. Except with probability $n/|K|$ it equals the minimal annihilating polynomial of $y$. Indeed, for each distinct irreducible divisor $f$ of the vector minimal polynomial $h_y$, dropping $f$ would force $v^T(h_y/f)(C)y=0$, a nontrivial linear equation in $v$. There are at most $n$ such divisors. On the good events $h(0)\ne0$ since $y$ is in the invertible part. The vector $h(C)x/h(0)$ is precisely the projection of $x$ to the kernel. For $y=0$ take $h=1$. Three fresh $x,v$ pairs yield three independent kernel vectors over $K$ except with probability $O(n/|K|)$, using $\dim\ker A\ge3$ and a union bound with the three ideal uniform projections. Expand their entries in the binary field basis. These binary components span a space of dimension at least three, since otherwise the three original vectors lie in the $K$-span of two binary vectors. Select and verify three independent binary vectors. A failure at any check can restart the phase. The CRT sign argument still applies after conditioning on these independent coins.

Each multiplication by $C$ consists of two sparse matrix-vector products and two diagonal products. On a fixed number of tapes, sort contributions by column for the vector lookup and then by row for summation, using external merge sort with $O(\log(n+s))$ sequential passes. Each scalar field operation costs $\mathrm{poly}(k)$ bits. Berlekamp–Massey uses $O(n^2)$ field operations with coefficient arrays scanned in order, and Horner evaluation needs at most $n$ further matvecs. The workspace fits in $O((n+s)\mathrm{polylog}(n+s))$ bits, in addition to the original relations.

Thus the whole matrix step costs $L_N[1/3,36]$. The original enumeration of the factor base costs $O(d B^2\mathrm{poly}(b))$, also $L_N[1/3,36]$. For the at most fourteen square-root calls of Lemma 5.2, $M_{\rm fac}\le n\,2r_0\exp((\ln\ln N)^2)$ and $B_{\rm bit}$ is at most this times $\mathrm{poly}(b)$ plus another bit polynomial. Its original schoolbook bound $O(d^3B_{\rm bit}^2)$ therefore costs $L_N[1/3,36]$. A full pass over the factor base for each **accepted** relation fits this same bound. The other setups, lifting checks, and the final modular evaluations have smaller exponents in the printed accounting. The phase finds a verified divisor with probability bounded below at every sufficiently large input. A constant expected number of phases and the $O(b)$ splitting-tree nodes preserve the constant 36. All caps retain the source's deterministic workspace guarantee.

The [genus-two screen replay](../experiments/cogentic-smoothness-screen.gp) accepts all 90 smooth inputs and rejects all 66 nonsmooth ones at cutoff 31, using 87 gcd splits inside Cantor arithmetic. It includes repeated prime factors. The [sparse nullspace replay](../experiments/cogentic-sparse-kernel.py) checks 1,120 binary matrices, including every $2\times5$ binary matrix. It recovers three verified independent null vectors in every case, with at most seven attempts at the small test field size. These tests exercise the replacement routines independently.
