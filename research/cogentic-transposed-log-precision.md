# Precision and tape cost of the transposed log extractor

For a realized relation list of length $M$, the projected log columns in the thinner-grid candidate can be selected and used in $(B^3+MB^2)\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit operations on a multitape machine. The workspace is $(B^2+M)\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bits, including the sparse input. These bounds use the existing root brackets, mark separation and algebraic unit extraction. They concern the linear algebra once the relation list has been found. ## Exact rank decisions from rounded columns

Let $V_f$ be the finite integer matrix and $U$ the true projected-log matrix with $s=2d-2$ rows. On a full-rank modular proposal let $C$ be its $R$-by-$R$ pivot minor and $U_P$ the log columns at its pivots. For a free column with selected finite rows $v_j$, its exact projected vector is
$$
g_j=U_j-U_PC^{-1}v_j .
$$
It is the image under $U$ of a rational kernel vector. The standard kernel vectors indexed by the free columns span the rational kernel. Write $\Delta=\det C$. Every $\Delta g_j$ is the log of a pair of units under the original algebraic hypotheses, since multiplying the kernel vector by $\Delta$ makes it integral.

Choose a computable power of two $J\ge1$ bounding the absolute determinant and all cofactors by Hadamard. Let $F\ge1$ bound the absolute finite matrix entries and $U_0\ge1$ bound the absolute true log entries. For instance, a bound $2^{-q_0}\le |a-b\theta|\le 2^{q_0}$ on all marked differences gives $U_0=2d(q_0+1)$ after Helmert projection. Here $R\le B\operatorname{poly}(L)$, $F\le\operatorname{poly}(L)$ and $q_0\le\operatorname{poly}(L)$. Cramer's rule gives $|(C^{-1})_{ij}|\le J$, and $K=sU_0(1+R^2JF)+1$ bounds all $\|g_j\|_2$. Computing these bounds requires no determinant evaluation.

The product unit-log lattice has no nonzero vector shorter than $1/3$. If all log coordinates of a totally real unit $u$ have magnitude below $1/3$, then $|\sigma(u^2)-1|<1$ at every embedding. The integer norm of $u^2-1$ forces $u^2=1$, and its log vector is zero. A unit's log lies in the trace-zero subspace, where Helmert projection is an isometry. The same shortest-vector bound holds for the lattice generated by any independent $t$-tuple of $\Delta g_j$. Packing balls of radius $1/6$ in its span gives covolume at least $(4t)^{-t}$. Dividing by $|\Delta|^t$ gives a uniform lower bound $\gamma=(4sJ)^{-s}$ on the exterior norm of any independent tuple of at most $s$ projected columns.

Use a common dyadic scale and compute each $\widetilde g_j$ within Euclidean error $\varepsilon\le\gamma/[8s(K+1)^{s-1}]$. For an extension of $t$ columns, replacing the factors of the exterior product in turn changes its norm by at most $t\varepsilon(K+1)^{t-1}\le\gamma/8$. Compare the exact determinant of the approximate Gram matrix with $\gamma^2/4$. A dependent tuple has determinant at most $\gamma^2/64$, and an independent one has determinant greater than $\gamma^2/4$. Keep the candidate exactly when it passes. Stop when $s$ columns have been selected, and reject if the list ends first. Store just the selected approximate columns and their indices.

For each real row, solve $C^T\lambda=u_P$ for a dyadic approximation to the pivot log row $u_P$. An entrywise error $\delta$ in that row causes at most $RJ\delta$ error in any coordinate of $\lambda$. Round $\lambda$ and the free log entry to the same dyadic accuracy, then take the dot product with the sparse integer $v_j$. The Euclidean error across the $s$ rows is at most $s\delta(2+RF+R^2JF)$. Choose a dyadic $\delta$ making this at most $\varepsilon$. Its precision is $B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bits. Integer determinants of the small dyadic Gram matrices use fraction-free elimination within this bit scale. A modular proposal of deficient rational rank need not satisfy the separation bound. Verify the exact finite equations for the selected and particular vectors and verify the modular exponent before trying the gcd. [Randomized pivot check](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md)

## Recover the last unit coefficients

Solve the primal system for the selected $s$ kernel directions and one particular direction, and clear denominators individually. Each common denominator divides $|\Delta|$ because integer right-hand-side solutions have denominators dividing $|\Delta|$. Let $Y$ be the square matrix of selected integral unit-log columns and $y_0$ the integral particular log vector. Choose a bound $K_0$ on the norms of these columns and of $y_0$ by Cramer's rule and the bound $U_0$. It has $\log K_0=O(\log J+\operatorname{poly}(L))$. Then $|\det Y|\ge(4s)^{-s}$, $|\det Y|\le K_0^s$, and the product unit-log lattice has covolume at least $(4s)^{-s}$. The coordinates of $-Y^{-1}y_0$ have denominators at most $I_0=(4sK_0)^s$, since the sublattice of the selected unit logs has index at most $I_0$. Cramer's rule gives a computable bound $K_{\rm inv}\ge\|Y^{-1}\|_2$ with $\log K_{\rm inv}=O(s\log(4sK_0))$.

Compute $Y$ and $y_0$ to operator and Euclidean errors at most a dyadic $\delta_0<[8K_{\rm inv}I_0^2(1+K_{\rm inv}K_0)]^{-1}$. Since $K_{\rm inv}\delta_0\le1/2$, the approximate inverse has norm at most $2K_{\rm inv}$. The coefficient error is at most $2K_{\rm inv}\delta_0(1+K_{\rm inv}K_0)<1/(4I_0^2)$. Continued fractions recover the unique bounded-denominator rationals within this error. Multiplying by their common denominator yields the original unit cancellation and order verification. All extra precision for evaluating the exact integer directions and for the recovery has $B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bits. [Algebraic cancellation](cogentic-collective-field-extraction.md)

## Multitape ledger

- Store the modular pivot vectors as sequential residue rows. Scanning the existing pivots for each relation costs $MB^2\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit operations. A row-major copy of $C$, its transpose and one modular inverse cost at most $B^3\operatorname{poly}(L)$ by ordinary elimination. In each inverse step copy the pivot row and rewind that copy once per updated row. The work per pivot is $B^2\operatorname{poly}(L)$.
- Each of the $O(s)$ exact solves uses $B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ base-$P_*$ lift steps. The prime $P_*$ has $O(\log B)$ bits. At each step, a product against the saved modular inverse and a product against the integer pivot matrix take $B^2\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit operations, including rewinds of the digit vector once per row. Updating the $R$ residual and accumulated coordinates of $B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bits costs the same order, since each shift or multiplication uses only a base-$P_*$ digit. Take the lift modulus larger than twice the product of the numerator and denominator bounds. Extended Euclid on $q$-bit integers takes $O(q^2)$ elementary bit steps here. With current lengths $n\ge m$, aligned binary long division costs $O(n(n-m+1))$, and the bit-length differences telescope over the Euclidean steps. The extended coefficient updates have the same bound after replacing $n$ by $q$. Reconstructing all $R$ coordinates and accumulating their least common denominator fit $B^3\operatorname{poly}(L)$.
- Put $P=B\operatorname{poly}(L)$ for the largest required precision. Root bisection and the range-reduced logarithm series use $P^2\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bit operations per root or mark with quasi-linear arithmetic on the $P$-bit operands. The $R$ pivot mark evaluations fit $B^3\operatorname{poly}(L)$. Scan each saved rounded transpose row at most once per nonzero entry of a free column. The saved row contains $R$ numbers of $P$ bits and the column has $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ nonzero entries. Exact arithmetic on a trial Gram matrix and on the last real solve involves only $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ coordinates of $P\operatorname{poly}(L)$ bits. This charges at most $MB^2\operatorname{poly}(L)+B^3\operatorname{poly}(L)$, even with classical quadratic arithmetic in those small matrices. The exact checks of the selected finite equations can stream over their sparse columns, allocating one accumulator per finite row and right-hand side.
