# A first moment for smooth cofactors of quadratic marks

Fix a complete parent-prime dyad \([P,2P]\), with \(V=P^\alpha\) and \(1/3<\alpha<1/2\) fixed. Write \(M\) for the radix scale, \(B\) for the rational smoothness cutoff, and assume the top scales \(\log P\asymp\log M\asymp d^2\log d\), \(\log B\asymp d\log d\), \(d\to\infty\). Put \(X_R=M^2 V/P\), \(u_R=\log X_R/\log B\). Under Dirichlet GRH, for any fixed \(J>0\), outside a fraction \(O((\log V)^{-J})\) of the parent primes one has
\[
 \mathbb E_{r,Q}\#\{h:\ p\mid h(r,Q),\ h(r,Q)/p\text{ is }B\text{-smooth}\}
 \ \ge\ \frac{V^3}{P}\exp(-(1+o(1))u_R\log u_R).
\]
Here the counted marks are primitive positive definite integral quadratics of height at most \(V(\log V)^{C_J}\), negative fundamental discriminant of absolute value \(\asymp V^2\), and parity \(h(X,Y)\equiv X(X+Y)\bmod2\). The expectation can use a uniform law on positive primitive anchors with odd \(Q\), both coordinates between \(M/(\log V)^{C_J}\) and \(M(\log V)^{C_J}\), and slope in a fixed subinterval of \((1/2,3/4)\) containing \(3/5\). Enlarge the fixed exponent \(C_J\) if necessary. There is no restriction of \(Q\) to prime or rough values. This is an averaged count, with no second-moment estimate for a fixed parent.

## Count over discriminants and smooth norms

Let \(D\) range over negative squarefree integers congruent to one modulo eight in a fixed dyadic interval of absolute size \(V^2\). For each parent \(p\) there are \(\gg V^2\) such discriminants split at \(p\). The unsigned count follows by the squarefree sieve. For the quadratic-character sum expand \(\mu^2\) and split the square divisors at \(T\). Pólya–Vinogradov in the fixed odd progression and the elementary large-divisor tail give
\[
 O(T\sqrt P\log P+V^2/T+V).
\]
Only odd square divisors enter and their range is below \(p\). One can take \(T=P^\epsilon\) for sufficiently small fixed \(\epsilon>0\), since \(V^2\gg P^{1/2+\epsilon}\). Also \(|D|<p\) for all large inputs.

For each \(D\), choose a split prime ideal above two and fix one orientation at each odd split prime. Build a squarefree ideal from distinct split prime bins near \(B\), at product size \(x=X_R/4\), using the single index-sum construction. The number \(S\) of products can be chosen identical for every \(D\): take the same number of split primes from each bin for all discriminants, a fixed small multiple of its interval length divided by \(\log B\), rounded down. The Dirichlet GRH error for the quadratic character is \(O(\sqrt t\,\log^{O(1)}(t|D|))\) at bin endpoints \(t=B^{1-o(1)}\), negligible against any bin population. The combinatorial density argument alone gives \(S\ge x\exp(-(1+o(1))u_R\log u_R)\). Indeed there are \(k\asymp d\) slots and \(\asymp k\log B/\log u_R\gg k\) bins; the discarded index-sum prefactor has logarithm \(O(\log d)\). Constant split-prime losses per slot are lower order. The stronger scale condition used for Fourier bounds in the balanced-value argument is unnecessary for this count. Multiply the product by the chosen ideal over two and call its norm \(n\). Then \(n\asymp X_R\), it is squarefree and \(B\)-smooth. [Prime-bin count](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md#a-disjoint-union-with-ordinary-leading-density)

When \(p\) splits in \(D\), fix a prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) over it and multiply by this smooth ideal. Its norm \(pn\asymp M^2 V\) is squarefree. The product is primitive and consequently has a primitive representation by the reduced form in the corresponding inverse class. Only the elementary form–ideal correspondence is being used. [Form–ideal correspondence](https://www.math.utoronto.ca/~ila/Cox-Primes_of_the_form_x2%2Bny2.pdf)

## Why most parent primes yield enough moderate shapes

In any fixed ideal class the number of integral ideals of norm at most \(2P\) is \(O(P/V+\sqrt P+1)=O(P/V)\), uniformly in \(D\) and the class. To see this, take an integral ideal in the inverse class. Principal generators in it, scaled by the square root of that ideal's norm, lie in a planar lattice of covolume \(\sqrt{|D|}/2\) and shortest length at least one. The latter follows since the relative ideal norm of any nonzero element is a positive integer. Counting a disk of radius \(\sqrt{2P}\) in a reduced planar basis gives \(O(P/V+\sqrt P+1)\). For example use parallel rows spaced by the covolume divided by the shortest length, and within each row points spaced by that shortest length. This uses \(P\gg V^2\).

Call a reduced class exceptional if its leading coefficient is below \(V/(\log V)^K\). There are \(O(V/(\log V)^{K-1})\) such classes by the discriminant root count. Fix \(D\) and one smooth input ideal. As \(p\) varies, at most \(O(P/(\log V)^{K-1})\) parent primes have the product in the exceptional inverse classes, by the preceding ideal count and translation in the class group. This bound counts all relevant ideals and needs no prime distribution within an individual ideal class. Summing over \(D\) and the equal-sized input lists, then dividing by the \(\asymp P/\log P\) parent primes, shows that the average lost fraction relative to \(V^2 S\) is \(O((\log V)^{-K+2})\). There are \(\gg V^2 S\) split choices for every \(p\) before this removal. Markov leaves \(\gg V^2 S\) choices for every parent outside the claimed exception, on taking \(K>J+2\). [Bound for exceptional reduced classes](keybreak-quadratic-class-correction.md#a-short-representative-avoiding-the-modulus)

## Anchors, parity and multiplicity

For the remaining classes, the reduced form \([A,B_0,C]\) has coefficients bounded by \(V(\log V)^{O(K)}\), and both eigenvalues of its positive quadratic matrix lie between \(V/(\log V)^{O(K)}\) and \(V(\log V)^{O(K)}\). Its primitive vector representing \(pn\) therefore has maximum coordinate magnitude between \(M/(\log V)^{O(K)}\) and \(M(\log V)^{O(K)}\). A fixed finite family of unimodular coordinate changes puts it in the indicated positive cone with the desired parity, with an absolute factor in heights. Here are details. First change signs and swap to make both coordinates nonnegative with the larger first. Modulo two the form has two distinct projective roots since \(D\equiv1\bmod8\). The representing vector, primitive and of even represented value, is one of these roots. Map it to \((0,1)\), the other to \((1,1)\), and the nonroot to \((1,0)\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Each of the finitely many invertible binary matrices has a lift with positive entries and determinant one. The two elementary positive shears generate the group modulo two, and one may multiply by a positive matrix congruent to the identity. Finally apply a fixed positive determinant-one matrix congruent to identity modulo two with column slopes in the desired cone, for example \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&4\\12&7\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has slopes in \((.57,.59)\). One may take the fixed anchor interval to contain \([.57,.63]\). These choices make \(Q\) odd and preserve all the required root labels modulo two. The transformed mark has the stated parity and remains positive definite. Moreover \(p\nmid Q\): otherwise, \(p\mid h(r,Q)\) and primitivity of the anchor would force the positive leading coefficient, which is smaller than \(p\), to be a multiple of \(p\).

Given a counted mark and anchor for fixed \(p\), one recovers \(D\) from the discriminant and the numerical squarefree \(n=h(r,Q)/p\). The latter identifies the entire input prime selection, with its orientations fixed for that \(D\). Taking one representative and one permitted transformation per input is therefore injective. There are at most \(M^2(\log V)^{O(K)}\) anchor pairs in the encompassing region. Division of the count by this upper bound gives the stated first moment. It is compatible with dyadically mixing anchor scales with polynomial weights; conditioning on prime \(Q\) does not follow. Nor does taking an expectation imply that a polynomial proportion of fixed environments attains that mean.

## Joint first moment with the algebraic value

The same counted pairs give a first moment for simultaneous rational and algebraic success. Fix a band with \(y=V^{c_0+o(1)}\) for fixed \(c_0>0\), Gaussian width \(\sigma\ge V^{1+\xi}\) polynomial in \(V\), and \(d\) the physical degree. Put \(u_A=\log(V^d M^2\sigma^2)/\log y\). For odd inputs with no prime factor at most \(B\), and eligible parents \(p\nmid N\), average over the primitive anchor, prime multiplier and coefficient Gaussian. For the same exceptional fraction of parents as above, the expected number of marks with smooth rational cofactor and a passing algebraic value is at least
\[
 \frac{V^3}{P}\exp\bigl(-(1+o(1))(u_R\log u_R+u_A\log u_A)\bigr).
\]
Here a passing algebraic value is the primitive relative ideal in the one-mark estimate, with all norm factors at most \(y\) except for a bounded number at most \(V(\log V)^{O(1)}\). In particular the displayed estimate is for a single mixed first moment. Several marks in the same draw use the same anchor, multiplier and polynomial. [Primitive class correction](keybreak-quadratic-class-correction.md) · [One-value distribution](keybreak-quadratic-one-mark.md)

To enforce a uniform upper bound on the absolute root, use a sufficiently large **fixed** power of \(G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}7&4\\12&7\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) in the final coordinate change. In the Riemann sphere the closed region of reduced upper-half-plane roots has only one real boundary point, infinity. The initial signed permutations and choice of positive imaginary root can add zero. The finitely many positive parity lifts send both points to positive real values. Their transformed compact sets thus avoid the repelling fixed point \(-1/\sqrt3\). The powers of \(G\) converge uniformly on that union to \(1/\sqrt3\), as follows either by diagonalizing or from
\[
 G^j z=A_j/C_j-\{C_j^2(z+D_j/C_j)\}^{-1},
 \qquad G^j=\begin{pmatrix}A_j&B_j\\ C_j&D_j\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently one fixed power ensures \(|\theta|\le s<1\), keeps the anchor in the stated cone and preserves parity. The coefficient height is still \(V(\log V)^{O(1)}\). The imaginary part \(\eta=\sqrt{|D|}/(2a)\) is at least an inverse power of \(\log V\), and \(|Q\theta-r|\) is between \(M/(\log V)^{O(1)}\) and \(M(\log V)^{O(1)}\).

For the centered sampler use the original radix \(M=\lfloor N^{1/d_0}\rfloor\), multipliers of order \(M^{C_k}\), and physical degree \(d=d_0+L_0\) with fixed even \(L_0>C_k+1\). The centered-digit height contribution \(kN/Q^d\) is at most \(M^{C_k-L_0+o(1)}\) since \(d\log\log V=o(\log M)\), even at the smallest allowed denominator. The remaining centered-digit and parity coefficients are \(O(d2^d M(\log V)^{O(1)})\). The one-mark kernel has monomial basis of length \(V(\log V)^{O(1)}\), so the fixed power margin in \(\sigma\) still gives its relative Gaussian approximation. The modulus \(H=pn\) is coprime to \(N\) by construction. The multiplier range exponent can be taken sufficiently large uniformly in this band. [Height and parity construction](keybreak-multiplier-residue-phases.md#height-parity-content-and-derivative)

For the particular mark set \(T=a^d\sigma|Q\theta-r|\). The columns corresponding to \(1,\theta\) give smallest standard deviation at least a constant times \(\eta T\). Summing the squared lengths of all the columns gives largest standard deviation \(O_s(T)\). The imaginary component before multiplication by the anchor factor has standard deviation \(O_s(\eta)\) in units of the coefficient width since \(|\Im(\theta^j)|\le j s^{j-1}\eta\). Thus the Gaussian area is \(O_s(\eta T^2)\). The centered term is \(o(\eta T)\). All coprime image points in a disk of radius \(c\eta T\), for a sufficiently small constant \(c>0\), therefore have multiplier-averaged probability \(\gg a^d\sqrt{|D|}/(\eta T^2)\) each. Apply the class count at norm cutoff \(c^2\eta^2T^2/a^d\). This costs a factor of order \(\eta\) in the prefactor. The cutoff differs from \(a^d M^2\sigma^2\) by powers of \(\log V\), and \(\log a=\log V+O(\log\log V)\). Hence the algebraic density lower bound holds uniformly with leading exponent \(-(1+o(1))u_A\log u_A\). Summing that conditional probability over the counted rational pairs proves the claim by linearity of expectation. The proof allows arbitrary dependence among different passing marks.
