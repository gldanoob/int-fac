# High-degree descent with a fixed cofactor

The two translated high directions support conditional score descent with the original cofactor fixed throughout. In each field the four residual coordinate exponents can be \((10000,10000,40000.5+\eta,40000.5+\eta)\) on the \(V\) scale, for any sufficiently small fixed \(\eta>0\). The optimized five-coordinate construction has the same leading scan infimum. A cyclic trial to remove a second correction encounters a large shared residue modulus, and even in the coprime case the available additive estimate misses the required comparison. [High directions and full lattice](cogentic-high-degree-two-directions.md) · [Five-coordinate score descent](cogentic-high-degree-score-descent.md)

## Descent using two corrections

Fix \(k=0\) before drawing coefficients, so both cofactors remain the original ones. In each field use
\[
  f(X)=f^{(0)}(X)+g(m-X)\big[x+Xy+u(A+X)^r+v(A+X)^{r+1}\big],
  \qquad r=d-3.
\]
Give \(u,v\) their independent intervals of length \(T=\lceil e^{(40000.5+\eta)V}\rceil\), and start \(x,y\) in the original broad intervals. At fixed \(u,v\) the new terms preserve the CRT classes and are bounded on \(|X|\le A\) by \(e^{(50001.5+\eta+o(1))V}\). This remains below the original \(X_{\rm sc}/K_1\) margin with strict exponential room. The original Gaussian moment proof, followed by the uniform-box comparison, gives conditional mean score \(o(1)\), uniformly in \(u,v\). The prime omissions and actual mark weights are determined by the fixed cofactors. There are no changing-cofactor omission tails in this construction. [Initial low mean and root margins](cogentic-high-degree-score-descent.md#the-initial-box) · [Fixed-cofactor score expansion](cogentic-conditional-score.md)

For two distinct marks set \(n_i=m-a_i\), \(b_i=A+a_i\), and \(\Delta=a_2-a_1>0\). Subtract the two base values and divide the \(i\)-th evaluation by \(gn_i\). The two coordinates of the projected lattice are
\[
   z_i=x+a_i y+b_i^r(u+b_i v).
\]
Here the low-coordinate image is exactly \(L'=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\mathbb Z^2:z_2\equiv z_1\bmod\Delta\}\), and the high sublattice has rows \(b_i^r(1,b_i)\). Reduce a vector of \(L'\) by the high basis, using floors in its real basis coordinates. The residual lies in \(L'\), with each entry \(O((2A)^{r+1})\). Interpolating the residual as \(x_0+a_i y_0\) gives integers \(x_0,y_0=O((2A)^{r+2})\). Thus \(x,y\) alone mix the quotient to total variation \(e^{-CH}\) once their lengths exceed \(X_0=(2A)^{r+2}e^{C_1H}\), with a sufficiently large fixed \(C_1\). The proof is the same averaging over all class translations as in the full-lattice construction. After a coefficient class modulo \(M=e^{O(H)}\), the same abstract quotient and representatives apply in the new index variables. Taking \(C_1\) large absorbs this division and the requested score precision.

Use \(Z=TA^r\), with leading exponent \(50000.5+\eta\). Keep the low widths comparable within \(e^{O(H)}\) by bisecting a longest side. When the smaller low width exceeds \(Z\), interpolate and freeze the high coordinates on \(e^{O(H)}\) pieces as in the broad regime of the five-coordinate construction. After each coupling, discard scalar pieces shorter than the low side times \(e^{-C_2H}\) using the usual area bound. The retained scalar lengths have leading exponent at least \(50000.5+\eta\). The arbitrary-modulus sharp scalar counter applies directly since the physical heights remain \(\exp((100001+o(1))V)\). No cofactor orbit is needed.

For smaller low widths, couple to the lattice \(L'\) in the frozen high parallelogram using the two correction widths just proved. The narrow-panel construction and its coupling error only use the singular widths \(Z e^{\pm O(H)}\) in the normalized coordinates, so its estimates transfer after dividing the two rows by \(gn_i\). Split retained axis-aligned squares into residues modulo the small \(M\Delta\). The physical strides now have the form \(gn_i e_i\), where \(e_i=e^{O(H)}\), while the lengths of the scalar progressions still have exponent \(50000.5+\eta\). The large factor \(n_i\) causes no factorization step. Exact mark eligibility requires \(\gcd(n_i,k_0N)=1\), and the marked value is congruent to \(k_0N\bmod n_i\). After dividing a prescribed squarefree factor at an eligible mark, its remaining value is still a unit modulo \(n_i\). Consequently all common divisors of the physical residue and stride are found by factoring the small factor alone. Divide out the eligible forced primes or reject the class in the usual way, keeping the scalar length and height exponents. [Sharp progression counter](cogentic-scalar-divisor-count.md#a-sharp-count-for-long-progressions-at-arbitrary-moduli)

Fix the retained row predicates in the original coefficient residues before either coupling. One-mark terms use the same estimates with a constant second factor. The remaining precision, bounded finite score weights and \(O(V)\) conditional comparisons work as in the five-coordinate descent. Both \(x,y\) reach their correction floors with mean \(o(1)\). The residual volume has exponent \(100001+2\eta\) per field. The five-coordinate widths can instead use \(T=\lceil e^{(39999.5+\eta)V}\rceil\), since their pooled small-step panels then have physical lengths above the square-root threshold and their broad low widths exceed the full-cofactor scalar cutoff \(49999.5V\). Hence that version also has exponent \(100001+2\eta\). Fixing the cofactor removes one averaged coordinate but gives no improvement against the optimized infimum.

## Cycling one correction instead of two

For a fixed \(y\) with the cofactor fixed, each high coset inside a physical panel separates into the \(\Delta\) choices \(j_1\equiv j_2\pmod\Delta\) where \(z_i=x+a_i y+b_i^r j_i\). In a fixed choice of this residue \(h\), mark \(i\) samples
\[
  z_i\equiv x+a_i y+b_i^r h\pmod {s_i},\qquad s_i=\Delta b_i^r.
\]
Thus the \(x\)-periods both have exponent \(10000V\). A complete \(x\)-cycle still couples the classes through \(\gcd(s_1,s_2)=\Delta\gcd(b_1,b_2)^r\). In particular every such pair satisfies \(z_2-z_1\equiv\Delta y\) modulo \(\gcd(b_1,b_2)^r\). This can be a modulus of leading exponent \(10000V\): choose an even \(b_1\) in \((A,4A/3]\) and \(b_2=3b_1/2\). When these marks pass the mark-unit conditions, cyclic pooling alone does not remove that modulus. The coprime case avoids this obstacle.

Even for coprime \(b_i\), apply the oscillatory Mellin bound to a high harmonic of the \(x\) orbit in one retained panel. Write its physical height and width as \(\mathcal X=\exp((100001+o(1))V)\) and \(W/\sqrt{\mathcal X}=\exp((\delta+o(1))V)\), where \(\delta\) is a fixed constant near the desired widths. Let \(q=gn_i e_i=\exp((1+o(1))V)\) be the physical stride of the normalized coordinate, allowing any retained small splits. We may suppose the physical residue \(c\bmod q\) is a unit by the division just described. Approximate the normalized phase \(\theta=j/s_i\) by \(a/r_0\) with \(r_0\le Q=\lfloor W/\sqrt{q\mathcal X}\rfloor\) and \(|\beta|=|\theta-a/r_0|\le1/(r_0Q)\). For reduced denominator of \(\theta\) exceeding \(Q\), \(\beta\ne0\). The case of interest for saving has \(\delta>1/2\), so \(Q\) grows.

The rational phase \(e(a(m-c)/(q r_0))\), supported on \(m\equiv c\bmod q\), can be expanded in multiplicative characters modulo \(q r_0\) after dividing off the squarefree \(\gcd(m,\operatorname{rad}(r_0))\). The divided value is a unit modulo \(q r_0\), and the supported class modulo \(q\) contains at most \(r_0\) units modulo \(q r_0\), since the forced factor is coprime to \(q\). Thus Parseval bounds its character coefficient absolute sum by \(\sqrt{r_0}\), including when \(q,r_0\) share primes. The residual physical phase has frequency \(\beta/q\). Its Mellin \(L^1\) bandwidth cost is bounded by \(\big(1+|\beta|W^2/(q\mathcal X)\big)^{1/2}\), using the demodulation in the additive-spectrum proof. The stationary shift stays away from the low contour because \(|\beta|W/q\ge W/(q s_i r_0)=\exp((40000+o(1))V)\) or larger. For the width range under consideration this also makes the two-derivative low-contour error exponentially small in absolute count units. The conductors and Mellin heights still have logarithms \(O(V)\), so the same uniform RH Euler bounds apply. [Oscillatory Mellin estimate](cogentic-band-additive-spectrum.md#additive-sums-in-a-physical-interval)

Taking the square root of \(r_0+(W^2/(q\mathcal X))/Q\) bounds the combined bandwidth and character cost by \(O(\sqrt Q)\). The orbit coefficient is a count normalized by \(W/q\), rather than by \(W\). The resulting high-mode bound is therefore \(\exp((3/4-\delta/2+o(1))V)\) after endpoint smoothing. For an incomplete \(x\)-length of exponent \(\lambda\) between \(10000\) and \(20000\), the same two-mark CRT kernel argument gives a high-mode error bound of leading exponent \(3/4-\delta/2+(20000-\lambda)/2\). The low-low part has error exponent at most \(3(\delta-1/2)-\lambda\), by the reduced denominator gap and Parseval, so it is already negligible at the stated widths. Boundary smoothing is removed in the incomplete and complete averages as before, since each individual period is smaller than the \(x\)-interval. If using this bound for cancellation, one needs \(\delta>1.5\) and \(\lambda>20001.5-\delta\). The two high coordinate exponents are \(39999.5+\delta\), so the remaining volume has exponent strictly greater than \(100000.5+\delta>100002\) for this choice. For the initial shorter widths the available bound does not certify a saving from this rank-one comparison. This calculation concerns the specific cyclic \(x\)-orbit and the current one-mark bound. It does not rule out a joint squarefree-product estimate or another structured sampler.

## Finite check

An exact check enumerated \(51794\) two-correction quotient classes and their bounded representatives in three finite mark pairs, as well as all \(23190\) corresponding full-cycle \(x\)-orbit pairs. It verified the surviving congruence at several \(y\)-origins and counted the support of the rational phase in each class modulo \(q\) for all \(2\le q,r_0<30\).
