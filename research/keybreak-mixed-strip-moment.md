# Two balanced prime-product tests after averaging the multiplier

Consider a fixed top band, with \(b_i\asymp V\) distinct prime mark denominators, \(h=d-2\), \(X=V^d\), and \(\log M\asymp\log V\). Use the fixed-top scaling \(d\asymp D\to\infty\), \(\log V=D^{O(1)}\), \(d\log\log X=o(\log V)\), and \(\log n=o(\log V)\) for the bit-length parameter \(n\). Here \(d\) is the actual field degree, including a fixed even increment if needed. Use the original slope and unit conditions \(1/6\le a_i/b_i\le11/12\), \(G_i\ne0\), \(b_i\nmid2G_i\), and \((N,G_1G_2)=1\). Use the exact target \(kN\) with \(k\) a fresh prime of size \(M^{C}\) as in the multiplier residue lemma. In this note \(k\) denotes this multiplier, not a count of factors. All averaging is on a fixed rational-good environment and, in the incoming case, conditional on one common admissible root only. Use the root and unit hypotheses of the multiplier residue lemma and require \(\sigma/q\to\infty\) for nonzero incoming tests, as holds for the source top cutoff. [Multiplier support and root probabilities](keybreak-multiplier-residue-phases.md)

Put the denominators in \((2V/3,(2V/3)(1+1/d)]\), so \(b^{d-1}\) varies by a bounded factor. The numerator constraint gives \(|G(P)|\asymp M V\). All top marginal widths before division are thus within fixed constants of a common \(X_*\asymp\sigma M V(2V/3)^{d-1}\), which is deterministic given the band and \(N\). The multiplier digit bound gives \(|F_0(P)|/T_P^{\mathrm{raw}}\ll\operatorname{poly}(d)2^d/\sigma=o(1)\) uniformly in \(k\); this ratio is unchanged by division. In particular the marginal Gaussian cutoff box has endpoints bounded by a polylogarithmic multiple of \(X_*/q\). Choose a squarefree balanced strip \(\mathcal S\subset[X_*,2X_*]\) using the disjoint prime-bin construction with cutoff \(y=V^{c+o(1)}\), fixed \(c>0\), and bin relative widths \(\asymp1/d\). The bins have lower endpoints \(y^{1-o(1)}\), so every bin prime is at least \(V^{c_0}\) for a common fixed \(c_0>0\). Let \(\mu=|\mathcal S|/X_*\). The ordinary test asks \(F(P)\in\mathcal S\). For a prescribed incoming child \(q\), its divided test asks \(F(P)/q\in\mathcal S_q=\{n/q:n\in\mathcal S,\ q\mid n\}\), with size zero outside its prime bins. Set \(q=1\), \(\mathcal S_1=\mathcal S\) in the ordinary case. For every allowed \(q\),
\[
 \mu_q=\frac{|\mathcal S_q|}{X_*/q}\le D^{O(1)}\mu.
\]
Indeed membership in a bin selects at most one prime, and the fraction of tuples using a prescribed prime there is at most the reciprocal of the bin's prime population. If the bin has relative width \(\asymp1/d\), this is \(O(d\log y/q)\). Each admissible pattern permits every prime in its bin. One can upper bound the probability that the bin is selected by one, without a lower bound on the number of its patterns. All prime factors in the strip have size \(V^{c+o(1)}\) for a fixed \(c>0\). [Squarefree prime-bin construction and localized variance](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md)

## Denominator of the Poisson quotient

Use \(\sigma\ge V^{1+\xi}\) for some fixed \(\xi>0\), \(\sigma=V^{O(1)}\), and retain short dual modes up to \(\sigma\|w\|\le(\log X)^2\) as before. The digit box then has \(2S\ll V^{1-\xi+o(1)}<b_i\), with the retained mode set consisting only of zero if the digit bound is below one. The conductor argument is injective in either step-frequency grid \(\mathbb Z/B_i\) with \(B_i=b_i^h\); each nonzero mode gives a nonzero grid point. Write \(m_i=2|G_i\Delta|/q\), \(x=X_*/q\), \(L_i=x/m_i\), and \(Q_i=L_i/B_i\). The width ratios satisfy \(Q_i\gg\sigma b_i/|\Delta|\gg V^{\xi-o(1)}\). On any progression phase and interval of length at most a polylogarithmic multiple of \(x\), an unweighted sum at step frequency \(a/B_i\ne0\) has absolute value \(O(1/\|a/B_i\|)\). Summing over the injective coordinate and dividing by \(L_i\) costs \(O(\log B_i/Q_i)\). The other coordinate has the trivial normalized bound \((\log X)^{O(1)}\). After the twisted Gaussian variation loss, the sum over modes is \(V^{-\xi+2\zeta+o(1)}\) on pairs with \(\vartheta\le V^\zeta\). The discarded Fourier tail is superpolynomially small, since the native dual mass at this width is at most \(X^{\max(1-\xi,0)+o(1)}\). Thus the Poisson denominator is \(1+O(V^{-c'})\) for **each multiplier** and fixed root restriction on each such pair when \(2\zeta<\xi\). This uses the restricted ideal image for normalization, regardless of the actual phase masses. ## Second moment for the numerator

For this pair temporarily remove from the allowed bins the primes dividing \(m_1m_2b_1b_2\). In the divided case keep the prescribed \(q\) as an external factor. These deletions depend on the fixed pair and root, independently of \(k\). At most \(O(1)\) primes in the allowed size range are removed, since \(m_1m_2b_1b_2=V^{O(1)}\). A removed prime \(p\) divides a tested divided value with probability \(O(1/p)+O(X^{-c})\) on the actual single-root one-point law, unless impossible. Indeed the one-point ideal support is an arithmetic progression of step \(2|G(P)|\), with coprimality at its prime divisors under the anchor and unit hypotheses. If \(p\nmid2G(P)\), the Gaussian progression mod \(p\) has the usual density since its width divided by \(2|G(P)|p\) is \(X^{1+o(1)}\). The one-point smoothing comparison holds as \(\sigma\gg V\,\mathrm{poly}(n)\), including image conditioning. Squarefreeness already excludes a second copy of \(q\). Hence the cost of the exclusions is \(V^{-c+o(1)}\), negligible against \(\mu^2=V^{-o(1)}\).

The remaining shape \(\mathcal S'_q\) is a disjoint union of products of primes coprime to both periods and grid moduli. Each free bin loses only \(O(1)\) primes from its \(V^{c+o(1)}\) population, and its twisted prime sum changes by \(O(1)\), absorbed by the same square-root GRH bound uniformly over the Mellin contour. In the divided case a surviving pattern contains the bin of \(q\) and fixes that prime. Removing the fixed slot preserves disjointness of patterns and leaves products with lower-endpoint scale comparable to \(x\), since \(q\) differs from its bin's lower endpoint by \(1+O(1/d)\). The bounded relative character estimate therefore still sums over the surviving patterns and gives interval supremums via the same common Mellin integral. The reference for these errors can be the full pruned shape mass, which is at most \(|\mathcal S_q|\).

For coordinate \(i\), a phase residue \(r_i\bmod m_i\), and a frequency \(a/B_i\), let \(P_i(r_i,a)\) be the supremum of the absolute interval sum of the divided test, with step phase \(e(a j/B_i)\), divided by \(L_i\). For the clipped Gaussian box only intervals within a fixed polylogarithmic multiple of \(x\) are needed; the support of the test itself is in \([x,2x]\). The multiplicative-character Parseval estimate for a product strip, with its common Mellin integral controlling all prefixes, gives
\[
 \frac{1}{\phi(m_i)}
  \sum_{r_i\in(\mathbb Z/m_i)^\times}
  \sum_{a\ne0} P_i(r_i,a)^2
 \ \ll\ (D^{O(1)}\mu)^2\, V^{o(1)}
           \left(\frac{m_i B_i}{x}+\frac1{b_i}\right).
\]
Changing the character from raw integers to the progression index multiplies the frequency by a unit modulo \(B_i\) and translates its phase. The factor \(m_i/\phi(m_i)=V^{o(1)}\) is allowed in this variance estimate. Every nonunit \(r_i\) gives zero on the pruned shape. [Prime-product variance, including interval maxima](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md)

Mix the Cartesian phases using their *ideal* Gaussian masses and average over the multiplier. Ideal phase weights are bounded above by a constant times uniform weights: on each translate tile comparison with the Gaussian integral has relative mesh \(O(\vartheta\sum m_i/T_i)=X^{-1+o(1)}\). The single-root conditioning changes the prime-multiplier law by a bounded Radon–Nikodym ratio since its conditional probability for each admissible multiplier is \(\asymp1/q\). Let \(\ell=|\Delta|/q\). The marginal probability of any \(r_i\bmod m_i\) is at most \(O(1/(\phi(2|G_i|)\ell))\), since the multiplier is pointwise comparable to uniform on its unit classes and the ideal phase supplies all \(\ell\) lifts of the base residue. Relative to \(1/m_i\), this costs \(2|G_i|/\phi(2|G_i|)=V^{o(1)}\). In the squared sums the nonunit \(r_i\) contribute zero, so the comparison to the average over unit \(r_i\) costs at most the same factor. Dirichlet GRH gives the stated prime sampling comparison at polynomial modulus \(V^{O(1)}\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(C\). Only marginal domination is used. The two residues may be strongly correlated. The progression periods, pruned test, endpoint and map from mode to step frequency are fixed across multipliers. The multiplier changes only the phase translates, the centers and phase constants in the Fourier representation. [Prime target multiplier and Cartesian phase marginals](keybreak-multiplier-residue-phases.md)

By Cauchy–Schwarz over the product of the mode-counting measure and the actual common multiplier/ideal-phase measure,
\[
 \mathbb E\sum_{s\ne0} P_1(r_1,a_1(s))P_2(r_2,a_2(s))
 \le \prod_{i=1}^2
       \left(\mathbb E\sum_{a\ne0}P_i(r_i,a)^2\right)^{1/2}
 \ll \mu^2 V^{-\min(\xi,1)+o(1)}.
\]
The Gaussian Fourier weights are at most one. In each squared factor, injectivity permits extending the sum over mode frequencies to the whole nonzero grid before applying the corresponding marginal bound. The triangular lift of each short mode has Mahalanobis frequency \(O((\log X)^2)\), and the normalized two-coordinate summation-by-parts loss is \(V^{2\zeta+o(1)}\). Choose \(2\zeta<\min(\xi,1)\). The finite sampler and Gaussian tails remain negligible even relative to \(\mu^2\). If the Poisson numerator and denominator at \(k\) are \(Z_k+E_k\) and \(1+A_k\), respectively, then \(Z_k\ge0\), \(\sup_k|A_k|=o(1)\) and \(\mathbb E_k|E_k|=o(\mu^2)\), with the same conditional multiplier measure throughout. In particular
\[
\mathbb E_k\!\left[\frac{Z_k+E_k}{1+A_k}\right]
\le (1+o(1))\mathbb E_k Z_k+o(\mu^2).
\]
Thus the multiplier average is taken over actual, individually normalized pair probabilities. This calculation uses the pruned test. For the original test, restoring the deleted primes increases the actual pair probability by at most the already bounded one-point deletion probability. Its ideal zero-mode term is at least the pruned zero-mode term by positivity. [Mixed variation](keybreak-gaussian-variation.md) · [Distinct step frequencies](keybreak-dual-major-arc-count.md)

The pair estimate requires the separate ideal zero-mode upper bound \(D^{O(1)}\mu^2\) and an ordinary one-point lower bound to complete a top second-moment argument. The balanced-strip incidence estimate, elliptic deletions, thin pairs, and interior hypotheses enter separately in any cost conclusion.
