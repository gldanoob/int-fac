# Factoring with order multiples and partial residue information

November 24, 2026. The verified Dirichlet GRH result discharges the analytic hypothesis in two classical reductions. The [leaderboard](https://bigospeedrun.com/integer-factoring) still lists $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log b})}$ for smallest-prime-factor computation on every $b$-bit input. All oracle times below must be charged in an end-to-end comparison.

## A linear number of queries on one cofactor

Bach's 1984 reduction splits an odd composite with at least two distinct prime factors given access to multiplicative orders. His proof permits a nonzero multiple of the chosen order. For a general discrete-log oracle with signed integer answers, Bach queries $(a^\ell)^y=a\bmod m$ for several small primes $\ell$. When a solution is returned, $\ell y-1$ is a nonzero order multiple. Some of these queries can be insoluble. The deterministic search for a witnessing base uses Dirichlet characters. Alpöge's theorem covers them for every modulus. [Bach, §§2–3](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1984/Archive/CSD-84-186.pdf) · [Independent verification, statements 3–5](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

**Consequence for the search.** Suppose a deterministic routine takes an odd modulus $m$ and a small prime unit $a$ and returns any positive, polynomial-bit multiple of $\operatorname{ord}_m(a)$. Complete factorization of $N$ takes at most $b$ calls, all at the same remaining cofactor, and polynomial bit work outside the calls. A routine returning **any nonnegative** solution of $a^x=a^{-1}\bmod m$ with polynomial bit length meets this contract via $x+1$. These particular discrete-log instances are always soluble. Allowing $x=-1$ would invalidate this conversion. This query bound combines the published reductions with the accumulation argument below. [Miller, Lemma 5 and Theorem 4](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf) · [Bach on small witnesses](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1990-1023756-8)

1. Take an effective $B=O(b^2)$ large enough for Bach's small-generator bound at every modulus up to $N$. Remove all prime divisors through $B$ with multiplicity, leaving $m$. Stop if $m=1$. The prime bases $a\le B$ generate the units modulo $m$. Indeed every unit in the small integer generating set factors into primes in it. Dirichlet GRH is the analytic input here.
2. Start at $E=1$. For each such prime $a$, test $a^E\bmod m$. If it is 1, continue. Otherwise obtain a positive order multiple $A$ and set $E=\operatorname{lcm}(E,A)$. All earlier bases remain annihilated.
3. For the query bound write $\lambda=\lambda(m)$, used only in the analysis. The old $\gcd(E,\lambda)$ divides the new one. The new gcd is divisible by $\operatorname{ord}_m(a)$, and the old one is not. Thus the gcd at least doubles on every call, even when $A$ contains large extraneous primes. After $k$ calls, $2^k\le\lambda\le m$, so $k\le b$. The accumulated $E$ has polynomial bit length since it divides the product of those replies. [Lean proof of the doubling step and count](../formal/OrderQueries.lean)
4. The final $E$ annihilates all units modulo $m$. For every divisor $d$ of $m$ the reduction of units modulo $m$ onto units modulo $d$ is surjective, hence $\lambda(d)$ divides $E$. Apply Miller's deterministic splitting procedure with this same $E$ throughout the recursion, treating primes and perfect powers separately. This gives all prime factors with multiplicities. [Miller's reduction](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/Mi76.pdf#page=11)

An oracle cost $Q(b)$ for these bases therefore gives time $bQ(b)+\operatorname{poly}(b)$ when $Q(b)$ bounds all queries of at most $b$ bits of modulus. The remaining target is an algorithm generating the order multiples from $m,a$ within the desired bound. A large-order element chosen by a different algorithm fills a different contract.

## Chinese remainder decoding with missing or incorrect levels

Boneh's Theorem 5.1 already gives the polynomial construction used in the [triprime handoff](round-3.md#two-upstream-field-evaluations-per-level-suffice-for-triprimes). For a list of admissible residues at each pairwise coprime level, form the product of the corresponding monic linear factors, pad to a common degree and combine coefficients by the Chinese remainder theorem. Recovery tolerating errors follows from the small-root theorem on an unknown divisor. [Boneh, §5](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82341411.pdf#page=14) · [Cohn–Heninger, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1284#page=2)

Specifically, let $M$ be the product of the levels, $d$ the common degree, and $G_w$ the product of levels where the list contains $w$ modulo that level. If $G_w\ge M^\beta$ and $\lvert w\rvert\le M^{\beta^2/d}$ for a fixed $0<\beta\le1$, the theorem lists $w$ in polynomial time because $G_w$ divides $\gcd(M,f(w))$. For the handoff's $M>N^{24}$, $d\le6$ and $w=p^2<N^2$, agreement on at least $1/\sqrt2$ of the **logarithmic weight** $\sum_q\log q$ suffices for each required prime square. Keep the handoff's exact square and divisibility tests on every candidate. This deduction relaxes the coefficient provider's correctness requirement. Its running time and guaranteed agreement on each input remain open. [Small roots on an unknown divisor](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1284#page=2)

The same theorem explains a limitation of searching a whole smoothness base with one lattice. Boneh's §4 uses one monic $f$ and the product $S$ of maximal prime powers through $Y$ to find values with large $\gcd(f(x),S)$. For $S=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,Y)$ one has $\log S=\Theta(Y)$. With nonzero $\lvert f(x)\rvert\le 2^{O(b)}$, the available threshold $T$ for that gcd has $\log T=O(b)$. Substituting $\beta=\log T/\log S$ in the displayed small-root radius gives $\log B\le(\log T)^2/(d\log S)=O(b^2/(dY))$. At the upstream cutoff $Y=\lceil\exp((\log b)^2)\rceil$ this numerical guarantee cannot reach $B=2$ for large $b$. This concerns the radius certified by the single full-base polynomial construction. It leaves searches with smaller selected products or different relation encodings open. [Boneh, Theorem 4.1 and discussion](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82341411.pdf#page=12) · [Upstream smoothness cutoff](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/integer-factoring/blob/1b331929bb774ae8dcb8f50cdd10a3243b7c649f/research/small-index-smooth-density.md)

## Other published cases with explicit promises

| Available information | Deterministic guarantee |
| --- | --- |
| A residue $r\bmod s$ for a divisor | For fixed $\alpha>1/4$, $s>N^\alpha$, $0<r<s<N$ and $\gcd(r,s)=\gcd(s,N)=1$, Coppersmith–Howgrave-Graham–Nagaraj construct all divisors in the class in polynomial time for sufficiently large $N$. This gives an existing final step for a short list of known residue classes. Finding a short list that contains a factor on each input is the cost left to bound. [Corollary 3.1](https://dominoweb.draco.res.ibm.com/reports/rc23466.pdf) |
| A prime divisor $p$ with $p-1$ smooth | For $B\ge\ln N$, Źrałek's Corollary 4.6 splits a composite in $O(B(\ln N)^5)$ deterministic time if some $p-1$ has no prime divisor above $B$. If all but at most one prime divisor have that property, the complete factorization takes $O(B(\ln N)^6)$. Knowledge of $p$ and a factorization of $p-1$ are unnecessary. [Corollary 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=7) |
| A high-multiplicity divisor | Harvey–Hittmeir list every positive integer $u$ with $u^r\mid N$ in $O_\epsilon(N^{1/(4r)}(\log N)^{10+\epsilon}/r^3)$ deterministic bit operations, uniformly for $1\le r\le\log_2 N$. For $r\ge(\log b)^\alpha$ this listing costs $2^{O(b/(\log b)^\alpha)}$. Prime factors of lower multiplicity can still occur in the remaining cofactor. [Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.12401#page=1) |
| A single exact ordinary divisor sum | For squarefree odd $N=p_1\cdots p_k$ with $p_1>\cdots>p_k$, $k\ge3$, put $E=p_{r+1}\cdots p_k$, $1\le r<k$. If $\gcd(N,\sigma(N))=1$, Theorem 5.5 gives deterministic polynomial-time recovery of $p_1\cdots p_r$ from $\sigma(N)$ when $p_r\ge E^2$. For $r=k-1$, the complementary factor is the smallest prime. General inputs still need a deterministic replacement for the polynomial and ring-element sampling in Bach–Miller–Shallit. [Morain–Renault–Smith, Theorem 5.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444#page=13) · [Bach–Miller–Shallit, Algorithm B](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/BMS86.pdf#page=6) |
| Multiples of projective elliptic-curve point counts | Dąbrowski–Pomykała–Shparlinski give a deterministic split for all but $O_\delta(X/(\log X)^\delta)$ integers through $X$, for fixed $0<\delta<1/4$. Their oracle supplies a polynomially bounded positive multiple of the product of the local projective counts for $y^2=x(x^2-b)$ on squarefree inputs coprime to $b$, with an error response on nonsquarefree inputs. With $\gamma=(9+\sqrt{561})/16$, the reduction uses $O((\log X)^\gamma)$ queries and $(\log X)^{\gamma+2+o(1)}$ work outside the oracle. The exception bound is over integers up to $X$ and need not spare a particular family of semiprimes. [Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.00345#page=5) |

Dryło–Pomykała give a different promise for an exact **point order**. For squarefree composite $n$ coprime to $6$, fix a good curve whose group order is even at some $p\mid n$. At least $27/80$ of the points in the CRT product group have local orders with unequal $2$-adic valuations. For such a point, its global order determines a split by successive doubling after multiplying by the odd part. The probability statement uses uniform points and does not give a deterministic seed schedule. On the [round-two trace-zero family](round-2.md), one exact projective count already gives $(p+1)(q+1)$, and hence $p+q$ for $n=pq$. Producing the count remains a separate task. [Dryło–Pomykała](https://doi.org/10.4064/cm7661-2-2019) · [Author’s quantitative statement, slide 44](https://nutmic2024.usz.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/NuTMiC-2024-slides-Pomykala.pdf#page=44)

For a squarefree $N$, every $p+1$ with $p\mid N$ divides $\sigma(N)$. The cyclotomic derandomization in Źrałek's Theorem 5.5 requires a smooth cyclotomic value and a fixed number-field polynomial with the required irreducibility at a hidden prime. A multiple alone gives neither property. A deterministic finite-field polynomial factorizer also takes the field characteristic as input. This leaves the distinguishing polynomial and ring-element choices in the general single-$\sigma(N)$ reduction unresolved. [Źrałek, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=8) · [Bach–Miller–Shallit, §§3–4](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~glmiller/Publications/Papers/BMS86.pdf#page=6)

On the coprime squarefree inputs of the divisor-sum row, a direct continued-fraction criterion gives a stronger output under an additional promise. For each prefix $M_j=p_1\cdots p_j$, $3\le j\le k$, suppose $\lvert\sigma(M_j)/M_j-(p_j+1)/p_j\rvert<1/(2p_j^2)$. Then $(p_j+1)/p_j$ is a convergent. Test the denominators for prime divisors and choose the smallest, then divide the current sum by $p_j+1$ to continue. The two-prime case follows from the sum and product of the primes. This deduction uses a single supplied $\sigma(N)$ for all steps. [Continued-fraction criterion, Propositions 3.4–3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.08444#page=7)
