# Adaptive exact recovery of the joint square kernel

Given exact smooth-prime ideal valuations and nonzero marked elements in the two monic fields, one can recover the full joint square kernel and a square root in each field for each output basis vector. The algorithm uses no prime-ideal character cutoff. Its cost is polynomial in the input times $2^r$, where $r$ is the actual rank of the joint square-class map restricted to the ideal-parity kernel. This gives the one-third time bound whenever $r=O(W)$. Simple exact ideal certificates and an infinite family of growing-degree fields below give unconditional cases of this rank bound.

## Searching only independent square classes

Let $R$ be the number of marked columns and $\gamma_{ij}=a_j-b_j\alpha_i$ the column in field $K_i$. Compute a basis $v_1,\dots,v_J\in\mathbb F_2^R$ of the joint ideal-parity kernel. For a mask $v$, let $\gamma_i(v)=\prod_{j:v_j=1}\gamma_{ij}$, and let $\Phi(v)$ be the tuple of its square classes. Then $\Phi$ is linear because the difference between $\gamma_i(v+w)$ and $\gamma_i(v)\gamma_i(w)$ is a square. Every square dependency lies in the parity kernel.

Process the basis in order. Keep masks $p_1,\dots,p_s$ from earlier basis columns whose images are linearly independent. For the new column $v_j$, enumerate the $2^s$ masks
\[
                     h=v_j+\sum_{\nu=1}^s c_\nu p_\nu,\qquad c_\nu\in\mathbb F_2.
\]
For each mask use the [exact power-basis square test](cogentic-monic-square-extraction.md) on $\gamma_i(h)$ in each field. If every test returns a root, emit $h$ and its roots, and continue to the next basis column. The matching coefficient tuple is unique by pivot independence. If none matches, append $v_j$ to the pivots. At the end, the pivots span the image of $\Phi$ and each emitted relation has a distinct leading basis coordinate $v_j$ with all its other coordinates drawn from earlier pivots. The emitted relations are therefore independent. There are $J-r$ of them, so they form a basis of the full joint kernel. To obtain roots for any combined dependency, run the same square test on its final support mask.

There are at most $J2^r$ joint tests. Build every candidate product directly from its support mask in the original marked columns. No intermediate expression contains an exponent larger than one in a marked column. With polynomial height $H_0\ge2$ and mark bound $A_0\ge2$, multiplication by $a-b\alpha$ in the power basis increases the coefficient maximum norm by at most $(H_0+2)A_0$. Multiplication by $\alpha$ shifts the coefficient vector and reduces its last term by the monic polynomial, which gives this bound. After at most $R$ marked factors, each reduced coefficient has $O(R(\log A_0+\log H_0))$ bits. Binary elimination, mask arithmetic, exact square decisions and root recovery thus give total cost
\[
  \operatorname{poly}(\text{input size})+
  J2^r\,\operatorname{poly}(R,J,\max_i[K_i:\mathbb Q],\log A_0,\log H_0).
\]
The first term includes computation of the parity kernel from the supplied exact valuations. The space can be polynomial in the same parameters and includes the output roots. On the monic schedule all polynomial factors are $\exp(O(W))$. Real-sign rows may also be imposed before taking a basis. Their signs are exact from real root isolation and the comparisons $a_j-b_j\alpha_i^{(\nu)}$.

A meet-in-the-middle improvement needs a computable class label or further arithmetic. In a membership-only model for one new class, every query that contains that class reduces to equality with one specific subset of the independent pivots. An all-negative transcript of fewer than $2^s$ such queries is consistent both with a new independent class and with equality to an untested subset. This is an obstruction to a generic search using just those membership answers.

## Rank certificates from principal ideal relations

For each field let $S_i$ be the exact finite ideal support and let $G_i$ be the subgroup of the ordinary class group generated by these ideals. The [parity exact sequence](cogentic-monic-square-rank.md#ideal-parity-and-units) sends an even-valuation class to the class of its half ideal and bounds
\[
          r \le \sum_i \bigl(r_{1,i}+r_{2,i}+\dim_{\mathbb F_2} G_i[2]\bigr).
\]
The unit dimension already accounts for real signatures. Adding sign rows can only reduce the rank that needs adaptive processing.

For each field, reduce any set of verified principal $S_i$-ideal relation vectors modulo two. If those vectors have rank $c_i$, then
\[
           \dim_{\mathbb F_2}G_i[2]=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}G_i/2G_i
                         \le |S_i|-c_i.
\]
Indeed, $\mathbb Z^{S_i}\to G_i$ is onto and kills the principal relation vectors. Reducing the presentation modulo two yields the bound. This holds even when an element of $G_i[2]$ is a double in the full class group, because the quotient is taken in $G_i$ itself. The verified valuation columns already present in each field are principal ideal relations, so their **separate** parity ranks yield such a certificate with no extra arithmetic. Other verified relations can be appended. A near-full certificate with total deficit $\sum_i(|S_i|-c_i)=O(W)$ is sufficient for the monic schedule, since the sum of field degrees is $O(W)$.

For good degree-one primes, one inexpensive additional certificate is a nonzero integral element $\delta$ together with an asserted product of simple-root prime ideals with nonnegative valuations $e_{\mathfrak p}$. Hensel-lift each indicated root modulo $p^{e_{\mathfrak p}}$ and verify that $\delta$ vanishes there. If its absolute norm equals $\prod_{\mathfrak p}p^{e_{\mathfrak p}}$, those valuation lower bounds exhaust the norm, hence give the exact principal relation. Here $\delta$ is given in the integral power order and $p$ avoids the index and the polynomial discriminant. The certificates are verified in time polynomial in the element bit length and expanded ideal norm bit length. The special case of a generator with norm $p$ certifies its sole ideal principal.

A rational gcd-free basis applied to norms cannot replace the prime-ideal labels or control the residual class term. For instance in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ the element $2+\sqrt{-5}$ has norm $9$ and ideal $\mathfrak p^2$, where $\mathfrak p$ is the prime over $3$ at the root $+1$. Its conjugate root is a unit for this element. The prime is nonprincipal because the ring of integers is $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ and $x^2+5y^2=3$ has no integer solutions. Consequently $2+\sqrt{-5}$ is a nonsquare with square norm and zero ideal parity. The exact integer norm and its factorization already exhibit the loss of information.

## An unconditional family with bounded residual rank

Suppose the normal closure $L/\mathbb Q$ of a monic field $K$ of degree $e$ has a $2$-group Galois group and is unramified outside a set $\Sigma$ of finite rational primes. Then
\[
                 \dim_{\mathbb F_2}\mathrm{Cl}(K)[2]\le 1+e|\Sigma|.
                 \tag{1}
\]
For the proof take the maximal elementary abelian unramified $2$-extension $H/K$ from the ordinary Hilbert class field. Its Galois group has dimension $\dim\mathrm{Cl}(K)[2]$. Form the compositum $E$ over $L$ of all conjugates of $HL$. Each conjugate extension over $L$ is elementary abelian and unramified at finite primes, because base extension and compositum preserve unramifiedness over the corresponding local fields. Thus $E/\mathbb Q$ is Galois with a finite $2$-group $G$ and unramified outside $\Sigma$ at finite primes. The minimal number of generators of $G$ is the dimension of its Frattini quotient, which also counts the independent quadratic characters of $G$. Every quadratic subfield of $E$ is $\mathbb Q(\sqrt b)$ for a rational squarefree representative $b$ whose prime factors lie in $\Sigma$, since every odd prime dividing $b$ ramifies and an even $b$ also ramifies at two. The sign supplies one further bit, so $G$ is generated by at most $|\Sigma|+1$ elements. Schreier's bound gives at most $1+e|\Sigma|$ generators for the index-$e$ subgroup $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$. Its quotient $\operatorname{Gal}(H/K)$ proves (1).

If $|\Sigma|=1$ the class group actually has odd order. An inertia group in any finite Galois $2$-extension of $\mathbb Q$ unramified outside a single finite prime must equal the full Galois group. Otherwise it lies in an index-two subgroup, normal in the $2$-group, whose fixed quadratic field is unramified at every finite prime. No quadratic field over $\mathbb Q$ has this property. Apply this to $E/\mathbb Q$. As $E/L$ is unramified, the inertia group has trivial intersection with $\operatorname{Gal}(E/L)$. Hence $E=L$. In particular $H\subset L$, and $L/K$ is totally ramified at the single finite prime, forcing $H=K$. This is consistent with the stronger strict-class-number criterion for totally ramified towers. [David S. Dummit, lemma in §3](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/nagoya-mathematical-journal/article/note-on-the-equivalence-of-the-parity-of-class-numbers-and-the-signature-ranks-of-units-in-cyclotomic-fields/DFAAD1914CA2C856EA78ECBE4E2E2BC9)

For example, fix a squarefree integer $a>1$ and use the monic family $X^{2^j}-a$. It is irreducible by Eisenstein at any prime dividing $a$. Its splitting field is obtained from $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{2^j})$ by adjoining one $2^j$th root of $a$, so its Galois group has power-of-two order. The polynomial discriminant has prime support in $2a$, which also bounds the ramification of the splitting field, by local unramified splitting at every prime of good reduction. Equation (1) gives $O(e)$ class two-rank for all $j$, and odd class number for $a=2$. This is a certified infinite family of increasing degrees. A tuple of such fields with total degree $O(W)$ and $\exp(O(W))$ marked input has unconditional one-third-scale kernel recovery. More generally (1) suffices for fields of degree $O(W/\log L)$ with at most $O(\log L)$ certified possible ramified primes.

These certificates require either the field structure in (1) or enough verified principal ideal relations in the actual supports. Neither is established for every input in the consecutive-degree Cogentic monic construction. The adaptive algorithm proves the stated output and rank-sensitive running time for the supplied marks. It does not furnish their smoothness supply or a dependency that provably splits the target modulus.

A finite binary check compared adaptive support-mask recovery with exhaustive kernel enumeration on 256 seeded rank-deficient and full-rank fixtures, checking independence and the $J2^r$ query bound. It exercised the search logic with exact binary class membership.
