# A randomized two-field extraction bound with coefficient 4.846

The [two-thirds parameter proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) supplies an independent pair of two-coordinate polynomials for each prime $\ell\in(B/2,B]$ with a full-rank certificate with probability $1-o(1)$. Sampling the two polynomials and collecting their smooth relations gives a uniform Las Vegas algorithm for complete factoring of each integer in expected bit time
$$
 L_N\!\left[\frac13,\left(\frac{32}{3}\right)^{2/3}\right],\qquad
 L_N[1/3,c]=\exp\!\left((c+o(1))(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\right).
$$
The coefficient is $4.8456549142\ldots$. The inputs are ordinary and Dirichlet RH, Cogentic's two-field order extraction, and the smooth-window and rank estimates in the parameter proof. The result is a new paper-level deduction from the deterministic companion. The separate randomized Cogentic paper gives coefficient 105 via a different algorithm. [Cogentic's deterministic Lemmas 7.4–7.5 and Proposition 10.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf) · [Cogentic's randomized Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf)

## Candidate generation and expected relation count

Use $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$ and $V=DH$. Fix rational parameters $x,v,b,a>0$ satisfying (3) in the [parameter proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md). Thus $d\sim xD$, $\log U\sim vV$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log A\sim aH$, $\log X_{\rm sc}\sim S V$ where $S=v+1/x$, and $u\sim (S/b)D$. Sample a uniform prime $B/2<\ell\le B$ by uniform integer sampling and polynomial-time primality recognition. Each attempt accepts with probability $\asymp1/\log B$. For each of the four integer coefficients sample a uniform interval of length $S_j$ inside $|u_j-\bar u_j|\le S_j$, with $S_0=UA,S_1=U$. These lengths are powers of two. Every point mass is bounded by an absolute constant times its mass under the source Gaussian; the same holds for the joint distribution. Thus the uniform sample retains its $1-o(1)$ rank-success probability. The zero-size and small-number branches can be covered by trial division. [Finite sampling argument](cogentic-finite-coins.md)

The local one-point formula with no additional row constraints bounds the probability of a smooth value at each mark by
$$
       \exp\!\left((-S/(3b)+o(1))H\right).
$$
Indeed $\log I_{\rm pure}=-u_Y\lambda_Y+(1+o(1))u_Y+O(\log L)$ with $u_Y\sim(S/b)D$, $\lambda_Y\sim z/3$. The nominal means at the root intervals change this by $o(H)$. Division by a forced generator prime, when applicable, costs at most $\ell^{1-\alpha_Y}e^{O(1)}=L^{O(1)}$, and coprimality factors are polynomially bounded. This bound is uniform in $\ell$ and the mark. The two fields are independent. Consequently the number $M$ of retained *joint* squarefree smooth columns satisfies
$$
             \mathbf E M\le\exp\!\left((a-2S/(3b)+o(1))H\right).          \tag{1}
$$
The full proof below charges the remaining work linearly in $M$, so a time limit for exceptionally large relation lists is unnecessary.

For a non-perfect-power composite with two different odd prime divisors, the negative-sign argument in the [single-exponent proof](cogentic-dense-exponents.md) applies. After initial trial division through $B$, at least $1/2-o(1)$ of the generator primes have different local two-parts and split the input from any verified $E_\ell$. Our target prime is sampled independently of the coefficient pairs. Thus a single trial has splitting probability at least $1/2-o(1)$, before the harmless modular rank proposal below. All returned divisors undergo an integer check.

## Bit cost of one extraction

Write $R=1+|F_{\rm clean}|\le 1+2dB=B L^{O(1)}$ and $P=64d^3 R L_0=B L^{O(1)}$ as upper bounds. $P$ is the bit precision used for the real unit columns. For each realized relation list of size $M$, there is a randomized version of TryPair with verified exponents, cost
$$
                  (A+B^4+M B^2)\exp(o(H))                         \tag{2}
$$
and probability $1-o(1)$ of reproducing the successful algebraic conclusion whenever the four hypotheses of source Lemma 7.5 hold. The only fresh randomness in this step is a prime used to propose a rational pivot basis. Quasi-linear bit multiplication and division, including division with remainder, are sufficient. [Schönhage and Strassen](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02242355)

1. **Collect columns.** A sieve through $B$ creates the factor base. The targeted polynomial construction of Lemma 8.1 costs $\exp(o(H))+O(B^2\mathrm{poly}(L))$: enumerate at most $2^d$ binary polynomial candidates, evaluate $r^d-r$ at $O(B)$ residues for the second polynomial's omitted value, form the split polynomial, and perform CRT, base expansion and coefficient rounding on polynomial-bit integers. For each admissible $p\le B$, evaluating $f_i(r),f'_i(r)$ at every $r<p$ costs $O(B^2\mathrm{poly}(L))$ in total. There are at most $d$ roots per polynomial per $p$. For each root list its marks in $[1,A]$ in that residue class. The total number of these records is $O(dB+dA\sum_{\sqrt B<p\le B}1/p)=(A+B)\mathrm{poly}(L)$. Sort by mark, multiply each prime once, and compare this product to the nonzero $|f_i(a)|$. Equality gives the squarefree smoothness check including repeated factors. Apply the source's derivative/shared-root and sparsity filters, and retain the sparse binary columns (at most $L_0$ ones apiece). Each $|f_i(a)|$ has polynomially many bits. Exact sorting and group accumulation cost $(A+B^2)\exp(o(H))$, including the sorted eligible grid. This replaces checking every possible prime separately at every mark.
2. **Propose a rational pivot basis.** Draw a uniform prime $P_*\in[B^2,2B^2]$, again by small-integer sampling and primality recognition. Determine the pivot rows and columns of the binary input modulo $P_*$ using ordinary incremental column elimination. Each candidate scans at most $R^2$ residues of $O(\log B)$ bits. This costs $MR^2\mathrm{poly}(L)$. Such scans also work on a multitape machine: store all prior pivot vectors in order, stream over their full length, and update the length-$R$ column vector at each pivot. The accepted lists have a nonzero leading modular minor at each rank increase. A nonzero full-rank rational minor of this fixed integer input has bit length $O(R\log L)$ by Hadamard and the column sparsity. It has at most $O(R\log L)$ distinct prime divisors, whereas the sampling interval has $\gg B^2/\log B$ primes. Except with probability $O(B^{-1}\mathrm{poly}(L))$, the computed modular rank equals the rational rank. Whenever this occurs, the selected pivot minor is a full-rank rational basis. For all outcomes recompute exact integer data and verify the final modular exponent.
3. **Exact adjugate and nullspace.** The pivot order supplies nonzero leading integer minors. The source bordered-adjugate formulas take $O(R)$ updates, $O(R^2)$ additions, multiplications or exact divisions per update, each on $O(R\mathrm{poly}(L))$ bits. This costs $B^{4+o(1)}$. The matrix products with a pivot's new row or column also fit this bound with sequential scans and sorting if needed. With final determinant $\Delta$ and adjugate $C$, the vectors $n^{(j)}$ have pivot part $-C v_j[I]$ and a single free entry $\Delta$ at $j$. The dense column-vector calculation is a sum of at most $L_0$ columns of $C$. Across all $j$ this takes $M B^{2+o(1)}$ bit operations. For an explicit tape organization sort the small requests $(i,j)$ for nonzero entries by $i$, stream the corresponding column $C_i$ to each requesting record, then sort the resulting blocks by $j$ and sum them. Each emitted block has $O(R^2\mathrm{poly}(L))$ bits. Tape sorting adds a factor polynomial in $\log(2+M R P)$, at most polynomial in $L$. The vector $c^{(0)}$ and its exact inhomogeneous check are obtained by the same formulas. A deficient modular proposal can give an unsuccessful trial; it cannot create an unverified factor.
4. **Real and unit coordinates.** The $O(d)$ root brackets and their $O(P)$ exact bisection steps require at most $B^{2+o(1)}$ operations using fast multiplication. The $O(dM)$ needed logarithms of positive polynomial-bit or $O(P\mathrm{poly}(L))$-bit rationals cost $P^{2+o(1)}\mathrm{poly}(L)$ apiece to compute to $P+O(P)$ fractional bits. One elementary implementation scales the argument by a power of two into $[1,2]$ and uses $\log r=2\sum_{k\ge0} ((r-1)/(r+1))^{2k+1}/(2k+1)$, whose ratio argument has absolute value at most $1/3$. The same series gives $\log2$. $O(P+\log L)$ terms and $O(P+\log L)$ working bits with logarithmic guard digits suffice under the source's separation check. Its square roots are obtainable by integer square roots at the same or lower cost. Apply the source's precision and sign checks. The $O(MdR)$ products of a real column entry with an integer nullspace coefficient use $O(P\mathrm{poly}(L))$-bit factors and therefore cost $M B^{2+o(1)}$. Pivot archimedean values reused across many columns can be copied sequentially per retained column at this same cost. The final greedy Gram selection and its bordered updates have dimension $2d-2=\mathrm{poly}(L)$, and use at most $M B^{2+o(1)}+B^{3+o(1)}$ operations, including the final continued-fraction tests. For the latter, classical Euclidean divisions suffice at the source's $O(d^4 P^3)$ bound. The explicitly computed final integer is accepted only after $\ell^{E_\ell}\equiv1\pmod N$ is checked. On a full modular proposal all exact spanning conditions coincide with the deterministic extractor's, and its correctness proof applies. [Cogentic, Lemma 7.5, steps 1–5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=35)

Indices in the preceding counts take $O(L)$ bits and fast integer operations have subexponential factors $\exp(o(H))$ at the indicated lengths. The tape implementation of large vector lookups uses streamed joins or repeated scans as described. The independent modular prime can be resampled in each trial.

## The coefficient and a single algorithm

Combining (1) and (2), the expected time of one trial has exponent coefficient at most
$$
                  \max\left\{a,4b,a+2b-\frac{2S}{3b}\right\}.
$$
As $v\downarrow v_*=(32/3)^{1/3}$, take strict schedules tending to $x=1/v_*$, $S=2v_*$, $b=\sqrt{S/3}$ and $a=4b=v_*^2$. All three entries tend to $v_*^2$. The trial has success probability bounded below by an absolute positive constant independently of $N$ for all sufficiently large inputs. For independent retrials the expected total cost is the expected single-trial cost divided by the success probability; within a trial its cost may correlate with success. Dovetailing finite trial division guarantees termination even on exceptional tapes and handles finite thresholds. Full recursion has at most $2\log_2 N$ nodes with arguments at most $N$, and polynomial-time primality recognition terminates the leaves.

### One algorithm for all strict schedules

Encode the rational schedules as finite binary programs for one fixed trial driver. At stage $j=1,2,\ldots$, run $j^2$ independent attempts sequentially. In one attempt, draw a self-delimiting schedule description from fair bits, abort decoding after $j$ bits, and simulate that schedule's complete factoring routine from the beginning for at most $2^j$ bit steps. Reject invalid descriptions and check each accepted complete factorization. Every fixed valid description has probability $\pi>0$ once $j$ exceeds its length. The stage controller can run the same driver on a fixed number of work tapes with a binary step counter. Clearing the tapes between simulations costs at most their previous length. Parsing, initialization and output verification can all be charged inside the attempt cap. In each stage also advance a deterministic trial-division factorer by $2^j$ steps to ensure termination on every tape.

For one fixed schedule let $T$ be its expected time including verification at the given input. From $j_0=\max(1,\lceil\log_2(2T)\rceil,\text{description length})$ onward, a single attempt finishes with probability at least $\pi/2$ by Markov's inequality. The probability of an entire failed stage is at most $\exp(-\pi j^2/2)$ conditional on reaching it. Each stage costs at most $2^j\operatorname{poly}(j)$ on a multitape machine. Summing through $j_0$ costs $O(2^{j_0}\operatorname{poly}(j_0))$; summing later stages with the preceding failure probabilities gives the same upper bound, up to a constant depending on the fixed schedule. Thus the single driver has the expected-time leading coefficient of the infimum of the strict schedule bounds. This argument concerns running time. A uniform workspace bound for the composite driver requires a separate workspace cap.
