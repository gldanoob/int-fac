# Small generators of full residue unit groups at almost all primes

Fix a number field K of degree k and an integral basis for O_K. Fix B>1 and put Y=(log X)^B. Outside at most X^(2k/B+o(1)) primes t∈[X,2X], the unit reductions of the algebraic integers in the coefficient box of radius C_K Y^(1/k) generate the full group (O_K/tO_K)^×. In particular the exceptional primes have density zero for B>2k. The group can have any splitting type. The conclusion also implies generation modulo scalar units.

## Smooth products in a box

Identify O_K with Z^k in the fixed basis. For each rational prime ell≤Y having a degree-one prime ideal above it, choose one such ideal p_ell. Its lattice has index ell in Z^k. Minkowski's theorem on the coordinate cube of radius 2 ell^(1/k) gives a nonzero a_ell∈p_ell with coefficient norm at most 2 ell^(1/k). The field norm is a fixed integer homogeneous polynomial of degree k in the coordinates. It follows that |Nm(a_ell)|=ell m_ell with 1≤m_ell≤C_K. Discard primes ell≤C_K. The prime ideal theorem gives ≫_K Y/log Y remaining primes, by keeping only degree-one prime ideals and allowing at most k per rational prime. Alternatively use the completely split primes, whose density is positive by fixed-field Chebotarev.

Let C'_K bound multiplication in coefficient max norm, after including the generator coefficient constant. Products of r distinct a_ell have coefficient norm at most (C'_K Y^(1/k))^r. Choose

    r=floor(2 log X / log(C'_K Y^(1/k))).

All products then lie in the coordinate cube of radius X². The prime factors of their absolute norms above C_K are exactly the selected ell, each to the first power. Thus distinct index subsets give distinct products. Write S for the product set and P for the number of markers. Since B>1, P/r→∞ and

    log |S| = log binomial(P,r)
            = r(log(P/r)+O(1))
            = (2k(1−1/B)+o(1)) log X.

All elements of S reduce to units at t∈[X,2X] for large X. Their norms have rational prime factors at most max(Y,C_K), since the norm is the determinant of multiplication and nonvanishing of its reduction is equivalent to invertibility.

## The additive large sieve

Suppose the units from the coefficient box fail to generate the whole residue unit group at t. Pick a nonprincipal multiplicative character χ of that group trivial on the generated subgroup. Extend χ by zero to the nonunits. On the additive group (Z/t)^k, its normalized Fourier coefficients satisfy χ̂(0)=0 and Σ_v|χ̂(v)|²≤1. The mean follows from the usual character sum over the full unit group, including if χ is trivial on some CRT factors. Parseval gives the norm bound directly. Since χ=1 on the image of S,

    |S|² ≤ Σ_(v∈(Z/t)^k, v≠0) |Σ_(a∈S) e(a·v/t)|².

There is an alternative that uses no multiplicative characters. If r_t(x) counts points of S in a residue class x and H_t is the subgroup generated by the box units, the above Fourier energy equals t^k Σ_x r_t(x)²−|S|². By Cauchy and |H_t|≤t^k/2, this is at least |S|².

Over all bad t and nonzero v, the directions v/t are distinct and are at least δ=1/(4X²) apart in the torus max norm. For different primes, a nonzero coordinate difference is a nonzero fraction modulo one of denominator at most the product of the primes. Equality in every coordinate would give zero vectors by coprimality. For one prime, distinct directions are at least 1/t apart.

The usual k-dimensional additive large sieve bounds the sum of Fourier energies at all these directions by O_k(X^(2k))|S|. A short proof uses the dual formulation and majorizes the indicator of the coordinate cube by a constant times the Gaussian e^(−π||x||²/L²), L=4X². Poisson summation turns its Gram kernel at difference z into L^k Σ_(m∈Z^k) e^(−πL²||z+m||²). All frequency lifts are δ-separated, and packing shows that the sum of the Gaussian in any row is O_k(1) after dividing by L^k. The dual estimate follows by the row-sum bound. Dividing by |S|² gives the claimed number of bad primes.

For a fixed squarefree modulus in F_t[X], the analogous product of fields is the relevant model at unramified t. The proof at a fixed number field depends only on the additive rank and the finite unit group. Thus it does not require squarefreeness, a cyclic local group, or a primitive multiplicative character. Ramified primes can also be left in the statement.

## Joint generation at two balanced primes

The same coefficient box generates (O_K/(pq)O_K)^× for almost all pairs of distinct primes p,q∈[X,2X], if B>2k. Restrict first to pairs where the reductions at p and at q each generate their own groups. If the image in the product is still proper, a killing character χ_pχ_q exists with both local components nonprincipal, by individual generation. Its additive expansion has support only on frequency vectors nonzero at both CRT factors, by local zero means. In particular the least common denominator of the vector equals pq, so directions for distinct unordered pairs are disjoint and are separated by at least 1/(16X^4).

Use the same markers and products with r chosen to fit a coordinate cube of radius X^4. The count becomes |S|=X^(4k(1−1/B)+o(1)). The additive sieve at spacing 1/(16X^4) gives at most X^(4k/B+o(1)) extra bad unordered pairs. Pairs with at least one individually bad prime number at most X^(1+2k/B+o(1)). Both are o(X²/(log X)²) when B>2k. This proof also indicates why separate generation does not on its own imply generation of the product.

## Prior work

Pappalardi's 1995 result for prime fields applies an additive large sieve to smooth integers whose images lie in the subgroup generated by small rational primes. This supplies the direct rational-field precedent for the method. Huxley developed the multidimensional additive large sieve for number fields. Hinz studies least primitive roots modulo individual prime ideals, and the finite-algebra work of Huang and Liu concerns generation in a given finite algebra by low-degree polynomials. A focused search did not identify the exact almost-all theorem for the full unit group at a rational prime in a fixed number field. [Pappalardi, Lemma 2 and proof](https://papparnta.github.io/pappa/papers/BULLCMS_38_1995.pdf#page=2) · [Huxley](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300002540) · [Hinz](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300010378) · [Finite algebra generating sets](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsc.2017.07.008)

## Exact fixture

Run `gp -fq experiments/smooth-norm-markers.gp`. From the repository root, the fixture takes eight bounded norm markers in each of three fixed fields using LLL on the exact integral-basis matrix of the chosen prime ideal. It verifies 56 distinct triple products per field and computes the exact index of the generated subgroup using idealstar and ideallog followed by HNF. It finds full groups in all possible splitting types for the quadratic, cubic and quartic fields, including split groups with up to four cyclic factors. In the quadratic field of class number two, some markers have norm 2 or 3 times the designated rational prime. Over 102 field/prime pairs, 101 generate and one quartic pair does not. The test gets an additional proper subgroup check at every pair by squaring all markers before computing the HNF index. These finite checks cover the algebraic construction and the subgroup test.
