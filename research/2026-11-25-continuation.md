# Deterministic factoring research, November 25 continuation

The starting point was the [08:00 handoff](2026-11-25-0800-handoff.md) and its [later morning results](2026-11-25-handoff/README.md). For unrestricted $b$-bit inputs the leaderboard's deterministic bound is $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log b})}$. The following results address specific approaches to that bound. [Benchmark and verification](https://bigospeedrun.com/integer-factoring)

| Question | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| How many arbitrary coefficient probes does a universal cyclotomic search need? | For each fixed odd prime index, $\Theta(\log X)$ probes suffice and are necessary for balanced primes in $(X,2X]$. The upper bound uses advice. The lower bound allows adaptive choices from failed probe responses and gives an unconditional growing-height obstruction for the exact lcm of two orders. [Proof and modular replay](cyclotomic-probe-libraries.md) |
| Which cubic fields does the height-six quartic force to split? | All 16 fields with resolvents $-3,-19,57$ split at eligible prime values. Direct factorizations at three certified prime values agree across all 369 fields. The other 54 active fields still enter the collision search. [Artin proof and reproduction](2026-11-25-handoff/height-six/quartic/README.md#splitting-at-prime-values) |
| How many balanced inputs satisfy the near-maximal cyclotomic gcd promise? | For fixed $k\ge3$, each dyadic interval contains $O_k(B^2)$ integer pairs of deficit at most $B$. For $\varphi(k)>2$ and bounded $B$, only finitely many such pairs occur over all scales. Degree two admits integer Pell families. [Proof, prior art and exact counts](cyclotomic-overlap-count.md) |
| What happens at moderate overlap? | For fixed $0<\theta<1/2$, $X^{2-\theta+o(1)}$ balanced prime pairs share a divisor of their cyclotomic values above $X^\theta$. The proof permits separated prime intervals. [Density and exact gcd checks](moderate-cyclotomic-overlap.md) |
| Can one designated binary digit of a three-square count carry the reduction? | For every fixed rational $0<\alpha<1/2$, the bit at position $\lfloor\alpha\log_2 m\rfloor$ suffices for complete deterministic factoring with linear-bit indices and unconditional $2^{O(b/\log b)}$ overhead. A second proof gives polynomial overhead using Dirichlet GRH. Producing the digit remains open. [Proof and counting replay](ramified-three-squares.md) |
| How many original-modulus magnitude comparisons suffice? | With $R$ distinct prime factors, $2^{O(R)}\operatorname{poly}(b)$ deterministic work and queries suffice, even with adversarial answers in the specified gap. For arbitrary inputs the overhead is $2^{O(\sqrt b)}$. [Fourier estimates and numerical checks](sparse-magnitude-threshold.md) |
| How much Fourier information survives the fixed-quarter parity lift? | At least one quarter of the squared Fourier mass is off the diagonal. This gives a Las Vegas factoring reduction with $\exp(O(R^2))\operatorname{poly}(b)$ expected overhead using the quarter-parity oracle. [Moment proof and exact checks](fixed-quarter-support.md) |
| Is the middle digit itself sufficient, and where can it be computed? | A middle-bit oracle recovers exact counts by locating thresholds. An independent polynomial-time evaluator from the index alone obtains the middle digit, together with almost $\log_2\log m$ lower digits, on a density-one set of ordinary indices. At cutoff 80,000 it returned 978 of 1,030 checked middle digits and abstained on 52. The exceptional set can include adaptively chosen factoring queries. [Reduction](middle-three-square-bit.md) · [Direct evaluator, density proof and exact counts](typical-three-square-digits.md) |
| How many balanced products can fixed public polynomial exponents factor? | For $t$ primes in $(X,2X]$, the sharp population exponent for degree-bounded full-order lists is $X^{1+o(1)}$. Maynard's prime-cluster theorem gives a matching family. Squared normalized partial divisibilities can also add up to a polynomial-time lattice search. [Full orders](polynomial-order-supply.md) · [Partial orders](partial-order-supply.md) |
| Can short arithmetic formulas predict the quarter bit? | A successful rational-interval and Jacobi predictor gives a deterministic divisor search using just its coefficients. For slopes $\lfloor\sqrt{jn}\rfloor$ on balanced pairs a short continued-fraction scan either factors the input or bounds the advantage. The five tested multipliers split 3,163 of 9,045 pairs. [Rational formulas](rational-quarter-predictors.md) · [Square-root slopes](square-root-quarter-schedules.md) |
| How long must the middle-bit queries be? | A deterministic polynomial-time reduction factors every input using the exact middle bit on $O(b^2/\log b)$-bit indices, unconditionally. Grouping primitive parts of composite cyclotomic orders gives linear-bit indices for blocks $p^e\ge n^\delta$ at fixed $\delta>0$. A logarithmic twist family makes the digit decoder work, and many coprime cyclotomic divisors enter a single univariate lattice. A 65-bit example verifies 79 rows and a separate four-row lattice recovers 10,007. [Proof and lattice replay](ramified-power-lattice.md) |
| Can deterministic selections among rational CM curves usually supply smooth orders? | Across all thirteen rational CM invariants and all their twists, the prescribed scalar and point families miss stage-one identity splits on a density-one set of balanced prime pairs. Finite checks included 2,030 ordinary and 1,724 supersingular reductions. [Scope, prior art and replay](rational-cm-obstruction.md) |
| Can an exact two-cut crossing seed beat the benchmark exponent? | The November 24 Euclidean tensor-price theorem implies that every exact decomposition satisfies $\sum_i\sqrt{r_ic_i}>d^{\sqrt2}$ over every field and in every finite dimension $d\ge2$. The same argument excludes the proposed improvement from any subexponential-in-label-bits number of exact pieces. Local checks covered 128 terminal distances and 96 tensor-power groups in three characteristics. [Proof and prior art](euclidean-crossing-barrier.md) · [Independent elementary bounds](nested-crossing-flags.md) |
| Where does a known input-only algorithm already give polynomial time? | Taking $B=b$ in Źrałek's deterministic algorithm gives complete factoring in $O(b^7)$ on at least $2^{b-o(b)}$ squarefree inputs at every large bit length. All factors can grow faster than every fixed power of $b$. A second estimate gives a positive limiting fraction of semiprimes in each prescribed unbalanced factor band. [Population theorem and arithmetic replay](smooth-predecessor-products.md) |
| Can count digits be evaluated on twists of each individual input? | An unconditional index-only calculation gives the middle digit and $O(\log b)$ additional digits on all but an inverse-polynomial fraction of the specified prime twists and norm multipliers, for each fixed input. The exceptional comparisons can still contain those needed by the exact decoder. [Large-sieve proof and reference counts](twisted-three-square-digits.md) |
| Can the height-six cubic dependency calculation be automated? | Cubic residue rows and exact cube witnesses recover the complete character matrices. At height 20 there are 380 relations among 13,845 fields. Reoptimizing across every height-six quadratic profile confirms the earlier degree-16 base optimum on the $p\equiv1\bmod3$ branch. [Certificates and limiting probabilities](cubic-radical-certificates.md) |
| How fast must the active quadratic rank grow with cubic height? | For $p\equiv1\bmod3$, pairing prime markers whose difference is twice a cube gives rank $\gg H^{3/2}/\log H$. Direct checks covered 726 disjoint pairs. [Proof using prime polynomial progressions](cubic-resolvent-profiles.md#bounds-as-the-coefficient-height-grows) |
| What is the mean cost of complete factoring from the integer alone? | A deterministic algorithm always finishes and has mean bit cost $\exp(O(\sqrt{b\log b}))$ over all $b$-bit integers, including fallback and recursion. Ordinary RH, covered by Alpöge's November 20 theorem, suffices. Repeating prime factors in a Hasse-interval estimate makes the number of exceptional primes small enough to pay for finding them. Binary small-bias shifts control the other searches. [Mean-time proof and finite replay](mean-time-elliptic-factoring.md) · [Pointwise rational marks](pointwise-conic-marks.md) · [Large-sieve marks on average](average-conic-signs.md) |
| How much can a genus-two search guarantee at a fixed least prime? | For each fixed $p$, the mean over prime partners $q\in(Z,2Z]$ is $\mathrm{poly}(b)\min\{\sqrt p,(1+p^4/Z)\exp(O(\sqrt{\log p\log\log p}))\}$. A quintic norm family forces opposite order parity and has four free parameters. The proof uses ordinary RH and a fixed Hecke zero-free strip. [Proof, prior art and Cantor replay](quintic-field-genus-two.md) |
| What can marked elliptic curves achieve using classical zero-free estimates? | For each fixed $\alpha>2/3$ below one, a deterministic algorithm factors a density-one set of balanced semiprimes in $\exp(O_\alpha(b^\alpha))$ bit operations. The original one-prime argument also gives a quantitative density-one cofactor recursion. [Curve construction](legendre-average-factoring.md) · [Repeated-prime improvement](many-prime-hasse-intervals.md) · [Full recursion](almost-all-complete-factoring.md) |
| Which reciprocal polynomial congruences expose an unbalanced factor? | For $N=xy$, $x^r\mid F(y)$, $y^s\mid G(x)$ and the stated unit conditions, $4rs\ge\deg F\deg G$ suffices for polynomial-time splitting. An actual lattice recovers 2,207 and 113,327 from 250,112,689. [Unbalanced lattice, population and tests](reciprocal-cofactor-congruences.md) |
| Can a crossing use the particular target product? | Odd operands satisfying a fixed product modulo $2^w$ admit a smaller local crossing, with a cost depending explicitly on $w$. Grouping both low halves together has a separate rank obstruction, verified on 63,281 tensor entries. [Modular product construction and rank checks](modular-product-crossings.md) |
| When is exact evaluation of a three-square quotient polynomial on average? | At fixed small fundamental discriminant, a trial-and-certify algorithm returns exact digits, with an explicit deterministic fallback. The mean bound holds also over every single fixed conductor's full norm-pad family. [Proof and 5,120 ordinary evaluations](fixed-discriminant-middle-digits.md) |
