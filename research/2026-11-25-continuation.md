# Deterministic factoring research, November 25 continuation

The starting point was the [08:00 handoff](2026-11-25-0800-handoff.md) and its [later morning results](2026-11-25-handoff/README.md). For unrestricted $b$-bit inputs the leaderboard's deterministic bound is $2^{O(b/\sqrt{\log b})}$. The following results address specific approaches to that bound. [Benchmark and verification](https://bigospeedrun.com/integer-factoring)

| Question | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| How many arbitrary coefficient probes does a universal cyclotomic search need? | For each fixed odd prime index, $\Theta(\log X)$ probes suffice and are necessary for balanced primes in $(X,2X]$. The upper bound uses advice. The lower bound allows adaptive choices from failed probe responses and gives an unconditional growing-height obstruction for the exact lcm of two orders. [Proof and modular replay](cyclotomic-probe-libraries.md) |
| Which cubic fields does the height-six quartic force to split? | All 16 fields with resolvents $-3,-19,57$ split at eligible prime values. Direct factorizations at three certified prime values agree across all 369 fields. The other 54 active fields still enter the collision search. [Artin proof and reproduction](2026-11-25-handoff/height-six/quartic/README.md#splitting-at-prime-values) |
| How many balanced inputs satisfy the near-maximal cyclotomic gcd promise? | For fixed $k\ge3$, each dyadic interval contains $O_k(B^2)$ integer pairs of deficit at most $B$. For $\varphi(k)>2$ and bounded $B$, only finitely many such pairs occur over all scales. Degree two admits integer Pell families. [Proof, prior art and exact counts](cyclotomic-overlap-count.md) |
| What happens at moderate overlap? | For fixed $0<\theta<1/2$, $X^{2-\theta+o(1)}$ balanced prime pairs share a divisor of their cyclotomic values above $X^\theta$. The proof permits separated prime intervals. [Density and exact gcd checks](moderate-cyclotomic-overlap.md) |
| Can one designated binary digit of a three-square count carry the reduction? | For every fixed rational $0<\alpha<1/2$, the bit at position $\lfloor\alpha\log_2 m\rfloor$ suffices for complete deterministic factoring with linear-bit indices and unconditional $2^{O(b/\log b)}$ overhead. A second proof gives polynomial overhead using Dirichlet GRH. Producing the digit remains open. [Proof and counting replay](ramified-three-squares.md) |
| How many original-modulus magnitude comparisons suffice? | With $R$ distinct prime factors, $2^{O(R)}\operatorname{poly}(b)$ deterministic work and queries suffice, even with adversarial answers in the specified gap. For arbitrary inputs the overhead is $2^{O(\sqrt b)}$. [Fourier estimates and numerical checks](sparse-magnitude-threshold.md) |
| How much Fourier information survives the fixed-quarter parity lift? | At least one quarter of the squared Fourier mass is off the diagonal. This gives a Las Vegas factoring reduction with $\exp(O(R^2))\operatorname{poly}(b)$ expected overhead using the quarter-parity oracle. [Moment proof and exact checks](fixed-quarter-support.md) |
| Is the middle digit itself sufficient, and where can it be computed? | A middle-bit oracle recovers exact counts by locating thresholds. An independent polynomial-time evaluator from the index alone obtains the middle digit, together with almost $\log_2\log m$ lower digits, on a density-one set of ordinary indices. At cutoff 80,000 it returned 978 of 1,030 checked middle digits and abstained on 52. The exceptional set can include adaptively chosen factoring queries. [Reduction](middle-three-square-bit.md) · [Direct evaluator, density proof and exact counts](typical-three-square-digits.md) |
| How many balanced products can fixed public polynomial exponents factor? | For $t$ primes in $(X,2X]$, the sharp population exponent for degree-bounded full-order lists is $X^{1+o(1)}$. Maynard's prime-cluster theorem gives a matching family. Squared normalized partial divisibilities can also add up to a polynomial-time lattice search. [Full orders](polynomial-order-supply.md) · [Partial orders](partial-order-supply.md) |
| Can short arithmetic formulas predict the quarter bit? | A successful rational-interval and Jacobi predictor gives a deterministic divisor search using just its coefficients. For slopes $\lfloor\sqrt{jn}\rfloor$ on balanced pairs a short continued-fraction scan either factors the input or bounds the advantage. The five tested multipliers split 3,163 of 9,045 pairs. [Rational formulas](rational-quarter-predictors.md) · [Square-root slopes](square-root-quarter-schedules.md) |
| What happens if middle-bit queries may have $O(b^2)$ bits? | A deterministic polynomial-time reduction factors every input using the exact middle bit at those indices, unconditionally. A logarithmic twist family makes the digit decoder work, and many coprime cyclotomic divisors enter a single univariate lattice. A 65-bit example verifies 79 rows and a separate four-row lattice recovers 10,007. [Proof and lattice replay](ramified-power-lattice.md) |
| Can deterministic selections among rational CM curves usually supply smooth orders? | Across all thirteen rational CM invariants and all their twists, the prescribed scalar and point families miss stage-one identity splits on a density-one set of balanced prime pairs. Finite checks included 2,030 ordinary and 1,724 supersingular reductions. [Scope, prior art and replay](rational-cm-obstruction.md) |
