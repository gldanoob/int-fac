# Determinants and sparse local relations from small prime products

A polynomial-size menu of small prime generators contains a short exponent word giving a proper gcd for every sufficiently large composite integer, unless the menu already finds a small prime divisor. The word uses coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and only $O(\log N/\log\log N)$ nonzero positions. At a one-third-size prime cutoff the mixed order lattice also has determinant almost $N$ on every balanced semiprime, and its local splitting words span a large rational subspace. These are supply statements. The product sets used in the proof are too large for the desired one-third search. ## A finite index and rank bound

Let $N$ be composite, and take $m$ distinct rational primes $a_1,\ldots,a_m\le B$ that are units modulo $N$. For any $D\mid N$ put
\[
\Gamma_D=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_m\bmod D\rangle,\qquad
\Lambda_D=\{v\in\mathbb Z^m:\prod_j a_j^{v_j}\equiv1\pmod D\},\qquad
h_D=|\Gamma_D|=\det\Lambda_D.
\]
The determinant is the index in $\mathbb Z^m$. For $1\le k\le m$ and $B^k<D$, all $\binom mk$ different $k$-subset products are distinct positive integers below $D$ by unique factorization. Hence $h_D\ge\binom mk$. This argument needs no assumption on local multiplicative orders.

For the local relations use instead $B^k<N$. The $M=\binom mk$ products remain distinct modulo $N$. Some fiber modulo a proper divisor $D>1$ has at least $F=\lceil M/h_D\rceil$ products. Fix one product $u_0$ in that fiber. Each other product $u$ gives
\[
1<D\mid\gcd(u-u_0,N)<N.
\]
The upper inequality holds because the distinct integers lie strictly between zero and $N$. The exponent vector of $u/u_0$ belongs to $\Lambda_D\setminus\Lambda_N$, has entries in $\{-1,0,1\}$, and has at most $2k$ nonzero coordinates. The ratios from the fiber remain distinct modulo $N$. Consequently the subgroup generated by these short local words has at least $F$ elements even before including other fibers.

There is also a rank bound. Let $d$ be the rational dimension of the differences of the indicator vectors of the products in a largest fiber. Choose $d$ coordinate positions on which projection is injective on that difference space, using independent columns of a basis matrix. It is injective on the fiber's affine span as well. Each projected indicator vector lies in $\{0,1\}^d$ and has weight at most $k$. Thus
\[
F\le\sum_{j=0}^{\min(k,d)}\binom dj.
\]
If $F>2^k$, then $d>k$. For $d\ge k$, evaluating $(1+x)^d$ at $x=k/d\le1$ proves $\sum_{j\le k}\binom dj\le(ed/k)^k$. This supplies a lower bound $d\ge(k/e)F^{1/k}$ whenever $F>2^k$. The reference differences are themselves splitting certificates, so this is a rank lower bound for short proper-gcd directions.

## Uniform witnesses from a polynomial-size menu

Write $L=\log N$ and take $B=\lfloor C L^2\rfloor$ for any fixed $C>e^{-2}$. Trial division through $B$ either gives a proper factor or ensures that all the primes through $B$ are units. In the second case put $m=\pi(B)$ and choose the maximal $k$ with $B^k<N$. Then $k=L/\log B+O(1)$, $m\sim B/\log B$, and $k/m\to0$. The prime number theorem and Stirling's formula give
\[
\log\binom mk
=k\bigl(\log(m/k)+1+o(1)\bigr)
=\frac{L}{2}+k\bigl(1+\tfrac12\log C+o(1)\bigr).
\]
Indeed $\log(m/k)=\log L+\log C+o(1)$, and the error $O(\log B)$ from flooring $k$ is $o(k)$. In particular $M>\sqrt N$ for all sufficiently large $N$. Apply the finite bound with the least prime factor $D\le\sqrt N$. It forces a proper gcd from one of the short ratios. In fact the largest fiber contains at least $\exp(\Omega_C(L/\log L))$ products. The shorter menu has $O(L^2/\log L)$ generators, and the coefficient support is at most $2k=O(L/\log L)$.

The menu and the support bound are computable from $N$. For an integer-only cutoff one may use the square of the bit length and find $k$ by integer powering. The short words can also be expressed as pooled unit corrections. Use the consecutive charts up to $\max(5,B)$, whose rational correction subgroup contains every prime in the menu. Check all chart eligibility integers by gcd, which are below $N$ for sufficiently large $N$. A failed unit check already gives a factor. The rational identities for consecutive charts express each menu prime in $O(B)$ correction factors. Consequently a sparse witness from the fiber has a pooled chart description with $O(kB)$ correction factors, polynomial in $L$. Its certified local equality is enough for the stated proper gcd. [Consecutive chart identities](cogentic-consecutive-unit-charts.md#specialization-to-a-small-integer-window) · [Local collision certificates](cogentic-unit-collision-search.md)

Already at this polynomial cutoff, binary coordinate injection gives $F\le 2^d$. Thus the short local words in one fiber span at least $(1+\tfrac12\log C+o(1))k/\log 2$ rational dimensions.

## One-third cutoffs and the mixed lattice

Take $N=pq$ for distinct primes with ratio bounded by a fixed constant. Put
\[
W=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3},\qquad V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3},\qquad B=\lfloor e^{cW}\rfloor
\]
with fixed $c>0$, and use all primes through $B$. For each $D\in\{p,q,N\}$ choose the maximal $k_D$ with $B^{k_D}<D$. As above, unique factorization and Stirling give
\[
\log h_D\ \ge\ \log D-\frac{\log D}{\log B}\log\log D
-O\!\left(\log B+\frac{\log D}{\log B}\right).
\]
Here $k_D\log B=\log D+O(\log B)$, and $\log(m/k_D)=\log B-\log\log D+O(1)$. Thus the global log determinant is at least $L-(1/c+o(1))V$. Both local log determinants are at least $L/2-(1/(2c)+o(1))V$.

The mixed order lattice is $\Lambda_N=\Lambda_p\cap\Lambda_q$. Its coupling index satisfies
\[
[\mathbb Z^m:\Lambda_p+\Lambda_q]
=\frac{h_p h_q}{h_N}
\le\exp((1/c+o(1))V).
\]
The identity follows from $\Lambda_p/\Lambda_N\simeq(\Lambda_p+\Lambda_q)/\Lambda_q$. It gives an upper bound for the common cyclic quotient of the two local images. This index also divides $\gcd(h_p,h_q)$. The one-sided image $\Lambda_p/\Lambda_N$ has size $h_N/h_p\ge\exp(L/2-(1/c+o(1))V)$, with the symmetric statement at $q$.

With $k=k_N$ the fiber bound gives substantially many short local directions. Since $\log(M/h_p)=L/2-O(V)\gg k$, the rank inequality applies. Stirling gives $M^{1/k}=(e+o(1))m/k$. Balance and $\log^2 B=o(L)$ give $h_p^{1/k}\le(1+o(1))\sqrt B$. Thus a single fiber yields splitting words whose rational span has dimension at least
\[
(1-o(1))\frac{m}{\sqrt B}=(1-o(1))\frac{\sqrt B}{\log B}.
\]
Every basis word can be chosen as an actual difference to the reference product, with the original support bound $2k$. Generating the prime menu itself has one-third cost, while enumerating the product set here has size $\exp(L-O(V))$.

For completeness the same indices transfer exactly through a shared public scalar. Write $h_D(E)$ for the determinant using bases $a_j^E$. At either prime $r$, the cyclic image has size $h_r(E)=h_r/\gcd(h_r,E)$. If $E$ is a positive multiple of $N-1$, then $h_p(E)$ and $h_q(E)$ are coprime. Indeed the minimum valuation of any prime in $p-1$ and $q-1$ is at most its valuation in $pq-1$, so powering kills that prime's contribution on at least one side. A subgroup of two coprime-order groups that surjects onto both is the whole product. Therefore
\[
h_N(E)=\frac{h_p h_q}{\gcd(h_p,E)\gcd(h_q,E)}
\qquad\text{when }N-1\mid E.
\]
This identifies how much the mixed determinant changes under the scalar, even if the local orders remain unknown. [Shifted-power scalars](cogentic-pooled-shifted-powers.md)

## Finite check and algorithmic limit

At $N=899=29\cdot31$, the nine primes through 23 give 36 distinct pair products below $N$. The products 6, 35, and 209 are one fiber modulo 29 and give two independent exponent differences. Both difference gcds against 6 are 29. The exact local and global determinants are 28, 30, and 840.

An exhaustive arithmetic check on 872 small prime-pair, cutoff, and product-size combinations verified the group indices, fiber counts, proper gcds, and powered index formula. Exact rational rank checks also confirmed the coordinate inequality on 3,501 finite fibers. At the polynomial prime cutoff the witness proof uses $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ products. Producing a proper local word in one-third time without enumerating such a product set remains open here.
