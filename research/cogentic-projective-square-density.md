# How many quadratic bases can give two independent square comparisons?

For a common quadratic chart, two independent modular signs from reduced linear evaluations constrain the base to a family of Pell equations. For Gaussian moduli up to \(X\) with an integral base, allowing coefficients of size \(\exp(O((\log X)^{1/3}(\log\log X)^{2/3}))\) leaves at most that many possible bases. Rational bases admit a larger family. These bounds apply even when the chart and both seeds are chosen after seeing the input. ## Counting rational bases

Let \(r=a/b\) in lowest terms, with \(|r|\le R\), \(1\le b\le B\), and \(R,B\ge1\). In a quadratic number field with generator \(\alpha\), suppose two field squares have reduced representatives \(\gamma_i^2=u_i+v_i\alpha\), with integral coefficients of absolute value at most \(U\ge2\). Suppose the chart maps \(\alpha\) to \(r\) modulo the input and each evaluation \(u_i+v_ir=x_i^2\) is a nonzero rational square. All denominators and comparison roots used for the normalized modular signs must be units at the input. We count bases where the two signs are independent modulo the global signs. Across all such choices their number is at most
\[
 O\!\left(\sqrt B\,U^4\,\max_{1\le n\le 2BU^3}\tau(n)\;\log(2RBU)\right).
\]
Here \(\tau\) is the divisor function. Clearing rational coefficients by multiplying a field root by the common denominator of its reduced coefficients preserves a valid normalized comparison if that denominator is a modular unit. This gives the same result with the resulting integral coefficient height.

If \(v_i=0\), then \(\gamma_i^2=x_i^2\) inside the field, so its normalized sign is global. If \(\Delta=v_2u_1-v_1u_2=0\) with both slopes nonzero, then \(\gamma_2^2=(v_2/v_1)\gamma_1^2\). At the selected base the ratio is the rational square \((x_2/x_1)^2\). Consequently \(\gamma_2=\pm(x_2/x_1)\gamma_1\) and the two signs agree up to global sign. Independence therefore requires \(v_1v_2\Delta\ne0\).

The square of the reduced denominator of \(x_i\) divides \(b\), by the equation \(x_i^2=(bu_i+av_i)/b\). Let \(k\) be the least common multiple of the two denominators, so \(k^2\mid b\). For \(w_i=kx_i\in\mathbb Z\) and \(D=v_1v_2\), eliminating \(r\) gives
\[
 (v_2 w_1)^2-D w_2^2=C,\qquad C=v_2 k^2\Delta\ne0.
\]
There are at most \(\sqrt B\) choices for \(k\). For a fixed pair of affine forms and a fixed \(k\), each solution determines at most one base through \(r=(w_1^2/k^2-u_1)/v_1\). The bound \(|C|\le 2BU^3\) includes all choices.

If \(D\) is a positive square, factor the left side into two integer factors to obtain at most \(2\tau(|C|)\) solutions. Otherwise \(z=v_2w_1+w_2\sqrt D\) is an algebraic integer in a quadratic field with absolute norm \(|C|\). There are at most \(\tau(|C|)\) integral ideals of this norm. For a split prime at exponent \(e\) there are \(e+1\) allocations between the two prime ideals, and each inert or ramified prime allows at most one. Generators of each principal ideal differ by units. An imaginary quadratic field has at most six units. In the real case both embeddings of \(z\) have absolute value at most \(M=2U\sqrt{BU(1+R)}\), so each also has absolute value at least \(1/M\). After fixing a sign, the ratio between two increasing possible absolute values is a norm-one unit greater than one whose integral trace is at least three. Their ratio is at least \((3+\sqrt5)/2\). This bounds the number per ideal by \(O(\log(2RBU))\). Finally there are \(O(U^4)\) choices of the four coefficients. ## Gaussian moduli

For the homogeneous Gaussian modulus \(N=a^2+b^2\le X\) one has \(|r|,b\le\sqrt X\), and a reduced base determines \(N\). For \(U=X^{o(1)}\), at most \(X^{1/4+o(1)}\) such moduli admit two independent signs from these comparisons, since \(\tau(n)=n^{o(1)}\). For the integral schedule \(N=r^2+1\), take \(B=1\). If \(U\le\exp(O(W))\) and \(W=(\log X)^{1/3}(\log\log X)^{2/3}\), the total is \(\exp(O(W))\), even over all allowed coefficient choices. This also bounds higher-rank sign images generated by individual reduced affine comparisons, since such an image contains an independent pair of generators. Products that first become rational squares only after combining several nonsquare labels require a separate count.

The exponent for rational Gaussian bases is attained with fixed integral coefficients. The three Gaussian seeds on a primitive two-parameter conic family supply at least \(X^{1/4-o(1)}\) distinct inputs with four sign blocks. They come with a representation of the public input. A one-parameter subfamily of size \(\Theta(X^{1/8})\) admits polynomial-time recognition and factor extraction from the integer alone.
