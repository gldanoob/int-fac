# A classical partner sieve for genus-two factor selection

Let $p\in(P,2P]$ be prime, $L=\log(2P)$, $G=\sqrt{L\log(2L)}$, and $H=\lceil e^G\rceil$. Call $p$ eligible if it is inert in at least a quarter of the cyclic quintic fields of prime conductor $\ell\in(H/2,H]$. Only primes $\ell\equiv1\pmod5$ furnish such a field. Write $F(L)=L^{2/3}(\log(2L))^{4/3}$ classically and $F(L)=G$ using the ordinary Riemann hypothesis announced by Alpöge on 20 November 2026 and independently kernel-verified. [Verification and exact statements](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

For every eligible $p$ and every $Z\ge p^4$, a deterministic algorithm given $d=pm$ finds a proper divisor in mean time $b^{O(1)}\exp(O(F(L)))$ over **all** integers $m\in(Z,2Z]$ with least prime factor of $d$ equal to $p$, where $b=\lceil\log_2(2pZ+1)\rceil$. The same bound holds when conditioning on prime $m$. All field and quadratic-sign selection estimates at these eligible $p$ are classical. The sole use of ordinary RH in the faster version is the uniform smooth-number theorem for the genus-two order interval. [Uniform odd and smooth genus-two orders](quintic-field-genus-two.md#odd-smooth-orders-at-every-prime) · [Younis, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.05761)

There are at most $\sqrt P\exp(O(G))$ ineligible primes per dyad. Thus for $Z\ge (2P)^4$ the arithmetic mean over **all** such $(p,m)$ in the rectangle obeys the same bound, including the cost of a deterministic small-divisor fallback on every exception. The fixed zero-free strip of Cureton–Thorner applied to four order-five Kummer characters of $p$ on the single base $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ makes every $p$ eligible. [Cureton–Thorner, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00200v2#page=3) The unconditional rectangle result permits exceptional individual $p$. Each assertion concerns one split. The algorithm enumerates the possible dyads for $p$ and uses only $d$.

## A sieve for inert and split cyclic fields

Let $M\asymp H/\log H$ be the number of available conductors. Choose either orientation of an order-five Dirichlet character $\theta_\ell$ for each. Set $g_\ell(n)=\sum_{j=1}^4\theta_\ell(n)^j$, equal to 4 on fifth powers and $-1$ on other units. Define $k=\lfloor L/(2\log H)\rfloor$. On an ineligible $p$, for $P$ large enough that $p>H$, the sum of the $g_\ell(p)$ is at least $2M$. Their elementary symmetric sum of degree $k$ differs from $(\sum g_\ell)^k/k!$ by at most $k^2 4^k M^{k-1}/k!$, by counting ordered tuples with a collision. This error is $o((2M)^k/k!)$. The symmetric sum expands into $4^k\binom Mk$ distinct primitive Dirichlet characters, each having conductor at most $H^k$. The dual multiplicative large sieve at length $2P$ bounds its squared sum over all integers by $O((P+H^{2k})4^k\binom Mk)$. Consequently the ineligible count is $O(P(Ck/M)^k)\le\sqrt P\exp(O(G))$. In the last inequality the loss from the floor is $O(\log H)$ in the exponent and $k\log(k\log H)=O(G)$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, multiplicative large sieve and duality](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/571s25/montgomery-vaughanII.pdf)

For an eligible $p$ use only its $M_0\ge M/4$ inert fields. Fix $U\ge P/2$ and put $W=\min(\sqrt P,U^{1/5})$. A prime $q\in(U,2U]$ splitting in none of the inert fields has $g_\ell(q)=-1$ for each of them. Its elementary symmetric sum of degree $k_0=\lfloor\log W/\log H\rfloor$ has absolute value $\binom{M_0}{k_0}$. Applying the same dual inequality at length $2U$ bounds these $q$ by
$$ O\big((U+H^{2k_0})4^{k_0}/\tbinom{M_0}{k_0}\big)\le U\exp(O(G))/W. $$

For the pointwise conditional assertion, the extension $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5,p^{1/5})/\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)$ is cyclic of degree five for $p\ne5$, with relative different norm $p^{O(1)}$ times an absolute constant by the power-basis discriminant. A smoothed explicit formula on the fixed degree-four base for each nontrivial character gives error $O(H^{1-\delta}L^{O(1)})$ at the scale $H$, under a fixed strip of width $\delta>0$. The principal character on the fixed base gives its usual prime-ideal asymptotic by the classical prime ideal theorem. Higher residue degrees and prime powers cost $O(\sqrt H\log^2 H)$. At degree-one primes above any rational $\ell\equiv1\pmod5$, the Kummer symbols for $p$ are either all trivial or all nontrivial. Hence a proportion tending to $4/5$ of these $\ell$ have $p$ non-fifth-power, using fixed smooth upper and lower approximations to the interval. A strip for those four Hecke characters suffices, independently of the cyclic quintic fields below. [Prime-ideal explicit formula used for the pointwise construction](quintic-field-genus-two.md#a-pointwise-menu-of-fields-and-marks)

## Independent marks of small prime conductor

Fix any of the cyclic quintics $K$ above, a Galois generator $\sigma$, and write $D=|\operatorname{disc}K|\le H^{C_0}$. Choose $H_1=H^A$ with $A$ a sufficiently large absolute constant. There are $M_1\gg H_1/\log H_1$ rational primes $r\in(H_1/2,H_1]$ splitting completely in $K$. Indeed the unconditional Chebotarev estimate of Thorner and Zaman has relative error $O(\exp(-c\log x/\log D)+\exp(-c\sqrt{\log x/5}))$ in fixed degree at $x\ge D^{C}$. There is no exceptional real zero: $\zeta_K$ factors into $\zeta$ and four Dirichlet functions in nonreal conjugate pairs. Any real zero of a pair has multiplicity at least two, whereas the possible exceptional zero is simple. Choosing $A$ large makes the two errors at $H_1$ and $H_1/2$ small enough to subtract their estimates. [Thorner–Zaman, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.02823#page=2)

For each such $r$ choose one prime $R$ of $K$ above it. Its Minkowski lattice in $\mathbb R^5$ has determinant $\sqrt D r$ and first sup-norm minimum at least $r^{1/5}$ since every nonzero integral element in $R$ has absolute norm at least $r$. Minkowski's second theorem gives five independent vectors with sup norm $\ll\sqrt D r^{1/5}$. Their integral span has covering radius at most five times this bound. Approximating a sufficiently large positive diagonal vector gives a totally positive $a_R\in R$ with each real embedding $\ll \sqrt D r^{1/5}$. Thus $(a_R)=R B_R$ for an integral ideal with $NB_R\ll D^{5/2}<H_1/2$, enlarging $A$ as needed. Here and below $N$ applied to an ideal denotes absolute norm.

Let $c_R=\sigma(a_R)/a_R$. This norm-one element is totally positive. Its quadratic Hecke character $\chi_R$ has conductor $\mathfrak f_R$ of norm $\le 4^5 |N(a_R)|^2\le H_1^{2.05}$, using the quadratic discriminant bound on the integral representative $a_R\sigma(a_R)$ of the same squareclass. Its valuations are odd at $R,\sigma R$, and every other selected rational prime is absent from its support. Every nonempty subset product therefore ramifies at a private odd prime. For a completely split $q>H_1^{O(1)}$, a successful $c_R$ means that some conjugate of $c_R$ is a quadratic nonresidue in $\mathbb F_q$. If all marks fail, $\chi_R(Q)=1$ for every $R$ and every $Q$ above $q$. For sufficiently large $P$ all $q\ge P/2$ exceed the norm bounds and the power-basis discriminants.

The public algorithm can enumerate these marks within $\exp(O(G))$ bit operations per field. Enumerate all five-dimensional integer coefficient boxes of bound $H_1^{C}$ in a Gaussian period power basis $1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^4$. Each $a_R$, up to multiplication by a nonzero rational scalar, occurs in this box by inversion of the integral trace matrix, whose determinant, cofactors and trace vector are polynomially bounded in $H_1$. A rational scalar cancels from $\sigma(a_R)/a_R$. The field arithmetic and the Gaussian period construction have cost polynomial in $H_1$ and the input length. One can discard zero or bad denominators, checking gcds against the input as in the original norm procedure. ## A lattice bound for the missed marks

For each prime ideal $Q$ of norm $q\in(U,2U]$ counted above, the same positive lattice argument supplies $x\in Q$ with $(x)=QB$, $NB\ll D^{5/2}$ and all conjugates bounded by $R_0=C\sqrt D(2U)^{1/5}$. There are $D^{O(1)}$ possible $B$: the number of integral ideals of norm $n$ in degree five is at most $d_5(n)$. For fixed $B$, a choice of $x$ determines $Q$. The lattice of possible $x$ is $B$.

By weak approximation choose $y_B\in K^\times$ generating $B$ locally at every conductor prime of this finite list. Then $A_B=B/(y_B)$ is coprime to all conductors. On the selected $x$, $\chi_R(Q)=\chi_R((x/y_B))/\chi_R(A_B)$. Each principal-ideal character is periodic as a function of $x/y_B$ modulo $\mathfrak f_R$, with zero extension on nonunits. This uses total positivity of $c_R$ to exclude infinite conductor. The same local generators identify $B/B\mathfrak m$ with $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m$ for every common multiple $\mathfrak m$ of the conductors in a given product. Constants $\chi_R(A_B)$ have absolute value one.

Set $j=\lfloor\log W/\log H_1\rfloor$ and form the sum of all products of $j$ distinct $\chi_R(Q)$. On a missed prime this equals $T=\binom{M_1}j\ge W\exp(-O(G))$. Bound its squared sum by summing over the possible $B$ and the elements $x$ of their lattices, majorized by a fixed nonnegative smooth weight on $\mathbb R^5$ scaled by $R_0$ and equal to one on the required box. For a diagonal term the weighted count is $O(D^{O(1)}U)$, since every integral ideal has lattice minimum at least one. For two distinct subsets use the product $\mathfrak m$ of all the conductors from both subsets. It has $N\mathfrak m\le H_1^{4.1j}\le W^{4.1}\le U^{0.82}$. The complete sum of the two characters' cross term over $B/B\mathfrak m$ vanishes. The exclusive odd prime of any index in the symmetric difference ensures the quotient character on the unit group is nonprincipal. Zero extension handles all nonunits.

To quantify the remaining modes, apply lattice Poisson summation separately in each coset of $B\mathfrak m$. Its trace-dual is $\mathfrak d_K^{-1}(B\mathfrak m)^{-1}$ and every nonzero vector in the dual has Euclidean norm $\gg(D\,NB\,N\mathfrak m)^{-1/5}$ by the norm bound for fractional ideals. If this lower bound is $s$, packing dual vectors into successive annuli and using smooth Fourier decay gives $\sum_{\xi\ne0}|\widehat w(R_0\xi)|\ll_h(R_0s)^{-h}$ for any fixed $h>5$, once $R_0s\ge1$. Here $R_0s\ge U^{0.03}$ for all sufficiently large $P$, since $D=\exp(O(G))=P^{o(1)}$ and $U\ge P/2$. The Poisson prefactor summed over the $N\mathfrak m$ cosets is $R_0^5/(\sqrt D NB)\ll D^{O(1)}U$. Choosing $h=200$ gives an off-diagonal contribution $\ll D^{O(1)}U P^{-3}$ for each pair. After dividing by $T^2$ and summing over $B$, the missed primes for this $K$ number at most $D^{O(1)} U(1/T+P^{-3})$. A union over the at most $H$ inert fields and the preceding split-field sieve show that **every** $U\ge P/2$ has at most
$$ U\,\exp(O(G))/\min(\sqrt P,U^{1/5}) $$
primes $q$ in its dyad that admit no suitable field and mark for the fixed eligible $p$.

## Charging all integer partners

The curve model $\operatorname{Norm}(X+c\beta^2)$ and its marked divisor now work unchanged. At $p$ a proportion $\exp(-O(L\log(2L)/\log Y))$ of the normalized four-coordinate chart gives an odd $Y$-smooth order. At a selected distinct $q\mid m$, the nonsquare coordinate forces even order independently of $\beta$, or a failed unit check already yields a divisor. The scaled starting word $\lfloor pm/2^{\lfloor\log_2p\rfloor}\rfloor$ is injective in $m$ and each four-coordinate bit word occurs $O(1+Z/p^4)$ times. The XOR miss estimate for one target can be summed over the public $\exp(O(G))$ fields, marks and chart choices at $p$. Their enumeration also adds only $\exp(O(G))$ to the stage-cost factor. Summing the dyadic stage tails as in the original algorithm proves the stated cost for partners with a suitable $q$. [Full selection algorithm](quintic-field-genus-two.md#deterministic-selection-from-a-long-partner-interval)

Partners divisible by $p$ or a square of any prime $q\ge p$ contribute $O(Z/p)$ in total: sum $2Z/q^2$ over all $q\ge p$ up to $\sqrt{2Z}$. If $m$ has a missed prime $q\ge p^{5/2}$, summing $2Z/q$ per prime over the preceding dyadic bound gives $O(Zb\exp(O(G))/\sqrt p)$ such partners. For the remaining squarefree $p$-rough partners with no usable prime, all prime factors are missed and lie between $p$ and $p^{5/2}$. For $a=1/6$ the preceding bound gives $\sum_{p\le q\le p^{5/2},\ q\ \mathrm{missed}}q^{-1+a}\ll\exp(O(G))p^{a-1/5}=o(1)$, by summing geometric dyads. Rankin's bound therefore gives at most $(2Z)^{1-a}\prod_{q\ \mathrm{in\ this\ set}}(1+q^{-1+a})\ll Z^{1-a}$ such $m$. For $Z\ge p^4$ this is $O(Zp^{-2/3})$. On all these exceptions a deterministic interleaved small-divisor search costs at most $\sqrt p\,b^{O(1)}$. There are $\gg Z/\log Z$ permitted partners, supplied by the primes alone, so renormalization absorbs only a polynomial. Conditioning only on prime partners gives the same bound directly.

Finally there are $\gg (P/\log P)(Z/\log Z)$ permitted pairs in the full rectangle. Every ineligible $p$ contributes at most $Z\sqrt{2P}\,b^{O(1)}$ before division by this total. Its dyadic count therefore proves the unconditional mean. Small bounded $P$ can use trial division throughout. These arguments preserve the use of the whole partner as the bit-cube start, including on inputs with repeated prime powers.
