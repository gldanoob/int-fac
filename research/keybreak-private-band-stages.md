# A staged private-coordinate test for one integer

Under Dirichlet GRH, the November 26 KeyBreak preprint's analytic inputs give a single-input randomized factoring schedule with expected bit time $L_N[1/3,2.510]$. The new step tests for a prescribed amount of prime factorization in each of a fixed number of bands, starting at the cheapest cutoff. Both coordinates must pass a stage before either is sent to the next. All factoring of partial coordinates, private root draws, field construction and extraction are charged to the current integer. The [single-band proof](keybreak-private-shape-work.md) gives the notation, first-split preprocessing, and cheap final square reconstruction. The source PDF has SHA-256 `2b534eaf77fb2b9712018879059d8d4667c40c0a4adf2fff90efc22fb356cfc9`. [KeyBreak, Appendices G–H](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105)

[Top rational counts, §G.3](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=108)
* At one top parent the rational smooth cofactor scale is $MV_0/p<M$ eventually, below the internal worst case $MV_1$. For the aggregate top rational progression estimate the smallest cofactor is at the unchanged *upper* parent cutoff $Y_0$. With the source's $\epsilon=1/32$, the exponent of $M$ in (G.17) is still $-4511/16512$. The top means use $V_0^2/Y_0$, with the same positive leading margin. All top algebraic heights and next cutoffs are unchanged. [Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)
* The ordinary top-band error in (G.18), (G.53) uses $Y_0/V_0^2+1/M$ and the incoming error uses $Y_1/V_0^2+Y_1/M$. Each is a negative power of $M$. Inverse rational densities here have logarithm $O(H)$, where $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}=o(\log M)$. The weight normalization in (G.54)–(G.55) compares primes within the same dyadic band. ## The shapes counted below

Use $J=128$ fixed stages, indexed from zero. Write $v_0=143/200$, $v_i=v_0+(1-v_0)i/(J-1)$ and $a_i=v_i^2$. Let $e_i^*=\lfloor a_i e_0\rfloor$ and $Y_i^*=2^{e_i^*}$ (these starred symbols apply only to the private search). The successive intervals have right endpoints $Y_i^*$, the first left endpoint is $Y_{\min}$, and only the right endpoints are included.

Take $h_*=70277/50000$, an upper bound for $(20001/10000)\sqrt{40/81}$. Define positive two-coordinate losses $w_i$ and log-size fractions $f_i$ by
$$
 w_i=h_*(v_{i+1}-v_i)\quad(i<J-1),\qquad
 w_{J-1}=9/20-\sum_{i<J-1}a_iw_i,\qquad f_i=\frac{20}{9}a_iw_i.
$$
In particular $\sum f_i=1$ and $w_i=(9/20) f_i/a_i$. Positivity and all numerical guards below are checked in [the rational replay](../experiments/keybreak-private-band-stages.py).

Allocate nonnegative integer bit lengths $L_i=f_i L_x+O(J)$ summing to $L_x$ from (H.18), by flooring the first $J-1$ and using the remainder. For each $i$, apply the finite H.5 band recipe with length $L_i$ and exponent $e=e_i^*$: set $k'=\lceil L_i/e\rceil$, $m_i$ its bit length, $k_i=k'+\lceil k'/m_i\rceil$, $S=k_i e-L_i$, $j_i=\lfloor 2(S+k_i)/k_i\rfloor$. Select the complete dyadic bands with upper exponents $e,e-1,\dots,e-j_i$. Since $f_i,a_i$ are fixed positive constants, $k_i\to\infty$ and $j_i/e=o(1)$. Each band's lower endpoint eventually lies in its own interval, including stage zero because $a_0>0.5108$. The central index $\lfloor k_i j_i/2\rfloor$ lies between $S$ and $S+k_i$, and $2m_i(j_i+1)\ge e$ eventually, as in the single-band calculation. The total $k=\sum k_i$ has $k/k_0\to\sum f_i/a_i<2$ for the source's $k_0$. Thus its original $K=2k_0$, child caps, and retained occurrence heights suffice. Check these finite guards explicitly; failed guards can use trial division below the eventual threshold.

On the good field event, (H.20) in each group gives a set $A_i$ of squarefree products of $k_i$ good primes, each in $[T_i,2^{L_i}]$, with $T_i=2^{L_i-2k_i}$. The lower bound per set is $2^{L_i}/[4(n+2)^2(128 k_i m_i)^{k_i}]$ for input bit length $n$. The factor $2k_i$ in the threshold accounts for both the central-index slack and choosing a prime in the lower half of its dyadic band. Distinct intervals give unique factorizations in $A=\prod_i A_i$. Thus $|A|\ge 2^{L_x}\exp(-[\sum w_i/2+o(1)]H)$, with an explicit product of the finite denominators just displayed. Here $k_i\sim (f_i/a_i)k_0$ and $k_0\log k_0/H\to9/40$.

As in H.5, at least $|A|^2/2^{\omega(N)}$ ordered pairs share a unit square class. The number sharing a prime is at most $kX|A|/Y_{\min}$, $X=\sqrt N/4$, negligible since $\log Y_{\min}\gg H$ and the first-split prepass may handle inputs with $\omega(N)\ne o(H)$ as in the single-band proof. Coprime pairs have distinct quotients modulo $N$ (their determinant is smaller than $N$), are the unique shortest vectors up to simultaneous sign, and each quotient has $2^{\omega(N)}$ private preimages. A fresh block therefore proposes a counted good shape with probability at least $\exp(-[\sum w_i+o(1)]H)$. For an explicit inverse lower bound $P$, use $2^{16}\prod_i[4(n+2)^2(128 k_i m_i)^{k_i}]^2$; the fixed prefactor covers the powers of two in the shortest-vector and private-block count. Keep $32(C+1)P$ attempts. The original good-state descent bound then supplies enough retained rows as in H.8. [Counting shapes and completing rows](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=135)

## Testing and counting a prefix

At stage $i$ collect the prime powers dividing each coordinate in the interval ending at $Y_i^*$. The product for **each** coordinate must reach $T_i$. At the end enforce full verified factorization, squarefreeness, unit conditions, interval membership, and the original count cap for each coordinate before launching. A candidate can fail any time a threshold is missed.

To collect small primes from a partly smooth integer, use the bounded first-split procedure in the proof of H.3 recursively on unresolved cofactors. Check primes and perfect powers exactly, trial-divide to $z_y$, try the bounded curve procedure, and recurse after each proper split. An exhausted composite contributes no further certified primes. Every composite non-prime-power containing a prime $p\le y$ has the same uniform first-split guarantee regardless of its other factors. At most $O(n)$ nodes need calls, by counting multiplicities. Thus missing a desired small factor has negligible probability with polynomial confidence overhead. Acceptance uses only verified primes and divisibility. This argument requires the useful-prime proof of H.3; a black box guaranteed only on completely smooth inputs would be insufficient. Use the fixed curve trial exponent $10001/10000$ justified in the [single-band analysis](keybreak-private-shape-work.md#narrowing-the-private-band). Its bit-cost coefficient at stage $i$ is at most $h_*v_i$. The random blocks can be indexed by private slot, coordinate, stage and factor-tree node. A polynomial confidence multiplier covers all calls on every permitted tape.

For the upper count, let $X'=\lceil\sqrt{2N}\rceil$. A coordinate surviving the first $j$ stages is divisible by the product of pairwise coprime certified band parts $d_i\ge T_i$ for $i<j$. Counting multiples and *then* dropping the disjoint-support conditions bounds their number by
$$
 X'\prod_{i<j}\sum_{d\ge T_i,\ P^+(d)\le Y_i^*}\frac1d .
$$
For $\eta_i=\log(\log X'/\log Y_i^*)/\log Y_i^*$ each sum is at most $T_i^{-\eta_i}\prod_{p\le Y_i^*}(1-p^{-1+\eta_i})^{-1}$. The log of the Euler product is $o(H)$ by the single-band Rankin estimate. Also $\eta_i\log T_i=(w_i/2+o(1))H$. The signed pair count and the unit-square fiber upper bound give probability at most $\exp(-[\sum_{i<j}w_i+o(1)]H)$ for a fresh raw private block to reach stage $j$ on the rough-input case of the prepass. This upper bound applies even if the recognizer misses some small factors. For bounding the loss of genuine good shapes use the separate coupling of the one-sided errors.

Put $s=1/10000$. Cap the number of candidates entering stage $j\ge1$ at coefficient $b+\sum_{i\ge j}w_i+s$, including the launch stage $j=J$ with empty tail. A cap with rational coefficient $q$ can be implemented as $2^{\lceil 4qt/3\rceil}$ with $t=dm_b$ from (G.1). Count over all raw slots with unused slots exposed virtually; the prefix upper bounds and Markov give overflow probability $\exp(-(s+o(1))H)$ at each stage. There are only $J$ caps. For stage zero the raw-attempt allowance already has coefficient $b+\sum_i w_i$. Caps, payments and band acceptance depend only on public residues and public bits, preserving the hidden-root sign argument. When the first-split prepass faces a small divisor it already succeeds with probability $1-o(1)$; the later caps give finite cost even if that pass misses.

## Whole-epoch ledger

Set $s_A=43/40$, $s_R=5926/12255$. Proposition G.13 proves a uniform algebraic loss at most $(s_A+o(1))H$ and a rational loss at most $(s_R+o(1))H$ throughout the descent. The enlarged top band preserves these estimates by the scale checks above. Keep all existing good-state definitions. In (H.15) use inverse-density caps of coefficients $s_A+s$ and $s_A+s_R+s$ respectively. The fixed margin absorbs constants and lower-order terms in (G.6), (H.16); thus the capped conditional success proof H.4 applies. Use the refined curve exponent also in the algebraic tests, making their worst per-call cost coefficient $(20001/20100)\sqrt{76313/322500}$. All earlier rational-side descent smoothness tests at $B$ cost $\exp(o(H))$ per trial.

The private-stage maximum is the same expression at every $j$ by the choice of $w_i$. The launch cap pays for failed trees as well as retained ones; its multiplicative tree-size bound is $\exp(o(H))$. For elimination use a fixed exact block multiplication scheme over the binary field (or a fixed finite extension) of exponent below $2.6$, which follows from Schönhage's arbitrary-field exponent bound below $2.522$. One may choose a sufficiently large fixed block and use exact bilinear rank, absorbing the fixed interpolation or bilinearization overhead into the slack. Recursive block scans and sorting indexed permutations preserve the exponent on multitape machines. Rank-revealing PLUQ handles singular rectangular matrices, and triangular solving gives a verified dependency. If a fixed field extension is used, expand the returned vector in a binary basis and take a nonzero coordinate vector. [Schönhage](https://doi.org/10.1137/0210032) · [Dumas, Pernet and Sultan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.4438) · [Tape implementation](cogentic-fast-full-grid-cost.md)

The root and occurrence bounds are those of the single-band proof. The original constant per-epoch success loses only $o(1)$, uniformly across fresh epochs. Trial division between failed epochs guarantees termination on every tape. The factor tree has polynomially many nodes, so the strict $2.510$ ceiling holds for the whole single-input computation. The replay checks all stage weights and the ledger by exact rational arithmetic and checks the prefix multiple count on a finite grid. This result depends on the pinned preprint's analytic propositions and is separate from the lower heuristic coefficients of NFS.
