# Exact square kernels through odd abelian cyclic subfields

Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be an odd-degree abelian Galois field given by a monic irreducible polynomial, and let $d=[E:\mathbb Q]$. For a list of explicit nonzero elements of $E$, the full square kernel is the intersection of the square kernels of their norms to at most $d$ cyclic subfields. Each cyclic subfield has degree at most the exponent of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$. The reduction is deterministic and takes polynomial bit work in the explicit input size, apart from the subfield kernel calls. It can serve as the bottom of a supplied quadratic tower. ## Detection by the cyclic quotient norms

Write $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$, and write the squareclass module $V=E^\times/E^{\times2}$ additively. For each subgroup $J\le G$ with cyclic quotient, let $F_J=E^J$. Norm to $F_J$ followed by restriction to $E$ acts on $V$ by $e_J=\sum_{g\in J}g$. Restriction of squareclasses from $F_J$ is injective because $[E:F_J]=|J|$ is odd. Indeed, if a class dies in $E$, taking the norm to $F_J$ makes its odd power trivial.

It is enough to work in the finite-dimensional submodule generated by all Galois translates of the input classes. Over $\overline{\mathbb F}_2$, its commuting action operators diagonalize simultaneously. Each has odd order, so its minimal polynomial divides a separable polynomial $T^m-1$, and commuting operators preserve each other's eigenspaces. Every joint eigenspace has a character $\chi:G\to\overline{\mathbb F}_2^\times$. Its image is cyclic, so $J=\ker\chi$ is one of the selected subgroups. The operator $e_J$ on this eigenspace is multiplication by $|J|=1$ in characteristic two. Consequently a vector killed by all $e_J$ has zero component in every character space and is zero. Equivalently, an element of $E$ is a square precisely when all its norms to these cyclic subfields are squares. This also proves the statement for products of the supplied columns. The rational subfield detects the trivial character.

There are $d$ characters over $\overline{\mathbb F}_2$, since on a cyclic factor of odd order $m$ every one of the $m$ roots of $T^m-1$ gives a character. One needs just one subfield per character kernel. More precisely, their number is $\sum_{J:G/J\ {\rm cyclic}}1$, with the identity $d=\sum_J\phi([G:J])$. It is at most $1+(d-1)/(p_{\min}-1)$ for nontrivial $G$, where $p_{\min}$ is its least prime divisor. Checking only a collection of cyclic subfields that generates $E$ under compositum can miss other character spaces.

## Explicit computation and costs

The automorphisms can be supplied as the images of a primitive element, or derived in polynomial bit work from the original polynomial $P$. Select a nonnegative integer $j$ for which
$$
R_j(T)=\operatorname{Res}_{X}(P(X),P(T-jX))
$$
is squarefree. Its roots have the form $\beta+j\alpha$ with $\alpha,\beta$ running independently over roots of $P$. Each distinct pair of geometric points can collide for at most one $j$, so scanning through $\binom{d^2}{2}$ suffices. Factor $R_j$ over $\mathbb Q$. Each root $\beta+j\alpha$ generates $E$, since squarefreeness makes its stabilizer the stabilizer of the ordered pair, which is trivial in $G$. For every irreducible factor $R$, the gcd of $P(Y)$ and $R(Y+j\alpha)$ in $E[Y]$ is therefore linear and gives one image of $\alpha$. Distinct factors give distinct images. Polynomial factorization over $\mathbb Q$, resultant and exact gcd bounds are polynomial in the input size. [Rational factorization and primitive-element scan](cogentic-order-square-kernel.md#recovering-a-root-from-a-degree-2d-polynomial)

Compute the group table by composition. An abelian decomposition can be obtained by Smith form using one generator symbol per group element and the relations $[g]+[h]=[gh]$. Enumerate the $d$ character kernels using the cyclic factors of this decomposition, representing character values by integer exponents modulo the exponent of $G$. For each distinct $J$, rational linear equations for the fixed space give a basis $v_0,\ldots,v_{k-1}$ of $F_J$. A primitive generator is found among $\sum_i j^i v_i$ for $O(k^3)$ successive integer values of $j$. For each pair of distinct restrictions of embeddings, their difference on that expression is a nonzero polynomial in $j$ of degree at most $k-1$, so the stated scan suffices. Identify the generator by taking the first with minimal polynomial of degree $k$. Linear algebra in its power basis expresses the norms $\prod_{g\in J}g(z)$ of each input column. All norm products have polynomial bit size, since each product contains at most $d$ factors in the known power basis. An integral primitive generator for a subfield is obtained by clearing denominators in the top integral power basis.

Compute the subfield kernels separately in the common mask coordinates and intersect the resulting binary subspaces. Each final basis mask has an explicit product of the original columns of polynomial bit size. One top-field square-root oracle call per final basis mask recovers its root directly. In particular, no primitive generator of a multiquadratic cover of the full list is formed. The residual squareclass exponent in the running time is the **largest** bound on a cyclic subfield, with only polynomial extra work for at most $d$ calls. The top residual rank can be larger than each of these bounds. [Exact square-root oracle and residual cost](cogentic-order-square-kernel.md)

For example, when all cyclic subfields have bounded degree and $\log(2|\Delta_{F_J}|)=O(W)$, their unit and class-group 2-ranks are $O(W)$ by the ideal count at the Minkowski bound. Thus with $W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$ and explicit inputs of size $\exp(O(W))$, the unconditional work for complete kernel recovery remains $\exp(O(W))$. The same conclusion holds with individual bounds from local class quotients or accessible Smith torsion in place of a small discriminant. [Class number bound and atom certificate](cogentic-order-square-kernel.md#integer-relations-and-sign-rows) · [Local class quotient](cogentic-local-class-square-kernel.md)

An explicit source of growing odd degrees is the compositum of the cubic subfields of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{p_i})$ for distinct primes $p_i\equiv1\pmod3$. These fields have group $C_3^r$ by the CRT on the cyclotomic Galois group. Every nontrivial cyclic subfield is cubic, unramified outside the $p_i$ and at worst tamely ramified there. Hence its discriminant is at most $(\prod_i p_i)^2$. There is an elementary supply with $\log\prod_i p_i=O(3^r)$. Given the first $i$ primes with product $P_i$, choose a prime divisor of $(3P_i)^2+3P_i+1$. It is new and has $3P_i$ of order three modulo it, and the recursion gives $\log P_{i+1}\le3\log P_i+O(1)$. Trial division and cyclotomic arithmetic in conductor $P_r$ cost $\exp(O(3^r))$. Taking the degree $3^r=O(W)$ therefore gives a single-input construction of fields and kernels on this scale. This does not provide a common modular root on arbitrary $N$. For primes outside the defining polynomial discriminant, a root of an irreducible polynomial defining a Galois field requires complete splitting in that field.

## A diagonal cubic missed by the generating fields

Take $a$ with minimal polynomial $X^3+X^2-2X-1$ and $b$ with minimal polynomial $X^3-3X+1$, the standard real generators at conductors 7 and 9. The compositum has group $C_3^2$. Let $\sigma(a)=a^2-2$ act on the first cubic, and $\tau(b)=b^2-2$ on the second. The element
$$
\gamma=\sum_{i=0}^2\sigma^i(a)\tau^i(b)
$$
generates the cubic fixed by $\langle\sigma\tau\rangle$. Its minimal polynomial is $X^3-21X-28$. The unit $u=-\gamma^2+3\gamma+11$ has minimal polynomial $X^3+9X^2-57X-1$. This polynomial has two negative roots, and its constant coefficient gives norm one. Thus $u$ is nonsquare even in the top field by odd-degree restriction. Its norm to the diagonal cubic is $u^3$. Each of the other three order-three subgroups restricts isomorphically onto the diagonal cubic's Galois group, so the norm to each of the other three cubics is one. The generating cubics alone both miss the class.

The identities and signs were checked in PARI/GP with exact polynomial and Sturm arithmetic.

~~~gp
z = polcompositum(x^3+x^2-2*x-1, x^3-3*x+1, 1)[1];
a = z[2]; b = z[3];
A = [a, a^2-2, (a^2-2)^2-2]; B = [b, b^2-2, (b^2-2)^2-2];
g = vector(3, j, sum(i=1, 3, A[i]*B[1+(i+j-2)%3]));
u = vector(3, j, -g[j]^2+3*g[j]+11);
if(minpoly(g[1]) != x^3-21*x-28, error("diagonal cubic"));
if(minpoly(u[1]) != x^3+9*x^2-57*x-1, error("unit polynomial"));
if(prod(j=1, 3, u[j]) != 1, error("cubic norm"));
H = [[1,0], [0,1], [1,1], [1,2]];
{for(k=1, #H,
  v = H[k][2]-H[k][1];
  w = prod(j=0, 2, u[1+(j*v+6)%3]);
  if(w != if(v==0, u[1]^3, 1), error("subfield norm"))
);}
if(polsturm(minpoly(u[1]), [-100,0]) != 2, error("unit signs"));
~~~
