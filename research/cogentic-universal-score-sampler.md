# Deterministic one-third mean time from short descriptions of the scalar tests

There is a uniform deterministic complete factoring algorithm that, for every \(b\), takes
\[
 \exp(O(H_b)),\qquad H_b=b^{1/3}(\log(2b))^{2/3},
\]
bit operations on **all but \(\exp(O(V_b))\)** of the \(b\)-bit inputs, where \(V_b=b^{2/3}(\log(2b))^{1/3}\). Its cost on every input is \(\exp(O(V_b))\). Consequently every fixed positive arithmetic moment of the actual complete factoring time on \(b\)-bit integers has \(r\)-th root \(\exp(O_r(H_b))\). The same conclusion holds over any set of \(J\ge\exp(C_rV_b)\) distinct \(b\)-bit inputs for a sufficiently large fixed \(C_r\). In particular this is a deterministic \(L_X[1/3,O(1)]\) arithmetic-mean bound on ordinary dyadic intervals. The input is the integer alone.

This is a consequence of Cogentic's two-field order extractor, the [retuned deterministic moment proof](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md), the [nonnegative score expansion](cogentic-conditional-score.md), and the proved Dirichlet Riemann hypothesis used there. The pinned paper states the separate deterministic worst-case bound \(\exp(3(\log N)^{3/4})\). Goldreich and Wigderson used extractors to derive typically correct simulations from advice good on all inputs. Here the simultaneous tests are elementary arithmetic events in the score. Their short descriptions are the reason the exceptional count is subexponential in \(b\). [Cogentic, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 10.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf) · [Goldreich–Wigderson, 2002](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/ABSTRACT/gw02.pdf) · [Verified Dirichlet theorem](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## A common trial space

Use one fixed strict schedule in the integer-mark two-thirds proof. For a current child modulus \(M\le2^b\) the two fields' four coordinates \(u_{ij}\), \(i=1,2,\ j=0,1\), originally have independent Gaussians centered at \(\bar u_j=L_0^2 S_j\) with \(S_0=UA,\ S_1=U\), powers of two. Instead sample uniformly from the \(4S_j\) integers in
\[
                   [\bar u_j-2S_j,\bar u_j+2S_j).
\]
All are admissible for large \(M\). Each uniform point mass is at most an absolute constant times its Gaussian point mass, coordinate by coordinate. For the nonnegative sufficient score \(P_{M,\ell}\) at a generator prime \(\ell\), the expectation on the uniform four-dimensional rectangle is therefore \(o(1)\), uniformly as \(M\) grows. The score below one guarantees a verified order multiple. [Rectangle domination and exact fair-bit encoding](cogentic-finite-coins.md#uniform-integer-boxes) · [Score and strict rank implication](cogentic-conditional-score.md#a-computable-nonnegative-score)

The four coordinates require \(4\log_2 U+2\log_2 A+8=O(V_b)\) bits. Fix a computable \(k_b=O(V_b)\) large enough for every current child of every \(b\)-bit input, by a monotone upper envelope of the integer schedule. A common \(k_b\)-bit trial word is read in four fields of the required lengths, ignoring the surplus bits. Uniform words give exactly the specified rectangle for every child. For children of at most \(\lceil\log_2(b+2)\rceil\) bits use direct trial division, in time polynomial in the original length \(b\). The uniform \(o(1)\) estimate then applies to all remaining children simultaneously as \(b\) grows.

## The elementary test family has \(\exp(O(V_b))\) members

For each \(M,\ell\) write the finite expansion \(P_{M,\ell}=\sum_j c_j F_j\) from the conditional score, combining both fields in each Boolean function \(F_j\). The signs and values of the real coefficients \(c_j\) may vary with the entire input. Every \(F_j\) is a product of a bounded number of the following predicates.

* A marked squarefree smoothness indicator tests the absolute value of \(C+D(u_{i0}+a u_{i1})\), where \(C=f_i^{(0)}(a)\), \(D=P_0\ell(a-m)\). Its allowed factors are the primes in \((\sqrt B,B]\) coprime to \(k_0k'_0\). There are at most two marks per field. All of \(C,D,a,B,k_0k'_0\) have \(O(V_b)\) bits. Indeed \(\log m,\log X_{\rm sc},\log c_d=O(V_b)\), \(\log A,\log B=O(H_b)\), \(k_0k'_0<4c_d^2\), and \(|C|\le X_{\rm sc}\exp(O(H_b))+P_0\ell m\,dA^{d-1}=\exp(O(V_b))\). The bound also follows from the coefficient and target estimates in Lemma 8.2. The fixed list of eligible primes is fully specified by these integers, without listing them individually. A deterministic mark restriction such as \(\gcd(a-m,k_0k'_0)=1\) determines which summands occur.
* The presence of a root row or a multiple root imposes boundedly many affine congruences on \((u_{i0},u_{i1})\) modulo primes at most \(B\). The constants for \(f_i^{(0)}(r)\) and its derivative are supplied as residue classes. Each modulus, coefficient and residue has \(O(H_b)\) bits. A shared root is tested in both fields. Protected target rows and real rows have deterministic membership.
* The word decoding lengths and interval offsets are a bounded tuple of integers of \(O(V_b)\) bits. Constant factors equal to one or zero can be included.

The number of slots in these descriptions is bounded by an absolute constant, independently of the input, degree, and prime base. Hence all Boolean summands in all scores of every \(M\le2^b\), including both large and small admissible bit lengths, lie in one family \(\mathscr F_b\) of at most \(\exp(C_1V_b)\) functions on \(\{0,1\}^{k_b}\). Encoding the full polynomial pair or the full modulus for each test would give an unnecessarily large family. The algorithm does not enumerate \(\mathscr F_b\). [Cogentic, fixed factor base and perturbation in §8.2](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=39)

We also need the more precise bound
\[
                 \sum_j |c_j|\le\exp(C_2 H_b) .                   \tag{1}
\]
There are \(\operatorname{poly}(A,B,d,L_0)=\exp(O(H_b))\) entries, marks, and terms after expansion of the squared Gram entries and row-mass statistics. For each mark the reciprocal one-field reference density is \(\exp(O(H_b))\) by the uniform saddle estimates, including omission of at most the prescribed primes. The normalized grid mass is at most one and \(q_0/\phi(q_0)\ge1\). Thus \(\sum_a w_{\rm eq}(a)\le\exp(O(H_b))\). The components of the reference row vector are bounded by \(B^{\alpha/2}\) or a polynomial in \(b\) times the absolute logarithmic-root bound \(O(b)\). The atomic one- and two-prime row means are products and ratios of boundedly many saddle densities, normalized row factors and prime powers up to \(B^{O(1)}\), so their absolute values are \(\exp(O(H_b))\) as well. Hence \(\sum_a|w_{\rm eq}(a)(r_j(a)r_k(a)-\widetilde U^{\rm ex}_{jk})|\le\exp(O(H_b))\) for every pair. Squaring such a sum multiplies its total coefficient bound by at most another \(\exp(O(H_b))\). In the other score terms \(\mu_0,\varphi''(\alpha),u\) and their required positive reciprocals are polynomially bounded, and the fixed peeling multipliers are \(B^{O(1)}\exp(O((\log b)^2))\). The weighted Frobenius sums involve only polynomially many factor-base weights bounded by \(B^{O(1)}\). These estimates give (1) with one constant uniform in \(M,\ell\). Real score coefficients are used solely in the existence proof. ## Few original inputs miss any elementary expectation

For large \(b\), put \(K_b=2k_b\le b\) and \(\epsilon_b=2^{-\lceil C_3H_b\rceil}\), with a sufficiently large fixed \(C_3\) so that \(2\epsilon_b e^{C_2H_b}=o(1)\). Use the explicit \((K_b,\epsilon_b)\) extractor of Guruswami, Umans and Vadhan on \(b\)-bit strings, with their constant loss parameter \(1/2\). Truncate its output to \(k_b\) bits and denote it by \(E_b(x,s)\). The seed length satisfies \(d_b\le\log_2 b+O(\log(K_b/\epsilon_b))=O(H_b)\), with uniform polynomial-time evaluation in the construction parameters, within \(\exp(O(H_b))\) here. [Guruswami–Umans–Vadhan, Theorem 1.5](https://scholar.harvard.edu/files/salil/files/acm2009.pdf#page=4)

For a Boolean test \(F\) write \(\overline F(x)=2^{-d_b}\sum_s F(E_b(x,s))\) and let \(U\) be a uniform \(k_b\)-bit word. Fewer than \(2^{K_b}\) masters \(x\) can have \(\overline F(x)-\mathbb EF(U)>2\epsilon_b\). Otherwise take a source uniform on any \(2^{K_b}\) such masters. Its min-entropy is \(K_b\), whereas the extracted distribution differs from uniform on the event \(F^{-1}(1)\) by more than \(2\epsilon_b\). The identical argument handles the negative deviation. Taking the union over \(\mathscr F_b\), there are at most \(2^{K_b+1}|\mathscr F_b|=\exp(O(V_b))\) masters failing even one estimate.

For every other \(b\)-bit master \(x\), linearity and (1) give, simultaneously for every admissible child modulus \(M\) and every target prime \(\ell\),
\[
         2^{-d_b}\sum_s P_{M,\ell}(E_b(x,s))
            \le \mathbb E P_{M,\ell}(U)+2\epsilon_b e^{C_2H_b}<1.
\]
Consequently at least one seed produces a strict rank certificate for each of these targets. The witnessing seed can depend on the target. No union over the possible real weights, full scores, or child moduli is needed.

This also gives a shorter **universal advice** construction than one based only on whole-trial success densities. Set the extractor's master length to \(K_b+\lceil C_1V_b/\log 2\rceil+3\) instead of \(b\), with all outputs and test functions as before. The exceptional count is unchanged and occupies less than half the new master space. Any one nonexceptional master, of \(O(V_b)\) bits, generates a list of \(\exp(O(H_b))\) trials that works for every input below \(2^b\). The choice of that master is nonuniform. [Generic density-only advice tradeoff](cogentic-extractor-advice.md)

## Uniform preprocessing for every input of a given size

That universal master can also be **constructed deterministically in \(\exp(O(V_b))\) bit operations once per input size bound**. Retain only the \(O(V_b)\)-bit master. With it, the subsequent worst-case cost of every complete factorization below \(2^b\) is \(\exp(O(H_b))\).

For the preprocessing, take all strings in the bounded-slot description format above, with explicit rounded upper envelopes for the size of each integer field. Reject malformed strings or interpret them as the constant zero function. This gives a computable superset of \(\mathscr F_b\) still having \(\exp(O(V_b))\) members. Enforce \(B\le\exp(O(H_b))\) by the fixed envelope and bound each decoded shift or coefficient by \(\exp(O(V_b))\). Every test can now be evaluated uniformly in \(\exp(O(H_b))\) bit operations by elementary integer arithmetic, sieving to \(B\) and trial division. For each description compute its exact uniform weight by counting the accepted \(k_b\)-bit words. Search through all candidate masters of the shorter length just given, increasing its fixed multiple of \(V_b\) to cover the explicitly chosen superset. For each master compute each empirical test weight by enumerating all its extractor seeds, and compare the two dyadic weights exactly at threshold \(2\epsilon_b\). The preceding exceptional-count proof ensures success. The numbers of descriptions, candidate masters, and words in an exact uniform sum are individually \(\exp(O(V_b))\); their product has the same asymptotic form. The comparisons need \(O(k_b+d_b+\log(1/\epsilon_b))\) bits. Recomputing means inside the search permits streaming the tests. Only the master and its size parameter need to be retained for future targets.

The per-target stage below uses rigorous scalar moment estimates in the bit model. The November 2026 *Fan Factories* study optimizes shared rational-side precomputation for many number-field-sieve targets under its stated smoothness and relation heuristics, obtaining leading constants in the RAM model. Its resource tradeoff and underlying construction are different. [Heninger and coauthors, *Fan Factories*, §1.1](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/papers/Fan_Factories_Multiple_Number_Fields_in_Factorization_Factories_and_Batch_NFS.pdf#page=2)

## Complete bit cost

The algorithm uses the binary encoding \(x\) of the **original input** as its master. It keeps that same master, its length \(b\), and its list of \(k_b\)-bit words at every recursive node. On each current modulus, recognize primes and powers, try small prime factors through \(B\), and perform the original preliminary gcd checks in the finite grid. On any surviving composite run the finite rectangle's exact \(\operatorname{TryPair}\) for every generator prime in \((B/2,B]\) and every decoded word, using the child modulus's actual integer parameters. Every order multiple and every proper gcd is checked. Under Dirichlet GRH, a positive fraction of the generator interval yields a separating order-valuation sign for any two distinct remaining prime factors, so the simultaneous certificates give a proper divisor. Recurse on the factors, keeping multiplicities. There are at most \(2b\) nodes including prime-power recognition, and the same universal estimate covers every node even when earlier splits depend on its decoded list. On a nonexceptional original input all of this costs \(\exp(O(H_b))\) bit operations. The code never evaluates the real score or determines whether a master is exceptional. [Prime-base splitting](cogentic-dense-exponents.md) · [Bit cost per call and finite rectangles](cogentic-finite-coins.md)

In parallel run the uniform deterministic complete \(\exp(O(V_b))\) factorer and return the first verified complete result. One may use either the [integer-grid](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) or [Farey-grid](cogentic-projective-selection.md) worst-case bound. This covers all exceptional masters and all bounded sizes. On an arbitrary set of \(J\) distinct \(b\)-bit inputs the sum of the \(r\)-th powers of complete running times is at most
\[
                J\exp(O_r(H_b))+\exp(O_r(V_b)).
\]
For the whole length \(J=2^{b-1}\), the second contribution to the normalized mean tends to zero. An interval \((X,2X]\) uses at most two bit lengths and has comparable size to \(2^b\). This accounting includes every exceptional input and the complete recursive verification.

The [finite replay](../experiments/cogentic-universal-score-sampler.py) builds 19 distinct scalar tests from affine squarefree smoothness windows and their pair products. It identifies 255 of 256 master strings that approximate all test expectations to error below \(1/16\). With the original input's master retained through each split, the resulting test lists certify 120 small composite factorizations, including powers, in 480 recursive calls. The finite model checks the simultaneous-sampling and recursion steps. Its arithmetic splitter uses gcds of products indexed by the tested local predicates.
