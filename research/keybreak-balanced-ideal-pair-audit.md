# Ideal pair estimates for a balanced top strip

Consider the top search with its original rational smoothness predicate, and an algebraic predicate restricted to balanced products in \([X_*,2X_*]\). There are two separate requirements: rational point geometry at the narrower denominator scale and upper counts for balanced algebraic values in the supported progressions of the ideal pair law. The rational geometry survives at polynomial loss. The prime-bin construction supplies the two bounds needed on unit progression phases.

## Denominator restriction in the rational argument

Restrict \(b\) to primes in \((2V/3,(2V/3)(1+1/d)]\); this is contained in the original range for large \(d\). In the notation of G.3, with \(x=MV/8\), the number of denominators is \(\asymp V/(d\log V)\). Partition the actual narrower interval into \(K\) comparable cells. The prime count in each is comparable to \(V/(dK\log V)\) under RH, since \(dK\log^2 V=o(\sqrt V)\); subtracting the bounds for the two prime-counting prefixes suffices. The numerator condition and its cell progression error (G.12) or (G.17) are unchanged. The mean count for root \(\ell\) now has scale \(V^2\rho/(d\ell\log V)\). Multiplicity one in the product convolution is unchanged and its energy \(E_A\) is divided by order \(d\). The large-sieve bound uses the same upper support length \(O(MV^2)\), and is linear in \(E_A\). Dividing the variance by the new squared mean therefore loses a factor \(O(d)\), both for whole populations and for each relative cell threshold. The same calculation for (G.18) loses \(O(d)\).

For the determinant count use \(a=(rb-u)/Q\), so \(\det((a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2))=(u_2b_1-u_1b_2)/Q\). With the first point fixed, the strip \(|\det|\ll V^2/K\) has \(u_2\)-width \(O(x/K)\), and its central line has slope \(u_1/b_1=O(M)\). Across a denominator cell of width \(O(V/(dK))\), its vertical displacement is \(O(x/(dK))\). It therefore meets only \(O(1)\) numerator cells in each denominator cell and \(O(K)\) pairs of cells in all. The relative thin-pair bound \(O(1/K)\) is preserved when normalized by the new mean. Polynomial losses leave a fixed negative exponent in the rational exception estimates.

For these denominators \(b^{d-1}\) varies by at most \(e\). The original slope range gives \(\|v(a,b)\|\asymp b^{d-1}\). Within a rational band the original numerator constraint makes \(|G(a,b)|\) comparable throughout the search, including after division by a common prescribed child. Thus the marginal Gaussian widths are uniformly comparable. This permits a single reference strip at a comparable size within the band; it does not imply that balanced counts there are uniform in residues. ## Short intervals and the separate moduli

Use the [prime-bin construction](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md#a-disjoint-union-with-ordinary-leading-density) at \(X\asymp T_r\), where \(T_r\) is the ordinary marginal Gaussian scale, with a common \(X\) inside each top band. All products of every pattern lie in \([X,\sqrt2X]\). The pattern masses add exactly to the reference mass \(S\) because bins are disjoint and the products recover their unique pattern. Its density \(\rho=S/X\) has the ordinary leading smoothness exponent, in particular \(\rho\ge e^{-O(H)}\) with \(H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}=o(\log V)\) at each fixed top knot.

For the \(q\)-divided test keep only patterns occupying the unique bin of \(q\), and remove that bin. Their residual patterns and products are still disjoint. The entire residual support lies in \([X',\sqrt2X']\), \(X'=X/q\). If \(S'\) is its mass, each retained pattern loses the bin population \(n_h\gg q/(k\log y)\), hence
\[
S'\le S/n_h,\qquad S'/X'\ll k\log y\,\rho .
\]
This needs no lower bound for the individual child. An absent bin or empty residual predicate contributes zero. Without division set \(X'=X\), \(S'=S\).

For an interval of length \(L\) freeze all but one remaining prime of any pattern. In a bin of scale \(P\) that prime is constrained to an interval of length \(O(PL/X')\), with all frozen products comparable to \(X'/P\). RH prime counting bounds its options by \(O(PL/(X'\log P)+\sqrt P\log^C P)\). The complete bin contains \(\gg P/(k\log P)\) primes. For \(L/X'\ge V^{-\zeta-o(1)}\) and fixed \(\zeta<c/2\), where every prime is \(V^{c-o(1)}\) or larger, the square-root error is absorbed by \(L/X'\) after normalization. Summing over patterns gives the principal bound \(O(D^C (L/X') S')\). The full mass handles \(L\ge X'\), and deleting primes dividing a modulus can only decrease the principal count. Reflection costs a constant.

In a unit progression modulo \(m=V^{O(1)}\), use the [localized prime-product character estimate](keybreak-prime-product-grid-variance.md#character-bounds-with-value-cutoffs) for nonprincipal characters. The bound holds on prefixes and hence on intervals by subtraction. After division the lower bin endpoints have product comparable to \(X'\) and the same proof uses \(k-1\asymp d\) free slots. Orthogonality bounds the progression count by
\[
O\left(D^C\frac{LS'}{X'\phi(m)}+ S'(X')^{-1/2} V^{o(1)}\right).
\]
The normalized error relative to \(S'/X'\) is \(O((mX'/L)(X')^{-1/2} V^{o(1)})=o(1)\), since \(\log X'\asymp d\log V\). Since \(m/\phi(m)\) is polylogarithmic, the progression average is \(O(D^C S'/X')\). The principal estimate and character error both use the full reference strip mass, with no extra loss for the number of bin patterns.

## One-point density and ideal Gaussian quadrature

For an ordinary mark the one-point progression has step \(2|G(P)|\) and unit residue on the continuing eligible branch. Removing the primes dividing the step costs \(o(S)\): only \(O(1)\) such primes can occur in the large prime bins, and each removes a fraction at most \(1/n_h\) in any pattern. Orthogonality and the whole-strip nonprincipal bound give \((1+o(1))S/\phi(2|G(P)|)\) points on this progression. The Gaussian center is \(o(T_r)\) by the original digit bound. Throughout the strip \(X\asymp T_r\) the normalized Gaussian weight per point is comparable to \(2|G(P)|/T_r\). The one-point mean is therefore bounded below by a constant times \(\rho\) and above by \(D^C\rho\), with no leading density loss. The actual law inherits this estimate when the original one-coordinate kernel smoothing applies.

For the ideal pair restrict to \(\vartheta\le V^\zeta\) as above. Retain Mahalanobis radii \(e^{-AH}\le R\le n\) for a sufficiently large fixed \(A\). The original ellipse lattice bounds and Gaussian mixture give omitted mass \(O(e^{-2AH})+e^{-\Omega(n^2)}\), with negligible lattice error. This is negligible relative to the product of ordinary references, including when bounding divided tests from above. In coordinates scaled by \(T_1,T_2\), cover an ellipse by squares of side \(\asymp R/\vartheta\). The perimeter bound in G.9 gives covering area \(O(R^2/\vartheta)\), comparable to the ellipse itself. The raw side lengths are \(\asymp RT_r/\vartheta\); dividing \(T_r,X\) by \(q\) leaves the ratio of side to target at least \(V^{-\zeta-o(1)}\). These sides dominate the separate periods by (G.23).

Within each covering box the support of a phase pair is a Cartesian product of progressions. The unit progression estimate gives an average \(O(D^C\rho_1\rho_2)\) there, also after division by one prescribed prime. The count of grid points in the whole covering is at most a constant times the ellipse count by (G.24) and the separate phase count. Positivity then bounds the retained ellipse averages and their Gaussian mixture by the same expression, after the nonunit contribution below. This proves the ideal pair estimate; an actual two-coordinate narrow-law comparison for this new predicate is a separate step. [Ideal Gaussian estimates, G.4–G.9](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=109)

## Prime factors of the period

For a supported phase, (G.22) gives unit values at primes dividing the \(G_r\) parts of the periods, apart from the original parity conditions. Any additional balanced prime factor of a period comes from the determinant part. If allowed shape primes are at least \(V^c\) and the period is \(V^{O(1)}\), there are \(O(1)\) possible exceptional primes for each fixed pair. The ideal Gaussian marginal modulo such a prime \(p\) can be bounded without any smoothness test. The restricted affine support projects onto the one-coordinate progression with step \(2|G_r|\) in the divided variables. When \(p\) is coprime to that step, the counting error for one residue in an ellipse grid is \(O(p\,m_r/L_r)\) relatively if one keeps the phase fixed and the prime is coprime to \(m_r\). For the determinant primes at issue it is simpler to count in the full ellipse: the uniform phase fraction with \(p\mid z_r\) is \(1/p\) by (G.22), and each such phase has at most \(O(1/k)\) of the ellipse points, by the same grid covering and lower count (G.24). This gives \(O(1/p)\) after mixing over retained radii. At primes dividing the step, unit support already excludes the event. Therefore the nonunit part contributes \(O(V^{-c})\) without independence, negligible against \(c_1c_2\) when both reference densities are \(V^{-o(1)}\). In divided values the prescribed child has been removed from the squarefree product. This argument concerns ideal ellipses and does not assume a pointwise comparison with the actual narrower law.

Thus the denominator thinning is compatible with G.3, and the prime-bin prefix estimate gives the ideal pair bound at retained angles. The conditional upper bound uses the ordinary strip references and requires no positive density for an individual prescribed child. [KeyBreak, G.5 and G.9](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=110)
