# Ideal pair estimates for a balanced top strip

Consider the top search with its original rational smoothness predicate, and an algebraic predicate restricted to balanced products in \([X_*,2X_*]\). There are two separate requirements: rational point geometry at the narrower denominator scale and upper counts for balanced algebraic values in the supported progressions of the ideal pair law. The rational geometry survives at polynomial loss. The modulus-one short-interval estimate alone does not prove the requisite progression estimate.

## Denominator restriction in the rational argument

Restrict \(b\) to primes in \((2V/3,(2V/3)(1+1/d)]\); this is contained in the original range for large \(d\). In the notation of G.3, with \(x=MV/8\), the number of denominators is \(\asymp V/(d\log V)\). Partition the actual narrower interval into \(K\) comparable cells. The prime count in each is comparable to \(V/(dK\log V)\) under RH, since \(dK\log^2 V=o(\sqrt V)\); subtracting the bounds for the two prime-counting prefixes suffices. The numerator condition and its cell progression error (G.12) or (G.17) are unchanged. The mean count for root \(\ell\) now has scale \(V^2\rho/(d\ell\log V)\). Multiplicity one in the product convolution is unchanged and its energy \(E_A\) is divided by order \(d\). The large-sieve bound uses the same upper support length \(O(MV^2)\), and is linear in \(E_A\). Dividing the variance by the new squared mean therefore loses a factor \(O(d)\), both for whole populations and for each relative cell threshold. The same calculation for (G.18) loses \(O(d)\).

For the determinant count use \(a=(rb-u)/Q\), so \(\det((a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2))=(u_2b_1-u_1b_2)/Q\). With the first point fixed, the strip \(|\det|\ll V^2/K\) has \(u_2\)-width \(O(x/K)\), and its central line has slope \(u_1/b_1=O(M)\). Across a denominator cell of width \(O(V/(dK))\), its vertical displacement is \(O(x/(dK))\). It therefore meets only \(O(1)\) numerator cells in each denominator cell and \(O(K)\) pairs of cells in all. The relative thin-pair bound \(O(1/K)\) is preserved when normalized by the new mean. Polynomial losses leave a fixed negative exponent in the rational exception estimates.

For these denominators \(b^{d-1}\) varies by at most \(e\). The original slope range gives \(\|v(a,b)\|\asymp b^{d-1}\). Within a rational band the original numerator constraint makes \(|G(a,b)|\) comparable throughout the search, including after division by a common prescribed child. Thus the marginal Gaussian widths are uniformly comparable. This permits a single reference strip at a comparable size within the band; it does not imply that balanced counts there are uniform in residues. [KeyBreak, G.3 and G.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=107)

## Short intervals and the separate moduli

At a retained pair the ideal grid intervals have lengths \(L_r\asymp R T_r/\vartheta\), where \(T_r\) is the marginal parameter and \(\vartheta=b_1b_2/|\Delta|\). In an interval of length \(L\) at size \(X_*\), hold all but one prime of the product fixed. In its dyad of prime size \(P\), the free variable has interval length \(O(P L/X_*)\). RH prime counting at modulus one then gives the expected proportional upper bound provided \(P L/X_*\gg\sqrt P\,\log^C P\). For example radii \(R\ge e^{-A H}\), with fixed \(A\), satisfy this when \(P\ge V^{c}\), \(\vartheta\le V^\zeta\) and \(\zeta<c/2\): here \(H=o(\log V)\) at a fixed top band. If the reference density is \(e^{-O(H)}\), choosing \(A\) sufficiently large makes the total smaller-radius mass negligible relative to the product of two reference densities. At the original much smaller minimum radius \(y^{-C_R}\), the same single-prime estimate need not be effective.

That estimate compares an interval count to the number of unrestricted ordered completions of the remaining slots. A comparison to the *actual mass in the reference strip* requires a further principal-term bound for its product distribution in log coordinates. The whole-index incidence estimate controls the entire product support \([2^{ke-S-k},2^{ke-S}]\); it gives no such comparison for every substrip of ratio two. [Whole-strip labelled incidence](keybreak-balanced-top-incidence.md)

For a unit class modulo a separate period \(m_r=2|G_r||\Delta|/q\), the free prime would additionally be fixed modulo \(m_r\) if the other primes are units. Even under Dirichlet GRH its main term is only of order \(PL/(\phi(m_r)X_*\log P)\), against a square-root prime error. No inequality of the requisite strength follows when \(m_r\) can exceed \(P\). In that range the progression can fix a unique possible integer prime, so pointwise comparison with the density \(1/\phi(m_r)\) is not a valid single-prime argument.

A distinct approach to the unit part is to use a smooth multiplicative majorant on the short interval and sum nonprincipal characters across all the free slots. For fixed dyad indices, the Mellin transform separates the product of prime sums. Using partial summation on the GRH prime-prefix error costs a factor \(O(1+|t|)\) on the vertical transform variable \(t\); hence this direct bound only gives a power saving per free prime on frequencies \( |t|\le V^\xi\) for \(\xi<c/2\). The useful short intervals have \(X_*/L\le V^{\zeta+o(1)}\). An implementation of that proof must bound the transform tail, remove or upper-bound repeated-prime tuples, compare the principal term with the actual strip density, and handle any sieving restrictions on the individual prime sets. The latter matters because arbitrary dyad subsets need not obey the GRH character-prefix bound for complete dyads. Division by one prescribed child leaves \(k-1\) free slots, so it changes no fixed-power saving per slot in this proposed upper estimate. No matching lower estimate for each conditioned phase is needed in G.9.

## Prime factors of the period

For a supported phase, (G.22) gives unit values at primes dividing the \(G_r\) parts of the periods, apart from the original parity conditions. Any additional balanced prime factor of a period comes from the determinant part. If allowed shape primes are at least \(V^c\) and the period is \(V^{O(1)}\), there are \(O(1)\) possible exceptional primes for each fixed pair. The ideal Gaussian marginal modulo such a prime \(p\) can be bounded without any smoothness test. The restricted affine support projects onto the one-coordinate progression with step \(2|G_r|\) in the divided variables. When \(p\) is coprime to that step, the counting error for one residue in an ellipse grid is \(O(p\,m_r/L_r)\) relatively if one keeps the phase fixed and the prime is coprime to \(m_r\). For the determinant primes at issue it is simpler to count in the full ellipse: the uniform phase fraction with \(p\mid z_r\) is \(1/p\) by (G.22), and each such phase has at most \(O(1/k)\) of the ellipse points, by the same grid covering and lower count (G.24). This gives \(O(1/p)\) after mixing over retained radii. At primes dividing the step, unit support already excludes the event. Therefore the nonunit part contributes \(O(V^{-c})\) without independence, negligible against \(c_1c_2\) when both reference densities are \(V^{-o(1)}\). In divided values the prescribed child has been removed from the squarefree product. This argument concerns ideal ellipses and does not assume a pointwise comparison with the actual narrower law.

Thus the denominator thinning is compatible with the G.3 error ledger. The missing input for G.9 is a uniform balanced-strip upper estimate on unit progression phases, relative to that strip's own density, including the divided residual tests. A positive whole-strip incidence estimate and modulus-one free-prime counting do not replace this input. [KeyBreak, G.5 and G.9](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=110)
