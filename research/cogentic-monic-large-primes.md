# Large-prime cycles and the full ideal-parity kernel on a bounded monic grid

For the growing-degree monic pair, deterministic bounded trial division and a binary kernel computation recover every joint ideal-parity dependency among distinct primitive marks of a bounded grid that pass the discriminant and unit tests. A prime above twice the squared grid height can occur at odd valuation in a given ideal row of only one mark, so that mark has zero coefficient in every dependency. This permits an exact integer square test on each residual cofactor after trial division. For the usual pool restricted to at most two odd large-prime rows, a graph gives the cancellation basis directly. On the fixed seven-input holdout, admitting even large-prime valuations produces a verified split of 247. ## Ideal coordinates

Let $P_i$ be one of the two monic irreducible polynomials, $\alpha_i$ its root, and $D$ the product of the two polynomial discriminants. Keep distinct primitive marks $(a,b)$ with $|a|\le A$, $1\le b\le A$, $\gcd(a-bm,N)=1$, and both homogeneous norms nonzero and coprime to $D$. For each $p$ dividing $\operatorname{Norm}(a-b\alpha_i)$, primitivity and monicity give $p\nmid b$, since otherwise the homogeneous norm is $a^{\deg P_i}\not\equiv0\pmod p$. The root $r=ab^{-1}\bmod p$ of $P_i$ is simple. Because $p\nmid D$, the power order agrees locally with the maximal order, and the prime ideals above $p$ correspond to the distinct irreducible factors of $P_i$ modulo $p$. An ideal above $p$ contains $a-b\alpha_i$ only when its residue root is $r$, so exactly one prime ideal occurs and has residue degree one. Its valuation is the rational norm valuation $v_p(\operatorname{Norm}(a-b\alpha_i))$. The binary matrix for the two fields thus has a separate row $(i,p,r)$ for each ideal. Both the field and the root matter when matching rows. [Direct ideal replay for the monic pair](cogentic-monic-pair.md#finite-cubic-and-quartic-relations)

## The two-large-prime graph

Choose a factor-base cutoff $B$ and retain relations for which at most two distinct rows with $p>B$ have odd valuation, counted across both fields. A bounded collection rule can require that both norms factor completely into primes at most $C$ for some $C\ge B$. Sieve primes through $C$, divide out their powers, and reject any residual above one after all divisions. This accepts a complete factorization exactly by bounded operations and has no composite-cofactor oracle. The large-prime graph has one vertex per odd out-of-base ideal row and an additional sentinel. A column with two such rows gives an edge between those vertices. With one row, connect it to the sentinel. With zero rows, use a loop at the sentinel, including columns with only even out-of-base valuations. Parallel edges are kept.

A subset of columns cancels all out-of-base parity if and only if its edges have even degree at every vertex. The ordinary degrees record exactly the required row sums. The sentinel degree must then also be even, since the sum of the degrees is twice the number of edges. For each edge outside a spanning forest, take that edge and the unique forest path between its endpoints. A loop has an empty path. Each resulting vector is independent because it contains a distinct nonforest edge. Subtract these vectors from any even-degree subgraph to remove its nonforest edges. An even-degree subgraph of a forest is empty by leaf elimination, proving the vectors form a basis of all out-of-base cancellations. Replace each basis vector by the sum of its in-base columns and compute the kernel of that smaller matrix. Expanding it back through the basis gives every simultaneous ideal-parity dependency in the retained pool.

## Removing the factorization bound for parity

If two distinct marks have the same ideal row $(i,p,r)$, then $p\mid ab'-a'b$. This determinant is nonzero because the marks are primitive and both denominators are positive. Its absolute value is at most $2A^2$. Therefore a prime $p>2A^2$ in any field gives a row that occurs in just one column, regardless of how many other residue roots the polynomial has modulo $p$. If its exponent is odd, the row forces the column out of every parity dependency. In particular, when $B\ge2A^2$, all odd out-of-base rows are private and their graph edges can never contribute to a cycle.

Take $Y=\max(B,2A^2)$ and divide both norms by all prime powers at primes up to $Y$. If either remaining absolute residual is nonsquare, it has an odd valuation at a prime $p>Y$. This is an unusable column by the private-row argument. Otherwise both residuals are squares. Their primes give only even ideal valuations because each rational norm valuation equals the single degree-one ideal valuation for its field and mark. The residuals can be discarded from the parity computation without factoring them. The remaining columns are known exactly in all ideal-parity rows. Ordinary binary elimination recovers the full joint ideal-parity kernel of the original acceptable grid, by reinserting zeros at the positions of the rejected columns. The graph gives the kernel of the restricted pool if the two-row condition is also imposed.

For $W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$ and $A,B,C\le\exp(O(W))$, the norm evaluations have polynomial bit length in $\log N$, and $Y\le\exp(O(W))$. Discriminant computation, bounded sieving, trial division, exact integer square roots, graph construction, and binary elimination take polynomial work in the grid size, cutoffs, and bit lengths. This is $\exp(O(W))$ including the expanded parity basis. The bound is for the parity stage. An even ideal factorization alone leaves field square classes, including units, to be tested. For any explicit product of at most $O(A^2)$ marks, exact field-square testing and root recovery have the same bit bound. If the genuine simultaneous square kernel has a known basis, the sign-quotient calculation can test its basis images. Applying exact square tests only to an ideal-parity basis does not compute that genuine kernel. For a verified split, require square roots of products with the same sign in both fields, valid modular evaluations, and a proper difference gcd. For the common negative sign the two exact roots of the signed mark products are represented by $-2X^2-9X-3$ modulo $F$ and $-2X^3+6X^2-7X-1$ modulo $G$. The remaining six inputs have no simultaneous ideal-parity dependency at the fixed geometry.
