# Deterministic factoring at the two-thirds scale from the Cogentic extractor

Write $L=\log N$, $z=\log L$, $D=(L/z)^{1/3}$, $H=Dz$ and $V=DH=L^{2/3}z^{1/3}$. Assume the algebraic extractor of Cogentic Lemmas 7.3–7.5 and its parameter-independent Gaussian conditional-moment identities. The [uniform squarefree smooth-window estimate](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) gives a deterministic integer factorization algorithm using $\exp(O(V))$ bit operations on every input. The model is the elementary bit model of the source. This is a manuscript-level modification of its explicit $O(\exp(3L^{3/4}))$ bound. The parameters, the bent principal contour and this corollary are outside the scope of its reported Lean formalization. [Cogentic, Theorems 1.1–1.3 and §§7–11](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf) · [Pinned README](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34)

The [fixed-schedule verification report](cogentic-worst-case-verification.md) records the independent contour calculations, rank checks, exponent guard, Sage examples and the scope of the local Lean checks.

## Fixed parameters

Use the objects of Cogentic §8.2 with the following limiting natural logarithms. The constants provide ample slack and are not optimized.
$$
\begin{array}{c|cccccc}
 &d=z_0&\log B&\log A&\log K_1&\log(1/w)&\log U\\\hline
\text{size}&(1+o(1))D&(1000+o(1))H&(10000+o(1))H&(100+o(1))H&(3+o(1))H&(100000+o(1))V.
\end{array}
$$
Take $m=\lceil N^{1/d}\rceil$, $P_0=\prod_{p\le d}p$, two degree-$d$ polynomials per generator prime $B/2<\ell\le B$, Gaussian parameter scales $S_1=U,S_0=UA$, and the paper's real-root grid, perturbation boxes and integer leading coefficient $c_d$. The conditions $4dA\le m$, $U\ge4B^4$, $\log P_0\sim d$, $\log m=(1+o(1))V$ hold. Also $d\log A=(10000+o(1))V<\log U$. The rule making $c_dA^d$ the first admissible power-of-three multiple above $6K_1BP_0 mL_0^2 UA$ therefore gives
$$
 \log X_{\rm sc}=\log(c_dA^d)=(100001+o(1))V,\qquad
 u=\frac{\log X_{\rm sc}}{\log B}=(100.001+o(1))D .
$$
It gives $c_d\ge4P_0B$ and $\log X_{\rm sc}=o(L)$. Original Lemma 8.1 applies verbatim. The finite reduction and integer guards at the end of this note specify the algorithm for small $N$ too.

Here and below all constants inside $o(1)$ tend to zero for this single fixed schedule. For an explicit integer realization, compute rational approximations to $L,z$ to absolute error $O(1)$ using their convergent binary-scaled logarithm series. Use, for example, $z^*=\log\lfloor L\rfloor+O(1)$ and $L^*=L+O(1)$, both positive for sufficiently large $N$. Approximate $D,H,V$ by rationals of relative error $o(1)$ via integer cube roots with binary scaling, then set $d=\lfloor D^*\rfloor$ and parameter magnitudes to powers of 2 with exponents rounded from the target logarithms divided by an $o(1)$-accurate rational approximation to $\log 2$. All exponents have $O(\log L)$ bits and all precision computations take $\mathrm{poly}(L)$ elementary operations. The induced relative error in $\log B,\log A,\log K_1,\log(1/w),\log U$ tends to zero.

## The grid, its sieve, and its real roots

The following checks replace the decimal power comparisons in Lemma 8.2.

* The correction multipliers $k_0,k'_0<2c_d$ are coprime to $P_0\ell$. Each prime dividing them exceeds $d$, and their number is $O(V/\log d)=L^{2/3+o(1)}$. Bounding the sum of their reciprocals by the first this many primes above $d$ gives $\sum_{p\mid k_0k'_0}1/p\le\log 2+o(1)$. In particular the product of $(1-1/p)$ is at least $1/4$.
* The weighted progression sieve of Lemma 8.2(2) holds with $E_{\rm Br}=e^{-4z^2}$, for every $hM_q\le B^4$ in its statement. In a short interval $I'$ used for Lipschitz approximation we have $|I'|\ge Aw E_{\rm Br}L_0^{-5}$, hence $|I'|/(hM_q)\ge e^{(5990+o(1))H}$. Let $Z_{\rm Br}=e^{100z^2}$, $r_0=\lceil z^2\rceil$ and use inclusion–exclusion through $2r_0$ on the primes $p\le Z_{\rm Br}$ dividing $k_0k'_0$ and coprime to the modulus $M=hM_q$. Each selected product imposes one class modulo $M$ times that product. Each class has counting error at most one. The aggregate relative counting error is $O(M\exp(O(z^3))/|I'|)$ since there are at most $(1+\omega(k_0k'_0))^{2r_0+1}$ selected products. The exponential-series tail is $O((2e/(2r_0))^{2r_0})$. The larger prime divisors cost relative $O(\omega(k_0k'_0)/Z_{\rm Br}+M\omega(k_0k'_0)/|I'|)$. All these are $o(E_{\rm Br})$. Removing primes already in $h$ gives exactly the source's $\psi_0(h)$ factor. Subdivision and the Lipschitz bound introduce $O(E_{\rm Br})$ relative to the prescribed sup norm. In particular each grid interval contains at least $Aw/5$ surviving integers.
* On $|t|\le1$, the coefficient rounding errors and all perturbations change $X_{\rm sc}^{-1} f_i(At)$ from the target monic polynomial by at most $K_1^{-1}$. Indeed $d A^{d-1}=o(U)$ and the definition of $X_{\rm sc}$ absorbs the perturbation bound $5P_0 B m L_0^2 UA$. The source tests root signs within $K_1^{-0.95}$ of the targets. The lower bound there is $K_1^{-0.95}(8e)^{-d}/2>2K_1^{-1}$. On the evaluation grid its bound is $|P_i^*(a/A)|\ge w(8d)^{-d}\ge\exp(-(3+1/3+o(1))H)$. Thus the logarithm of every target magnitude differs from $\log X_{\rm sc}$ by at most $4H$, and halving or omitting up to three prime factors changes it by at most another $3\log B+O(1)$.
* The preceding estimates retain the source's width and displacement constants. The one-point ratio $\mu_i/S_{1,i}^*$ lies between $K_1^{0.90}$ and $K_1^{1.10}$, and the two-point ratio lies between $K_1^{0.85}$ and $K_1^{1.65}$ for $\Delta=|a'-a|>AK_1^{-1/2}$. For the first ratio the extra grid factor costs at most $4H$, while $0.1\log K_1=(10+o(1))H$. For the second ratio the additional factor is $O(A/\Delta)$. The actual one-point and conditional means deviate relatively by at most $K_1^{-0.95}$ and $K_1^{-0.94}$, respectively, by $L_0^{O(1)}K_1^{-1}e^{4H}$ and the Gaussian conditioning identity. The true logarithmic-root coordinates have error at most $K_1^{-0.90}$ since $w^{-1}=\exp((3+o(1))H)$. All coefficient heights, point separations, relation sparsities and guards needed in Lemma 7.5 follow from $\log X_{\rm sc}=o(L)$, $5u<L_0$ and $w/2>2^{-L_0}$. ## All required progression estimates

Delete the medium- and high-conductor exceptional sets from the notation. Every $\ell\in(B/2,B]$ is allowed in the analysis and $F_{\rm med}$ is empty. A two-point pair is called clean here when $\Delta>AK_1^{-1/2}$ and $p^2\nmid\Delta$ for $p\mid Q_{\rm pair}\ell$. Every one-point input is clean. We use the paper's independent one- and two-coordinate Gaussian conditioning, its coprime inversions, and its factors $\Phi_i(a;R_i)$ and $\gamma(a,a')$. The following comparisons justify their uniform relative errors.

For the actual one- and two-point progressions in Lemma 9.5, including its auxiliary classes used to remove a prime dividing $\Delta$ or $Q_{\rm free}$, one has $\log q\le (2+o(1))V$. Indeed each progression modulus is bounded by $P_0 m A B^{O(1)}$ up to absolute constants or by the smaller product of one-point terms. The mean after the stated divisions has $\log Y=(100001+o(1))V$, including a possible extra divisor $G\le AB^3$, and $\log R=O(H)$ throughout the clean regime. Thus the absolute errors on the right of (2) in the [window proof](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md) are $\ll\exp(-49000V)$, uniformly. Meanwhile
$$
  \lambda=(1/3+o(1))z,\qquad
  I_{\rm pure}^{-1}\le\exp((34+o(1))H).
$$
Here $u_Y=(100.001+o(1))D$ and the bound follows from $\log(I_{\rm pure}^{-1})\le u_Y\lambda+O(\log L)$. The relative window error is therefore $O(e^{-50 z^2})$ for large $L$, since $u/\log^2(2u)\gg z^2$. The same error applies to replacing the true means by their nominal values, by their relative displacement and $|dI_{\rm pure}/d\log Y|\ll I_{\rm pure}$. In particular $\log R\asymp H$ is much larger than $u$; integration on the original fixed saddle with an $L^1$ cost of $R$ would lose this estimate.

The principal saddle formulas of Lemma 9.2(2) extend with the same $O(u^{-1})$ and $O(u^{-2})$ errors. In particular $|\log (\mu_i(a)/X_{\rm sc})|\le4H=O(\log B)$, which gives $|\alpha_{\mu_i(a)}-\alpha_{X_{\rm sc}}|\log B=O(u^{-1})$ from $\varphi''\asymp u(\log B)^2$. The proof of the two-prime covariance uses the second finite difference of the saddle logarithm, together with the $O(u^{-2})$ Edgeworth remainder. These are derivative estimates for the same prime band and deletions. No change in the algebraic covariance model is needed.

The rough bounds for every pair, including unclean ones, follow from the parameter-free Lemmas 9.1 and 9.4. For either loss factor in Lemma 9.4(2)–(3),
$$
 (1-\alpha_Y)\log\frac{\mu^{\rm ex} B^{O(1)}}{U}
       \le (1+o(1))\frac {z}{3\log B}\log m
       = (1/3000+o(1))H.
$$
Here $\log X_{\rm sc}=\log U+\log m+O(H)$ and $(1-\alpha_Y)O(H)=O(z)$. Factors $\sqrt{\varphi''}$ and $L^{O(1)}$ are $e^{o(H)}$. Each normalized one-point bound costs at most $e^{0.01H}$, and a two-point bound costs at most $e^{0.02H}$ per field. For $G\mid\Delta Q_{\rm free}\ell$ in the proof of Lemma 9.5 we use its saddle ratio bound at $Y/G$, which cancels $G^{-(1-\alpha_Y)}$ up to $\exp(O(\log G/(u\log B)))$ and an absolute prefactor. The remaining application of Lemma 9.1(2) costs at most $e^{0.01H}$. There are at most $O(\log A/\log B)=O(1)$ primes above $\sqrt B$ dividing $\Delta$. Removing an extra prime from the class therefore costs at most $e^{0.04H} B^{-0.5\alpha_s}=o(e^{-50z^2})$. All Gaussian tail cuts and exclusions involving $\ell^{-1}$ satisfy the same error. These are the terms in the four local cases of Lemma 9.5(2). Its clean one-point and two-point formulas follow with common error $O(e^{-50z^2})$. ## Centering, the discarded rows, and the rank certificate

Use $E_{\rm rel}=e^{-7z^2}$ in Proposition 10.1. Its entrywise claim is $\mathbf E[|\widetilde A_{\alpha,\beta}|^2\mid E_{\alpha,\beta}]\le M_{\alpha,\beta}^2 E_{\rm rel}$ with the same excluded equal-prime off-diagonal entries. Here are the quantitative data for the proof.

1. In the cells with progression modulus $M_c\le B^3$ one has $|g_*(a)|\Pi_R(a)\ll M_cM_{\alpha,\beta}$ and normalized cell mass $O(M_c^{-1})$. Polynomial factors from the $O(d)$ cells cost $e^{o(H)}$. The diagonal $a=a'$ costs at most
   $$
   e^{0.05H}B^2(Aw)^{-1}\exp((68+o(1))H)M_{\alpha,\beta}^2
     \ll e^{-7000H}M_{\alpha,\beta}^2 .
   $$
   The inputs are the exact one-point conditional formula or its rough bound, $|g_*|\ll B^2 M_{\alpha,\beta}$ and the preceding bounds on the two reciprocal densities. On $0<\Delta\le AK_1^{-1/2}$, conditional class counting introduces relative $O(w^{-1}K_1^{-1/2}+B^3/(Aw))$, at most $e^{-(47+o(1))H}$, before the rough factor $e^{0.04H}$. If $p^2\mid\Delta$ for a prime $p\mid Q_{\rm pair}\ell$, the cell modulus $M_{c'}$ is squarefree, so $\operatorname{lcm}(M_{c'},p^2)\ge pM_{c'}$. This costs $O(1/p+B^3/(Aw))$ prior to the same rough factor, and $p>\sqrt B$. With GRH the two excluded-conductor contributions in the source are empty. The factor $\gamma(a,a')^2$ when restoring excluded pairs into the main sum is $L^{O(1)}$ by the product estimate over small primes.
2. In the clean main term, expand $\gamma^2$ into its positive divisor sum as on p. 53. The long-divisor tail costs $O(B^{-0.49})$ with polynomial factors; class counting for its shortest prefix $h_0\in(\sqrt B,B]$ is valid since $M_c h_0\le B^4\ll Aw$. For $h\le\sqrt B$ and fixed coprime class modulo $h$, the weighted progression sieve above gives the centered sum $J(h,r_h)=O(M_{\alpha,\beta}E_{\rm Br}/\psi_0(h))$ after the $O(dB^{-0.49})$ special-generator correction. The cancellation uses the source's exact main covariance $\widetilde U^{\rm ex}$ prior to its saddle expansion. The remaining divisor sum is bounded absolutely because $\eta(h)/\psi_0(h)$ has local factors $O(p^{-2})$. Consequently this contribution is $O(M_{\alpha,\beta}^2 E_{\rm Br}^2)$, which is $o(M_{\alpha,\beta}^2E_{\rm rel})$. The one-point mean of the total normalized mass is $1+O(e^{-50 z^2})$; its variance is $O(\sum_{p>d}p^{-2}+e^{-7z^2})=o(1)$. These are precisely the centered moment inputs.
3. For clarity, introduce $T_0= (7/0.15)z^2$ in the real-valued thresholds of the original peeling proof, replacing the quantity printed there as $L^{0.06}L_2$. Thus $\tau_{\rm diag}=e^{-0.0375T_0}$, $\delta_{\rm peel}=e^{-0.01875T_0}$, and $\mu_*=e^{-0.07T_0}$. The Markov inequalities for $F_A^2,D_A,F_\ell^2$ continue to hold at thresholds $e^{-0.148T_0}$ (and $\ell^{-\alpha_s}e^{-0.148T_0}$ where applicable), since their bounds are $\mathrm{poly}(L)e^{-0.15T_0}$ and $z^2\gg\log L$. Squarefree shared- and multiple-root errors remain $O(B^{-0.49})$. All terms involving $F_{\rm med}$ are zero. The deterministic peeling inequalities then hold with the unchanged coefficients: $\delta_{\rm peel}^{-2}F_A^2\le e^{-0.1105T_0}$, the survivor mass defect stays $\le\mu_*$, and the perturbation of the surviving Gram matrix is $\le e^{-0.015T_0}$. In each step every free polynomial factor is absorbed because $T_0/\log L\to\infty$. The probability of the required event tends to one for each $\ell$.
4. Finally $\|w_i\|^2\asymp u(\log B)^2$, and eliminating the two exact norm relations preserves norms up to $L^{O(1)}$. More explicitly the source's transformed coordinates obey $\|v'_{\rm arch,i}\|^2=\|v_{U,i}\|^2+dt_i^2$. Thus $|t_i|\le\|v'_{\rm arch,i}\|/\sqrt d$ and the inverse shifts of $v_0$ and $v_{F_i}$ have norm bounded by $O(1+\log X_{\rm sc}+\|w_i\|)$ times the transformed norm. On the perpendicular factor-base subspace the $O(u^{-1})$ correction to the covariance is supported solely on the factor-base block, where the positive diagonal term absorbs it. For the real-root block the diagonal of $M_{\rm avg}$ has absolute value $(3+o(1))H$. Each off-diagonal entry has absolute value at most $\log(8d)+O(1)$ by the $1/(4d)$ grid spacing, the interval widths $w$, and the baseline at $3/4$. Hence the off-diagonal operator norm is bounded by $2d(\log(8d)+O(1))\le(2/3+o(1))H$. The baseline vector has squared norm $O(d)$. The same interval Jensen estimate as on p. 55 now gives $\sum_a\pi_0(a) g_y(a)^2\gg d^{-1}(|y_0|^2+\|y_{\rm arch}\|^2)$, since $H^2/d\gg d$. The positive covariance is at least $L^{-O(1)}$ on every unit vector after undoing the normalization; root errors and the Gram perturbation are smaller. Strict positivity is all Lemma 7.4(3) requires. ## Deterministic search and complete factorization

After the even and perfect-power cases, one can retain the original index-saturation lemma. Take $K_{\rm idx}\ge B\exp((L/\log B)\log(6L))$ via the upward binary rounding in §8.2 with the new integer $\log_2 B$. Thus $\log K_{\rm idx}=(1/1000+o(1))V$. Trial divide to this cutoff and check $\gcd(N,a-m)$ for $a\le A$. Use the paper's integer guards for TryPair (its $e_{\rm rt}$ comes from the actual $K_1$). For all sufficiently large surviving $N$ the guards hold, the preceding Gram certificate exists for every $\ell$, and the deterministic search over four integer perturbation coordinates finds an even $E_\ell$ for which $\ell^{E_\ell}\equiv1\pmod N$. Each candidate output is tested by modular exponentiation. The exhaustive search has $O(BL_0^8U^4A^2)$ calls, each of cost $\mathrm{poly}(A,B,d,64d^3(2dB+1)L_0)$, by Lemma 7.5.

When there are at least $B/(6\log B)$ verified generators, multiply their exponent lcm by $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,K_{\rm idx})$ as in the source. For the dyadic $B$, take $\beta=\log_2 B$ exactly and accept when $3\beta|P_{\rm ok}|\ge B$. This implies $|P_{\rm ok}|\ge B/(6\log B)$ since $3/\log2<6$, and all sufficiently large dyadic prime intervals contain enough targets. For input bit length $b$, an integer cutoff is $K_{\rm idx}=B\,2^{\lceil b\lceil\log_2(6b)\rceil/\beta\rceil}$. [Extraction and guard audit](cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.md) The elementary product-count proof in Lemma 11.1 yields an even multiple of every $p-1$ for $p\mid N$. Its bit length is bounded by $O(B\cdot64d^3(2dB+1)L_0+K_{\rm idx})=\exp((1/1000+o(1))V)$. The source's fixed $\exp(L^{0.65})$ output cap must be removed or enlarged, for example to $\exp(0.01 V)$ asymptotically. Its subexponential order-splitting theorem then costs $\exp((1/1000+o(1))V)$ since $\sqrt L\log L=o(V)$. Alternatively one can use the Dirichlet-RH bounded-base order-splitting theorem or omit the index lcm by a separate generator argument. Under failed guards, a verified proper divisor can always be obtained by finite trial division. Only boundedly many sizes require this fallback.

The $U^4$ search gives $\log T(N)\le (400000+o(1))V$. Computing the index lcm by serial trial divisions and schoolbook arithmetic costs $O(K_{\rm idx}^4\mathrm{poly}(L))$, which is smaller. Complete factorization uses polynomial-time primality recognition and recursion on verified divisors, at most $2\log_2 N$ nodes with arguments at most $N$. This proves the claimed $L_N[2/3,O(1)]$ worst-case bit bound. The [finite replay](../experiments/cogentic-smooth-windows.py) checks the analytic identities on finite examples; it does not execute the asymptotic integer search at these large constants. [Cogentic, Lemma 11.1 and §§11.2–11.3](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=55)

## A smaller coefficient using all two-point widths

This section strengthens the $400000$ coefficient. The fixed schedule and its proof above stand separately. The improvement uses the full $\log R=O(H)$ range of the smooth-window estimate for *unclean* pairs as well as clean pairs. It also uses an elementary row and column estimate for the real-root matrix.

Fix positive constants $x,v,b,a$ satisfying
$$
 v>1/x,\qquad v>xa,\qquad
 a>4b,\qquad a>2b+\frac{2(v+1/x)}{3b}.                        \tag{3}
$$
Take $d\sim xD$, $\log U\sim vV$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log A\sim aH$,
$\log K_1\sim H$ and $\log(1/w)\sim D\sqrt z$. The preceding integer rounding applies. Then $\log m=(1/x+o(1))V$, $d\log A=(xa+o(1))V$ and $\log X_{\rm sc}=(v+1/x+o(1))V$. On *all* evaluation pairs $a_1\ne a_2$, with $\Delta=|a_2-a_1|\ge1$, the Gaussian ratio in the second conditional progression is at most $O(K_1^{1.1}A/\Delta)$ and at least $K_1^{0.8}$. The actual conditioned center is within relative $K_1^{-0.94}$ of its nominal value on the same $L_0^3$-standard-deviation cutoff as in Lemma 9.5. The following details account for the refinements.

* Each grid point is either in the baseline interval or within $2w$ of a target. When measuring distances to one of the two sets of $d$ roots, the $j$th nearest root, $j\ge2$, is at distance at least $(j-1)/(16d)$ for large $L$. The nearest distance is at least $w$. Thus the absolute product of the distances is at least $w(d-1)!/(16d)^{d-1}\ge w e^{-O(d)}$. Consequently the maximum logarithmic loss is $o(H)$. The original fixed powers of $K_1$ for root brackets, widths and mean stability follow. In $M_{\rm avg}$ each row and each column has off-diagonal absolute sum $O(d)$: at the $j$th nearest target the absolute logarithm is at most $\log(16d/j)+O(1)$, and $\sum_{j=1}^{2d}\log(32d/j)=O(d)$. The baseline contributes $O(d)$ as well. The operator norm is at most the square root of the maximum row and column sums multiplied together. The diagonal magnitude $\log(1/w)\gg d$ therefore retains the real-root positivity used above.
* In every two-point conditional progression the actual modulus is at most $P_0 m A B^{O(1)}$, by Lemma 9.4. The $m^2$ appearing in the exceptional-conductor *test* in the original paper is absent. After division by $G=\gcd(r,q)$ the quotient progression still has $\log(q/G)\le(1/x+o(1))V$ and $\log(Y/G)=(v+1/x+o(1))V$. On the event under consideration $G$ is squarefree and made of eligible primes, and $G\le AB^3$ by the local factors in Lemma 9.5. Otherwise the event is empty. For any eligible $G$ normalize by $q/S$, substitute $n=Gn'$ and obtain exactly the prefactor $(q/G)/(S/G)$ with the same ratio $R$. The inequality $v>1/x$ now makes the absolute progression errors of (2) exponentially small in $V$, even when $\Delta=1$. The main density at $Y/G$ grows over the density at $Y$ by at most
  $$
    G^{1-\alpha_Y}\exp\left(O\!\left(1+\frac{\log G}{u\log B}\right)\right)
    =L^{O(1)},
  $$
  since $\log G=O(H)$ and $1-\alpha_Y=O(z/H)$. The omission of $O(L)$ primes affects the pure ratios by $1+o(1)$ and the relative coprimality factors are at most $O(\log\log(3q))$. This bounds the conditional probability at $a_2$, given the scalar at $a_1$ and its prescribed modular rows, by $L^{O(1)}Z_i^{(0)}(a_2)\Phi_i(a_2;R_i)$. Applying the one-point formula at $a_1$ gives the same polynomial loss in the normalized arbitrary two-point upper bound. Terms outside the Gaussian cutoff cost $\exp(-cL_0^6)$ before normalizing by any of the densities. This replaces every exponential rough Rankin loss in the previous proof. In the clean 2-point calculation a further prime divisor above $\sqrt B$ costs $p^{-\alpha_s}L^{O(1)}$, and there are $O(\log A/\log B)=O(1)$ such divisors. The relative errors in the clean estimates are still $O(e^{-50z^2})$.
* The interval sieve requires $Aw\gg B^4\exp(O(z^3))$, supplied by $a>4b$. The two-point discard costs for close marks and a squared prescribed prime are now $L^{O(1)}(w^{-1}K_1^{-1/2}+B^3/(Aw))$ and $L^{O(1)}(B^{-1/2}+B^3/(Aw))$. Centered Gram diagonal terms cost at most $B^2(Aw)^{-1}\exp((2(v+1/x)/(3b)+o(1))H)$ times the norm squared, since $u\sim((v+1/x)/b)D$. These terms are exponentially small in $H$ precisely under the last strict inequality of (3). The other positivity and peeling estimates use polynomial factors in $L$, the large-prime cutoffs, and $E_{\rm rel}$, so their previous proof applies.
* To keep possible index overhead independent of this search constant one may split directly using each verified $\ell^{E_\ell}\equiv1\pmod N$. For two distinct odd prime divisors $p,q$, use the nonprincipal quadratic character $\chi=(\cdot/p)$ if $v_2(p-1)>v_2(q-1)$ after ordering them, or $\chi=(\cdot/p)(\cdot/q)$ if the valuations are equal. Every $\ell$ with $\chi(\ell)=-1$ has different local 2-parts of its order. Its verified exponent then produces a proper divisor by the standard squaring chain. The Dirichlet GRH prime estimate makes half the primes in $(B/2,B]$ negative up to $o(B/\log B)$, whereas the search works for every sufficiently large target prime. Actual execution verifies gcds and requires neither hidden prime. Trial divide initially through $B$ to remove primes in the factor base, and retain the finite fallback if no split is found. The resulting overhead besides the four-coordinate search is $\exp(O(H))$. [Full single-exponent splitting argument](cogentic-dense-exponents.md)

The infimum of admissible $v$ is $v_*=(32/3)^{1/3}$. For $S=v+1/x$ the least possible value of $\max(4b,2b+2S/(3b))$ is $4\sqrt{S/3}$ at $b^2=S/3$. For fixed $v$, the quantity $x^2(v+1/x)$ increases with $x$, and the permitted range is $x>1/v$. Hence feasibility holds arbitrarily close above $v^3=32/3$. For each fixed $v>v_*$ sufficiently close, choose rational $x,b,a$ satisfying all the strict inequalities. The resulting four-coordinate algorithm takes $L_N[2/3,4v]$. A single algorithm attaining coefficient $4v_*=8.805\ldots$ interleaves all rational schedules satisfying (3), giving each fixed one a positive constant fraction of simulated steps. Every reported divisor is verified and primality is recognized explicitly. A multitape simulation overhead logarithmic in the best running time changes its exponent by $o(V)$. This limiting constant describes this parameter ledger and search; a different selection algorithm can have a smaller coefficient.
