# Extracting an order multiple from many partially overlapping fields

Let $N$ be an odd integer. Let $m$ be a common root modulo $N$ of primitive irreducible polynomials $f_i$ of degree $d$, totally real and with a common leading coefficient $c_d$ coprime to $N$. For field $i$ choose its own finite set of integer marks $S_i\subseteq\mathcal A$ with $a-m$ a unit modulo $N$. Require $|f_i(a)|$ to be squarefree on a common factor base, and require each of its prime divisors $p$ to be coprime to $c_dN$ and to satisfy $f_i'(a)\not\equiv0\pmod p$. One target field has $d$ simple roots modulo $\ell$ for a factor-base prime $\ell$. Give each incidence $(i,a)$ a separate column. The integral equations on its coefficients are
$$
 \sum_{a\in S_i}c_{ia}=0,\qquad
 \sum_{i:a\in S_i}c_{ia}=0,\qquad
 \sum_{a\in S_i,\ a\equiv r\bmod p}c_{ia}=-E\,b_{ipr}.
$$
Here $b$ selects exactly the $d$ target rows $(i,\ell,r)$. Denote the integral matrix by $V$ and extend $b$ by zero on all field and mark sum rows. The real matrix $U$ has a separate block of $d-1$ trace-zero logarithm rows for each field. On an incidence $(i,a)$ its nonzero block is $H(\log|a-\theta_{ij}|)_{1\le j\le d}$ for an orthonormal matrix $H$ whose kernel consists of constant vectors, as in Cogentic's Helmert construction. The following criterion permits arbitrarily many fields and different sets of smooth marks. [Cogentic, Lemmas 7.3–7.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=31)

Assume every left dependency $(x,y)$ of the stacked $(V,U)$ has $x^Tb=0$ and $y=0$. Then $b\in\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb Q}V$ and $U(\ker_{\mathbb Z} V)$ spans the full real logarithm space. Indeed a rational functional annihilating $V$ extends to a left dependency by taking $y=0$. If $y$ annihilates $U(\ker_{\mathbb Z} V)$, the integer lattice spans the real nullspace because $V$ is integral, so $y^TU$ lies in the real row space of $V$. The hypothesis then gives $y=0$. Take an integer particular solution $Vc^{(0)}=-E_0 b$, $E_0>0$, and integer homogeneous solutions $c^{(j)}$ whose logarithm vectors form a basis over the reals.

For any such solution $c$ to $Vc=-Eb$, the field elements $\gamma_i=\ell^{E1_{i=\mathrm{target}}}\prod_{a\in S_i}(a-\theta_i)^{c_{ia}}$ are units. The field-sum row cancels the pole offsets of nonmonic valuations. The ordinary valuation rows cancel all finite prime ideals, including each simple factor of the completely split prime in the target. Their logarithms give $Uc$ because the rational scalar $\ell^E$ contributes zero to each trace-zero vector. The possible logarithms of units lie in a product lattice. If there are $k$ fields, its covolume is at least $(3\sqrt{d-1})^{-k(d-1)}$ by the single-field bound in Lemma 7.4(1). The sublattice generated by the chosen $Uc^{(j)}$ has finite index; multiplying $Uc^{(0)}$ by this index makes it an integer linear combination. Equivalently, solve the real linear equation for the coefficients of $-Uc^{(0)}$ in that basis and clear the rational denominators. The resulting vector $c$ has $Uc=0$, so each $\gamma_i$ is one of $\{1,-1\}$. Evaluating all of them at $m\bmod N$ in their localizations and multiplying uses the mark-sum rows to cancel every $a-m$. Hence $\ell^{2E}\equiv1\pmod N$.

All integer and analytic extraction costs are polynomial in the dimensions, the coefficient bit bounds, and the field/mark input precision bounds in Lemma 7.5. To see the precision dependence for varying $k$, put $r=\operatorname{rank}V$, $s=k(d-1)$, and let $M$ bound the number of columns. A pivot minor, a cleared particular solution, and a spanning list for the integral kernel over the rationals have bit length $O((r+1)\log(2+r+M))$ by Cramer's rule. The true archimedean unit columns after applying this list therefore have norm at most $2^C$ with $C$ polynomial in the input dimensions and in the logarithmic height bounds of the marks. Every independent $j$-tuple of these columns, $1\le j\le s$, has exterior norm at least $(3\sqrt{s})^{-j}$: the lattice it spans has no nonzero vector shorter than $1/3$, by the single-field norm argument componentwise and Minkowski in its own span. Thus Gram determinants for independent tuples have an explicit gap at zero, permitting greedy certified selection by rational approximations with $O(s(C+\log(2s)))$ precision plus polynomial logarithmic guard bits. The unit-basis determinant is at most $2^{sC}$, and its index against the full product lattice is at most $2^{sC}(3\sqrt{d-1})^s$. The inverse matrix, the coordinate numerators and the denominator rounding precision all have polynomial bit length. Exact rational rank operations, root isolation from the designated brackets, logarithm evaluation and continued fractions give the verified exponent in polynomial time in these quantities. This also covers repeated isomorphic fields because the product of their unit lattices is taken in separately indexed coordinates.

## Collective rank on the marks

For each field let $R_i$ be its row-function space on $S_i$, generated by the constant, its prime rows and the trace-zero logarithms. A dependency of the stacked matrix supplies a function $h$ on $\mathcal A$ whose restriction to every $S_i$ belongs to $R_i$. If the only such $h$ are constants, one more local condition suffices. For each field suppose it has some set of surviving prime rows and marks avoiding every other row, on which its constant, all surviving finite rows and trace-zero rows are independent. Require all target rows to survive. In a dependency, the constant value of $h$ can be represented only by the corresponding constant field coefficient on this submatrix. All real coefficients and all target-row coefficients must therefore vanish. The preceding criterion applies, even if further dependencies occur among prime rows without surviving marks.

A quantitative separator builds a family with the required global property. Give the marks fixed positive masses, let $n=|\mathcal A|-1$, and work in the mean-zero real mark space with this inner product. For each candidate field, define $Q_i(h)$ as the weighted squared distance between $h|_{S_i}$ and $R_i$. This is a positive semidefinite quadratic form. Suppose $Q_i\preceq\Lambda I$ and an algorithm can find, for each unit $h$, a field with $Q_i(h)\ge\delta$, where $0<\delta\le1$. Start with $S=0$. While its least eigenvalue is below one, supply a unit least-eigenvalue vector to the separator and add the returned form to $S$. Each step satisfies
$$
 \frac{\det(I+S+Q_i)}{\det(I+S)}
 \ge 1+\operatorname{tr}((I+S)^{-1}Q_i)
 \ge 1+\delta/2.
$$
The first inequality expands the eigenvalue product of the conjugated positive semidefinite matrix. After $K$ steps, $\det(I+S)\le(1+K\Lambda)^n$, so the loop terminates within $O((n/\delta)\log(2+n\Lambda/\delta))$ steps. For example $K\ge32(n/\delta)\log(2+n\Lambda/\delta)$ contradicts the two determinant bounds. The same bound, up to constants, holds using an approximate least eigenvector of Rayleigh quotient at most two and a separation threshold $\delta/2$. More precisely, $\operatorname{tr}((I+S)^{-1}Q_i)\ge Q_i(h)/(1+h^TSh)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. For rational positive forms a polynomial-time symmetric eigensolver at polynomial precision suffices, or one can work on the mean-zero space using a basis and its rational positive definite mass matrix. The summed form then dominates the identity, which forces the common exact kernel to be zero. This lemma supplies the rank and extraction step once the stated separator is available; the existence and cost of a separator from polynomial smoothness are separate arithmetic questions.
