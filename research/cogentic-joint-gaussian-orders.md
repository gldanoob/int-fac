# Joint norm and quotient orders in Gaussian charts

One verified Gaussian order multiple annihilates both a rational residue of order dividing \(p-1\) and, at an inert prime, a norm-one residue of order dividing \(p+1\). Full ideal valuations in an imaginary quadratic source can produce this certificate from the input. An exact example gives both maximal local orders at both factors and splits by the norm-one quotient. For balanced inputs, products of small Gaussian primes also give a pointwise population of explicit charts with large orders in both components. ## The two local orders

Work in \(R_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)[i]\) with \(i^2=-1\) and conjugation \(i\mapsto-i\). For a unit \(z\), put \(n(z)=z\bar z\) and \(u(z)=z/\bar z\). If \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) divides \(N\), the local ring \(R_p\) is \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\), and conjugation is the \(p\)-power map. Consequently \(n(z)=z^{p+1}\), \(u(z)=z^{1-p}\), and
\[
\operatorname{lcm}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_p n(z),\operatorname{ord}_{R_p} u(z)\bigr)
=\operatorname{ord}_{R_p}(z^2).
\]
Indeed the lcm is the least positive exponent \(E\) with both \(z^{(p+1)E}=1\) and \(z^{(p-1)E}=1\), and \(\gcd(p-1,p+1)=2\). The two marginal orders divide \(p-1,p+1\), respectively. In particular the odd part of the joint order is the product of the two odd parts. At a split prime \(p\equiv1\bmod4\), write \(z=(v,w)\) with conjugation swapping coordinates. The norm and quotient then have orders dividing \(p-1\), since they are represented by \(vw\) and \(v/w\) in \(\mathbb F_p^\times\).

## Full Gaussian order certificates from one quadratic source

For odd nonsquare \(N\), take \(c=\lceil\sqrt N\rceil\) and \(D=c^2-N\). In the imaginary quadratic order \(\mathbb Z[\theta]\) with \(\theta^2=-D\), substitution \(\theta\mapsto ci\) gives an evaluation map to \(R_N\). Use primitive marks \(a+b\theta\), whose norms are \(a^2+Db^2\), and check that each norm is coprime to \(N\). The Gaussian image \(a+bci\) then has unit norm modulo \(N\) as well. For \(|a|,|b|\le H\), the source norms are \(O(\sqrt N\,H^2)\) and the Gaussian norms are \(O(NH^2)\).

Keep marks whose source and Gaussian norms have all prime factors at most a public cutoff \(B\). For the source also require the norms to be coprime to \(2D\). For any source norm prime \(\ell\), primitivity gives \(\ell\nmid b\), and \(\theta=-a/b\bmod\ell\) is a simple root of the source polynomial. Exactly one prime ideal above \(\ell\) divides the mark. Its exponent is the rational norm valuation, since its residue degree is one. Thus source ideal rows are computable by trial division without a maximal-order computation. Use separate rows for **all** Gaussian prime factors of the image, including both primes over each split rational prime. Trial division in \(\mathbb Z[i]\) uses the integer coordinates and norms. These rows record each image up to a Gaussian unit.

Let the target row be the Gaussian prime \(\pi\), with \(\gcd(N,\operatorname{Norm}\pi)=1\). If the target unit vector lies in the rational column span, clear denominators to obtain an integer combination with zero source rows and Gaussian rows \(k e_\pi\). Zero source rows make the field product a unit in the maximal order. Every unit in an imaginary quadratic field is a root of unity of order dividing 12. The Gaussian product is a unit times \(\pi^k\), and Gaussian units have order dividing four. Evaluation therefore gives \(\pi^{12k}=1\) in \(R_N\). For the evaluation with negative exponents, the fractional product has a denominator made of marks with unit evaluations. Its source root of unity need not belong to the power order, but the equality of the two products after taking the 12th power does, after clearing those denominators. The same exponent simultaneously kills \(n(\pi)\) and \(u(\pi)\) in \(R_N\). Every step including matrix solving and verification is polynomial in \(H,B,\log N\). The column-span criterion requires a supply of jointly smooth marks and an isolated Gaussian row.

At \(N=989=23\cdot43\), the source has \(c=32\), \(D=35\), \(H=40\) and \(B=397\). The matrix has 138 columns and rank 129. It gives \(E=221760\) at \(\pi=1+2i\). The two pairs of local norm and quotient orders are \((22,24)\) and \((42,44)\). The norm squaring chain gives global tests only, while the quotient chain gives the proper divisor 43. The certificate computation receives no factors or orders. The replay uses the factors afterward to check both exact local order pairs and the full orders of \(\pi\). The cutoff in this finite example exceeds the two factors. [Exact GP replay](../experiments/cogentic-joint-gaussian-orders.gp)

## Large orders from publicly sampled products

Let \(N=pq\) with \(p<q\le2p\), fix an integer \(C\ge3\), and put \(B=\operatorname{bitlength}(N)^C\). For every prime \(\ell\le B\) with \(\ell\equiv1\bmod4\), choose one Gaussian prime of norm \(\ell\) deterministically, for example by an integer search for a representation as a sum of two squares. Choose a uniform subset of \(J=\max\{j:B^j\le N/9\}\) such primes and multiply them to get \(z\). The prime list and the sample are computable in expected polynomial time in \(\log N\) for fixed \(C\). The output uses \(O(\log N)\) bits. For sufficiently large \(N\) all chosen primes are below \(p,q\), so their products are units at both factors.

Write \(L=\log N\) and let \(K\) be the number of Gaussian primes available. The prime number theorem in the fixed progression gives \(K\asymp B/\log B\), whereas \(J\sim L/\log B\). Unique factorization gives \(\binom{K}{J}\) distinct products. They are distinct modulo each factor as well. Their complex absolute values are at most \(\sqrt N/3<p/2\), so a nonzero difference has absolute value below \(p\) and cannot belong to \(p\mathbb Z[i]\). The same bound applies to \(q\). Thus the local sampling support at each prime has cardinality at least
\[
\binom{K}{J}\ \ge\ (K/J)^J\ =\ N^{1-1/C-o(1)}.
\]

At either factor \(r\), each of the norm and quotient maps has fibers of size at most \(r+1\), in both the field and split cases above. A cyclic group of order \(m\in\{r-1,r+1\}\) has at most \(r^{1-\delta}\tau(m)\) elements of order at most \(r^{1-\delta}\), by summing over the possible orders. The local failure set for either component then has size \(r^{2-\delta+o(1)}\). Comparing it to the injective sample support proves that, for any fixed \(2/C<\delta<1\), with probability \(1-o(1)\) both marginal orders exceed \(r^{1-\delta}\) simultaneously at both factors. The \(o(1)\) bound is uniform over the indicated balanced inputs. At an inert factor their lcm also exceeds \(r^{2-2\delta}/2\). Taking instead \(B=2^{\lfloor\operatorname{bitlength}(N)^{1/3}\rfloor}\) makes the loss \(1/C\) in the sample-size exponent \(o(1)\), so the same conclusion holds at this larger cutoff for every fixed \(0<\delta<1\). The replay checks distinctness and the joint-order identity on the 15 products of two primes at \(B=41\), reduced at 131 and 139.

This gives pointwise large orders from a computable distribution and a separate way to use full Gaussian relations if the necessary rows are present. Large orders alone do not supply an annihilating exponent, and their large prime factors are unconstrained.
