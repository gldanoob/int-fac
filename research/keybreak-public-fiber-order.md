# The public comparison quotient and the cost of producing it

For a specified group of certified words on one input \(N\), the attainable parity space modulo *all* public identity words has binary dimension at most \(\omega(N)\). There exist sparse public comparisons leaving only polynomial dimension. Constructing them requires a presentation of the modular image generated by the words, or a smaller collection of witnessed relations in that image. The ordinary ideal class group does not supply those comparisons. The [public-span budget](keybreak-public-span-schedule.md) requires both rank and a construction charged to the current input.

## Exact quotient and bounded witnesses

Let \(\Gamma\) be the free abelian group on a specified list of certified tokens \(g_1,\ldots,g_m\). Two maps are part of the certificate. The map \(\epsilon\) sends a word to its checked value in \(U_N=(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\), and the map \(\pi\) sends it to its additive binary parity data. Formal tokens let us retain occurrence bits even if two presentations represent the same algebraic element. Put \(I=\epsilon(\Gamma)\). Since \(\pi\) kills squares, the map \(I\to\pi(\Gamma)/\pi(\ker\epsilon)\) induces a surjection
\[
 I/I^2\longrightarrow \pi(\Gamma)/\pi(\ker\epsilon).
\]
Indeed, changing a lift of a value changes its image by an identity word, and a square value can use a square lift. When parity consists of the free token-occurrence bits, this is an isomorphism by \(\Gamma/(\ker\epsilon+2\Gamma)\simeq I/I^2\). For odd \(N\), the group \(I[2]\) is contained in the CRT sign cube of size \(2^{\omega(N)}\). Squaring in the finite group \(I\) gives \(|I/I^2|=|I[2]|\). This bounds the quotient of the attainable space \(\pi(\Gamma)\), including every private row only if its parity is in that space. The statement neither bounds the quotient of arbitrary formal columns nor gives an algorithm for computing \(\pi(\ker\epsilon)\).

There also exist short comparison witnesses on the fixed token list. A generating sublist for \(I\) of length at most \(\log_2 N\) can be obtained existentially by repeatedly adding a token value outside the current subgroup; each addition at least doubles its size. For each remaining token \(g_j\), express \(\epsilon(g_j)\) as a product of the selected values, using exponents between \(0\) and \(N-1\). Such exponents suffice by reducing modulo the orders of the selected values. Comparing \(g_j\) with this word gives an exact identity and eliminates its parity generator modulo the public span. Thus witnesses of \(O(\log N)\) token support and polynomial bit description exist for a residual dimension of at most \(\log_2 N\). This does not prescribe an efficient way to select the sublist or find its exponent representations.

The exponent sizes do not by themselves force expanded algebraic products of size \(N\). If a witnessed identity has \(\epsilon(\prod_jg_j^{a_j})=1\), write \(a_j=2h_j+b_j\) with \(b_j\in\{0,1\}\), including for negative exponents. The parity word \(\prod_jg_j^{b_j}\) has the same parity data and a known modular square root
\[
 \prod_j\epsilon(g_j)^{-h_j}\pmod N.
\]
Repeated squaring computes this correction from the binary exponents. The retained payload is now a word using each listed token at most once. The argument needs each token to have a checked unit evaluation and a parity and extraction representation that can actually be carried through the verifier. It applies directly to factored rational constants, to completed descent trees with explicit signed atom records, and to public atoms with certified rational correction and ideal factors. A compact algebraic element is usable only after checking its represented factors, sign and characters as applicable, and accounting for the size of its retained parity representation. An ideal class, or a vector of ideal exponents asserted to be principal, is not such a token without a lifted generator. [KeyBreak, E.1 and E.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=85)

If the supported rational primes include all unit primes up to a sufficiently large constant times \((\log N)^2\), Dirichlet GRH makes their values generate \(U_N\): a proper subgroup would omit a prime below that bound. In that case a basis of the **full integer** kernel of \(\mathbb Z^m\to U_N\) gives its lattice index \(\varphi(N)\), enough to factor \(N\) by the standard randomized order-multiple procedure. A public span obtained only modulo two does not supply this index. This distinction leaves open a direct construction of enough parity comparisons. [Lamzouri, Li and Soundararajan, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.3595)

## The integral order and its sizes

Let \(g\) be KeyBreak's monic equation for \(\beta=A\alpha\), put \(\mathcal O_0=\mathbb Z[\beta]\), and let \(\psi_0\) send \(\beta\) to the checked root modulo \(N\). The derivative \(g'(\beta)\) evaluates to a unit. The trace-dual inclusion \(g'(\beta)\mathcal O_K\subseteq\mathcal O_0\) therefore extends \(\psi_0\) through \(\mathcal O_0[g'(\beta)^{-1}]\) to \(\mathcal O_K\). This uses the derivative at the selected evaluation. It needs no claim that the full index \([\mathcal O_K:\mathcal O_0]\) is prime to \(N\). The kernel \(\mathfrak m\) of the extended map has norm \(N\), since the map is surjective on rational integers. The order
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z\times\mathcal O_K:u\bmod N=\psi(v)\}
\]
has index \(N\) in \(\mathbb Z\times\mathcal O_K\). Its trace discriminant is \(N^2\Delta_K\); its conductor in that product is \(N\mathbb Z\times\mathfrak m\), as one sees by multiplying separately by the two idempotents. After localizing at rational primes prime to \(N\), its units encode exact evaluation identities with the rational component inverted. Only units whose allowed supports agree with the chosen token model give the required public comparisons. The same construction using \(\mathcal O_0\) has discriminant \(N^2\operatorname{disc}(g)\). The fiber-product order is in the split algebra \(\mathbb Q\times K\). Just applying an algorithm stated for orders in number fields to \(\mathcal T\) would go outside its input class. [KeyBreak, E.2 and E.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=90)

Equivalently, for a set \(S\) of allowed prime ideals away from \(\mathfrak m\), quotient the ray and ordinary ideal class groups by the classes of those ideals. The resulting exact sequence is
\[
 \mathcal O_{K,S}^{\times}\longrightarrow U_N
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m,S}(K)
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Cl}_S(K)\longrightarrow 1.
\]
The kernel at \(U_N\) consists of residues of \(S\)-units: if a principal lift is ray-trivial modulo the allowed ideals, divide its generator by a generator congruent to one. The quotient is then an \(S\)-unit. This description locates the modular comparison in the ray data. Knowing the ordinary class group gives neither those residues nor certificates expressing a requested residue as a word. [Eisenträger and Hallgren, §7](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/a/166357/files/2023/06/rcg.pdf)

In the current schedule, let \(L=\log N\), \(D=(L/\log L)^{1/3}\), \(H=D\log L\), and \(V_*=DH\). Then \(d=\Theta(D)\) and the primitive **nonmonic** defining polynomial \(f\) has log coefficient height \(O(V_*)\). The binary-form order associated with its homogenization has discriminant \(\operatorname{disc}(f)\); a Sylvester determinant bound gives \(\log|\operatorname{disc}(f)|=O(dV_*)=O(L)\). In particular \(\log|\Delta_K|=O(L)\). This is an upper bound, and does not establish that the field discriminant is of this size on every trial. [Wood, §2.1 and its binary-form order basis](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~ila/Wood1.pdf#page=5)

The supplied monic equation \(g(T)=A^{d-1}f(T/A)\) has log height \(O(dV_*)=O(L)\), and
\[
 \operatorname{disc}(g)=A^{(d-1)(d-2)}\operatorname{disc}(f).
\]
On the original Gaussian law, \(\log|A|=\Theta(V_*)\) with probability \(1-o(1)\). Indeed, conditional on the environment, \(A\) is an affine function of one Gaussian coefficient with step \(2Q\) and width on the scale \(Q\sigma\); an interval smaller than that width by \(\exp(-cV_*)\) has probability \(O(\exp(-cV_*)+\sigma^{-1})\) for a fixed sufficiently small \(c>0\). On the continuing irreducible path \(f(0)\ne0\), so \(|g(0)|\ge |A|^{d-1}\). Also \(|\operatorname{disc}(f)|\ge1\). Hence, with high probability, the monic equation has log height \(\Theta(L)\) and the extractor's order has log discriminant \(\Theta(dL)\), despite the smaller bound on the binary-form order. Even an \(L_{|\operatorname{disc}(g)|}(1/3,c_0)\) guarantee for fixed \(c_0>0\) has exponent \(\Theta(L^{4/9}(\log L)^{5/9})=\omega(H)\) there. This compares available upper guarantees, not the intrinsic run time of class-group algorithms.

## What the class-group algorithms supply

- **Small ideal generators.** Bach's bound under number-field GRH gives generators of the ordinary ideal class group of norms at most \(12(\log|\Delta_K|)^2\). It does not give their relation matrix or principal generators, let alone a prescribed modular evaluation. The stronger assumption here is about Hecke or number-field \(L\)-functions; the KeyBreak schedule uses Dirichlet GRH. [Grenié and Molteni, discussion of Bach's bound and class group algorithms](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3281)
- **Class group, units and principal ideal lifts.** Theorem 1.3 of the de Boer, Pellet-Mary and Wesolowski preprint, under ERH, gives expected work polynomial in input length, \(L_{|\Delta_K|}(1/2)\), \(L_{d^d}(2/3)\) and the specified zeta-residue term, given an LLL-reduced integral basis. Theorem 21.2 supplies \(S\)-units in compact form, and §12.2 explains the additional ideal decomposition and linear algebra for principal generators. The \(d^d\) term alone has logarithm \(o(H)\) on this schedule. The available field-discriminant bound \(\log|\Delta_K|=O(L)\) does not put the \(L_{|\Delta_K|}(1/2)\) term in \(\exp(O(H))\). For that comparison one would need a field bound of order \(V_*\) on \(\log|\Delta_K|\), with constants and the other terms still to check, or a sharper algorithm. Enforcing the evaluation condition would remain necessary. [De Boer, Pellet-Mary and Wesolowski, Theorem 1.3, §12.2 and Theorem 21.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01588v2)
- **Smaller heuristic exponents.** Biasse's \(L(1/3)\) analysis and the Biasse–Fieker restricted-family theorem assume specific fixed power-law relations between degree and coefficient height in the logarithm of the relevant discriminant, together with relation heuristics. They give no direct guarantee for the supplied monogenic order. Gélin's sieving preprint allows a degree of order \((\log|\Delta_K|/\log\log|\Delta_K|)^\alpha\), but uses a suitably small *monic* defining polynomial and smoothness and spanning heuristics. If \(\log|\Delta_K|\asymp L\), the supplied \(g\) only gives the \((\alpha,\gamma)=(1/3,1)\) degree and height scales of that analysis, whose small-degree bound is \(L_{|\Delta_K|}(\gamma/2)\). A smaller monic polynomial or a different order algorithm would need its own proof and charge. These algorithms concern ordinary ideal relations; they do not impose the evaluation congruence. [Biasse, §2 and §6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-2014-02651-3) · [Biasse–Fieker, Theorems 6.1–6.2](https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157014000345) · [Gélin, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.12010)
- **Defining-polynomial reduction.** Gélin and Joux give a reduction algorithm whose bound depends on the minimum height already attainable by a monic defining polynomial. Even for primitive fields, their general existence estimate gives log height only \(O(\log|\Delta_K|+d)\). It therefore supplies no \(O(V_*)\) height guarantee at \(\log|\Delta_K|\asymp L\). [Gélin–Joux, Corollary 3.3 and §6](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/lms-journal-of-computation-and-mathematics/article/reducing-number-field-defining-polynomials-an-application-to-class-group-computations/8A2022D2F9DC244A606F4EB264EDF2D9)
- **The ray presentation.** The standard reduction computes the unit image in the residue group, the class group, and principal lifts, and uses finite-group discrete logarithms and factorization of the finite modulus. Here \(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m\simeq\mathbb Z/N\), and a prime-ideal factorization of \(\mathfrak m\) yields the rational prime powers of \(N\) by norms. Computing a basis of \(S\)-units without the gluing condition leaves a modular relation problem on their images. [Eisenträger and Hallgren, Theorem 7.1](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/a/166357/files/2023/06/rcg.pdf)

The recent ideal sampler gives a more direct comparison. Theorem 9.5 of de Boer, Pellet-Mary and Wesolowski can prescribe a ray residue, and the full-group version has no subgroup oracle. Its density guarantee uses a norm scale \(r^d\ge\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak m)=N\). At that scale the smooth-family choice itself has too small a density to certify the needed success rate. For \(B=\exp((b+o(1))H)\), use \(\eta=\log D/(2\log B)\) in Rankin's bound. Each rational prime has at most \(d\) prime ideals above it, with norms at least that prime, so
\[
 \frac{\Psi_K(x,B)}x
 \le x^{-\eta}\prod_{\operatorname{Nm}\mathfrak p\le B}
       (1-\operatorname{Nm}(\mathfrak p)^{-1+\eta})^{-1}
 \le \exp(-\Omega(DH))
 \qquad(x\ge N).
\]
The log Euler product is \(O(d(\log\log B+\sqrt D))=o(DH)\), by partial summation of the prime counting upper bound. In the theorem's density normalization the inverse residue \(1/\rho_K\) is negligible here under ERH: its equation (98) compares \(\rho_K\) with a finite Euler ratio at a polynomial-in-\(\log|\Delta_K|+d\) cutoff, whose lower bound is the ordinary prime product \(\prod_{p\le x}(1-1/p)\). Also \(N/\varphi(N)\) is negligible. Taking the norm \(r^d\) in the minimum defining local density now bounds the full-group theorem's smooth-input success guarantee on an \(\exp(-\Omega(DH))\) scale. This is a limitation of that guarantee for a direct smooth-family substitution, not a lower bound on a specialized sampler. [De Boer, Pellet-Mary and Wesolowski, Theorem 9.5 and (98)](https://koendeboer.com/publication/rigorousmethods/RigorousMethods.pdf#page=47)

The algorithms checked here do not give a bound for constructing enough lifted, modularly balanced comparisons on an attainable token space that contains the retained private rows. The small abstract quotient and the short existential witnesses leave that work open.
