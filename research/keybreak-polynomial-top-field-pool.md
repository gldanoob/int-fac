# A polynomial field pool in place of top concentration

The top concentration estimate in KeyBreak can be replaced by a polynomial per-prime success probability, if the relevant ordinary one- and two-point bounds remain available both before and after imposing an incoming child root. A polynomial pool of independent public environments and fields then covers almost every prime in each used dyad. This is a conditional transfer of the existing proofs, with no improved coefficient-law estimate assumed implicitly. [KeyBreak, Propositions G.10–G.12 and Lemma H.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=116)

## A deterministic set of primes with a polynomial hit probability

Fix a complete private dyad $J$ and use the original notation $Z_p$ for verified candidate counts at top $p$, $\tau_p$ for the conditional mean on rational-good environments, and $\bar\tau_p$ for the deterministic comparable reference in G.12. Assume the following statements with the original geometric and height hypotheses.

1. Outside the top rational exception, the ordinary one-point estimate gives $\mathbb E_F[Z_p\mid g]\ge a\bar\tau_p$ and $\tau_p\asymp\bar\tau_p$, with fixed $a>0$. The ordinary two-mark bound yields $\mathbb E_F[Z_p^2\mid g]\le D^{c_0}\bar\tau_p^2$ for a fixed $c_0$. The diagonal term is harmless under the existing large mean condition.
2. The internal G.11 proof remains valid and gives normalized bad-child mass $\delta_1$ smaller than any fixed negative power of $D$. The G.12 rational exceptions $\epsilon_{0,J}$ and $\bar\epsilon^{\rm in}_{0,J}$ satisfy the same estimate uniformly.
3. The incoming-root probability is $O(1/q)$. The one-point upper bound (G.55) and the normalized ordinary two-point upper bound on the incoming-good part hold in the law conditioned on $g,R_{q,s}$, where $R_{q,s}$ requires the field to have the specified child root.

Let $B_p$ count all formal bad-child incidences of the verified top candidates. This dominates the number of candidates to discard because they have a bad child, even if one candidate contributes several times. The reordering (G.54) is an equality after normalizing by $|J|\bar\tau_p$ and summing $B_p$. On the incoming-rational exceptional part, (G.55), the $O(1/q)$ root probability, and the rational second moment bound its expectation by $C W_1\sqrt{\bar\epsilon^{\rm in}_{0,J}}$. On the incoming-good part, the child-root-conditioned two-point upper bound gives
$$
\mathbb E_F[(H_{0,J}^{\rm det}(q,s)/B_1(q))^2\mid g,R_{q,s}]\le D^{O(1)}.
$$
Cauchy–Schwarz first in that conditional coefficient law and then in the deterministic root/profile measure bounds the child-bad expectation by $D^{O(1)}\sqrt{\delta_1}$. This is the weighted incidence argument from G.54–55 before any use of top concentration. Since $W_1\le D^{O(1)}$, it gives
$$
\frac1{|J|}\sum_{p\in J}\mathbb E_{g,F}[B_p/\bar\tau_p]
 \le D^{O(1)}\bigl(\sqrt{\delta_1}+\sqrt{\bar\epsilon^{\rm in}_{0,J}}\bigr).
$$
The top rational exceptions also have average $\epsilon_{0,J}$. By Markov, for each fixed $B>0$, outside a deterministic fraction at most $D^{-B}$ of primes in the dyad, both the rational exception probability and $\mathbb E[B_p/\bar\tau_p]$ are at most $D^{-B}$. The exponents in the imported errors can be chosen sufficiently large for this statement. No good set is computed by the algorithm.

For one of the other primes, condition first on a rational-good environment. Paley–Zygmund gives
$$
\Pr_F(Z_p\ge a\bar\tau_p/2\mid g)\ \ge\ (a^2/4)D^{-c_0}.
$$
The upper cut $Z_p\le D^{c_0+2}\bar\tau_p$ discards at most $D^{-c_0-4}$ by the second moment. Remove $B_p>a\bar\tau_p/4$ by the per-prime Markov bound and take $B$ large relative to $c_0$. The resulting unconditioned event has probability at least $D^{-C_0}$ for a fixed $C_0$. On it the good-child survivors number at least $a\bar\tau_p/4$, and their fraction among verified candidates is at least $D^{-O(1)}$. The two one-point success scales $D_p/|\mathcal R_p|\gg p_A$ and $Z_p/|\Omega_p|\gg p_{\rm den}$ used in H.4 follow from the same rational-good size bounds, as in (H.16). Only the survivor fraction among verified candidates has become polynomially small.

The unconditional top pair bound in item 1 does **not** imply the child-root-conditioned bound in item 3. Conditioning on $R_{q,s}$ can lose a factor of order $q$, much larger than any power of $D$ here. A modified field law for which only ordinary unconditioned estimates are known has not met the incoming-incidence requirement.

## Building and using the pool

Generate $D^{C_0+c}$ independent public draws $(Q,r,F)$ for fixed $c$ large enough. The option of independent environments matters: success averaged over $g$ does not yield a repeated-trial lower bound at a common fixed $g$. In the pinned setup, failure to find a prime $Q$ within the finite cap is exponentially small in the input bit length. The Gaussian sampler has negligible total variation error. A nonunit content or leading coefficient yields a proper factor under the original height bound. A full-gcd derivative failure has probability $O(1/\sigma)$, by the one-coordinate argument of H.1. Thus setup rejection without a proper factor is negligible compared with the polynomial hit bound, or needs to be checked separately for a new law. No analysis conditions on acceptance of all the fields. [KeyBreak, Appendix H.1, pp. 126–127](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=126)

The chance a covered prime misses every pool entry is at most $\exp(-D^{c+o(1)})$. This can beat a union over all primes up to $Y_0$, since $\log Y_0=O(DH)$. Hence, except with negligible probability, the pool covers all but the deterministic exceptional fraction in each used dyad, without checking any good-parent predicate. In particular the staged shape count can use the retained primes in the union of good sets. The fields themselves are sampled on the current input and all their construction and storage count.

For a prescribed top prime, try the fields in a public order, with a fresh bounded search for each. Enlarge the top H.4 candidate and trial quotas by a polynomial in $D$ to accommodate the smaller survivor fraction. The internal calls retain their own good-state and per-call conditional guarantees. The top probability proof is otherwise unchanged: among verified candidates a polynomial fraction have all children good, every such candidate completes or factors with constant probability given any earlier history, and the fractions among rational opportunities and raw points have the ordinary scales. A factor is absorbing. Completion is verified by the original identities and factor records, so a field that was not proved good may still return a useful valid descent. If the prime is covered by a good field, the bounded attempts fail with only the prescribed small error, uniformly in prior history, because the good field is reached unless an earlier attempt already succeeded. Increase confidence parameters polynomially to pay for the pool trials, including failed ones. This preserves leading exponents and does not require identifying the winning field in advance.

## Field-labelled rows and exact extraction

Allow different initial primes of a private row to be descended in different fields; keep each subtree in its chosen field. Its checked contribution is an identity modulo the same current input $N$, using that field's own evaluation map. Multiplying the contributions gives the original row identity with one rational correction and one component in each field used. The rational parity coordinates are shared, including a coordinate for each public $Q$ and the rational sign. The ideal, norm **including sign**, and character columns are kept separately for each field. A shared algebraic norm row alone would not certify the individual fields' square conditions. With a polynomial field pool the total column bound grows by only a polynomial factor, as do the row population cap, character work, and sparse solve. Retained identities still use only the successful bounded-depth trees. The height bound for each field's product is at most the original bound with the enlarged row count. [KeyBreak, multi-field square certificate and column types, Appendix E](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=75)

For a verified dependence, the field-specific ideal and character checks give a rational squareclass for that field's product. Its own norm-square rows and the odd field degree then make the class a square. Compute the exact field root separately for each nonempty field component. Multiply their evaluated roots and the square root of the global rational correction, and verify the resulting modular square before reading any private signs. Character setup and the all-class certificate failure bounds can be amplified by polynomial overhead and union-bounded over the pool. This uses separate field certificates, rather than claiming a pooled algebraic component lies in one field.

The original hidden-root argument still applies if field selection, all searches, row retention, matrix operations and public root extraction use only public data and independent public randomness. Every retained row keeps its own private root record, even for repeated numerical rows. This establishes the transfer within the charged model. It does not by itself prove the three analytic inputs for a narrowed coefficient law. The incoming conditioned estimates and the internal G.11 hypotheses must in particular be verified before attributing a cheaper algorithm to it. [KeyBreak, hidden-root theorem and epoch proof](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=141)
