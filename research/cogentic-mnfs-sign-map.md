# When a multiple-number-field sieve congruence gives a factor

For one input, put $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. Standard GNFS has heuristic coefficient $g=(64/9)^{1/3}$. Coppersmith's multiple-field construction reuses a smooth rational value across algebraic fields, giving heuristic factoring coefficient $a=((92+26\sqrt{13})/27)^{1/3}$. Lee and Venkatesan give a randomized multiple-polynomial square-congruence analysis at $a$. Their useful-square argument uses a conjecture and semiprimes with both primes $3\bmod 4$. [Coppersmith](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00198464) · [Lee and Venkatesan, Theorem 2.3 and Remarks 2.7, 4.4, 6.20 and 7.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08873)

## A test on the acquired dependencies

Fix an odd input with at least two distinct prime divisors. Work on a completed trial where the values and denominators in the evaluations are units modulo $N$. For relation $j$, let $r_j$ be its rational value and $u_{ij}$ its value in field $i$, with unused entries one. Write $\phi_i$ for evaluation and assume $r_j=\prod_i\phi_i(u_{ij})\pmod N$. Let $D$ be a binary subspace of relations whose selected rational product is a square in the rationals and whose selected products in each field are squares there. Require their roots to admit the indicated evaluation. The rational square condition applies across all fields together.

For $v\in D$, take a rational root $X_v$ and form $Y_v$ by multiplying the evaluated field roots. Then $\chi(v)=[X_v/Y_v]$ belongs to the square roots of unity modulo $N$ modulo the global signs. Each field root is determined up to a global sign, so its choice preserves this class. For $v+w$, remove from the product for $v$ times the product for $w$ the squares of the entries indexed by their intersection. Their rational and evaluated algebraic contributions cancel in the ratio by the relation identities. Thus $\chi$ is linear over $\mathbf F_2$.

A uniform dependency gives a proper gcd with probability $1-2^{-\operatorname{rank}\chi}$. Indeed, a unit root of unity at an odd prime power is a sign. Distinct signs at two prime powers make $\gcd(X_v-Y_v,N)$ proper, and the kernel consists exactly of globally equal signs. In particular, any nonzero image suffices for at least one-half success. This requires a certified dependency space and the ability to sample and lift within the trial budget. The matrix nullity alone gives no lower bound on the sign image.

## Cost of independent retries

If fresh trials have expected cost at most $\exp((a+o(1))H)$ and per-input split probability at least $\exp(-(\delta+o(1))H)$, the expected cost through the first split is at most $\exp((a+\delta+o(1))H)$. This follows by conditioning the cost of each fresh trial on reaching it. Costs and success within the same trial may be correlated. Assuming this estimate uniformly on eligible inputs, prime and perfect-power tests and recursive splits add polynomial overhead in $\log N$. The available margin is $g-a=0.0211158152117\ldots$. Any fixed $\delta<g-a$ suffices. In the uniform-dependency procedure above the unresolved input is a lower bound on the probability that the acquired space has nonzero sign image, with sampling and lifting charged.

Our full-grid method pays for two smooth algebraic values at once. On its current supply criterion $2c\ge b+\rho$ with $\rho\ge4\sqrt c/(3b)$, arithmetic–geometric mean already gives sieve coefficient $2c\ge2(4/3)^{2/3}=2.42282746\ldots$ even with free extraction. The multiple-field approach would have to preserve its cheaper relation supply while securing useful square congruences. [Full-grid supply estimates](cogentic-averaged-projective-trace.md)

## Fan Factories and the cost of the first input

In the Fan Factories model the stored rational smooth pairs depend only on the bit length. A target supplies its own algebraic fields. Write $L[c]=\exp((c+o(1))H)$. With setup $L[s]$ and online cost $L[t]$, one input from scratch costs $L[\max(s,t)]$. For $L[\mu]$ same-size inputs sharing that setup, the amortized coefficient is $\max(s-\mu,t)$. At the paper's GNFS-budget point $(s,t)\approx(1.9230,1.7929)$ this gives $1.9230$ for one fresh input. At its separate no-precomputation batch point $\mu=0.0213$, the paper reports $1.8839$ per integer. The single-input MNFS benchmark remains $1.9019$ under the NFS heuristics.

Section 2.1 assumes smoothness statistics and independence across the rational and algebraic sides (H1). It also assumes that dependencies produce proper factors with probability at least one half, independently across dependencies (part of H3). The sign-map calculation above identifies an arithmetic condition for this probability once the field squares and their evaluations are certified. Its nonzero image remains to be proved for the acquired relations. H2 already uses sparse binary linear algebra with time quadratic in the matrix dimension up to subexponential-order factors. The published Lean checks cover identities for the optimized constants and exact feasibility witnesses. ## Comparing field roots for one integer from scratch

A two-field dependency can be found by matching rational squareclasses. In each field take a set of marks whose algebraic product has a certified root, with evaluated root $y_i$ and rational product $r_i$. If $r_1/r_2=s^2$ over the rationals and the evaluations are units, then $y_1/(s y_2)$ is a square root of unity modulo $N$. A proper gcd follows precisely when the two signs differ across prime powers. Write $r_i=d u_i^2$ with $d$ signed and squarefree and $u_i$ positive. In a table keyed by $d$, retain the first $y_i/u_i$ modulo $N$. Compare each subsequent root to that one by gcd of the difference. If two entries in the bucket differ up to more than a global sign, at least one differs from the first up to more than a global sign. Thus one comparison per new entry detects every productive bucket, given factored rational values and certified local roots. This statement makes no distribution assumption. The cost of supplying such roots and locating productive matches is open.

For $N=341$ set $m=18$. Two members of the input's fan are $f_2=x^2+2x-19$ and $f_3=x^2+3x-37$, both equal to $341$ at $m$. In field 2 the single mark $5-t$ equals $((t-1)/2)^2$. In field 3 the product $(-7-t)(2-t)(5-t)$ equals $(t-2)^2$. Their rational products at $m$ are $-13$ and $-5200=-13\cdot20^2$. Jointly the rational root is $260$ and the evaluated algebraic root is $(17/2)16=136$. Their difference has gcd $31$ with $341$.

The [exact Sage replay](../experiments/cogentic-single-input-fan.py) constructs all fields and relations anew for each input. For shifts 1 through 3, it keeps irreducible quadratic fields with $\gcd(2\operatorname{disc}(f),N)=1$, primitive marks $|a|\le13$, $1\le b\le3$, and unit rational and algebraic norms smooth through 13. It enumerates the entire kernel of signed rational and field-norm parities, tests each product for squareness in its actual field and evaluates the roots. On $341$, 23 marked relations give 64 certified dependencies, of which 32 split. Every split uses more than one field. The same search on $119$ gives 16 certified dependencies and zero splits. A second search builds the squareclass table using at most three marks per local product and splits $341$ without enumerating the joint kernel. These are fixed illustrative inputs. Enumerating the kernel is exponential in its dimension.

For a quadratic fan, the deterministic [basis solver and sign-image calculation](https://github.com/gldanoob/int-fac/blob/c5cb231/research/cogentic-rational-root-matching.md#the-complete-sign-image-for-a-single-quadratic-fan) now give the exact split probability for a complete finite menu in polynomial time in its explicit size. Each sign-basis vector refines a partition of $N$ by gcd. Relative signs on the resulting blocks give $\operatorname{rank}\chi$ without factoring $N$ first. Widening the single-input search on $119$ gives 28 certified dimensions and rank one. On $4199$, the complete rank is two and the blocks are $13,17,19$. For supplied multiquadratic fields there is also a [growing-degree local square-kernel bound](cogentic-multiquadratic-square-kernel.md).

## What Lee–Venkatesan supplies for a single integer

The randomized NFS theorem fixes the input and averages over the radix and field polynomial. Theorem 2.1 gives square congruences at the GNFS coefficient $1.92299\ldots$; the authors describe the multiple-polynomial extension at $a=1.90188\ldots$. In §5 stochastic deepening varies how long it searches an environment before restarting, so that the mean supply of smooth relations can support an expected-work bound. In §7 the extraction question is separate. Theorem 2.3 uses Conjecture 7.1 for two primes both congruent to 3 modulo 4. Remark 7.4 transfers that same conjecture to the multiple-polynomial construction. All these are single-input statements. [Lee and Venkatesan, §§2, 5, 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08873#page=5)

For a trial with verified square roots, the missing event is that the sign map on the dependencies it can sample has positive rank. A uniform dependency then succeeds with probability at least one half. To beat the GNFS coefficient using the stated multiple-polynomial cost, it suffices to bound the expected cost of a fresh trial by $L_N[1/3,a]$ and the probability of that event below by $L_N[1/3,-r]$ for a fixed $r<0.0211158$, uniformly on the unresolved inputs. To retain $a$ itself, a probability $\exp(-o(H))$ suffices. Both criteria charge all per-input work. Congruence counts alone give neither bound.

There is a simple obstruction to attaching a hidden square after collecting an ordinary dependency. Multiplying both roots by an arbitrary unit $t$ preserves $X/Y$ at every prime power dividing $N$. Multiplying just one root while preserving the square congruence requires $t^2=1\bmod N$; a mixed-sign such $t$ already gives a proper divisor by a gcd. Thus the private-root argument in KeyBreak needs relations that incorporate an independently hidden choice during collection. Its constant extraction probability does not transfer to ordinary NFS rows just by rerandomizing the completed squares. [KeyBreak, Theorem E.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=93)
