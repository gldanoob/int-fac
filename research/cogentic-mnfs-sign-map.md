# When a multiple-number-field sieve congruence gives a factor

For one input, put $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. Standard GNFS has heuristic coefficient $g=(64/9)^{1/3}$. Coppersmith's multiple-field construction reuses a smooth rational value across algebraic fields, giving heuristic factoring coefficient $a=((92+26\sqrt{13})/27)^{1/3}$. Lee and Venkatesan give a randomized multiple-polynomial square-congruence analysis at $a$. Their useful-square argument uses a conjecture and semiprimes with both primes $3\bmod 4$. [Coppersmith](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00198464) · [Lee and Venkatesan, Theorem 2.3 and Remarks 2.7, 4.4, 6.20 and 7.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.08873)

## A test on the acquired dependencies

Fix an odd input with at least two distinct prime divisors. Work on a completed trial where the values and denominators in the evaluations are units modulo $N$. For relation $j$, let $r_j$ be its rational value and $u_{ij}$ its value in field $i$, with unused entries one. Write $\phi_i$ for evaluation and assume $r_j=\prod_i\phi_i(u_{ij})\pmod N$. Let $D$ be a binary subspace of relations whose selected rational product is a square in the rationals and whose selected products in each field are squares there. Require their roots to admit the indicated evaluation. The rational square condition applies across all fields together.

For $v\in D$, take a rational root $X_v$ and form $Y_v$ by multiplying the evaluated field roots. Then $\chi(v)=[X_v/Y_v]$ belongs to the square roots of unity modulo $N$ modulo the global signs. Each field root is determined up to a global sign, so its choice preserves this class. For $v+w$, remove from the product for $v$ times the product for $w$ the squares of the entries indexed by their intersection. Their rational and evaluated algebraic contributions cancel in the ratio by the relation identities. Thus $\chi$ is linear over $\mathbf F_2$.

A uniform dependency gives a proper gcd with probability $1-2^{-\operatorname{rank}\chi}$. Indeed, a unit root of unity at an odd prime power is a sign. Distinct signs at two prime powers make $\gcd(X_v-Y_v,N)$ proper, and the kernel consists exactly of globally equal signs. In particular, any nonzero image suffices for at least one-half success. This requires a certified dependency space and the ability to sample and lift within the trial budget. The matrix nullity alone gives no lower bound on the sign image.

## Cost of independent retries

If fresh trials have expected cost at most $\exp((a+o(1))H)$ and per-input split probability at least $\exp(-(\delta+o(1))H)$, the expected cost through the first split is at most $\exp((a+\delta+o(1))H)$. This follows by conditioning the cost of each fresh trial on reaching it. Costs and success within the same trial may be correlated. Assuming this estimate uniformly on eligible inputs, prime and perfect-power tests and recursive splits add polynomial overhead in $\log N$. The available margin is $g-a=0.0211158152117\ldots$. Any fixed $\delta<g-a$ suffices. In the uniform-dependency procedure above the unresolved input is a lower bound on the probability that the acquired space has nonzero sign image, with sampling and lifting charged.
