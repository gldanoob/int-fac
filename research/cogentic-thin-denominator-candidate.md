# A thinner projective grid and a smaller exact lift

The high-trace two-field construction may give a per-input expected bit-time coefficient \(3(8/9)^{1/3}=2.884499\ldots\) at \(L_N[1/3]\). The candidate combines a smaller denominator menu with exact solves for a few right-hand sides. It uses the existing finite-order smoothness and rank estimates under Dirichlet GRH. The transfer and cost checks below are still open. For comparison, the existing full-grid trace ledger gives \(2(16/9)^{2/3}=2.935046\ldots\), and KeyBreak's Theorem 1.2 states \(2.97\) under primitive Dirichlet GRH. [Full-grid trace bound](cogentic-projective-trace.md) · [KeyBreak, Theorem 1.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=3)

## Scan fewer denominators

Use \(L=\log N\), \(z=\log L\), \(D=(L/z)^{1/3}\), \(H=Dz\), \(V=DH\), \(\log A\sim cH\), \(\log B\sim bH\), and degree \(d\sim xD\). Work with the variable-multiple Gaussian and finite-order trace proof. Write \(S=xc+1/x\) for the norm-height coefficient and \(\rho=2S/(3b)\) for the joint smoothness exponent. Fix strict parameters with \(c>2b\), and choose \(\eta>0\) so that \(r=b+\rho+\eta\) lies strictly between \(c\) and \(2c\). Choose \(\lceil e^{(r-c)H}\rceil\) distinct prime denominators in the original interval \([A(1-e^{-30z^2}),A]\). Keep all the original numerator intervals and apply all their original checks at each selected denominator. The menu can be sampled before the Gaussian pair. The prime density is inverse polynomial in \(L\) by RH and the number of available primes is \(e^{(c+o(1))H}\). Rejection of duplicate denominators has negligible effect on the expected number of proposals. Even a sequential scan of the chosen denominators for each accepted candidate costs only \(e^{(2(r-c)+o(1))H}\), below the grid cost since \(r<2c\). [Original grid and norm height](cogentic-homogeneous-marks.md) · [Variable-multiple Gaussian](cogentic-variable-multiple-lattice.md)

At any one denominator, the weighted residue estimates of the trace proof use numerator intervals of length \(\asymp Aw\), and every denominator remains a unit at the small and main-band primes. The reference weights and means can be formed on the chosen grid. Its normalized one-mark atom is at most \(e^{(-r+o(1))H}\). In the distinct-position expansion the restoration of coincident positions therefore costs \(B^2 e^{(-r+o(1))H}\). In the repeated-position expansion the inverse effective count becomes \(n^{-1}=e^{(-r+\rho)H}\). Thus \(n/B=e^{\eta H}\), the margin needed for the same repeated-position bound with fixed even trace order taken large enough. The target size \(\log \ell\sim z^3\), all per-denominator numerator progressions, and the real logarithm grid stay on the scales of the full-grid proof. [Trace expansion and peeling](cogentic-projective-trace.md)

The original root enumeration costs \(B^{2+o(1)}\). For each selected denominator, process each numerator interval in segments of length at most \(B\), scanning the root list at each segment and emitting the corresponding numerator hits. There are \(O(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L))\) records per segment. Sort by numerator, multiply the distinct hit primes, and compare with the two field norms. Also run the original gcd sweeps on this smaller grid. The time is \(e^{(r+o(1))H}+B^{2+o(1)}\) and the sieve scratch space is \(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bits. The uniform one-mark upper bound gives \(\mathbf E M\le e^{(b+\eta+o(1))H}\) for the number \(M\) of joint columns. Abort the trial if \(M>e^{(b+2\eta)H}\), which has probability \(e^{(-\eta+o(1))H}\) by Markov's inequality. This cap bounds the retained relation list size and the subsequent work on every trial. [Exact residue sieve](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md)

## Project the logarithms without forming an adjugate

Use the existing randomized modular pivot proposal. Write \(C\) for its nonsingular square pivot minor with dimension at most \(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\), and \(P_*\in[B^2,2B^2]\) for the sampled modulus. A nonzero full-rank minor has only \(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bits, so the modular basis has the rational rank with probability \(1-o(1)\). Rectangular modular elimination costs \(M B^{2+o(1)}\). Compute \(C^{-1}\bmod P_*\) by ordinary elimination in \(B^{3+o(1)}\) bit operations. [Modular pivot bounds](cogentic-randomized-relation-time.md)

For each of the \(O(d)\) real rows let \(u\) be its restriction to the pivot columns as a column vector. Form a sufficiently accurate dyadic approximation to \(u\) and solve \(C^T\lambda=u\) on that rational approximation. After clearing its dyadic denominator, solve exactly by base-\(P_*\) lifting with the already computed inverse. For an integer right-hand side \(w\), start with residual \(w\), compute its next solution digit from the residual modulo \(P_*\), and subtract the matrix times this digit before dividing the residual by \(P_*\). Hadamard bounds and rational reconstruction give the exact solution after \(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\) digits for the required right-hand sides. Each digit costs \(B^2\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bit operations, including the updates on the longer residual and accumulated digits. Reconstructing all coordinates by the ordinary Euclidean algorithm fits the same total \(B^{3+o(1)}\) time. The inverse and lifted vectors occupy \(B^2\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bits.

For a free column with finite pivot-row vector \(v\) and real entry \(u_j\), its projected entry is \(u_j-\lambda^Tv\). This computation uses the actual sparse \(v\). The proposed selection can test projected columns one at a time and keep only \(O(d)\) independent columns. A bound on \(|\det C|\) suffices for a separation test, since multiplying all exact projected columns by \(\det C\) puts them in the unit-logarithm lattice used in the original extraction. Cramer's rule bounds \(\|C^{-1}\|\), so logarithm rounding error can be made small enough with \(B\,\operatorname{poly}(L)\) bits. Ordinary quadratic bit-time logarithm evaluation is enough at this precision. After choosing the columns, solve the original finite equations only for these few kernel directions and a particular solution, clear denominators, and verify all their finite equations. Now apply the existing unit-logarithm recovery and verify the resulting modular order multiple. No full integer adjugate or list of full kernel vectors is needed. [Unit-logarithm extraction](cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.md)

## Candidate cost and remaining checks

The proposed capped cost has coefficient \(\max(r,3b+2\eta)\) and workspace at most \(B^2e^{o(H)}+e^{(b+2\eta+o(1))H}\) bits. Take \(c\) down to \(2b\), \(x\) to \(1/\sqrt c\), and \(\eta\) down to zero through fixed strict schedules. The limiting cost is \(\max\{b+4\sqrt{2b}/(3b),3b\}\). At \(b=(8/9)^{1/3}\) the two bounds agree. If the success proof transfers, the original random generator has separating sign with probability \(1/2-o(1)\), the capped trial succeeds with probability bounded away from zero, and the verified gcd and recursive factorization give the per-input expected bound. [Single-generator splitting](cogentic-dense-exponents.md#a-single-successful-prime-can-give-the-divisor-directly)
