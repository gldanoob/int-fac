# Partial odd-order relations for the unit chart

A bounded search at one unknown prime converts a partial scalar into a divisor or a verified relation for the odd part of a word. Mixed-word relations can be pooled by saturation. For a semiprime, the public odd part of $N-1$ removes every shared odd factor of the two local orders. The first local collision then gives a split for a word with a nontrivial residual and a small cofactor at either prime. ## Search from an odd partial scalar

Let $N$ be odd, $b=\operatorname{bitlength}(N)$ and $Q=2^b$. For a unit $w\bmod N$ and an explicitly computable odd $E>0$, let $z=w^{QE}\bmod N$. At a prime $p\mid N$, write $h_p$ for the odd part of the order of $w\bmod p$. Then the order of $z\bmod p$ is
\[
                 r_p=\frac{h_p}{\gcd(h_p,E)}.
\]
The analogous identity holds modulo the full prime power. Computing $z$ needs $O(b+\log E)$ modular multiplications. Given a cutoff $R$, search for the first positive $k\le R$ with $\gcd(z^k-1,N)>1$. If the gcd is proper, it gives a divisor. If the gcd equals $N$, then $w^{QEk}=1\bmod N$, so $Ek$ annihilates the global odd-order component of $w$. All local orders of $z$ are odd, and the first hit is $\min_{p\mid N}r_p$. In particular $k$ is odd. It suffices that the residual order at any one unknown prime be at most $R$.

The existing prefix test uses blocks of width $\lceil\sqrt R\rceil$. For a prefix ending at $T\le R$, evaluate the product of the linear factors with roots $z^{-j}$ for $1\le j\le\lceil\sqrt R\rceil$ at the giant steps $z^{i\lceil\sqrt R\rceil}$ for the complete blocks below $T$. Compare the at most $\lceil\sqrt R\rceil-1$ remaining powers directly. A nonunit product detects a hit even when its gcd is $N$. Binary search finds the first positive prefix, then a single modular power gives the actual gcd. Monic product and remainder trees give bit cost $\widetilde O((\sqrt R+\log E)\operatorname{poly}(b))$ per word. This is the [earlier prefix test](round-3.md#splitting-from-an-unfactored-global-order-multiple), with the on-input local collision taking the place of a supplied full order multiple.

There is also a direct version for an eligible chart $t=w^{2^{h-1}}$ in which $h$ is the maximum local 2-height and the local 2-heights differ. Any odd $M$ that kills the odd component at **one** prime suffices. At that prime $t^M$ is its local sign. At every prime in the opposite 2-height class the residue either has the opposite sign or has a nontrivial odd component, so the gcd with the first sign is proper. Test both $\gcd(t^M-1,N)$ and $\gcd(t^M+1,N)$. The exponent $(M-1)/2$ gives the same root comparison in the two corrected unit charts. This can be a locally valid comparison when the two squares differ modulo $N$.

## Pooling rows whose local promises refer to different primes

Fix a public list of words $w_c=\prod_i a_i^{c_i}$ in small generators and use the same $E$ and $R$, or a specified $E_c$ and $R_c$ per word. Apply the search on a block $D\mid N$, always retaining $Q=2^b$. If a proper divisor appears, recurse on that block's two factors. Otherwise, for each word with $r_p\le R_c$ at any $p\mid D$, the search certifies the genuine modular relation $QE_ck_c c$. The bits of $c$ and the costs of evaluating all words are charged. A global hit certifies the whole relation even when the supplied promise names only one prime.

On a block that survives these searches and the pooled 2-primary comparison, let $k$ be the rational codimension of the span of all words with a local hit anywhere in that block. The saturation theorem bounds the number of distinct primes in the block by $k+1$. As a sufficient condition through recursion, suppose that for **every** prime $p\mid N$ the words whose residual order at $p$ is at most their cutoff span a subspace of codimension at most $k$. Any remaining block contains such a prime, and all its promised words either split that block or supply verified relations there. With $k=0$, prime-power recognition finishes the complete factorization. If the promise instead holds at only one unknown prime, this argument controls only blocks still containing that prime. Neither promise needs the local residual orders to be small on individual generators.

For example, modulo $91$, with $E=R=1$, neither base $2$ nor $17$ has a local hit at either prime. Both mixed words $2\cdot17$ and $2\cdot17^4$ do have global hits. Their exponent vectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,4)$ span $\mathbb Q^2$, and $2^3$ has unequal local 2-heights and splits $91$ by squaring. The word list is supplied in this example. ## The public shared factor on a semiprime

For $N=pq$ with distinct odd primes, take $E$ divisible by the odd part of $N-1$. For any word, $\gcd(h_p,h_q)$ divides both $p-1$ and $q-1$, hence also $N-1$. It follows prime power by prime power that $\gcd(r_p,r_q)=1$. If $z\ne 1$ then the two local residuals cannot be equal, so **any** first hit gives a proper divisor. If $z=1$ then $E$ itself certifies the odd part of this word. One can multiply $E$ by the odd maximal-prime-power product through a cutoff $Y$ (using powers at most $N$), absorbing the $Y$-smooth part of every local odd order as well.

One sufficient input-dependent condition is that for at least one prime $p\mid N$, the odd part $H_p$ of $p-1$ satisfies $H_p/\gcd(H_p,E)\le R$. Take the small generator list under Dirichlet RH. Test $z=a^{QE}$ on these generators until a nonidentity occurs. Any such generator has a residual at most $R$ at the promised prime, so one bounded search suffices. If all generators give the identity, $E$ kills the global odd component and the small generators' squaring chains split $N$. This bound is weaker than demanding any $p-1$ be smooth. A pointwise variant only requires a generator with $z\ne1$ and $r_p\le R$ at either prime, in which case scan the list. Both use only computed gcds and exponents. On $153253=331\cdot463$, taking $E=38313$ and $w=3$ changes the two odd orders from $165,231$ to $5,7$, so the first hit at five exposes $331$ without either full predecessor dividing $2^bE$. With $\log Y,\log R=O((\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3})$ the costs match the proposed one-third scale on the stated promises. ## Density of failure for short integer bases and the shared-factor scalar

Let $A,Y\ge2$ be arbitrary functions of the dyadic endpoint with $A,Y=X^{o(1)}$. For a proportion $1-o(1)$ of the prime pairs $X<p<q\le2X$, the residual at **both** primes is $X^{1/2-o(1)}$ or larger simultaneously for every integer $2\le a\le A$. Here use $E$ equal to the odd part of $N-1$ times the odd maximal-prime-power product through $Y$. This includes the odd parts of the orders of all their repeated squares, so it applies to the short equal-power chart library and any subselection of those parameters.

Take
\[
\eta=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2A)+\log(2Y)+\log\log X}{\log X}},\qquad K=\lfloor X^{1/2-\eta}\rfloor .
\]
If $\operatorname{ord}_p(a)\le K$ for any listed $a$, then $p$ divides $\prod_{2\le a\le A}\prod_{1\le j\le K}(a^j-1)$. Its log is $O(AK^2\log(2A))$. Since each prime in the dyad costs at least $\log X$ in this log, and there are $\asymp X/\log X$ primes there, only a proportion $O(AK^2\log(2A)/X)=o(1)$ can fail. This count takes a union over the whole base menu before choosing the pair.

For the common factor, if $\gcd(p-1,q-1)>X^\eta$, some $d>X^\eta$ divides both. For $d\le2X$ there are at most $2X/d$ integers in the dyad that are one modulo $d$ (when the progression is nonempty), up to an absolute factor. Summing the squared bounds over $d>X^\eta$ gives a relative exceptional proportion $O((\log X)^2X^{-\eta})$. Also every $p-1$ has a $Y$-smooth part at most $X^\eta$ outside a proportion $o(1)$. In fact the probability of a prime power $\ell^j\mid p-1$ with $\ell\le Y$ and $\ell^j>\sqrt X$ is $O(Y\log X/\sqrt X)$, by checking the first such power for each $\ell$. For the other powers the expected total logarithmic contribution is at most
\[
C\sum_{\ell\le Y}\sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\log\ell}{\varphi(\ell^j)}
  =O(\log(2Y)).
\]
The bound follows from Brun–Titchmarsh for each modulus at most $\sqrt X$, the dyadic prime number theorem, and partial summation of the ordinary Chebyshev bound for primes in the last sum. Markov's inequality gives an exceptional fraction $O(\log(2Y)/(\eta\log X))=o(1)$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, Brun–Titchmarsh](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/large_sieve.pdf)

On each remaining pair, $\gcd(p-1,N-1)=\gcd(p-1,q-1)$, and the factor removed by $Q$ and the smooth scalar is bounded by the full $Y$-smooth part of $p-1$. The latter includes the powers of two since $Y\ge2$. Thus the total divisor removed from any local order at $p$ is at most $X^{2\eta}$. Dividing the order lower bound by this quantity gives the assertion, and the same proof applies at $q$. There is no hit at the one-third cutoff in this search on these inputs. The estimate is the multiplicative analogue of the height bound for [fixed elliptic point order cofactors](smooth-marked-order-tail.md#a-uniform-rational-torsion-sieve), where the inputs are point coordinates and the group-law height has quadratic growth. It leaves open additional public scalars tailored to the input, higher-entropy bases, and obtaining a spanning supply of mixed words on every block.

The same argument covers any dyad-specific catalogue of $M$ nonidentity positive rational words $u/v$ with $\log\max(u,v)\le H_0$, provided $M H_0=X^{o(1)}$. Replace the integer product by the nonzero differences $u^j-v^j$, also including $uv$ to exclude nonunit reductions. The log of the resulting absolute product is $O(MH_0(K^2+1))$. Use $\log(2+M H_0)$ in place of $\log(2A)$ in the definition of $\eta$. The common-factor and smooth-part estimates are unchanged. Thus the obstruction permits arbitrary input-dependent selection from the entire catalogue. For example, take all nonzero integer words on the primes through $(\log X)^C$, with each coefficient of magnitude at most $\exp(O(W))$ and at most $o(\log X/W)$ nonzero coefficients, where $W=(\log X)^{1/3}(\log\log X)^{2/3}$. Counting supports and coefficients gives $\log M=o(\log X)$, and the log height is bounded by the sum of the coefficient magnitudes times $O(\log\log X)$. Such a catalogue supplies no local rows at the one-third cutoff on the density-one set, even if only a few rows would have completed a saturated lattice. This bound is on the total catalogue across a dyad.

## Relation to prescribed cofactor marks

[Constructing one or two prescribed smooth norm marks](cogentic-cofactor-point-construction.md) addresses finding coefficients in polynomial boxes. A smooth marked norm enters this search only through a verified modular relation on the chosen generators or through a justified bound on a local residual order. Giving one or two smooth norms supplies neither of these consequences by itself. The local relation promise above specifies the missing implication while permitting the witnessing prime and the word to vary.

The finite arithmetic check compared 28,900 first hits with exact prime-field orders on semiprimes and prime-square times prime inputs, verified 14,430 eligible one-sided chart comparisons, and checked both fixtures above. The search complexity uses the existing fast prefix test.
