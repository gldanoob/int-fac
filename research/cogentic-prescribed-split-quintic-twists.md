# Factoring from either twist of a fixed split quintic

Fix five distinct rational branch points. After a fixed rational affine change use distinct integers \(e_1,\ldots,e_5\), and put \(f(X)=\prod_i(X-e_i)\). Assume Dirichlet RH for the [short linear-form color estimate](cogentic-cm-linear-forms.md#uniform-colors-of-short-linear-forms). For an odd \(N\) with at least two distinct prime factors, there is a deterministic proper-divisor search of cost \(B\operatorname{poly}_f(\log N)\) if the odd part of the Jacobian exponent of either twist of \(Y^2=f(X)\) is \(B\)-smooth at any good prime \(p\mid N\). The prime and twist are unknown. A residual bound \(D\) gives the more general cost \(\widetilde O((B+\sqrt D)\operatorname{poly}_f(\log N))\) below. This extends the [fixed elliptic twist guarantee](cogentic-prescribed-elliptic-twists.md) to split quintics with no elliptic isogeny factor required.

## Prescribing the twist and the divisor coset

For \(c=u/v\) put \(d=f(c)\). The monic model
\[
 C_d\colon Y^2=d^5f(X/d)=\prod_i(X-de_i)
\]
has point \(R=(dc,d^3)\). It is the twist \(Y^2=d f(X)\) in rescaled coordinates. Work at a good odd prime with \(v,d\) units. Let \(A_i\) be the quadratic-character bit of \(c-e_i\), and let \(\omega=\sum_i A_i\). Then \(\omega\) is the twist bit.

For the Jacobian class \(P=[R-\infty]\), the five descent bits are \(B_i=\omega+A_i\). Indeed \(X-de_i\) has divisor twice the difference of its branch point and infinity. Evaluating the reduced Tate pairing on \(P\) gives the squareclass of \(dc-de_i\). At infinity, use local parameter \(X^2/Y\), for which the leading value of \(X\) is one. The first four branch differences form a basis of rational two-torsion, with the sum of all five equal to zero. By perfectness of the pairing, the first four bits identify \(J_d(\mathbb F_p)/2J_d(\mathbb F_p)\), and \(\sum_i B_i=0\). Thus a prescribed \(\omega\) and an arbitrary four-bit coset uniquely determine all the \(A_i\). The identification is independent of the choice of representative of the twist class, since the isomorphism scales all five differences by a square. [Frey and Rück, Tate pairing](https://doi.org/10.2307/2153546) · [Local parameter and composite arithmetic](quadratic-cubic-genus-two.md#a-two-torsion-character-of-the-marked-point)

Apply the short-form estimate to \(v,u-e_1v,\ldots,u-e_5v\), with square signs for \(v\) and prescribed signs for the other forms at two distinct primes. These six forms are pairwise nonproportional. A fixed positive box, with \(u\) bounded away from zero and \(v\) sufficiently small compared with \(u\), places all six values in the positive short range. The estimate supplies a polynomial-height public menu of fractions that realizes every prescribed twist and coset at the pair. Trial division handles primes below the public form-value and root-difference bounds. Gcd tests on all denominators and root differences either certify good reductions throughout or return a proper divisor when a nonunit occurs at only part of the modulus.

Choose the promised twist at \(p\) and either twist at a second prime \(q\). At the prime with larger local two-primary group exponent, choose the coset of an element of maximal two-primary order. Every representative of that coset attains the maximal order, by projection to a maximal cyclic summand. At the other prime choose the double coset, whose representatives have strictly smaller two-primary order. The chosen pair therefore yields unequal two-primary order heights. These choices prove existence of a menu entry. They are not needed as input to the enumeration.

## Checked multiplication and the odd residual

Use Cantor arithmetic on reduced monic Mumford pairs. Check by gcd every nonzero coefficient used for a leading-term decision or an inverse in the Euclidean algorithms and reductions. If none exposes a factor, the degrees and operations commute with reduction at every prime of \(N\). In particular an identity at one prime and a nonidentity at another cannot survive, because a monic polynomial of positive degree cannot reduce to the identity polynomial of degree zero. This also works for repeated prime factors of \(N\). [Gcd-checked Cantor code](../experiments/genus-two-cantor.gp)

Let \(U\) be the product of the largest odd prime powers at most \(16N^2\) for primes at most \(B\). Weil's bound \(\#J(\mathbb F_p)\le(1+\sqrt p)^4\le16N^2\) covers all local orders. After multiplying the marked class by \(U\), repeatedly double up to the public bound \(h=\lceil\log_2(16N^2)\rceil\). At the promised prime the odd order is killed. If it survives at the other prime, the reductions reach the identity on only one side. If it dies at both, the two-primary heights are still different because \(U\) is odd. Either event exposes a factor. Enumerating charts costs only a fixed polynomial in \(\log N\), and the stages of odd multiplication cost \(B\) times such a polynomial. [Lenstra, Pila and Pomerance, hyperelliptic smoothness test](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/pila/HyperII.pdf)

For the second stage it suffices that the promised twist have residual odd exponent at most \(D\) after division by its gcd with \(U\). Put \(Z=[2^h U]P\) and search for a positive local annihilator of \(Z\) at most \(D\). The elliptic block decomposition works with the full reduced Mumford pair in place of a single abscissa. Partition \([1,D]\) into centered intervals of length \(2\lceil\sqrt D\rceil+1\) and one shorter tail. Check the centers and tail directly. Form both signed baby multiples for offsets from 1 to \(\lceil\sqrt D\rceil\) and the giant multiples at the centers. Every local annihilator in a full block away from its center yields equality between a giant and one of the signed babies at the corresponding prime. Identity discrepancies and local degree mismatches are already handled by checked Cantor arithmetic. Within each positive Mumford degree, use all coefficients of \(u,v\) other than the known leading coefficient of \(u\), padding \(v\) to the appropriate fixed length.

A true equality of tuples modulo a prime either occurs globally or has at least one coefficient equal only modulo a proper factor. Detect global tuple equality by sorting. For each of the at most four coefficient coordinates, use monic product and remainder trees for the scalar collision calculation. Deduplicate the residues in the baby coordinate list. Evaluate its product polynomial at the giant residues, using the derivative value in place of an evaluation at an exact root to omit that equal factor. A nonunit bucket yields a proper gcd by scanning that one row and skipping globally equal scalar residues. This can find a proper gcd even if the other coordinates of the pair disagree, which is already sufficient. If there is no proper scalar gcd and no global tuple equality, there was no local tuple equality. The costs of sorting, group operations and scalar multipoint calculations are soft-linear in \(\sqrt D\) times a fixed polynomial in \(\log N\). [Scalar collision and tuple code](../experiments/cogentic-unit-collision-search.py)

A global tuple match gives a verified positive annihilator of \(Z\). Multiply \(U\) by its odd part and restart the doubling test on the original marked class. This kills the odd order in every prime-field reduction and preserves the two prescribed heights. A hit at the promised prime thus suffices even when the second stage returns a global relation. The group-exponent promises suffice for every marked class on the chosen twist. Extraction only needs the appropriate annihilation or residual bound for the selected marked class.

## An example without an elliptic isogeny factor

Take roots \(0,1,3,7,11\). At \(p=439\) the untwisted Frobenius polynomial is
\[
 X^4+8X^3+366X^2+3512X+192721.
\]
It is irreducible modulo five and hence over the rationals. A product of elliptic curves up to rational isogeny would reduce at this good prime to a product up to isogeny and give a quadratic factor. The fixed rational Jacobian therefore has no elliptic isogeny factor over \(\mathbb Q\). Its group at 439 has order \(196608=3\cdot2^{16}\).

On \(N=152333=439\cdot347\), neither prime meets the \(B=3\) promise for the two torus orders, the two Gaussian twists, or the two twists of the previously tested elliptic model with roots \(0,1,3\). The candidate orders in that order are \((438,440,440,440,412,468)\) and \((346,348,348,348,368,328)\). The genus-two chart \(c=-4\) is untwisted at both primes and its marked orders are 32 and 15238. Checked multiplication by 3 followed by doubling gives a proper gcd. The stage-one executable uses the same Cantor law and verifies this example. The second-stage replay uses the tuple collision routine. It splits 1073 by a coordinate gcd and 2923 after recovering a shared global relation of 5. The first stage misses both at the same bounds. Run the executable with `--replay-stage-two` to reproduce these two cases.

~~~sh
python3 -B experiments/cogentic-split-quintic-twists.py 152333 --bound 3 --menu 1
~~~

This adds a verified source of local orders outside those fixed elliptic and torus examples. It does not guarantee a suitable twist or residual exponent for arbitrary inputs. The short menu chooses the local point coset and the twist. It cannot change either twist's Jacobian exponent at a fixed prime.
