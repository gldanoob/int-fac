# Extracting order certificates modulo a composite in a norm-one torus

Take $R_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)[i]$ with $i^2=-1$, with conjugation denoted by a bar. For every prime $\ell\equiv1\pmod4$, choose a Gaussian prime $\pi$ of norm $\ell$. If $\ell\nmid N$, set $t_\ell=\pi/\bar\pi\in R_N^\times$. A verified exponent for this base can split $N$ with the same type of gcd chain as in the [ordinary order extractor](cogentic-dense-exponents.md). This choice of bases also permits CM number fields whose ordinary unit group has positive rank. The suggested relation calculation needs only rational ideal valuations. Williams' $p+1$ method uses the same local norm-one groups. [Williams, 1982](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1982-0658227-7) · [CM fields and their units, Milne, Proposition 5.12](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT301.pdf)

## Which prime bases split the input

At an odd prime $p\nmid\ell$ the norm-one subgroup of $R_p^\times$ is cyclic of order $M_p=p-(\frac{-1}{p})$, and
$$
 t_\ell^{M_p/2}=(\tfrac{\ell}{p}).
$$
If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, reduction realizes $\pi$ as $(u,v)$ with $uv=\ell$. Then $u/v$ has quadratic character $(\frac{uv}{p})$. If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, $R_p$ is the quadratic field and $t_\ell=\pi^{1-p}$. Its $M_p/2$ power equals $\ell^{-(p-1)/2}$, the same sign.

For distinct odd $p,q\mid N$, if $v_2(M_p)>v_2(M_q)$, a negative symbol at $p$ makes the two local orders have different 2-parts. Exchange $p,q$ if needed. In the equal-valuation case opposite symbols suffice. The character being tested on $\ell$ is therefore either the quadratic character modulo $p$ or the product of the quadratic characters modulo $p$ and $q$. It and its product with the nonprincipal character modulo 4 are nonprincipal. Dirichlet GRH and the ordinary prime theorem consequently give $(1/2+o(1))$ useful signs among the primes $\ell\equiv1\pmod4$ in a dyadic interval $(B/2,B]$; for example $B\ge(\log(2N))^6$ suffices for the usual explicit-formula error on this interval. The statement also holds after deleting prime divisors of $N$. Trial division can return such a divisor directly. Alternatively any subset with a fixed density strictly greater than half **within** these split primes includes a useful base.

If $t_\ell^E=1$ in $R_N$, start at $w=t_\ell^{E/2^{v_2(E)}}$ and repeatedly square. At each step form $\gcd(N,\Re(w)-1,\Im(w))$ using integer representatives. An element equal to one modulo $p$ and different from one modulo $q$ gives a proper divisor, also when there are repeated prime powers. Distinct local 2-parts ensure that one of these steps separates the two primes. One may construct $\pi$ by checking $a^2+b^2=\ell$ for $a\le\sqrt\ell$ when enumerating the factor base. No square root of $-1$ modulo the composite is required.

## A uniform discriminant menu for smooth $p+1$ or $p-1$

Assume Dirichlet RH. Let $N$ be odd with at least two distinct prime divisors. If some $p\mid N$ has $B$-smooth odd part in either $p+1$ or $p-1$, a deterministic search finds a proper divisor in $B\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$ bit operations. In particular the time is polynomial in the input bit length when $B$ is a fixed power of $\log N$. The search uses no input nonresidue and imposes no congruence class on the promised $p$. It can also be used at each recursive block when all but at most one distinct prime factor of the original input satisfy either smoothness condition with the same cutoff. Prime powers can be recognized separately.

Put $Q=\lceil\log_2(2N)\rceil^6$. First trial divide through $Q$. Enumerate primes $s\le Q$ with $s\equiv3\pmod4$, and for each enumerate odd primes $\ell\le Q$ with $(-s/\ell)=1$. Set $D=-s$, $\omega=(1+\sqrt D)/2$, and $h=h(D)$. The class number is odd since $D$ is a prime discriminant. This parity can also be checked directly on reduced primitive positive forms. Away from the boundaries $|b|=a$ and $a=c$, forms $(a,b,c)$ pair under $b\mapsto-b$, and $b$ is odd. On $b=a$ the relation $s=a(4c-a)$ and $a\le\sqrt{s/3}$ force $a=b=1$. On $a=c$, write $s=(2a-b)(2a+b)$ with $0<b\le a$. Since $s$ is prime this forces $a=b=1$ and $s=3$, already counted. Thus there is exactly one unpaired form. Enumerating the reduced forms using $a\le\sqrt{s/3}$ and $|b|\le a$ also computes $h$ in $\operatorname{poly}(s)$ bit time.

Choose either prime ideal $\mathfrak l$ over $\ell$. Since $\mathfrak l^h$ is principal, a generator $z$ has norm $\ell^h$. To obtain it, find a root of $X^2-X+(s+1)/4\bmod\ell$ by trial through $\ell$ and Hensel lift that root to $r\bmod\ell^h$. The root is simple because $\ell\nmid D$. The lattice with basis $(\ell^h,0),(-r,1)$ in the integral basis $1,\omega$ is $\mathfrak l^h$. Exact planar Gauss reduction for the positive definite norm $a^2+ab+(s+1)b^2/4$ finds a shortest nonzero vector. Its norm is $\ell^h$, since any nonzero element of this principal ideal is a generator times an algebraic integer of positive integral norm. All entries have polynomial bit length in $\log N$, since $s,\ell\le Q$ and $h$ is at most the number of form candidates. This computation uses no bounds on class-group generators or regulator.

Set $t=z/\bar z=z^2/\ell^h$ in $(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)[\omega]$. For a prime $p\mid N$, the local norm-one subgroup has cyclic order $M_p=p-(D/p)$. In the split case write the two local components of $z$ as $u,v$, so $uv=\ell^h$ and the first component of $t$ is $u/v$. Its quadratic character is $(\ell/p)^h=(\ell/p)$. In the inert case conjugation is Frobenius, so $t=z^{1-p}$ and $t^{(p+1)/2}=(\ell^h)^{-(p-1)/2}=(\ell/p)$. Thus both cases give
\[
                         t^{M_p/2}=(\ell/p).
\]
The odd exponent
\[
                   E_B=\prod_{\substack{r\le B\\r\ {\rm odd\ prime}}}r^{\lfloor\log_r N\rfloor}
\]
kills the odd part of the local order at the promised $p$ whenever $M_p$ has the promised smoothness. It need not kill the odd order at any other prime. Starting at $t^{E_B}$, square at most $\lceil\log_2 N\rceil$ times and at each step compute the gcd with $N$ of the two coordinates of the difference from one. A second prime $q\mid N$ whose odd order survives never reaches one. Otherwise the first identity indices are the respective 2-adic order heights, which are unchanged by $E_B$. If those heights differ the chain again returns a proper gcd. These statements compare reductions modulo distinct primes, so hold also for non-squarefree $N$.

To see that the public menu succeeds, fix the promised $p$ and any other $q\mid N$ in the proof. The three characters modulo $4,p,q$ are independent, so one can force $s\equiv3\pmod4$ and prescribe both $(D/p)$ and $(D/q)$. Set $(D/p)=-1$ for a smooth $p+1$ and $+1$ for a smooth $p-1$. After choosing $s$, the characters in $\ell$ modulo $s,p,q$ are again independent, with $(D/\ell)=(\ell/s)$. Prescribe $(D/\ell)=1$ and choose $(\ell/p),(\ell/q)$ to be negative on the larger of $v_2(M_p),v_2(M_q)$ and positive on the other, breaking ties arbitrarily. The resulting local 2-adic order heights differ. For each character triple, orthogonality and the Dirichlet-RH prime sum show that any prescribed sign pattern has von Mangoldt weight $Q/16+O(\sqrt Q\log^2(QM))$ in $(Q/2,Q]$, where $M$ is the product of the relevant moduli. Replacing prime powers by primes or deleting primes of $N$ leaves a positive main term for all sufficiently large $N$, since $\log M=O(\log N)$. All patterns have effective uniform thresholds. Trial division covers the finite range below that threshold. [Uniform Dirichlet character estimate](cogentic-pooled-order-certificates.md#what-density-of-certified-primes-suffices)

The search tries polynomially many pairs $(s,\ell)$ in the input bit length. The exponent has $O(B\log N)$ bits and can be assembled by sieving and a balanced product tree. Modular powering costs $B\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$ per pair, with the stated bound also absorbing the menu size. The $p+1$ branch removes the fixed-nonresidue hypothesis of Źrałek's Theorem 5.1 using Dirichlet RH. That theorem is unconditional for a fixed admissible parameter and its cost may depend on the field and its class number. For $p-1$, Źrałek's Corollary 4.6 already gives an unconditional uniform split. Williams introduced the $p+1$ method using Lucas sequences. Bach and Shallit's general cyclotomic algorithm uses random elements and gives a randomized polynomial-time bound under ERH. [Źrałek, Corollary 4.6 and Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.4102#page=7) · [Williams, 1982](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1982-0658227-7) · [Bach and Shallit, Theorems 4–5](https://www.ams.org/journals/mcom/1989-52-185/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1/S0025-5718-1989-0947467-1.pdf#page=14)

At a one-third cutoff $\log B=O((\log Y)^{1/3}(\log\log Y)^{2/3})$, the smoothness promise holds for a proportion tending to zero of primes $p\in[Y,2Y]$. Indeed for $B\ge2$ the odd-part condition is equivalent to the full $B$-smoothness condition. For large $B$ use Rankin's estimate at $\sigma=1-1/\log B$. The truncated Euler product has logarithm $O(\log\log B)$, since $r^{-\sigma}\le e/r$ for $r\le B$. It follows that
\[
 \Psi(2Y+1,B)\ll Y\exp(-\log Y/\log B)(\log B)^{O(1)}=o(Y/\log Y).
\]
Each of the maps $p\mapsto p-1$ and $p\mapsto p+1$ is injective. By the prime number theorem the count for either shift is $o(\pi(2Y)-\pi(Y))$. The same vanishing conclusion holds if $B$ is bounded, by bounding it with any fixed sufficiently large cutoff. This gives density zero in balanced semiprime pools with both primes in that interval, for the promised input class itself.

The [finite replay](../experiments/cogentic-varying-norm-torus.py) checks 57 principal generators and 1,311 local half powers. It splits $5459=53\cdot103$ with $D=-23$, $h=3$, $\ell=3$ and $B=3$. Independently, PARI/GP verified the ideal generators $1+2\omega$ for $D=-23$, $h=3$ and $13+2\omega$ for $D=-47$, $h=5$, both above $\ell=3$. With $E_B=2187$ these give gcd chains $(1,53)$ on $N=53\cdot47$ and $(107)$ on $N=107\cdot53$, respectively. In each example the promised prime is inert, the other is split, and neither predecessor is $3$-smooth.

## CM fields remove the archimedean rows

Let $K$ be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field. For $\alpha\ne0$ put $u(\alpha)=\alpha/\bar\alpha$. Every complex embedding sends $u(\alpha)$ to a number of modulus one. If an integer product of such elements has zero valuation at every finite prime, it is an algebraic integer unit. Kronecker's theorem then makes it a root of unity. Only one signed valuation row per conjugate pair of distinct prime ideals is needed, and fixed primes contribute zero. In degree $D$ any root of unity of order $j$ satisfies $\phi(j)\le D$, hence $j\le2D^2$ by $\phi(j)^2\ge j/2$ on prime powers and multiplication. Thus $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2D^2)$ is a computable annihilator of polynomial bit length. For $D=4$ the possible orders divide 120. Integer relations among these signed valuations replace the real-unit logarithms and continued fractions of Cogentic's totally real extractor. [Original Lemma 7.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=34)

The executable example takes $m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$, $s\in\{2,3,4,5\}$, $c=N-m^2-sm$ and $f(T)=T^2+sT+c$. Keep the positive nonsquare discriminants. Each $K=\mathbb Q(\theta,i)$ with $f(\theta)=0$ is a quartic CM field and sends $\theta\mapsto m$ in $R_N$ on the indicated order. For a Gaussian integer $z=a+bi$, the norm of $z-\theta$ down to $\mathbb Q(i)$ is $f(z)$. Keep marks for which every prime of this Gaussian integer lies over a rational prime $p\le B$ coprime to $2\operatorname{disc}(f)$. At those primes the quadratic order is locally maximal and unramified. The only prime above the given Gaussian prime dividing $z-\theta$ has $\theta\equiv z$ and residue degree one relative to the Gaussian prime. Its coefficient is the Gaussian valuation of $f(z)$. At a split $p$, conjugation exchanges the two images of $i$ and fixes the root value of $\theta$. At an inert $p$, it conjugates the projective root $a+bi$ in $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. The program uses these two explicit row conventions.

The rational image $z-m$ has a partial Gaussian factorization. Divide out its rational integer content, powers of $1+i$, and all split Gaussian primes above the factor base. Rational content has trivial conjugate quotient. The contribution from $1+i$ and changes of associate have order dividing four. Its signed rational row at a split prime is minus the valuation at $\pi$ plus the valuation at $\bar\pi$. Match the remaining Gaussian factor exactly up to a Gaussian unit, retaining all complete columns and the differences of partial columns with the same remaining factor. Inert primes disappear after rational content is removed. Reject marks with $\gcd(N,\operatorname{Norm}(z-m))>1$ so the localized evaluations are defined, including for negative exponents. Every $K$ uses the same rational rows.

When a rational column-space solve gives a combination $Vz=k e_\ell$ targeting a single rational row, all field products are norm-one units. Their 120th powers are one, and the partial rational factors cancel. Thus $t_\ell^{120k}=1$ in $R_N$. The program explicitly verifies this modular equation before testing either coefficient for a factor. The construction depends only on the input and search limits. It uses no class-number, regulator or element-order oracle.

At height 40 and cutoff 71, the [GP implementation](../experiments/cogentic-cm-torus.gp) splits $5767=73\cdot79$ using $\pi=1+2i$, exponent $57{,}600$, and a matrix with 94 columns and rank 29. Both factors lie beyond the trial primes used for the norm relations. Quantum or analytic assumptions are unnecessary for verification of any finite certificate. A worst-case factoring bound from this construction would require a supply theorem for its Gaussian smooth values on every input.
