# Extracting order certificates modulo a composite in a norm-one torus

Take $R_N=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)[i]$ with $i^2=-1$, with conjugation denoted by a bar. For every prime $\ell\equiv1\pmod4$, choose a Gaussian prime $\pi$ of norm $\ell$. If $\ell\nmid N$, set $t_\ell=\pi/\bar\pi\in R_N^\times$. A verified exponent for this base can split $N$ with the same type of gcd chain as in the [ordinary order extractor](cogentic-dense-exponents.md). This choice of bases also permits CM number fields whose ordinary unit group has positive rank. The suggested relation calculation needs only rational ideal valuations. Williams' $p+1$ method uses the same local norm-one groups. [Williams, 1982](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1982-0658227-7) · [CM fields and their units, Milne, Proposition 5.12](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT301.pdf)

## Which prime bases split the input

At an odd prime $p\nmid\ell$ the norm-one subgroup of $R_p^\times$ is cyclic of order $M_p=p-(\frac{-1}{p})$, and
$$
 t_\ell^{M_p/2}=(\tfrac{\ell}{p}).
$$
If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, reduction realizes $\pi$ as $(u,v)$ with $uv=\ell$. Then $u/v$ has quadratic character $(\frac{uv}{p})$. If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, $R_p$ is the quadratic field and $t_\ell=\pi^{1-p}$. Its $M_p/2$ power equals $\ell^{-(p-1)/2}$, the same sign.

For distinct odd $p,q\mid N$, if $v_2(M_p)>v_2(M_q)$, a negative symbol at $p$ makes the two local orders have different 2-parts. Exchange $p,q$ if needed. In the equal-valuation case opposite symbols suffice. The character being tested on $\ell$ is therefore either the quadratic character modulo $p$ or the product of the quadratic characters modulo $p$ and $q$. It and its product with the nonprincipal character modulo 4 are nonprincipal. Dirichlet GRH and the ordinary prime theorem consequently give $(1/2+o(1))$ useful signs among the primes $\ell\equiv1\pmod4$ in a dyadic interval $(B/2,B]$; for example $B\ge(\log(2N))^6$ suffices for the usual explicit-formula error on this interval. The statement also holds after deleting prime divisors of $N$. Trial division can return such a divisor directly. Alternatively any subset with a fixed density strictly greater than half **within** these split primes includes a useful base.

If $t_\ell^E=1$ in $R_N$, start at $w=t_\ell^{E/2^{v_2(E)}}$ and repeatedly square. At each step form $\gcd(N,\Re(w)-1,\Im(w))$ using integer representatives. An element equal to one modulo $p$ and different from one modulo $q$ gives a proper divisor, also when there are repeated prime powers. Distinct local 2-parts ensure that one of these steps separates the two primes. One may construct $\pi$ by checking $a^2+b^2=\ell$ for $a\le\sqrt\ell$ when enumerating the factor base. No square root of $-1$ modulo the composite is required.

## CM fields remove the archimedean rows

Let $K$ be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field. For $\alpha\ne0$ put $u(\alpha)=\alpha/\bar\alpha$. Every complex embedding sends $u(\alpha)$ to a number of modulus one. If an integer product of such elements has zero valuation at every finite prime, it is an algebraic integer unit. Kronecker's theorem then makes it a root of unity. Only one signed valuation row per conjugate pair of distinct prime ideals is needed, and fixed primes contribute zero. In degree $D$ any root of unity of order $j$ satisfies $\phi(j)\le D$, hence $j\le2D^2$ by $\phi(j)^2\ge j/2$ on prime powers and multiplication. Thus $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2D^2)$ is a computable annihilator of polynomial bit length. For $D=4$ the possible orders divide 120. Integer relations among these signed valuations replace the real-unit logarithms and continued fractions of Cogentic's totally real extractor. [Original Lemma 7.5](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=34)

The executable example takes $m=\lfloor\sqrt N\rfloor$, $s\in\{2,3,4,5\}$, $c=N-m^2-sm$ and $f(T)=T^2+sT+c$. Keep the positive nonsquare discriminants. Each $K=\mathbb Q(\theta,i)$ with $f(\theta)=0$ is a quartic CM field and sends $\theta\mapsto m$ in $R_N$ on the indicated order. For a Gaussian integer $z=a+bi$, the norm of $z-\theta$ down to $\mathbb Q(i)$ is $f(z)$. Keep marks for which every prime of this Gaussian integer lies over a rational prime $p\le B$ coprime to $2\operatorname{disc}(f)$. At those primes the quadratic order is locally maximal and unramified. The only prime above the given Gaussian prime dividing $z-\theta$ has $\theta\equiv z$ and residue degree one relative to the Gaussian prime. Its coefficient is the Gaussian valuation of $f(z)$. At a split $p$, conjugation exchanges the two images of $i$ and fixes the root value of $\theta$. At an inert $p$, it conjugates the projective root $a+bi$ in $\mathbb F_{p^2}$. The program uses these two explicit row conventions.

The rational image $z-m$ has a partial Gaussian factorization. Divide out its rational integer content, powers of $1+i$, and all split Gaussian primes above the factor base. Rational content has trivial conjugate quotient. The contribution from $1+i$ and changes of associate have order dividing four. Its signed rational row at a split prime is minus the valuation at $\pi$ plus the valuation at $\bar\pi$. Match the remaining Gaussian factor exactly up to a Gaussian unit, retaining all complete columns and the differences of partial columns with the same remaining factor. Inert primes disappear after rational content is removed. Reject marks with $\gcd(N,\operatorname{Norm}(z-m))>1$ so the localized evaluations are defined, including for negative exponents. Every $K$ uses the same rational rows.

When a rational column-space solve gives a combination $Vz=k e_\ell$ targeting a single rational row, all field products are norm-one units. Their 120th powers are one, and the partial rational factors cancel. Thus $t_\ell^{120k}=1$ in $R_N$. The program explicitly verifies this modular equation before testing either coefficient for a factor. The construction depends only on the input and search limits. It uses no class-number, regulator or element-order oracle.

At height 40 and cutoff 71, the [GP implementation](../experiments/cogentic-cm-torus.gp) splits $5767=73\cdot79$ using $\pi=1+2i$, exponent $57{,}600$, and a matrix with 94 columns and rank 29. Both factors lie beyond the trial primes used for the norm relations. Quantum or analytic assumptions are unnecessary for verification of any finite certificate. A worst-case factoring bound from this construction would require a supply theorem for its Gaussian smooth values on every input.
