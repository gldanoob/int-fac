# Verification of the deterministic two-thirds worst-case bound

The internal review supports the following paper result. With the extraction lemmas of Cogentic's deterministic companion, the fixed four-coordinate search completely factors every integer $N\ge2$ in
$$
 \exp\!\left((400000+o(1))(\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}\right)
$$
deterministic bit operations. The coefficient is a conservative upper bound. Write $L=\ln N$, $z=\ln L$, $H=L^{1/3}z^{2/3}$ and $V=L^{2/3}z^{1/3}$. The conclusion is a deduction from the pinned paper and the new smooth-window argument. The pinned paper's own Theorem 1.1 gives a proper divisor in $O(\exp(3L^{3/4}))$. Ordinary RH and Dirichlet GRH over the rationals used in our deduction have independent Lean verification. The coefficient $400000$ refers to the paper’s polynomial-time extraction routine. The local Lean proof bounds the bit trace of a concrete exhaustive-minor implementation with its own leading constant. For coprime forced prefactors, the expected loss of normalized absolute score at boxed smooth marks that fail actual first-stage retention is at most $\exp(-Kz_b^2)\sum_a|w_a|$. This bound includes shared primes and singular derivatives. For ordinary finite rows, Lean also proves that the sum of admitted entrywise second-moment errors suffices to select one positive boxed tuple with small total squared error. For polynomially many centered cell tests of absolute size and total variation at most one, a single compatible positive tuple also gives normalized retained smooth scores of magnitude below $2\exp(-Kz_b^2)$ for every test, when the forced prefactors are coprime throughout the tests. At most $2d$ rows are listed per prime across both polynomials, so an error of at most $\delta$ in each entry supported on same-prime blocks changes a quadratic form by at most $2d\delta\lVert v\rVert^2$. ## Independent contour at the fixed parameters

Put $b_B=\ln B$, $\beta=199/200$, $T_1=B^{1/200}$ and $T=B^{1/5}$. The squarefree Euler product uses primes in $(\sqrt B,B]$. For the clean windows the ratio $R=Y/S$ is eventually at most $B^{0.17}$, including after division by $G=\gcd(r,q)$. The compact Gaussian's high-frequency bound from the proof of Cogentic Lemma 8.4 gives $\exp(-c(|t|/R)^{2/3})$ when $|t|/R$ is large. At $T$ its exponent has magnitude at least $cB^{0.02}$, which dominates the trivial Euler product bound $\exp(O(B^{0.005}))$ on $\sigma\ge\beta$. The integrated tail is negligible even after summing the characters. [Cogentic, p. 43](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=43)

Below $T$, apply the GRH estimate for $\theta(x,\chi)$ and sum by parts. The error in the prime integral for $\log F_\chi(\sigma+it)$ is bounded by $(1+|t|)B^{(1/2-\beta)/2} L^{O(1)}=O(B^{-0.04})$. Prime powers and the polynomially many removed factors satisfy this tolerance as well. The nonprincipal integral on $\sigma=\beta$ costs at most $\exp((-498.005+o(1))V)$ in total, using $\log q\le(2+o(1))V$ and $\log Y=(100001+o(1))V$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, Theorem 13.7, p. 425](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/MNTI/17.0_pp_419_451_Conditional_estimates.pdf#page=7)

For the principal character let $\alpha$ be the saddle and $\lambda=(1-\alpha)b_B$. At height $1$ move to $\sigma_1=\alpha-z/(6b_B)$, then at $T_1$ move to $\beta$. Use the reflected segments at negative heights. The first shift costs $\exp(o(u))$ in the Euler product at height $1$, against central mass $F(\alpha)=\exp((1+o(1))u)$. Between the shifts the factor $Y^{\sigma_1-\alpha}$ has logarithm $-(16.6668\ldots+o(1))H$, against segment length $\exp((5+o(1))H)$. On and after the second shift the prime integral is $O(B^{1-\sigma}/(|t|b_B))=O(1/b_B)$. The far principal contribution is $\exp((-500.005+o(1))V)$. Dividing by the main density costs only $\exp((100.001/3+o(1))H)$. For $1/b_B\le |t|\le1$, averaging $1-\cos(t\log p)$ on the top band of relative log-width $1/\lambda$ gives loss $\gg u/\lambda^2\gg z^2$ in the logarithm of the Euler product, including near a multiple of $2\pi$. The low-frequency saddle estimate and the rough bound for unclean pairs are the ones in the window audit. [Saddle and close-pair bounds](cogentic-fixed-window-audit.md)

For a nonunit residue, an ineligible or repeated prime in $G$ gives zero mass. Otherwise divide $Y,S,q$ by $G$ together and delete every prime of $G$ from the allowed band, including primes absent from $q/G$. This preserves $R$ and the normalized Gaussian and is precisely the support on p. 50. [Cogentic, pp. 49–51](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=49)

## Reproduction and scope

Run `make check-two-thirds-lean` for the factoring proofs. The Lake project pins Lean 4.33.1, the matching Mathlib commit and the frozen Dirichlet GRH source. A fresh checkout needs network access to fetch the dependencies and their caches. Run `make check-two-thirds` with a Python interpreter that imports `sage.all` to also run the five finite replays. To reproduce in this workspace use `env DOT_SAGE=/tmp/rsa-sage-check make check-two-thirds`.

The default Lake target checks the deductions from the precise critical-line statement in the frozen upstream source. Its public endpoints are audited at build time for dependence on additional axioms. The optional `lake build CogenticWithGRH` target imports the original all-character proof and applies it to the character bound, the small generating bases and the complete two-thirds factoring theorem. The default target and the application types were checked here. A local replay of the full combined import would require restoring the large original proof archive. The independent checks published for that exact upstream theorem are available separately.
