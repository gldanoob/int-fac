# Verification of the deterministic two-thirds worst-case bound

The internal review supports the following paper result. With the extraction lemmas of Cogentic's deterministic companion, the fixed four-coordinate search completely factors every integer $N\ge2$ in
$$
 \exp\!\left((400000+o(1))(\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}\right)
$$
deterministic bit operations. The coefficient is a conservative upper bound. Write $L=\ln N$, $z=\ln L$, $H=L^{1/3}z^{2/3}$ and $V=L^{2/3}z^{1/3}$. The conclusion is a deduction from the pinned paper and the new smooth-window argument. The pinned paper's own Theorem 1.1 gives a proper divisor in $O(\exp(3L^{3/4}))$. Ordinary RH and Dirichlet GRH over the rationals used in our deduction have independent Lean verification. The complete modified factoring theorem still has a paper proof and internal agent review only. [Fixed construction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) · [Cogentic's release and formalization status](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/README.md) · [Sutherland's independent checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## What the reviewers checked

| Step | Result at the fixed schedule |
|---|---|
| Individual smooth windows | Two fresh reviewers independently checked the one- and two-point progressions against pp. 48–51. Every clean progression admits relative error $O(e^{-50z^2})$. One referee checked the bent contour and attempted to produce a resonance. Another derived a shorter rectangular contour, given below. The derivation by the contributor who wrote the original window proof is also available. [Window audit](cogentic-fixed-window-audit.md) |
| Moments and rank | Two reviewers checked that centering the exact saddle atoms, deleting bad rows, and removing the two norm directions preserve the target rows. In the original coordinates the true Gram matrix has least eigenvalue $\gg L^{-5/3}z^{-1/3}$, eventually greater than $L^{-2}$. The covariance error $O(1/u)$ is supported on the finite-prime coordinates, where the positive diagonal absorbs it. This yields exactly the spanning hypothesis of Cogentic Lemma 7.4(3). [Rank audit](cogentic-fixed-rank-audit.md) |
| Extraction and runtime | A separate review traced the rank certificate through Lemmas 7.3–7.5, the subgroup-index bound of Lemma 11.1, the order splitter and complete factorization. Exhaustion tests at most $16 B L_0^8 U^4 A^2$ candidates. Each costs $\exp(O(H))$, and $\log U=(100000+o(1))V$. The Gaussian argument establishes existence separately for each generator prime. Enumerating all four integer coordinates makes the running-time bound deterministic. [Extraction audit](cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.md) |

One guard changes when reusing the extractor. Stage 3 of the original exponent algorithm caps $\ln\widetilde M$ at $\exp(L^{0.65})$. The new algorithm omits that cap and charges the larger exponent to the bit cost. Its bit length is at most $\exp((1/1000+o(1))V)$. The verified-prime count uses the integer comparison $3\log_2(B)\,|P_{\rm ok}|\ge B$ for dyadic $B$. Trial division covers bounded exceptional sizes. Primality tests and recursive splits use fewer than $2\log_2 N$ nodes even when prime factors repeat. [Integer guards and recursion](cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.md)

## Independent contour at the fixed parameters

Put $b_B=\ln B$, $\beta=199/200$, $T_1=B^{1/200}$ and $T=B^{1/5}$. The squarefree Euler product uses primes in $(\sqrt B,B]$. For the clean windows the ratio $R=Y/S$ is eventually at most $B^{0.17}$, including after division by $G=\gcd(r,q)$. The compact Gaussian's high-frequency bound from the proof of Cogentic Lemma 8.4 gives $\exp(-c(|t|/R)^{2/3})$ when $|t|/R$ is large. At $T$ its exponent has magnitude at least $cB^{0.02}$, which dominates the trivial Euler product bound $\exp(O(B^{0.005}))$ on $\sigma\ge\beta$. The integrated tail is negligible even after summing the characters. [Cogentic, p. 43](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=43)

Below $T$, apply the GRH estimate for $\theta(x,\chi)$ and sum by parts. The error in the prime integral for $\log F_\chi(\sigma+it)$ is bounded by $(1+|t|)B^{(1/2-\beta)/2} L^{O(1)}=O(B^{-0.04})$. Prime powers and the polynomially many removed factors satisfy this tolerance as well. The nonprincipal integral on $\sigma=\beta$ costs at most $\exp((-498.005+o(1))V)$ in total, using $\log q\le(2+o(1))V$ and $\log Y=(100001+o(1))V$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, Theorem 13.7, p. 425](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/MNTI/17.0_pp_419_451_Conditional_estimates.pdf#page=7)

For the principal character let $\alpha$ be the saddle and $\lambda=(1-\alpha)b_B$. At height $1$ move to $\sigma_1=\alpha-z/(6b_B)$, then at $T_1$ move to $\beta$. Use the reflected segments at negative heights. The first shift costs $\exp(o(u))$ in the Euler product at height $1$, against central mass $F(\alpha)=\exp((1+o(1))u)$. Between the shifts the factor $Y^{\sigma_1-\alpha}$ has logarithm $-(16.6668\ldots+o(1))H$, against segment length $\exp((5+o(1))H)$. On and after the second shift the prime integral is $O(B^{1-\sigma}/(|t|b_B))=O(1/b_B)$. The far principal contribution is $\exp((-500.005+o(1))V)$. Dividing by the main density costs only $\exp((100.001/3+o(1))H)$. For $1/b_B\le |t|\le1$, averaging $1-\cos(t\log p)$ on the top band of relative log-width $1/\lambda$ gives loss $\gg u/\lambda^2\gg z^2$ in the logarithm of the Euler product, including near a multiple of $2\pi$. The low-frequency saddle estimate and the rough bound for unclean pairs are the ones in the window audit. [Saddle and close-pair bounds](cogentic-fixed-window-audit.md)

For a nonunit residue, an ineligible or repeated prime in $G$ gives zero mass. Otherwise divide $Y,S,q$ by $G$ together and delete every prime of $G$ from the allowed band, including primes absent from $q/G$. This preserves $R$ and the normalized Gaussian and is precisely the support on p. 50. [Cogentic, pp. 49–51](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=49)

## Reproduction and scope

Run `make check-two-thirds` with Lean and a Python interpreter that imports `sage.all`. This runs the five finite replays and the local Lean file. To reproduce in this workspace use `env DOT_SAGE=/tmp/rsa-sage-check make check-two-thirds`.

The reference PDF is the deterministic companion at commit `4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a`, SHA-256 `0ccf115516dab3cbeee15167f2dea62fcb29d03a19224cb4a9ae69a5ce402993`. The original linked paper makes a separate private-coin, per-input expected-time claim. [Pinned manuscripts and their guarantees](cogentic-factoring-papers.md)

* The [window replay](../experiments/cogentic-fixed-window-audit.py) covers 1,542 divided residue classes, six pole contours and four Gaussian inversions. On the fixtures, the two-step rectangular and vertical integrals agree within $7\cdot10^{-16}$ absolute error. The [rank replay](../experiments/cogentic-fixed-rank-audit.py) checks 2,556 norm-coordinate evaluations and the deletion inequalities. The [extraction replay](../experiments/cogentic-two-thirds-extraction-audit.py) checks 30 degree-ten CRT field instances, 546 subgroup examples and 57,618 integer guards. Run each file with Python 3.
* Sage 10.10 certified a lower bound of $2.707H$ for the least singular value on each of five example root grids, with degrees 11, 18, 27, 42 and 100. Outward-rounded interval arithmetic encloses all positions in each mark interval and allows each root to move by $w^2$. [Sage grid check](../experiments/cogentic-interval-grid-sage.py)
* A second Sage check uses two real quadratic fields with a common evaluation modulo 15. It computes the prime-ideal valuations of 53 primitive homogeneous marks, a valuation matrix of rank 51, and three independent directions for the target exponent and the two free unit coordinates. Cancelling both free coordinates yields two units equal to $-1$. The resulting certificate is $7^{88}\equiv1\pmod{15}$, and the gcd step gives $\gcd(4-1,15)=3$. These fields and mark bounds were selected to exercise the algebraic deduction. The asymptotic construction uses a different degree and integer marks. [Exact Sage certificate](../experiments/cogentic-real-extractor-sage.py)
* `make check` checks the dyadic cost scale, multiplication of supplied loop bounds, the recursion-tree count, and the subgroup-index algebra in Lean. Given the counting lower bound on subgroup size, the last check derives the index bound. It also proves that multiplication by the index lcm makes the exponent divisible by the full group order. These are finite arithmetic theorems. The smooth estimates, rank transfer, upstream number-field extractor, and full runtime theorem remain paper-level dependencies of this review. The estimated coefficient in the displayed bound comes from the four-coordinate schedule alone. [Lean statements](../formal/TwoThirdsCost.lean)
