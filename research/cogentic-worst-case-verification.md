# Verification of the deterministic two-thirds worst-case bound

The internal review supports the following paper result. With the extraction lemmas of Cogentic's deterministic companion, the fixed four-coordinate search completely factors every integer $N\ge2$ in
$$
 \exp\!\left((400000+o(1))(\ln N)^{2/3}(\ln\ln N)^{1/3}\right)
$$
deterministic bit operations. The coefficient is a conservative upper bound. Write $L=\ln N$, $z=\ln L$, $H=L^{1/3}z^{2/3}$ and $V=L^{2/3}z^{1/3}$. The conclusion is a deduction from the pinned paper and the new smooth-window argument. The pinned paper's own Theorem 1.1 gives a proper divisor in $O(\exp(3L^{3/4}))$. Ordinary RH and Dirichlet GRH over the rationals used in our deduction have independent Lean verification. The complete modified factoring theorem remains a paper result. Lean checks the arithmetic components and the implications listed below. [Fixed construction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md) · [Cogentic's release and formalization status](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-research/google-research/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/README.md) · [Sutherland's independent checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## Lean verification status

The requested statement would exhibit one deterministic algorithm whose output is the prime factorization of every binary input $N\ge2$, together with a uniform bound on its elementary bit operations. The current Lean endpoint still takes a proof that the four-coordinate search finds a verified hit for each target prime on all sufficiently large surviving inputs. Its runtime conclusion multiplies a supplied per-node charge by the number of recursive calls. The cap on those charges is an independent obligation. [Factoring driver](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/Driver.lean) · [Finite search and its counter](../formal/TwoThirdsSearch.lean)

One guard changes when reusing the extractor. Stage 3 of the original exponent algorithm caps $\ln\widetilde M$ at $\exp(L^{0.65})$. The new algorithm omits that cap and charges the larger exponent to the bit cost. Its bit length is at most $\exp((1/1000+o(1))V)$. The verified-prime count uses the integer comparison $3\log_2(B)\,|P_{\rm ok}|\ge B$ for dyadic $B$. Trial division covers bounded exceptional sizes. ## Independent contour at the fixed parameters

Put $b_B=\ln B$, $\beta=199/200$, $T_1=B^{1/200}$ and $T=B^{1/5}$. The squarefree Euler product uses primes in $(\sqrt B,B]$. For the clean windows the ratio $R=Y/S$ is eventually at most $B^{0.17}$, including after division by $G=\gcd(r,q)$. The compact Gaussian's high-frequency bound from the proof of Cogentic Lemma 8.4 gives $\exp(-c(|t|/R)^{2/3})$ when $|t|/R$ is large. At $T$ its exponent has magnitude at least $cB^{0.02}$, which dominates the trivial Euler product bound $\exp(O(B^{0.005}))$ on $\sigma\ge\beta$. The integrated tail is negligible even after summing the characters. [Cogentic, p. 43](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=43)

Below $T$, apply the GRH estimate for $\theta(x,\chi)$ and sum by parts. The error in the prime integral for $\log F_\chi(\sigma+it)$ is bounded by $(1+|t|)B^{(1/2-\beta)/2} L^{O(1)}=O(B^{-0.04})$. Prime powers and the polynomially many removed factors satisfy this tolerance as well. The nonprincipal integral on $\sigma=\beta$ costs at most $\exp((-498.005+o(1))V)$ in total, using $\log q\le(2+o(1))V$ and $\log Y=(100001+o(1))V$. [Montgomery–Vaughan, Theorem 13.7, p. 425](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/personal/Publications/MNTI/17.0_pp_419_451_Conditional_estimates.pdf#page=7)

For the principal character let $\alpha$ be the saddle and $\lambda=(1-\alpha)b_B$. At height $1$ move to $\sigma_1=\alpha-z/(6b_B)$, then at $T_1$ move to $\beta$. Use the reflected segments at negative heights. The first shift costs $\exp(o(u))$ in the Euler product at height $1$, against central mass $F(\alpha)=\exp((1+o(1))u)$. Between the shifts the factor $Y^{\sigma_1-\alpha}$ has logarithm $-(16.6668\ldots+o(1))H$, against segment length $\exp((5+o(1))H)$. On and after the second shift the prime integral is $O(B^{1-\sigma}/(|t|b_B))=O(1/b_B)$. The far principal contribution is $\exp((-500.005+o(1))V)$. Dividing by the main density costs only $\exp((100.001/3+o(1))H)$. For $1/b_B\le |t|\le1$, averaging $1-\cos(t\log p)$ on the top band of relative log-width $1/\lambda$ gives loss $\gg u/\lambda^2\gg z^2$ in the logarithm of the Euler product, including near a multiple of $2\pi$. The low-frequency saddle estimate and the rough bound for unclean pairs are the ones in the window audit. [Saddle and close-pair bounds](cogentic-fixed-window-audit.md)

For a nonunit residue, an ineligible or repeated prime in $G$ gives zero mass. Otherwise divide $Y,S,q$ by $G$ together and delete every prime of $G$ from the allowed band, including primes absent from $q/G$. This preserves $R$ and the normalized Gaussian and is precisely the support on p. 50. [Cogentic, pp. 49–51](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=49)

## Reproduction and scope

Run `make check-two-thirds-lean` for the factoring proofs. The Lake project pins Lean 4.33.1, the matching Mathlib commit and the frozen Dirichlet GRH source. A fresh checkout needs network access to fetch the dependencies and their caches. Run `make check-two-thirds` with a Python interpreter that imports `sage.all` to also run the five finite replays. To reproduce in this workspace use `env DOT_SAGE=/tmp/rsa-sage-check make check-two-thirds`.

The default Lake target checks the deductions from the precise critical-line statement in the frozen upstream source. Its public endpoints are audited at build time for dependence on additional axioms. The optional `lake build CogenticWithGRH` target imports the original all-character proof and applies it to the character bound, the small generating bases and the conditional fixed-schedule factorization theorem. The default target and the application types were checked here. A local replay of the full combined import would require restoring the large original proof archive. The independent checks published for that exact upstream theorem are available separately. [Original theorem](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/3091d9fb6eab9943787c5d19d0b7848bf364eac2/src/Main.lean) · [Independent verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Application of the original proof](../formal/cogentic/CogenticWithGRH.lean)

The reference PDF is the deterministic companion at commit `4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a`, SHA-256 `0ccf115516dab3cbeee15167f2dea62fcb29d03a19224cb4a9ae69a5ce402993`. The original linked paper makes a separate private-coin, per-input expected-time claim. [Pinned manuscripts and their guarantees](cogentic-factoring-papers.md)

* The [window replay](../experiments/cogentic-fixed-window-audit.py) covers 1,542 divided residue classes, six pole contours and four Gaussian inversions. On the fixtures, the two-step rectangular and vertical integrals agree within $7\cdot10^{-16}$ absolute error. * Sage 10.10 certified a lower bound of $2.707H$ for the least singular value on each of five example root grids, with degrees 11, 18, 27, 42 and 100. Outward-rounded interval arithmetic encloses all positions in each mark interval and allows each root to move by $w^2$. [Sage grid check](../experiments/cogentic-interval-grid-sage.py)
* A second Sage check uses two real quadratic fields with a common evaluation modulo 15. It computes the prime-ideal valuations of 53 primitive homogeneous marks, a valuation matrix of rank 51, and three independent directions for the target exponent and the two free unit coordinates. Cancelling both free coordinates yields two units equal to $-1$. The resulting certificate is $7^{88}\equiv1\pmod{15}$, and the gcd step gives $\gcd(4-1,15)=3$. These fields and mark bounds were selected to exercise the algebraic deduction. The asymptotic construction uses a different degree and integer marks. [Exact Sage certificate](../experiments/cogentic-real-extractor-sage.py)
* The [dyadic cost arithmetic](../formal/TwoThirdsCost.lean) checks multiplication of supplied loop bounds. The [integer schedule](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/FixedSchedule.lean) uses a binary length and integer cube root. Its explicit candidate count and saturation bounds concern the actual search loops. A bound on the bit operations inside one successful or failed candidate is still required for the displayed running time.
