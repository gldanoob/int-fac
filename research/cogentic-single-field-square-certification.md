# Quadratic characters in one unrestricted number field

One odd-degree number field can certify the whole square kernel on its ideal-parity subspace using random degree-one prime ideals of polynomial bit length. The expected cost of finding the probes is $d!\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$, where $d$ is the field degree. A possible exceptional zero of the base field can lower the acceptance rate of the sampler. It lowers the number of detecting ideals by the same factor, so it does not increase the number of character rows. A separate deterministic lift at a small auxiliary prime tests and reconstructs one selected square in nearly linear time in its expanded bit length, times $2^d\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$. Neither procedure calls for hidden prime factors of $N$, a maximal order, or a second field admitting an evaluation modulo $N$.

## Uniform inputs and character rows

Let $K=\mathbf Q(\theta)$ have odd degree $d>1$ and monic irreducible $g\in\mathbf Z[T]$. Write $\Delta_K=|\operatorname{disc}K|$. Suppose the accepted relations have even valuations outside primes over a specified nonzero integer $D$, which may include the order discriminant, the leading coefficient of the original field presentation, and any other exclusions. The permitted integral atoms have a common upper bound $U$ on the absolute values of their nonzero norms. Rational factors and denominators can be entered as atoms after clearing denominators. Assume $d$, the coefficient bit lengths of $g$, $\log(2|D|)$ and $\log(2U)$ are polynomial in $\log N$. No factorization of $D$ is needed here.

Let $H_D$ be the group of square classes with even valuations away from $D$. The square-ideal map bounds its size by $2^{d+|S|}h_K$, where $S$ is the set of ideals over $D$ and $h_K$ is the class number. Dirichlet's unit theorem bounds the kernel by $2^{d+|S|}$ and the image lies in the two-torsion of the $S$-ideal class group. By Minkowski and the ideal-count bound $\sum_{n\le X}\tau_d(n)\le X(1+\log X)^{d-1}$, a computable upper bound for $\log |H_D|$ is polynomial in the presentation size. For each nonidentity class $\gamma$, put $E=K(\sqrt\gamma)$. Its relative discriminant is supported over $2D$. Local normalization of the valuation to zero or one bounds its relative discriminant divisor by the norm contribution $8^d|D|^d$, hence $\Delta_E\le\Delta_K^2 8^d|D|^d$. These are the same support and size bounds used by KeyBreak. [KeyBreak, Lemma E.2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=88)

Choose an integer $T$ larger than $\log N$, $d\log(2d)$, $\log(2U)$ and the computable upper bound on $\log(2\Delta_E)$ for the entire group. Use $x=2^{T^4}$ and sample degree-one prime ideals over rational primes in $(x/2,x]$. All atoms are units there and the primes are good for the power order once $T$ also bounds $\log(2|\operatorname{disc}g|)$ and $\log(2|D|)$. For $\mathfrak p=(p,\theta-u)$ take the Legendre symbol of each atom at $u$. On $H_D$, a value of $-1$ is equivalent to inertness of $\mathfrak p$ in $E/K$. The characters are computed columnwise before the product is formed.

## Counting detecting ideals when the base field has an exceptional zero

Use Thorner–Zaman's Theorems 1.1 and 1.4 for the trivial extension $K/K$ and for $E/K$. Their exceptional term is signed. With $n_F=[F:\mathbf Q]$, the bound in Theorem 1.1 has relative error

$$O\left(\exp\left[-\frac{c\log y}{\log(\Delta_F n_F^{n_F})}\right]+\exp\left[-c\sqrt{\frac{\log y}{n_F}}\right]\right)$$

for $y\ge(\Delta_Fn_F^{n_F})^{c'}$, where $F$ is the top field. The abelian form in Theorem 5.1 gives the exceptional coefficient $\chi_1(C)$ and uses denominator $\log Q$ in the first error exponent. Theorem 3.1 takes the zero-free width at $t=3$ at least $c_4/\log(3^{n_K}Q)$, with $Q=\Delta_K n_K^{n_K}$ times the largest conductor norm. For $E/K$ that conductor norm is the norm of the relative discriminant. The trivial character has $L$-function $\zeta_K$, and the nontrivial quadratic character has $L$-function $\zeta_E/\zeta_K$. The exceptionally close real zero, if present, is simple and belongs to one factor. [Thorner–Zaman, Theorems 1.1, 3.1 and 5.1](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf)

Call a zero close if $1-\beta\le T^{-2}$. This is inside both exceptional regions for all sufficiently large $T$. A designated zero outside this smaller band contributes $o(x/\log x)$ at both ends of the dyadic interval. The relative errors tend uniformly to zero since $d=O(T)$, $\log(\Delta_F n_F^{n_F})=O(T)$ and $\log x\asymp T^4$.

- If $\zeta_K$ has a close zero $\beta$, the same zero occurs in every $\zeta_E$ by the quadratic factorization. Its character in $E/K$ is trivial, so both the full count in $K$ and the inert count use $J(y)=\operatorname{Li}(y)-\operatorname{Li}(y^\beta)$, with leading factors one and one half. Since $J(y)=(1+O(T^{-2}))y(1-e^{-(1-\beta)\log y})/\log y$ at either endpoint, $J(x/2)=(1/2+o(1))J(x)$ uniformly even when the zero is arbitrarily close. This follows also by concavity of $1-e^{-z}$. The relative error therefore remains $o(1)$ after taking dyadic differences. Stark's Lemma 8 implies $1-\beta>1/(4d!\log\Delta_K)$, since the odd-degree field has no quadratic subfield. Thus the full count in the interval is $\gg x/(d!\operatorname{poly}(T))$. [Stark, Lemma 8](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN356556735_0023/PPN356556735_0023.pdf)
- If $\zeta_K$ lacks a close zero, its dyadic count is asymptotic to $\operatorname{Li}(x)-\operatorname{Li}(x/2)$. Any close zero of $\zeta_E$ belongs to the nontrivial quadratic character, whose value at the inert Frobenius is $-1$. Its contribution to the dyadic inert count is nonnegative. In this case the inert count is at least $(1/2-o(1))$ times the count in $K$.

At most $d\sqrt x$ prime ideals of $K$ of norm at most $x$ have absolute residue degree greater than one. This is negligible even against the suppressed lower bound, since $\log(d!)=O(T)$ whereas $\log x\asymp T^4$. Removing those ideals from both counts leaves conditional detection probability at least $1/3$ for large $T$, uniformly over every nonidentity class, including rational nonsquares that remain nonsquare in $K$.

## Sampling cost and correctness

Propose a uniform integer $p\in(x/2,x]$, certify primality and find the roots of $g$ over $\mathbf F_p$. If there are $r$ roots, accept with probability $r/d$ and choose one uniformly. Every degree-one ideal in the range has the same single-proposal probability $2/(dx)$. Use deterministic primality testing and the usual random finite-field splitting with exact output checks. A per-proposal cap can preserve this exact distribution up to a coupling error. Each recursive attempt to split a squarefree product of two or more factors uses a uniform polynomial residue and has success probability bounded below by an absolute constant, so $\operatorname{poly}(T)$ trials make timeout probability $e^{-\Omega(T^2)}$ per proposal. Polynomial-bit interval sampling and its caps admit the same coupling. No preliminary prime catalogue is required.

The lower count yields acceptance probability at least $1/(d!\operatorname{poly}(T))$. More explicitly, the ratio $J(x)/(x/\log x)\gg\min(1,\log x/(d!\log\Delta_K))$ and $\log\Delta_K\le T$ give acceptance probability $\gg 1/(d!T^5)$. A cap $d!T^7$ per accepted sample therefore has vanishing timeout probability, including a union bound over polynomially many samples. The count of direct proposals and the aggregate bit work have the same leading coefficient, since every direct input and operation has polynomial bit length. Taking $O(\log |H_D|+k)$ independent accepted characters makes the probability of a missed nonidentity class $O(2^{-k})$. The simultaneous event permits relations chosen after the probes are disclosed. An output square is still verified exactly.

For $d=(\delta+o(1))(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, the probe setup has coefficient $\delta/3$ on the $L_N[1/3]$ scale, with only polynomial overhead per retained atom. At the larger range $\log x=d!\operatorname{poly}(\log N)$, one can also use the additive signed prime ideal estimate, equation (1-2), and write the inert count as $\pi_K(y)-\pi_E(y)/2+O(d\sqrt y+\log\Delta_E)$. In this argument the negative exceptional term in $\pi_E$ can be dropped for the lower bound on the dyadic inert count. The resulting probe bit length has coefficient $\delta/3$ instead of the $2\delta/3$ from $(2d)!$. The relative-error theorem puts that cost only in the number of proposals. [Thorner–Zaman, equation (1-2)](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf)

## Exact square test at a small auxiliary prime

For a selected product, clear negative exponents by a known square and multiply by $g'(\theta)^2$ to obtain $\Gamma\in\mathbf Z[\theta]$. An algebraic square root then lies in $\mathbf Z[\theta]$ because $g'(\theta)\mathcal O_K\subset\mathbf Z[\theta]$ by the trace-dual identity. A Vandermonde bound on the power-basis coefficients gives a computable $B$ bounding the absolute values of the root coefficients. Its bit length is at most polynomial in the field presentation times the bit length of the product. A balanced product tree keeps the aggregate work over each level nearly linear in the sum of the individual mark bit lengths times a polynomial in the field presentation. [Power-basis and height bounds, KeyBreak, Theorem E.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=91)

Choose the first odd prime $p\nmid\operatorname{disc}g$. The prime number theorem bounds $p=O(1+\log|\operatorname{disc}g|)$ since otherwise the product of the smaller odd primes would divide the discriminant. Factor $g\bmod p$ deterministically by Berlekamp and lift the coprime factors to precision $p^{2a}$, with $p^a>2B$. Work in the unramified quotient for each lifted factor. If the reduction of $\Gamma$ is zero modulo $p^{2a}$ in that component, the only possible square root modulo $p^a$ is zero. Otherwise compute the minimum $p$-valuation of its power-basis coefficients. An odd valuation rules out a square; for valuation $2v$, divide by $p^{2v}$ and solve the unit square problem modulo $p$. This unit search can use deterministic Berlekamp splitting of the quadratic algebra over $\mathbf F_p$: its Frobenius-fixed space has dimension one in the irreducible case and two if split. A nonscalar fixed element splits the two components by scanning the $p$ possible residues. Newton lifting gives both roots modulo $p^{a-v}$, then multiply by $p^v$. The input precision $2a-2v$ after division is sufficient.

Recombine the at most $2^d$ choices by polynomial CRT at precision $p^a$, center the coefficients, and square each candidate exactly modulo $g$. A true square has its root on one of the enumerated branches, and every accepted candidate has the claimed square by the final check. Polynomial multiplication and the lifts use a polynomial number of coefficient operations per precision-doubling step. Fast integer multiplication, division and the geometric precision schedule give bit work $2^d\operatorname{poly}(d,\log(2|\operatorname{disc}g|))\widetilde O(1+\log B+h_\Gamma)$ with $h_\Gamma$ the expanded coefficient bit bound. This extends the unit, split-prime reconstruction in KeyBreak and the inert-prime lift in Cogentic to a deterministic auxiliary prime chosen independently of both the probe list and the selected product. [KeyBreak, Theorem E.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=92) · [Cogentic, Lemma 5.2](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization/paper.pdf)

An exact Sage replay at $p=3$ and $a=2$ exercised six cases. For $g=T^3-T+3$ and $\Gamma=T^2$ the three local valuations were $2,0,0$. For $g=T^3-T+3^6$ and the same target, one component was zero at precision $3^4$. For $g=T^3+T^2-T+3$ and $\Gamma=(T+2)^2$ the local degrees were one and two. Centered exact roots matched one of the lifted sign choices in each case. In the first field, $T$ failed the odd valuation test, $2$ failed the local unit square test, and $13$ passed every local test while exact squaring rejected it. Sage's field square test agreed in all six cases. The replay used digit-by-digit lifts and finite enumeration to check the branch criterion.

At KeyBreak's fixed GRH schedule, $\delta=3/4$ and the retained occurrence and matrix-dimension coefficients are $19/20$. Character setup drops from $2$ to $1/4$, and retained character evaluation from $49/20$ to $19/20$. Expanded products and the exact square lift now also fit $19/20$ when performed by fast arithmetic. The $57/20$ dense binary elimination term still dominates that part of the computation. Raw proposals are charged at $237/80=2.9625$, and algebraic smoothness recognition at $62/25+\sqrt{76313/322500}=2.966445739\ldots$. The largest displayed cost and the rounded $2.97$ claim therefore agree with the original fixed schedule. The replacement operates on public data and retains the private-root independence used for splitting. [KeyBreak, Theorem H.8 and Table 2](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=142)

The unconditional KeyBreak schedule gives a different numerical comparison. Keep the pool size, accepted-occurrence cap, recursive calls and tape archive charges in equations (F.3)–(F.11). Use a single polynomial $T$ that also bounds $\log J$, where $J$ is the number of fields, so the sampler and separation caps can be union-bounded over the pool. In the notation of (F.10), the new probe setup across all $J$ fields has coefficient $f+1/3$, since $d\sim(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$. Even scanning all pool records before every field's probe setup costs at most $2f+1/3$. The per-field expansion and exact root require $L\exp(o(\Lambda))$ bit operations, where the paper's $L$ bounds the expanded coefficient and lifting lengths. Processing one field at a time allows a full scan of the retained occurrence list and the saved factor records for each field. Polynomial-bit character rows and table lookups fit this budget even with a second full pool scan per field. The combined coefficient for these retained operations is bounded by $2f+r_*+1<77{,}309{,}411{,}491$. The arithmetic and scans can run before and after the recursive search, respectively, so the archive bound at each recursive call still applies. The direct-cost terms left in (F.9) are bounded by the original $3r_*$, $s_*+v_*+f+b_0$, and $s_*+v_*+2r_*$. The third is largest at $103{,}099{,}215{,}487$. The unchanged recursive thresholds from (6.1) are

## Raw point budget of the GRH descent

Rescheduling the rational and algebraic predicates in a KeyBreak search preserves the jointly eligible points if the same full point stream and exact tests are used. A capped one-sided test can be coupled to the exact stream by separate random blocks per trial and predicate, as in Lemma H.4. This can reduce the charge for calls to the more expensive predicate. The raw point charge can be isolated independently of both predicates and the new square certificate. [KeyBreak, Lemma H.4 and its coupling](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=133)

Consider retuning the published style of budget, with $D_0=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, $H_0=D_0\log\log N$, $V_*=D_0H_0$, $d\sim\delta D_0$ and $\log B\sim bH_0$. Retain the following features of its accounting.

- The rational scale is $M=N^{1/d+o(1/d)}$ and the top cutoff satisfies $\log Y_0\sim\alpha\log M$ for $0<\alpha\le1$. The top point box has $V_0^2\ge Y_0$ at leading order and the Gaussian width has $\log\sigma\ge2\log V_0-o(V_*)$. The $k$-prime shape estimate budgets $k\sim\log N/(2\log Y_0)$ factors in each of the two private coordinates, with inverse-density coefficient $2k\log k/H_0\sim\delta/(3\alpha)$. These are the scales and the counting mechanism in (G.1), (G.2) and (H.18)–(H.20). [KeyBreak, §G.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105) · [Private shape count](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=135)
- The last active internal stage uses both rational $B$-smoothness and algebraic $B$-smoothness after removing its incoming prime $q>B$. Its point box has $\log V_{\mathrm{last}}\sim h bH_0$ for fixed $h>0$. The rational and algebraic density calculations use the sizes $MV_{\mathrm{last}}$ and $\sigma M V_{\mathrm{last}}^d/q$, respectively. As in (G.5) and (G.25), the point count is charged at $V_{\mathrm{last}}^2/q$ and the successful point rate uses the two Dickman density estimates. The model requires a nonnegative leading exponent for the mean count. Since we only need an upper bound on the mean exponent, we can set $q$ to $B$ in the point count. A larger $q$ with $\log q=O(H_0)$ still changes the algebraic norm height only by $o(V_*)$. [KeyBreak, §G.1 and Theorem G.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105) · [Density reference (G.25)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=112) · [Mean and height checks in Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)
- The full factor base requires $B^{1+o(1)}$ retained rows. As in (H.15) and (H.30), the raw proposal budget multiplies the private shape-attempt cap by a uniform per-request trial cap that covers the last active stage. In particular this budget charges at least one such trial allowance per retained row, with the inverse private-shape density included. This is a conservative budget convention. The deduction makes no claim about how many last-stage requests a particular implementation actually visits or how many its successful trees can share. [KeyBreak, (H.15)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=133) · [Raw proposal count (H.30)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=140)

Let $U$ be the leading inverse joint density coefficient of the last active stage. The norm sizes above, $\log q=O(H_0)$ and $\log\sigma\ge(\alpha/\delta+o(1))V_*$ give the first inequality below by the same Dickman exponent as in Proposition G.13. The mean and the charged raw proposal count give the other two inequalities. Denote the coefficient of that raw budget by $C_{\mathrm{raw}}$.

$$U\ge\frac{2+\alpha}{3\delta b}+\frac{\delta h}{3},\qquad U\le(2h-1)b,\qquad C_{\mathrm{raw}}\ge b+\frac{\delta}{3\alpha}+U.$$

Indeed, a value with logarithm $sV_*$ has Dickman loss $s/(3b)$ at cutoff $B$. The rational height contributes $1/\delta$ to $s$, and the algebraic height contributes at least $(1+\alpha)/\delta+\delta h b$. At the published parameters $\alpha\to5/9$, $\delta\to3/4$ and $b=19/20$. The private term is then $9/20$, so $b+\delta/(3\alpha)=7/5$, the coefficient of the search-state bound in (H.35). The original mean test uses a strict positive margin. Allowing equality only enlarges the parameter region in this comparison. [KeyBreak, Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123) · [Leading search costs (H.35)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=142)

The sharp infimum of these three scalar constraints is
$$
 C_{\mathrm{raw}}\ \ge\ \left(\frac{20+14\sqrt7}{3}\right)^{1/3}=2.6690337752\ldots .
$$
To see this, put $l=\delta/(3b)$. Feasibility requires $0<l<2$. For fixed $l,b,\alpha$, the objective increases with $h$, so the relaxation's minimizing $h$ has $U=(2h-1)b$ and $b(2-l)h=b+(2+\alpha)/(9lb^2)$. Substitution gives
$$
 C_{\mathrm{raw}}\ge b\left(\frac{2}{2-l}+\frac l\alpha\right)
       +\frac{2(2+\alpha)}{9l(2-l)b^2}.
$$
Minimizing the right-hand side in $b>0$ and cubing leaves $\frac32(2+\alpha)(2+y/\alpha)^2/[l(2-l)^3]$, with $y=l(2-l)$.

For fixed $\alpha$ the sign of the derivative in $l$ is the sign of $y+4\alpha l-2\alpha$. It has exactly one zero below $1/2$, is positive on $[1/2,2)$, and is already positive at $l=2/5$ for every $0<\alpha\le1$. Thus the minimizing $l$ is interior and below $2/5$. The partial derivative in $\alpha$ has sign $2\alpha^2-(\alpha+4)y$. At that $l$, use $y=2\alpha(1-2l)$ and $l<2/5\le2/(\alpha+4)$ to see this sign is negative. The minimized expression therefore decreases up to $\alpha=1$. There $l=3-\sqrt7$ and substitution gives the displayed value. Equality is attained by the scalar relaxation allowing $\alpha=1$ and zero mean margin. The original descent may impose additional restrictions. The [symbolic replay](../experiments/keybreak-raw-point-budget.py) verifies the derivative identities, endpoint, and feasibility of the minimizing parameters.

Thus this raw point budget stays above $2.443$ even with free recognition, linear algebra and square certification. An output-sensitive proposal law, shared or selectively avoided bottom searches, a different private shape construction, or a smaller Gaussian width would require a corresponding change to the relation analysis. The bound applies to the specified expression for charging raw proposals.
