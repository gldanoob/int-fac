# Conductor gluing and the sign image of ordinary marks

The resultant order gives the same modular sign group as gluing directly over the common root. For a finite mark pool, the signs actually attained are the image of a half-lattice computed in the two fields' groups of units with the mark divisors inverted. Equivalently, a candidate sign must pass a Picard test and a square test in a quotient of the glued unit group.

## The resultant order

Let $F,G\in\mathbb Z[X]$ be distinct monic irreducibles of coprime degree with common root $m$ modulo an odd $N>1$. Put $R=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, $A_F=\mathbb Z[X]/(F)$, $A_G=\mathbb Z[X]/(G)$, $P=A_F\times A_G$, $T=\mathbb Z[X]/(FG)$ and $C_0=\mathbb Z[X]/(F,G)$. Evaluation defines $C_0\to R$. The injection $T\to P$ identifies $T$ with $A_F\times_{C_0} A_G$. Indeed $(F)\cap(G)=(FG)$ by monicity and coprimality over $\mathbb Q$, and representatives agreeing modulo $(F,G)$ can be made equal by adding multiples of $F$ and $G$. The conductor of this inclusion in $P$ is
\[
 (G(\alpha))A_F\times(F(\beta))A_G,
\]
where $\alpha,\beta$ are the two generators. A pair in the conductor must still agree after multiplying by $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$, which forces both components to vanish in $C_0$. Conversely every such pair multiplies $P$ into $T$. The difference of the two quotient maps gives $P/T\cong C_0$ as additive groups. Since $C_0=A_F/(G(\alpha))$, its order and the index $[P:T]$ are $|\operatorname{Res}(F,G)|$ by the determinant of multiplication by $G(\alpha)$.

Localize at the images of the polynomials $h$ with $h(m)\in R^\times$, and call the localized overlap $C$. The surviving maximal ideals of $C_0$ are exactly $(p,X-m)$ for $p\mid N$. For any other maximal ideal, the Chinese remainder theorem gives an element that vanishes there and equals one at every chosen maximal ideal. Such an element is inverted by the localization. Thus $C$ is a product of finite local rings, one per prime dividing $N$, with residue fields $\mathbb F_p$. The map $C\to R$ is onto and pairs these factors. On any local factor, $2$ is a unit and $z^2=1$ implies $z=1$ or $z=-1$ because one of $z-1$ and $z+1$ is a unit. Consequently
\[
 \mu_2(C)\ \xrightarrow{\sim}\ \mu_2(R).
\]
If $F$ is simple at the chosen root modulo $p$, its factor in the $p$-adic power order is $\mathbb Z_p$ by Hensel lifting. The corresponding overlap factor is $\mathbb Z/p^{t_p}\mathbb Z$ with $t_p=v_p(G(a_p))$, where $a_p\in\mathbb Z_p$ is the Hensel root of $F$. Here $a_p\equiv m\pmod{p^e}$ for $p^e\parallel N$, so $t_p\ge e$. The sign is unchanged even if $t_p>e$.

The ratio of component quotient maps gives a surjection from the localized product's units to $C^\times$. For a target $c\in C^\times$, lift $c$ to a localized element on the first side and take $1$ on the second. Its reduction at $m$ is a unit and hence it is a unit in the localization. The kernel is the unit group of the localized $T$. Thus every sign occurs for the square of some pair of units whose square glues, even for the full localized resultant order. This conclusion permits arbitrary units, leaving open whether the square lies in the group generated by the prescribed marks.

For the [scheduled obstruction family](cogentic-monic-productive-signs.md), $F=X^d+2$ and $G=F+X^d(X-m)$ with $N=m^d+2$. Here $C_0=\mathbb Z[X]/(X^d+2,2(X-m))$, so $C_0[1/2]\cong\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$. Also $|\operatorname{Res}(F,G)|=2^dN$ by the norm of $-2(\alpha-m)$. Every modular sign is available to the localized unit pairs, even though that family's entire growing grid of marks has trivial sign image modulo the global signs.

## Capture a finite pool using units with mark divisors inverted

Assume both reduction maps extend to the maximal orders $\mathcal O_F,\mathcal O_G$. Unit derivatives on both sides at the common root suffice. For each $p^e\parallel N$, embed the field into $\mathbb Q_p$ via the Hensel root and reduce from $\mathbb Z_p$ modulo $p^e$. The embedding sends algebraic integers to $\mathbb Z_p$, and the resulting maps agree with evaluation on the power orders. [Simple-root evaluation](cogentic-monic-index.md)

For each polynomial generator $\alpha_i$, let $s_{ij}=a_j-b_j\alpha_i$ for a finite pool of ordinary marks with $a_j-b_jm\in R^\times$. Define
\[
 w_i=\prod_j s_{ij},\qquad B_i=\mathcal O_i[1/w_i],\qquad B=B_F\times_R B_G,
 \qquad U=B_F^\times\times B_G^\times,\qquad V=B^\times.
\]
The reduction maps extend to $B_i$ since $w_i$ reduces to a unit. Inverting $w_i$ preserves the selected branches even when its rational norm has a prime factor in common with $N$ from another branch. Let $M\le V$ be generated by the diagonal mark pairs $(s_{Fj},s_{Gj})$, with $(-1,-1)$ included if the relation pool allows a shared sign. A root in the field of a square in $B_i^\times$ belongs to $B_i^\times$. Both the root and its inverse are integral over $B_i$, which is integrally closed. This choice of $U$ therefore captures every field-square relation from $M$ and supplies the root reductions.

Define $\delta:U\to R^\times$ by the quotient of the reductions, with kernel $V$, and let $D=\delta(U)$. The finite-index inclusion $V\subset U$ has index $|D|$. The exact sequence obtained by taking elements killed by squaring in $1\to V/M\to U/M\to D\to1$ is
\[
 1\longrightarrow(V/M)[2]\longrightarrow(U/M)[2]\xrightarrow{\delta}D[2]
 \xrightarrow{\partial} V/(M V^2),\qquad
 \partial(z)=r^2\pmod{M V^2}\quad (\delta(r)=z).
\]
The formula is well defined since different lifts differ by a unit of $V$. Its kernel consists exactly of the realizable signs. If $r^2=sv^2$ for $s\in M,v\in V$, then $(r/v)^2=s$ and $\delta(r/v)=z$. The converse follows by taking an aligned root pair with square in $M$. In particular the realizable sign group has dimension $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}D[2]-\operatorname{rank}\partial$, and contains $\{1,-1\}$ via independent global signs of the roots. A productive sign exists exactly when this dimension is at least two.

The same formula applies after replacing $U$ by the saturation $U_0=\{u\in U:u^k\in M\text{ for some }k\ge1\}$, and $V,D$ by its kernel and image. The index $[V\cap U_0:M]$ is finite since $U$ is finitely generated. If this index is odd, the boundary is zero and the full $\delta(U_0)[2]$ is attained. Indeed raising a lift of a sign to that odd index preserves the sign and puts its square in $M$. Odd index is equivalent to the images of $M$ spanning $(V\cap U_0)/(V\cap U_0)^2$.

### Compute the sign image from a presentation

Present $U$ as $\Lambda/L$ with $\Lambda$ a free integer lattice on unit generators and $L$ the relation lattice. Lift the mark pairs to vectors $v_j$ and form $L_M=L+\sum_j\mathbb Z v_j$, including the vector for the shared sign when used. The lattice of all possible root exponents is
\[
 \Lambda_2=\tfrac12(L_M\cap 2\Lambda),\qquad \Lambda_2/L_M=(U/M)[2].
\]
For each basis vector of $\Lambda_2$, take the product of generator residue ratios raised to those exponents. These values generate the exact modular sign image, since every lattice vector squares into $M$ and every root pair has a lift to $\Lambda_2$. To obtain the actual mark exponents of a witness, express twice the chosen basis vector in the generators of $L_M$. Testing the basis suffices because $\{1,-1\}$ is a subgroup. Integer normal-form computations and modular exponentiation implement this step from the presentation and coordinates without a factorization of $N$. There is no need to enumerate all parity dependencies or extract their field roots.

A presentation can in principle be computed by the unit and class group calculations for the two maximal orders. If $S_i$ is the set of prime ideals dividing $(w_i)$, the valuation vectors of $B_i^\times$ are precisely the kernel of $\mathbb Z^{S_i}\to\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_i)$, with basis ideals sent to their classes. A kernel vector gives a principal fractional ideal supported on $S_i$ and any generator is a unit of $B_i$. Combining these generators with the integral units gives a presentation. The expensive inputs are the ideal factorizations, class and unit data, principal generators and coordinates of the marks. The calculation gives no one-third running-time bound for them.

### Picard obstruction before the square test

For any of the finite localizations $B_i$ above, a residue unit $z$ glues two free rank-one modules to the invertible $B$-module
\[
 L_z=\{(x,y)\in B_F\times B_G:\rho_F(x)=z\rho_G(y)\}.
\]
The units–Picard sequence of this fiber product is
\[
 1\to V\to U\xrightarrow{\delta}R^\times\xrightarrow{z\mapsto[L_z]}
 \operatorname{Pic}(B)\to\operatorname{Pic}(B_F)\times\operatorname{Pic}(B_G)\to0.
\]
The last map is onto because every rank-one module restricts to a free module over the finite ring $R$, so any two can be glued. In particular $L_z$ is free exactly when one generator is a pair of component units with ratio $z$. Thus a sign can be realized by marks precisely when it first gives a trivial gluing class and then has trivial boundary in $V/(M V^2)$. A mixed sign can give a nonzero gluing class for the integral orders that disappears after inverting the marks. [Weibel, *The K-book*, §I.3.10](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~weibel/Kbook/Kbook.I.pdf)

## A one-mark certificate and its exact unit index

Use $N=15$, $m=0$, $F=X$ and $G=X^2+X+15$. The overlap is $R$ before any localization. The quadratic order is maximal of discriminant $-59$ and has units $\{\pm1\}$. Before inverting marks, the product's unit ratios are $\{\pm1\}$, and the mixed sign $4\pmod {15}$ gives a nontrivial Picard gluing class of order two.

Set $r=4+\beta$. Then $r^2=1+7\beta$, so the ordinary mark $(1,-7)$ has pair $(1,r^2)$ and roots $(1,r)$. It reduces to $1$ on both sides and the roots reduce to $1,4$. The difference gcd is $3$. The norm $\mathrm N(r)=27$ is supported just at $\mathfrak p=(3,\beta+1)$, since the two roots of $G$ modulo $3$ are $0,-1$ and $r$ is a unit at $\beta\equiv0$. Thus $(r)=\mathfrak p^3$. The ideal $\mathfrak p$ is nonprincipal because $a^2-ab+15b^2=3$ has no integral solution. Its class has order three, so $\mathcal O_G[1/r^2]^\times=\{\pm r^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$.

In the localized product $U$ these units and the signs give $D=\{1,4,11,14\}$ and $[U:V]=4$. The glued units are $(\epsilon,\epsilon r^{2k})$ for $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ and $k\in\mathbb Z$. With the shared sign allowed they are exactly $M$. Without the shared sign the boundary is still zero because each of the four signs lifts to a pair with square in $\langle(1,r^2)\rangle$. Inverting the norm of the mark would invert $3$ and lose its evaluation map. Inverting the mark itself keeps both selected branches and realizes every sign in $R$.

PARI/GP verifies the resultant, factor certificate and localized unit generator in the example.

```sh
gp -fq <<'GP'
g=x^2+x+15; root=Mod(x+4,g); nfdata=bnfinit(g,1);
if(polresultant(x,g)!=15 || root^2!=Mod(1+7*x,g) || norm(root)!=27 || gcd(1-4,15)!=3,quit(1));
support=select(q->idealval(nfdata,root,q)==3,idealprimedec(nfdata,3));
if(#support!=1 || nfdata.cyc!=[3] || bnfisprincipal(nfdata,support[1],0)==[0]~,quit(1));
generators=bnfsunit(nfdata,support)[1];
if(#generators!=1 || Mod(generators[1],g)^2!=root^2,quit(1));
if(Set(vector(4,i,lift(Mod((-1)^((i-1)\2)*4^((i-1)%2),15))))!=[1,4,11,14],quit(1));
print("Verified the one-mark certificate and its four unit ratios");
GP
```

The same degree-one and degree-two construction realizes any CRT sign existentially for any odd $N$ and any factorization $N=D_1D_2$ with $\gcd(D_1,D_2)=1$. With $s=(D_1+D_2)/2$ and $t=(D_2-D_1)/2$, the pair $(t,s+\beta)$ squares to the mark $t^2+(2s-1)X$ for $F=X,G=X^2+X+N$. Both reduced roots are units, since $D_1,D_2$ are coprime, and $s-t=D_1$, $s+t=D_2$. This directly gives the prescribed mixed sign. Its construction uses the factorization and can require mark height quadratic in $N$.
