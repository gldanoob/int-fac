# Square-class dimension for simultaneous smooth marks

Consider the [growing-degree monic pair](cogentic-monic-pair.md), with distinct fields $K_1,K_2$ of degrees $e_1=d$ and $e_2=d+1$, and roots $\alpha_i$. For $R$ retained primitive marks $(a_j,b_j)$, let $z_{ij}=a_j-b_j\alpha_i\ne0$. Assume the absolute norms of these elements are $B$-smooth. In field $i$, let $S_i$ be the set of prime ideals actually occurring in the principal ideals $(z_{ij})$. The map

$$
  \Phi:\mathbb F_2^R\longrightarrow
  K_1^\times/K_1^{\times2}\oplus K_2^\times/K_2^{\times2},\qquad
  (v_j)\longmapsto \left(\left[\prod_j z_{ij}^{v_j}\right]\right)_{i=1,2}
$$

has a nonzero kernel if

$$
 R > |S_1|+|S_2|+u_1+u_2,\qquad u_i=r_{1,i}+r_{2,i},
 \tag{1}
$$

where $r_{1,i},r_{2,i}$ count the real embeddings and pairs of complex embeddings. Thus $R>(2d+1)(\pi(B)+1)$ suffices without computing the occupied supports. A kernel vector gives square products in both fields.

## Ideal parity and units

For one field $K$, write $U_S$ for the group of elements with valuations zero outside $S$ and $U=\mathcal O_K^\times$. The valuation map $U_S\to\mathbb Z^S$ has kernel $U$ and image the kernel of the map sending formal $S$-ideals to their classes in $\mathrm{Cl}(K)$. That image has finite index in $\mathbb Z^S$. Dirichlet's unit theorem gives free rank $r_1+r_2-1$ for $U$, and its cyclic torsion subgroup has even order because it contains $-1$. Therefore $U_S/U_S^2$ has dimension $|S|+r_1+r_2$. Its map into $K^\times/K^{\times2}$ is injective, since $x^2\in U_S$ implies $2v_{\mathfrak p}(x)=0$ outside $S$. This proves (1) directly and counts the torsion-unit contribution.

For a bound that uses the observed valuation matrix, define $G_S\le\mathrm{Cl}(K)$ as the subgroup generated by the classes of the primes in $S$ and $t_S=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}G_S[2]$. Write $E_S$ for the kernel of the valuation-parity map on $U_S/U_S^2$. Then

$$
 0\longrightarrow U/U^2\longrightarrow E_S
   \longrightarrow G_S[2]\longrightarrow0.
$$

For a class $[z]\in E_S$, write $(z)=\mathfrak a^2$ with $\mathfrak a$ supported in $S$, and send $[z]$ to $[\mathfrak a]$. Replacing $z$ by $zx^2$ changes $\mathfrak a$ by a principal ideal. A principal $\mathfrak a$ leaves a unit after division by a square. Conversely, each class in $G_S[2]$ has a fractional representative supported in $S$ whose square is principal. A unit square in $K$ has a unit square root, proving the left injection. In particular the parity image has dimension $|S|-t_S$ and $\dim E_S=u+t_S$.

Let $\rho$ be the rank of the **joint** ideal-parity matrix of the actual columns, retaining separate prime-ideal rows for each field. Rank-nullity applied to parity on $\operatorname{im}\Phi$ gives

$$
 \operatorname{rank}\Phi\le \rho+u_1+u_2+t_{S_1}+t_{S_2}.
 \tag{2}
$$

A joint square dependency exists if $R$ exceeds the upper bound in (2). Equivalently, more than $u_1+u_2+t_{S_1}+t_{S_2}$ dimensions of joint parity kernel suffice. This can sharpen the support bound when the observed parity rank lies below its maximum. One may substitute $\dim\mathrm{Cl}(K_i)[2]$ or any upper bound on it for $t_{S_i}$. The exact support bound (1) already accounts for the class-group term through the restriction on parity rank.

## Rational primes, index primes, and ramification

A prime ideal dividing $(z_{ij})$ has rational norm $p^f$ and contributes $f v_{\mathfrak p}(z_{ij})$ to the exponent of $p$ in the absolute element norm. Thus it lies over a rational prime $p\le B$, and at most $e_i$ prime ideals lie over each $p$. This gives $|S_i|\le e_i\pi(B)$ without a ramification or index exclusion, provided the rows are actual maximal-order prime ideals. If $p\mid b_j$, primitivity gives $p\nmid a_j$ and no prime over $p$ can divide $z_{ij}$.

Away from $[\mathcal O_{K_i}:\mathbb Z[\alpha_i]]$, a prime that divides $z_{ij}$ has residue root $a_j/b_j\bmod p$. The element $\alpha_i$ generates the residue algebra there, so exactly one prime ideal corresponds to that root, and its residue degree is one. The $p$-exponent of the norm is its exact ideal valuation. Excluding primes dividing the polynomial discriminant excludes both index and ramified primes because $\operatorname{disc}(P_i)=D_{K_i}[\mathcal O_{K_i}:\mathbb Z[\alpha_i]]^2$. In that setting, a bound sharper than $e_i\pi(B)$ is the total number of eligible roots of $P_i$ modulo the allowed rational primes, or the number actually used. At an index prime, a power-order root label alone may fail to distinguish the maximal-order ideals, so a parity matrix based on those labels needs the exclusion or a direct ideal calculation.

## Elementary bound for the class-group term

Let $e=[K:\mathbb Q]$ and let $D_K$ be its field discriminant. Minkowski's ideal bound gives a representative of every ideal class of integral norm at most

$$
 X=\max\left(1,(4/\pi)^{r_2}\frac{e!}{e^e}\sqrt{|D_K|}\right).
$$

For a rational prime $p$, the local ideal counts by norm are the coefficients of $\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid p}(1-t^{f_{\mathfrak p}})^{-1}$. Each factor is coefficientwise bounded by $(1-t)^{-1}$, and there are at most $e$ factors. Multiplicativity bounds the number of integral ideals of rational norm $n$ by the ordered divisor count $\tau_e(n)$. Summing over $n\le X$ and fixing the first $e-1$ factors gives

$$
 h_K\le\sum_{n\le X}\tau_e(n)
 \le X\left(\sum_{1\le n\le X}\frac1n\right)^{e-1}
 \le X(1+\log X)^{e-1}.
$$

Consequently $t_S\le\log_2 h_K\le\log_2X+(e-1)\log_2(1+\log X)$. [Minkowski ideal bound, Theorem 14.18](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/mit18_785f21_full_lec.pdf)

For a monic defining polynomial $P$ of degree $e$ and coefficient height $H_0\le2m$, the Sylvester determinant for $P,P'$ and Hadamard's inequality give

$$
 |D_K|\le |\operatorname{disc}P|
 \le (\sqrt{e+1}\,H_0)^{e-1}(e\sqrt e\,H_0)^e.
$$

The two row bounds follow directly from the coefficient vectors of $P$ and $P'$. Hence $\log X=O(e\log(2eH_0))$. With $L=\log N$, $e\asymp(L/\log L)^{1/3}$ and $\log m=O(L/e)$, the class-group term in (2) is $O(L)$ in each field. It is a polynomial-logarithmic *dimension* overhead. This estimate does not provide a comparably cheap class-group computation.

## Character cutoff and splitting boundary

For $B=\exp(O(W))$ on the [one-third schedule](cogentic-monic-pair.md#homogeneous-norm-scale), the support bound in (1) is $\exp(O(W))$, and the threshold in (2) adds $O(\log N)$ to the measured joint parity rank. Under the separate [conditional character cutoff](cogentic-monic-squareclass-cutoff.md), the stacked probe matrix computes the kernel of $\Phi$ at the same scale for $\exp(O(W))$ given marks. In particular, among the probe restrictions to the ideal-parity kernel, at most $u_1+u_2+t_{S_1}+t_{S_2}$ can be linearly independent. Reaching a proved dimension bound for the total square-class image would certify that all further probes are redundant. Below that bound, the argument gives no earlier cutoff on the size of primes to test.

These are conditional counts given a list of smooth marks. They do not establish the supply of such marks. Even with a nonzero square kernel vector, one still needs usable square roots and reduction modulo $N$. A verified congruence of squares can give only trivial gcds, for example when the two unit residues are equal or opposite modulo all of $N$. The dimension argument gives no productive modular sign guarantee.
