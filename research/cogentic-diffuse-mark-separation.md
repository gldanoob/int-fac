# Separating a prescribed diffuse function by one field

Fix the affine grid of the [two-thirds construction](cogentic-deterministic-two-thirds.md), with $d\asymp D$, $u\asymp D$, $\log A\sim aH$, $\log B\sim bH$, $\log U\sim vV$, and $\log X_{\rm sc}\sim (v+1/x)V$. Set $\sigma=(v+1/x)/(3b)$. Take the usual thin intervals with $\log(1/w)=D\sqrt{\log L}$, and $\log K_1=kH$ for a fixed sufficiently large constant $k$. Write $\pi$ for the equal-interval mark law, $I(s)$ for the squarefree smoothness indicator at mark $s$, and $Z(s)$ for its deterministic one-point estimate in a field whose fixed CRT polynomial has no root modulo $\ell$. This latter choice is compatible with either real grid: Lemma 8.1 chooses its target coefficients and congruence polynomials independently. Here $\pi_{\max}=\exp((-a+o(1))H)$ and $\rho=\min Z(s)=\exp((-\sigma+o(1))H)$. Write $J(F)=\sum_s\pi(s)I(s)F(s)/Z(s)$, and $J_*$ for this sum after the local row peeling below. Let $T$ be the span of the constant and the $d$ nominal functions $\log|s/A-\rho_j^*|$. All norms without a subscript are for the mark probability $\pi$.

Suppose the fixed schedule has $v>xa$, $v>1/x$, $a>4b$, and $a>2b+\sigma$. For any fixed $\epsilon>0$ and real $h$ satisfying
$$
 \|h\|_2=1,\qquad h\perp T,\qquad
 \|h\|_\infty^2\le \exp((a-\sigma-b-\epsilon)H),               \tag{1}
$$
one can find a field in $U\exp(O(H))$ bit operations, with the following properties. Its constant, surviving prime rows and true trace-zero archimedean rows are independent on a set of smooth marks avoiding all nonsurviving primes. The weighted squared distance of $h$ from their row space on those marks is bounded below by a positive absolute constant. The estimate persists if the $d-1$ trace-zero rows are computed at the nominal roots: in the original constant, finite and trace-zero coordinates the surviving Gram has a polynomial lower bound, and the entry perturbation is exponentially small. The full set of $d$ real logarithms below is paired with the orthogonality restriction on the finite coefficients. The estimates also apply to a polynomial-bit rational approximation to $h$ with an exponentially small error. This note proves the diffuse case of the [collective separation criterion](cogentic-collective-field-extraction.md).

## Single-field local rank

In Proposition 10.1 suppress the second field, its prime and real rows and its likelihood factors. Shared rows do not occur. The derivation of its entry variances uses the same one- and two-mark formula, with one factor $\gamma$ instead of its square. The diagonal summands lose only one factor $1/Z$ rather than two, and cost $B^2\pi_{\max}/\rho=\exp((2b-a+\sigma+o(1))H)$. The remaining cell estimates still apply since $Aw>B^4\exp(\Omega(H))$. The resulting local quality event has probability $1-o(1)$ and admits the [nonnegative two-mark score](cogentic-conditional-score.md) with the second-field terms removed. The original induction deletes all initial multiple roots and diagonal-bad rows, and then rows that lost too much mass in previously deleted columns. Its operator error on the surviving coordinates is $e^{-c(\log L)^2}$. The same entry error estimate holds on all smooth marks restricted to surviving rows, before removing the affected columns. The local rank argument is in [Cogentic, Proposition 10.1](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/blob/4a7bc8ab0bc770e71a259b7966c500496b99010a/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34/paper.pdf#page=52).

For the norm comparisons, on a surviving ordinary row $(p,r)$ put $z_{pr}=p^{\alpha/2}1_{s\equiv r(p)}$, where $\alpha$ is the common saddle at $X_{\rm sc}$, and subtract $p^{-\alpha/2}$ from each row. Every function in the true row space on surviving marks has the form
$$
            g_{\rm true}+\sum v_{pr}(z_{pr}-p^{-\alpha/2}),
       \qquad v\perp (p^{-\alpha/2}\log p)_{pr}.
$$
This follows by transferring the missing finite direction to the constant and the sum of the $d$ real logarithms using the exact norm identity on those marks. Write $g\in T$ for the same linear combination of nominal logarithms. The single-field model quadratic form on $(g,v)$ is bounded above and below by positive absolute multiples of $\|g\|_2^2+\|v\|_2^2$. Indeed the finite diagonal variance is $1+O(u^{-1})$. Its negative outer product is generated by a vector $c_{pr}$ satisfying $c_{pr}=p^{-\alpha/2}\log p\,(1+O(1/u))/\sqrt{\varphi''(\alpha_{\mu(s)})}$; its scalar product with $v$ is $O(1/u)\|v\|_2$. The exact two-prime correction has finite-block operator norm $O(1/u)$, as the total surviving $\sum p^{-\alpha}=O(u)$. Finally the residual vector of empirical means has coordinates $m_{pr}(s)-p^{-\alpha/2}=O(p^{-\alpha/2}/u)$ and Euclidean norm $O(u^{-1/2})$. Expanding the square gives the claimed comparisons. Replacing the model by the raw or peeled empirical form preserves them on the local quality event. The coordinate vector of a nominal $g$ has norm $L^{O(1)}\|g\|_2$: comparing the averages on the $d$ root intervals to the baseline interval has diagonal $\log(1/w)+O(1)$ and off-diagonal norm $O(d)$, by factorial spacing. The constant is recovered on the baseline. This polynomial bound absorbs both the operator errors and the error $K_1^{-0.9}$ in a true logarithm on the grid.

The same comparison across intervals also gives the pointwise evaluation bound $\sup_s|g(s)|\ll\sqrt d\|g\|_2$. Each interval average is at most $\sqrt{2d+1}\|g\|_2$. Within an interval the vector of logs differs from its average by $O(1)$ in Euclidean norm: the closest log varies by at most $\log 2$, and all other variations are $O(wd)$ per coordinate. The norm of the log coefficient vector is $O(d/\log(1/w))\|g\|_2$ by the same matrix of differences. In particular for an orthonormal basis $e_j$ of $T$ one has $\sum_j e_j(s)^2\ll d$ uniformly.

## One- and two-mark bounds for arbitrary signed weights

With no prescribed rows, every distinct pair of marks has normalized two-point likelihood $\gamma(s,t)(1+O(e^{-50(\log L)^2}))$. The conditional affine width at distinct integer marks is at least a constant times the unit spacing of the coefficient lattice times its free parameter $U$, and $\log R=O(H)$ even for nearest neighbors. The proved smooth-window estimate applies there with the same strict margin $v>1/x$. The fixed $\ell$ contributes no extra zero-class because the field is root-free modulo $\ell$. If one ordinary row $(p,r)$ is prescribed, the same formula holds with its one-point factors $\Phi_s\Phi_t$ unless $p^2\mid s-t$. For all pairs a rough bound $L^{O(1)}\Phi_s\Phi_t$ holds. All weights on the hitting class satisfy $\Phi_s=p^{1-\alpha}(1+O(1/u))$, from the exact one-prime ratio. These statements follow from the same one- and two-point arithmetic cases in Lemmas 9.4 and 9.5. [Uniform window and its error range](cogentic-dirichlet-smooth-windows.md)

Uniformly in $s\ne t$, $\gamma(s,t)=1+o(1)$. Its contributing primes lie above $d$ and divide $|s-t|\le A$. Those up to $d(\log L)^2$ have reciprocal sum $O(\log\log L/\log L)$ by the prime number theorem, and those larger have reciprocal sum $O(\log A/[d(\log L)^2\log d])=o(1)$. Thus $\mathbf E(J(h^2)-1)^2=o(1)$ by (1), since the diagonal costs $O(\pi_{\max}\|h\|_\infty^2/\rho)$.

A stronger bound for the off-diagonal kernel is needed after summing many coordinates. The positive symmetric kernel $|\gamma(s,t)-1|$ has operator norm $O(1/(d\log d))+e^{-\Omega(H)}$ on $L^2(\pi)$ with its diagonal set to zero. For a fixed $s$, expand the upper bound as $\sum_{k>1,\,k\mid s-t}\eta(k)$, where $\eta$ is multiplicative on squarefree products of $d<p\le\sqrt B$ and $\eta(p)=1/(p-1)$. The mass of one class modulo any $k$ is $O(1/k+1/(Aw))$, since each interval has positive sieve density. Thus the row sum is $O(\sum_{p>d}p^{-2})+O((\log B)^{O(1)}/(Aw))$. Symmetry gives the operator bound. With the additional requirement $p\mid s-t$ at an eligible large prime, the row sum is at most $O(1/(pd\log d))+O((\log B)^{O(1)}/(Aw))$, by coprimality. In particular
$$
 \mathbf E\sum_j |J(he_j)|^2=o(1),\qquad u\,\mathbf E|J(h)|^2=o(1).
$$
The mean-zero terms from the constant kernel vanish by orthogonality. Apply the operator bound and $\sum_j e_j^2\ll d$, accounting for the single-mark diagonal by $O(d\pi_{\max}/\rho)$. Here and below constants in these moments may depend on the fixed parameter schedule.

For the finite rows one has
$$
     \mathbf E\sum_{(p,r)\text{ admitted}} |J(h z_{pr})|^2=o(1).       \tag{2}
$$
Condition on the row and use its admission probability $(1+o(1))/p$. In the leading quadratic form the coefficient after the two one-point factors and the admission probability is $p^{1-\alpha}$. The main sums are bounded by
$$
  \sum_{p}p^{1-\alpha}\sum_{r\bmod p}
                \left|\sum_{s\equiv r(p)}\pi(s)h(s)\right|^2 .
$$
Adding the missing residue $m$ only increases this upper bound. By finite Fourier inversion, the zero frequency vanishes and the remaining expression is at most $B^{-\alpha/2}(A+O(B^2\log B))\sum_s\pi(s)^2 h(s)^2=o(1)$. For this elementary additive large-sieve bound, the distinct nonzero fractions $j/p$ have circular separation at least $1/B^2$; the dual Dirichlet-kernel Gram has absolute row sum at most $A+O(B^2\log B)$ by ordering the neighboring points. The $O(1/u)$ relative variation in $\Phi_s$ only contributes $O(1/u)$ to the squared norm: apply the same calculation to $|h|$ with the zero frequency now contributing $O(\sum p^{-\alpha})=O(u)$. The Schur bound with $p\mid s-t$ controls the absolute $\gamma-1$ error by $O(u/(d\log d))+e^{-\Omega(H)}=o(1)$. The rough factors at $p^2\mid s-t$ cost at most $L^{O(1)}\sum_{p>\sqrt B}p^{1-\alpha}(1/p^2+1/(Aw))=e^{-\Omega(H)}$. The true diagonal costs $L^{O(1)}B\pi_{\max}/\rho=o(1)$. This proves (2). On a locally good field, subtracting the row means in (2) costs at most $O(u)|J(h)|^2$.

## The mass deleted by peeling

For an admitted row $j=(p,r)$ define a nonnegative defect $D_j$ as the sum of its multiple-root indicator, the squared centered diagonal divided by the squared bad-diagonal threshold, and $p^\alpha$ times the sum of squared centered off-diagonal errors against other-prime rows divided by $e^{-c_0(\log L)^2}$. Take $c_0>0$ small enough for the source's protected-row induction. The local conditional moment bounds give $\mathbf E[D_j\mid j]\le\eta=e^{-c_1(\log L)^2}$ uniformly: conditioning on the two implicated rows provides each summand in the last term, with the second row admitted at probability $(1+o(1))/p'$. The sum of its $p'^{-\alpha}$ weights is $O(u)$. The derivative is uniform given one root, so its conditional multiple-root probability is $O(1/p)$. On a locally good field, if $D_j<1$ this row survives. Indeed the source's protected-row estimate applies to this individual row, with $p$ in place of $\ell$: a deleted row of the same prime has a different residue and cannot remove any mark hitting $j$, and the incremental loss is bounded by $2\mu_*+\mu_*^{1/2}p^{\alpha/2}(\sum_{p'\ne p}A_{jj'}^2)^{1/2}$. No simultaneous probabilistic bound for every row is required.

Set $M_j=\sum_{s\equiv r(p)}\pi(s)h(s)^2$ and $H_j=J(h^2 1_{s\equiv r(p)})$. The rough conditional two-point upper bound and the diagonal give
$$
 \mathbf E[H_j^2\mid j]\le L^{C}\left(p^{2(1-\alpha)}M_j^2
             +p^{1-\alpha}(\pi_{\max}\|h\|_\infty^2/\rho)M_j\right)=:B_j.
$$
Take $t_j=\max(e^{-C'H},\sqrt{B_j/\eta})$ with $C'$ fixed sufficiently large. The total $h^2$-mass lost on a locally good field is at most the sum, over admitted rows, of $t_jD_j+H_j^2/(4t_j)$ by the elementary inequality $H_j\le t_j+H_j^2/(4t_j)$ whenever a deleted row has $D_j\ge1$. The mean of this computable sum is $o(1)$. In fact it is at most $L^{O(1)}\sqrt\eta$ times
$$
    \sum_p p^{-\alpha}+
    \sqrt{\pi_{\max}\|h\|_\infty^2/\rho}\sum_p p^{-\alpha/2}+o(1),
$$
because $\sum_r M_j=1$ and $\sum_r\sqrt{M_j}\le\sqrt p$. The first sum is $O(u)$ and the second term is exponentially small by (1).

Combine the local quality score with fixed sufficiently large multiples of the nonnegative quantities just bounded: $(J(h^2)-1)^2$, the squared archimedean and finite correlations, $u|J(h)|^2$, and the upper estimator of the deleted $h^2$-mass. Their combined expectation is $o(1)$. If the score is below one, then $J_*(h^2)$ is bounded away from zero and the correlations on the surviving marks are arbitrarily small absolute multiples of $(\|g\|_2^2+\|v\|_2^2)^{1/2}$. To see this, subtract the deleted-mark contribution from the raw correlations by Cauchy–Schwarz and the raw upper norm bound on surviving rows. The local lower norm bound now controls the squared projection of $h$ and gives the asserted positive distance. The same argument permits the exponentially small true-log perturbation. Every score term has at most two marked smoothness indicators and at most two prescribed rows. Its finite sums have $\exp(O(H))$ terms and bit requirements at most that size; the cutoff on $t_j$ bounds the numerical coefficients. Applying the [two-coordinate convolution](cogentic-batched-affine-fibers.md) fixes a field attaining the desired score in $U\exp(O(H))$ bit operations.
