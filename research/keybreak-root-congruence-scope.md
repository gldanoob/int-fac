# Scope of the coefficient congruences under GRH

In the pinned KeyBreak proof, (G.20) states a comparison for a compatible system of up to three root or parent equations. The moment and initialization arguments use only one conditioned coefficient root at a time. In the interior incoming second moment, two parent divisibilities and one child root can occur together as a product of indicators. An optional change to condition on all three would have the scale bound \(Y_1^2Y_2\). The existing proof retains the parent indicators inside the conditional moment. [KeyBreak, Lemma G.4 and Appendices G–H](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=109)

## Conditioning in the GRH proof

- **Individual requests and rational counts.** In (G.5)–(G.6), a prescribed interior label \((\ell,s)\) carries the one event \(R_{\ell,s}=\{F(s,1)\equiv0\pmod\ell\}\). The top private prime \(p\) divides the rational numerator \(G(P)\). The coefficient law for its own request is unconditional. Lemma G.3 averages over one root parameter on the rational side. The measure (G.42) and the bad-rational-mass estimate (G.43) multiply that average by the corresponding single-root mass from (G.20). [KeyBreak, (G.5)–(G.6), Lemma G.3, and (G.42)–(G.43)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=105)
- **Value laws and moments.** In G.4–G.5, two marks satisfying \(a_i\equiv s b_i\pmod q\) with \(q\nmid b_i\) both express the same root event, because \(F(a_i,b_i)\equiv b_i^d F(s,1)\pmod q\). The Gaussian image is restricted once and both values are divided by \(q\). In G.6 the probability in (G.28) includes the parent factor \(\ell^{-1}\) for the event \(0<|F(P)|/q\in\ell S_y\). The parent condition is part of that event. G.8–G.10 use the same single-root law with one- or two-value tests. Expanding the complete-band test (G.9) retains its divisibility indicators, and G.9 evaluates each on the separate progressions. The phase and common-gcd conditioning in G.7–G.10 is performed within the value distribution already obtained in G.4–G.5. [KeyBreak, Lemmas G.4–G.9 and Proposition G.10](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=109)
- **Interior incidences.** An incidence \(X_{\ell,u;(q,s)}\) in (G.46) vanishes off the parent and child roots. The proof first bounds the normalizing denominator using the *separate* parent mean \(\tau_{\ell,u}\), then drops the parent-good restriction by a positive majorant. After (G.48) the incoming sum is evaluated under only \(R_{q,s}\). The parent prime appears through \(1_{\{0<|F(P)|/(q\ell)\in S_{y_i}\}}\), initially with a denominator mask. Its second moment in (G.50), one-point upper bound in (G.51), and exceptional root-average bound in (G.52) all use the one child root. Here \(q\le Y_{i+1}\le Y_2\) and \(\ell\le Y_i\le Y_1\). [KeyBreak, Proposition G.11 and (G.46)–(G.52)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)
- **Top band and search.** In (G.54)–(G.55), the analogous incoming law has one root \(q\le Y_1\), with the private parent label fixed by the rational mark. The child-bad indicator is correlated with the whole field, and (G.53) bounds it by Cauchy–Schwarz in the conditional law of that one root. Once a field is fixed, H.4 uses the deterministic good-state counts in that field and fresh search randomness. Its guarantee given public history leaves the reference mean in the original single-root law. Neither the previous path nor simultaneous goodness of the bands imposes new coefficient congruences. H.8 combines setup and supply on the original law before fixing the field. [KeyBreak, Proposition G.12, Lemma H.4, and Proposition H.8](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=121)

In particular, the use of the phrase “at most three” in G.4 covers more systems than the displayed conditional moment estimates require. Appendix G uses the reordering above for its GRH recursion. The unconditional comparison in (3.4) serves a different proof. [KeyBreak, §7.2 and the introduction to Appendix G](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=104)

## A sufficient guard for the actual root probabilities

Fix the rational environment \((Q,r)\). For an admissible odd \(q\nmid NQ\), the coefficient of the constant coordinate \(c_{d-1}\) in \(F(s,1)\) is \(2G(s,1)\), a unit modulo \(q\). After fixing the other coefficients, \(R_{q,s}\) asks that this independent one-dimensional Gaussian coordinate lie in one residue class. For \(\sigma/q\ge1\), one-dimensional Poisson summation gives, uniformly over residue classes \(a\),
\[
\Pr\{c_{d-1}\equiv a\pmod q\}
=\frac1q\left(1+O(e^{-\pi(\sigma/q)^2})\right).
\]
The nonzero terms in the Gaussian sum modulo \(q\) are bounded by \(2\sum_{h\ge1}e^{-\pi(\sigma h/q)^2}\), and the denominator has the analogous bound at modulus one. Averaging over the other coefficients retains the same relative bound. The excluded slope has \(G(s,1)=0\) and \(F_0(s,1)=NQ^{-d}\) modulo \(q\), so its root event is empty.

For the root comparisons actually used, \(\sigma/Y_1\to\infty\) suffices for uniform relative \(1+o(1)\) flatness. The stronger explicit bound \(\sigma\ge n^6Y_1\), with \(n\) the input bit length, gives negligible relative error of the same order as the finite Gaussian sampler. At the leading log scales \(\sigma=V_0^\chi n^{O(1)}\) this comparison follows from \(\chi v_0>y_1\). This scale check is separate from the two-mark kernel comparison \(\sigma/V_i^2\) and the separate-period endpoint checks. In the tiered interior the kernel guard already implies the single-root guard, since \(V_1^2\ge Y_1\). The literal algorithm in (H.1) checks the stronger \(2^{20A^2t}Y_1^3\) inequality. Using a smaller guard in an adapted proof requires replacing that finite check. [KeyBreak, (G.20) and (H.1)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=126) · [Interior kernel and endpoint checks](keybreak-narrow-interior-guards.md)

## Finite sampling and initialization

Lemma H.1 compares the bounded sampler and the full product Gaussian globally at total variation error \(\epsilon=e^{-n^{10}}\). If an event has ideal probability \(p>\epsilon\), both conditional laws exist and their total variation distance is at most \(2\epsilon/(p-\epsilon)\), by comparing the two numerators and denominators of every conditional probability. In the actual single-root applications \(p\asymp1/q\), so the cost is \(O(Y_1\epsilon)\). This remains negligible after inverse smoothness densities and the prescribed moment roots. It is a separate adjustment from relative Gaussian image comparison, which survives restriction to an image event directly. [KeyBreak, (G.20), Proposition G.8, and Lemma H.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=127)

At setup, a nontrivial content or leading-coefficient gcd either yields a factor or is excluded by the coefficient bounds. The possible rejection without a factor in the polynomial gcd screen comes from \(F'(m_N)=0\pmod N\). With the other coefficients fixed, varying \(c_{d-1}\) changes this derivative by the unit \(2Q\) modulo \(N\). Since the supported interval has fewer than \(N\) integers, at most one value in it causes that rejection. Its unconditional Gaussian mass is \(O(1/\sigma)\), plus the global finite-sampler error after transfer. H.8 combines the unconditional setup failure and supply failure on the original law. [KeyBreak, §H.1 and Proposition H.8](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=127)

A setup estimate conditional on a hypothetical compatible system of affine congruences is also available. Let \(M_{\rm root}\) be the product of the distinct primes of the system. After all other coefficients are fixed, the allowed constant coordinates form an empty set or one residue class modulo a divisor of \(M_{\rm root}\). In a nonempty fiber and for \(\sigma/M_{\rm root}\ge1\), its Gaussian atom bound is \(O(M_{\rm root}/\sigma)\), by counting points of that progression in \([-\sigma,\sigma]\). The exceptional derivative value still contributes at most one supported atom. Averaging over the other conditional coordinates gives this ideal rejection bound, and conditional total variation adds the appropriate event-mass error. G.13's \(O(Y_1^3/\sigma)\) scale covers that more general estimate. [KeyBreak, Proposition G.13 and §H.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=123)

## A restricted triple and a counterexample to the generic improvement

If one chooses to condition an interior incoming pair on both parent divisibilities as well as the child root, the primes satisfy \(q\le Y_{i+1}\) and \(Y_{i+1}<\ell_1,\ell_2\le Y_i\). The parent tests are affine congruences modulo \(\ell_r\) on the raw evaluations after removing the coprime \(q\). For any compatible such system the distinct-prime product \(M_{\rm root}\) and the rank-weighted product \(M_{\rm rank}=\prod_p p^{r_p}\) are at most \(Y_1^2Y_2\). Here \(r_p\) is the local rank, so repeated primes in two equations can affect the latter product even when they do not affect the former. With independent Gaussian coefficients and \(\sigma/M_{\rm root}\to\infty\), the system has ideal mass \((1+o(1))/M_{\rm rank}\). To see this, select a set of pivot coordinates for each prime and take their union, of size at most three. Conditional on the other coordinates, the pivot residues are relatively uniform modulo \(M_{\rm root}\) by the one-dimensional bound, and CRT gives the rank-weighted mass. Thus the optional restricted use has finite conditional TV loss \(O(Y_1^2Y_2\epsilon)\). Its counterpart at the top has just the algebraic child root. [KeyBreak, Lemma G.4 and (G.48)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)

The bound \(Y_1^2Y_2\) is false for arbitrary compatible triples of roots in the stated ranges. When \(Y_2=o(Y_1)\), take three distinct primes in \((Y_1/2,Y_1]\) avoiding \(2NQr\), and prescribe slope zero at all three. At the source scales the interval contains far more than the \(O(n)\) excluded primes, so these are admissible active first-level labels. Since \(F(0,1)=F_0(0,1)-2r c_{d-1}\), each equation fixes the same independent coordinate modulo its own prime, and CRT gives a compatible residue class modulo a product greater than \(Y_1^3/8\). The rank-weighted product is the same. The three unrelated roots belong to the generic comparison, while an incoming pair has the prescribed parent and child ranges above. [KeyBreak, (G.5) and Lemma G.4](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=109)

## Averaged single-root flatness from three coefficients

Let \(q\) be an odd prime, and let three independent integer Gaussians of width \(\sigma\) multiply the consecutive powers \(1,s,s^2\) at a finite slope \(s\bmod q\). Additional independent coefficients and deterministic centers are allowed. For \(q^{1/3+\varepsilon}\le\sigma\le q\), uniformly in a separate target residue \(u_s\) for every \(s\), the polynomial value \(S(s)\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{s\bmod q}\left|\Pr(S(s)=u_s)-\frac1q\right|
 \ll q^{o(1)}\left(\frac q{\sigma^3}+\frac1\sigma\right).
\]
The exceptional fraction of slopes where a uniform superpolynomial *relative* error bound fails is of this same order, as is the total actual root probability on those exceptional slopes. The estimates are for one prescribed root at a time. They leave the Gaussian law after conditioning on a root to be treated in each application.

Indeed the discrete Gaussian characteristic function at real frequency \(x\) has absolute value at most \(C\exp(-c\sigma^2\|x\|^2)\), by periodizing the real Gaussian Fourier transform and bounding the denominator; real translates have the same uniform bound when \(\sigma\to\infty\). Fourier inversion bounds each absolute error by the sum over nonzero \(t\) of
\[
 \frac{C^3}{q}\exp\left(-c\sigma^2\max_{j=0,1,2}\|ts^j/q\|^2\right).
\]
For centered integer lifts of \((t,ts,ts^2)=(x,y,z)\bmod q\) of height at most \(L\), the map from \((s,t)\) is injective. One has \(x\ne0\) and \(xz=y^2+jq\). There are \(O(1+L^2/q)\) choices of \(j\) for each \(y\) and \(q^{o(1)}\) signed divisor pairs when the right side is nonzero. If it vanishes then \(y=z=0\), contributing \(O(L)\). Hence the number of triples is \(\ll q^{o(1)}(L^3/q+L)\). Sum the Gaussian bound over dyadic heights starting at \(q/\sigma\), extending trivially once the height exceeds \(q/2\). This proves the aggregate estimate. Exclude slopes that have a triple at height at most \((q/\sigma)(\log q)^2\). Dividing the count by \(q\) gives the asserted fraction, and every remaining slope has relative error \(O(q\exp(-c(\log q)^4))\). The aggregate absolute error bounds the actual mass on the excluded set by its uniform mass plus the displayed error. [Conic count and its Lean chart](keybreak-quadratic-mark-geometry.md#count-for-one-prime)

For the anchored form \(F_0+2G S\), multiplying all frequencies by \(2G(s,1)\) permutes the nonzero frequencies wherever the multiplier is a unit. When \(q\mid Q\) every finite slope is admissible. When \(q\nmid Q\), the one excluded anchor slope is a deterministic nonroot if \(q\nmid kN\); including it in an absolute-error sum adds \(1/q\). A finite sampler at total variation distance \(\epsilon_0\) costs at most \(q\epsilon_0\) in the sum or in the relative error for a single slope. Infinity is a single additional coefficient event, with mass \(O(1/\sigma+1/q)\) where the appropriate multiplier is a unit.

The exponent \(1/3\) is sharp in fixed-power terms for this statement with three Gaussians and arbitrary separately chosen targets. At \(\sigma\le q^{1/3-\varepsilon}\), take all further coefficients zero and the three Gaussians centered at zero. Their central coefficient box \(|c_i|\le (1+\sigma)(\log q)^2\) has probability \(1-q^{-\omega(1)}\) and maps at each slope to at most \(O((1+\sigma)^3(\log q)^6)=o(q)\) residues. Pick a missed target at every slope. The aggregate absolute error is at least \(1-o(1)\).
