# A prescribed genus for the half-width quadratic sampler

Let \(N>1\) be odd, let \(v\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times\) be public, and put \(\pi=\sqrt{-2N}\), \(\mathcal O=\mathbb Z[\pi]\), and \(G=\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O)\). Under Dirichlet GRH one can choose an oriented prime ideal \(P\) in polynomial expected bit time such that translating a uniform square class by \([P]\) selects precisely the genus with leading norm in the unit squareclass of \(v\). The canonical reduced form \([A,2b',C']\) has \(A\le\sqrt{8N/3}\). For unit \(A\), the sampler computes a root \(z^2\equiv vA\pmod N\) whose signs are exactly uniform conditional on the reduced form in the ideal uniform-class law. The result includes repeated prime factors in \(N\) and both Jacobi signs for \(v\).

## An oriented prime in the requested residue class

Choose \(t\equiv v\pmod N\) and prescribe \(t\equiv1\pmod8\) if the Jacobi symbol \((v/N)\) is \(+1\), or \(t\equiv5\pmod8\) if it is \(-1\). In either case \(t\equiv1\pmod4\), so quadratic reciprocity gives \((N/t)=(t/N)=(v/N)\) for prime \(t\) in the progression. The choice modulo eight gives \((-2/t)=(v/N)\). Thus \((-2N/t)=1\). The single class modulo \(8N\) is reduced. These computations use Jacobi symbols and CRT and do not require the prime factors of \(N\).

The Dirichlet GRH estimate for primes in that class on \([N^3,2N^3]\) gives a weighted main term \(N^3/\phi(8N)\gg N^2\), with error \(O(N^{3/2}\log^2 N)\). Uniform draws from the progression followed by verified primality therefore have success probability \(\gg1/\log N\), uniformly in \(v\) for sufficiently large \(N\). The small-input fallback can enlarge this interval to a fixed sufficiently large range. For example the preferred progression at \(N=3,v=1\) has only the composite 49 in \([27,54]\), while the alternate split class modulo eight gives 31. Pick a verified root \(R^2\equiv-2N\pmod t\) and use \(P=(t,R+\pi)\). Either orientation gives the same class modulo squares. At \(v=1\), take the unit ideal with \(t=1\), omitting prime generation. [Lee, GRH estimates for prime progressions](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13457#page=2)

## Integer squaring, translation, and reduction

Use the norm-basis convention from the prescribed split divisor calculation. A reduced primitive seed \([a,2b,c]\) with \((a,N)=1\) corresponds to \(I=(a,b+\pi)\), with \(ac=b^2+2N\). Keep even \(a\) in the direct weighted sampler. At two the congruence has one root modulo two and no roots modulo four. If \(a\) is even, \(v_2(a)=1\), \(b\) is even, and \(c\) is odd. For odd primes dividing \(a\), \(b\) is a unit. In particular \(g=(a,2b)\) is either one or two, and \(q=a/g\) is odd. [Norm-basis convention](keybreak-prescribed-form-divisor.md#which-class-is-translated) · [Direct reduced-form sampler](keybreak-direct-reduced-class-sampler.md)

The integral ideal \(I^2/g\) is generated by \(a^2/g\), \(q(b+\pi)\), and \((b^2-2N+2b\pi)/g\). Its norm is \(q^2\), and its projection to the imaginary coefficient is all of \(\mathbb Z\). Choose \(u,w\in\mathbb Z\) with \(u q+w(2b/g)=1\). Then
\[
I^2/g=(q^2,B+\pi),\qquad B\equiv uqb+w(b^2-2N)/g\pmod {q^2}.
\]
The Bézout combination supplies a generator with imaginary coefficient one and the norm fixes the horizontal step. In the direct sampler \(a<t\) when \(t\) is a prime from the prescribed interval, since \(a\le\sqrt{8N/3}\). Hence \((q,t)=1\). Multiply the ideals by CRT:
\[
(I^2/g)P=(L,B'+\pi),\quad L=q^2t,\quad B'\equiv B\pmod{q^2},\quad B'\equiv R\pmod t .
\]
This also applies to \(t=1\), where the last congruence is vacuous. The product is primitive and has odd norm, so the prime above two causes no overlap in this step.

Properly reduce \([L,2B',(B'^2+2N)/L]\), retaining the first column \((x,y)\) of the determinant-one basis change. Put \(\gamma=Lx+(B'+\pi)y\), and let \(\eta\) be the second new basis element for the reduced form \([A,2b',C']\). Taking norms and the oriented basis determinant gives
\[
\operatorname{Nm}(\gamma)=AL,\qquad \overline\gamma\eta/L=b'+\pi,\qquad
(I^2/g)P=(\gamma/A)J,\quad J=(A,b'+\pi).
\]
Use \(\rho(a_0+b_0\pi)=a_0\bmod N\), the quotient by \((N,\pi)\), also on the localization at rational integers coprime to \(N\). The result is
\[
z=\rho(\gamma)/q\pmod N,\qquad z^2\equiv AL/q^2\equiv Av\pmod N.
\]
Verify the square directly and choose an independent fair global sign. For \(N\ge3\), \(A\le\sqrt{8N/3}<N\), so a nonunit \(A\) yields a verified proper gcd. The scaling by \(g\) is already absorbed in the normalization \(\rho(g\gamma)/a=\rho(\gamma)/q\). All the integer operations have polynomial bit cost. [Proper reduction and root normalization in the untargeted order](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md#the-root-attached-to-a-square)

## Exact sign fibers and the genus label

For a two-torsion class \([T]\in G[2]\), take an ideal representative prime to \(N\), write \(T^2=(\beta)\), and define \(\sigma([T])=[\rho(\beta)/\operatorname{Nm}T]\) in \(U_2/\{\pm1\}\), where \(U_2=\{s\in(\mathbb Z/N)^\times:s^2=1\}\). Units of \(\mathcal O\) are \(\pm1\). Rescaling a representative by \(\lambda\) changes its sign by \(\rho(\lambda^2/\operatorname{Nm}\lambda)=1\) in the localization. This proves that \(\sigma\) is a well-defined homomorphism. For fixed target \(J\) and fixed \(P\), changing \(I\) to a representative of \([I][T]\) multiplies the raw generator \(g\gamma\) by \(\pm\lambda^2\beta\), and its seed norm by \(\operatorname{Nm}(\lambda)\operatorname{Nm}(T)\). The relative modular root changes by \(\sigma([T])\). The public multiplier \(P\) is common to both identities. [Sign homomorphism argument](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md#the-sign-homomorphism)

For every unitary divisor factorization \(de=N\), the ideal \((d,\pi)\) has square \((d)\), since \(\gcd(d,2e)=1\). Its equivalent integral representative and a generator of that representative's square are
\[
T_d=\frac{d+\pi}{d}(d,\pi)=(d+2e,\pi-2e),\qquad
\beta_d=d-2e+2\pi.
\]
Its norm is \(d+2e\), an odd integer coprime to \(N\). The ratio \((d-2e)/(d+2e)\) is \(-1\bmod d\) and \(+1\bmod e\). Unitary divisors use whole prime powers and yield all relative signs, so \(\sigma\) is onto without a squarefreeness assumption. The unitary divisors serve only in the proof. Conditional on any unit reduced class of \(I^2P\), a uniform seed class ranges uniformly over a torsor for \(G[2]\). Surjectivity and the independent global sign give the exact uniform root law of \(vA\).

To identify the entire genus quotient, the canonical ambiguous forms have \(b=0\), \(A=2b\), or \(A=C\), by inversion of the middle coefficient and the boundary conventions. If \(A=2b\), \(2N=b(2C-b)\) is odd or divisible by four according to the parity of \(b\). If \(A=C\), the two factors of \(2N=(A-b)(A+b)\) likewise have the same parity. Thus only diagonal primitive forms occur. They are exactly the unordered coprime splittings \(AC=2N\). Hence \(|G/G^2|=|G[2]|=2^{\omega(N)}\). The dyadic class \([(2,\pi)]\) is nontrivial, since its canonical form is \([2,0,N]\), and it lies in \(\ker\sigma\), since its square is \((2)\). The sign kernel has order two and consists exactly of this class and the identity. [Ambiguous canonical forms, Lenstra–Pomerance §2](https://cr.yp.to/bib/1992/lenstra.pdf#page=5)

The unit squareclass of the norm defines a homomorphism from \(G\) to \((\mathbb Z/N)^\times/((\mathbb Z/N)^\times)^2\). A prime-to-\(N\) representative exists in every class: choose \(x\) so the form at \((x,1)\) is nonzero modulo each odd prime dividing \(N\), then make this primitive vector the first basis vector. A principal rescaling changes the norm by \(\rho(\lambda)^2\bmod N\). Thus the map factors through \(G/G^2\), and the primes chosen above make it surjective. The orders of source and target are both \(2^{\omega(N)}\), so the map is an isomorphism on the quotient. Consequently \([P]G^2\) is exactly the genus specified by the unit squareclass of \(v\), including Jacobi-negative classes. Conditional on the public choice of \(P\), a fresh uniform seed class gives a uniform output on this coset because squaring has equal fibers.

## Sampling work and staged supply

The direct reduced-class proposal works with \(b^2\equiv-2N\bmod a\) and the same dyadic factored-integer weights. The odd local root bounds are unchanged. The bound at two is one instead of four, and there is no restriction to odd leading coefficients. The missing prime-to-\(N\) fraction is proved by the same ramified root sum. For the denominator one can count split primes in a dyad near \(\sqrt N/4\) under Dirichlet GRH and center each local root. This gives \(h(-8N)\gg\sqrt N/\log N\); the full root-count sum gives \(h(-8N)\le\sqrt N(\log N)^{O(1)}\). Thus the direct sampler has inverse-polylogarithmic acceptance and class distance \(e^{-\omega(H)}\) on the rough branch. Its effect on the joint class and sign law is bounded by the same distance by pushforward. Bounded samples and transcript coupling use this joint estimate, since a total variation estimate can otherwise dominate the mass of a particular rare fiber. [Direct sampler and ramified counts](keybreak-direct-reduced-class-sampler.md) · [Uniform GRH character estimate](keybreak-mpqs-root-distribution.md#sampling-the-coefficient-and-mixing-its-crt-multipliers) · [Conditional and joint sign laws](keybreak-reduced-form-root-signs.md#distribution-needed-by-a-sampler)

For a sampler without the roughness assumption, the wide factored-ideal proposal also transfers to \(\pi^2=-2N\). Its local norm at primes dividing \(2N\) is \(x^2\) times a unit, so the lattice-count proof and its uniformity in the class are unchanged. If the sampled norm can exceed \(t\), reject only when divisible by \(t\), enabling the same CRT formula. The weighted count of such norms among the primitive ideals up to \(Y\asymp N^4\) is \(O((Y/t)(1+\log Y))\), by the squarefree-divisor sum. This incurs probability \((\log N)^{O(1)}/t\) under the ideal law, whose total count is \(\gg Y/\operatorname{poly}(\log N)\). This version costs polynomial expected bit time under Dirichlet GRH and has inverse-polynomial class distance. [Wide factored-ideal sampler](keybreak-random-quadratic-ideal.md)

On the rough branch the staged smooth-coefficient count applies uniformly to every prescribed \(v\). For odd split primes outside \(2N\), the genus character of a chosen prime ideal is the corresponding character of the prime modulo \(N\), independent of orientation. A nontrivial genus character is nonprincipal modulo \(N\). Its product with the splitting character remains nonprincipal, since the splitting character of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-2N})\) has a conductor-eight component. Dirichlet GRH thus bounds the bias in any private prime dyad by \(e^{-\Omega(DH)}\) before the existing good-parent and eligibility deletions. If those deletions have fraction \(O(D^{-B_0})\), the retained bias is \(O(D^{-B_0})\), and multiplication over \(\Theta(D)\) prime positions makes the error \(o(1)\) relative to \(1/|G/G^2|\). Orthogonality gives the same lower count for the prescribed genus as for the principal genus. Centering roots of \(-2N\) modulo the counted squarefree coefficients gives primitive reduced forms below the same \(\sqrt N/2\) target, and the genus-index factor cancels in the class denominator. The upper prefix count uses the same leading-coefficient bounds with adjusted absolute constants. [Staged genus mixing and prefix counts](keybreak-reduced-form-smooth-supply.md#field-good-prime-bands-and-staged-prefixes)

The sampler gains the ability to choose the public unit squareclass of the half-width coefficient at polynomial extra cost, with root identity \(z^2=vA\). It removes any need to find an auxiliary prime by sampling from all residue classes and then waiting for residue \(v\). The prime \(t\) serves only in the auxiliary form arithmetic. If a later public row uses \(v\), its own public representation and the associated relation costs still need to be supplied. The half-width coefficient and the leading staged smoothness loss are the same as in the untargeted sampler. [Half-width schedule and charged work](keybreak-quadratic-class-integration.md)

An exact finite check for every odd \(3\le N\le201\) tested all unit \(v\), obtaining a prime in a split progression inside the original interval in all 8,282 cases. It checked 100,848 seed reductions, including 30,262 even seeds, and verified every root multiplicity in 36,356 unit output fibers after adding the fair global sign. Nonunit outputs passed the proper-gcd check.
