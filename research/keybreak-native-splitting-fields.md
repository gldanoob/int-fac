# Splitting fields of the ordinary single-input coefficient draws

On the native Gaussian schedule, an accepted degree-$d$ field has full symmetric Galois closure except on a raw-draw event of probability $\exp(-\Omega(DH))$. This also excludes proper intermediate fields. More generally, from a pool of at most $\exp(CH)$ fresh ordinary draws, every subfamily of at most $cD$ distinct accepted draws for a sufficiently small fixed $c>0$ has linearly disjoint Galois closures over the rationals, simultaneously with probability tending to one. Here $C$ is fixed, $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$ and $H=D\log\log N$. The statements concern field selection from this particular law on one input. They give no bound on the density of smooth pairs, nor on a purpose-built family of correlated fields. ## A local certificate

Condition on the native environment $r,Q$ and take an auxiliary odd prime $p\nmid NQ$. The $d$ uniform coefficient residues of the perturbation give the uniform polynomial $F$ of degree at most $d$ with $F(r/Q)=NQ^{-d}\ne0$ in $\mathbb F_p$. This affine space has $p^d$ points. Given a monic irreducible $h$ of degree $q<d$ with $q>1$, the conditions $h\mid F$ and the prescribed value leave $p^{d-q}$ possibilities, by polynomial division or CRT. At most one monic factor of degree greater than $d/2$ occurs in any such $F$, including multiplicities.

Sum over primes $q\in[3d/5,4d/5]$. For prime $q$ there are $(p^q-p)/q$ monic irreducibles of degree $q$, by counting elements of degree $q$ in $\mathbb F_{p^q}$. The probability of a factor in the indicated degree range is exactly $\sum_q(1-p^{1-q})/q$, hence $\gg 1/\log d$ by the ordinary prime number theorem. Discard leading-coefficient zero and nonsquarefree reductions. For monic polynomials of degree $d\ge2$ the fraction that are nonsquarefree equals $1/p$. This follows from the squarefree generating function $(1-pt^2)/(1-pt)$. A degree-$d$ member of the affine space normalizes to a uniform monic polynomial avoiding the specified linear root. Indeed each such monic polynomial has exactly one admissible nonzero scalar. Counting that restriction, and the $1/p$ chance of a zero leading coefficient, costs $O(1/p)$ in the probability. For $p$ polynomially large in $d$ the full-degree, squarefree witness thus has probability $\gg 1/\log d$.

This factor has residue degree $q$ in the number field. Scaling the native primitive element by the leading coefficient makes it integral and preserves the factor degrees and squarefree test at this $p$. Dedekind–Kummer then applies because $p$ is outside the power-basis discriminant. In a nontrivial tower of absolute degree $d$, both step degrees are at most $d/2$. Their residue degrees multiply to $q$ and are at most the corresponding step degrees. Primality of $q$ makes this impossible. Lean verifies this tower implication and the conductor-qualified polynomial factor formulation. [Dedekind–Kummer theorem](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/ArithmeticEquivalenceLectureNotes.pdf) · [Lean field and polynomial results](../formal/cogentic/Cogentic/RandomizedSubfieldDegree.lean)

For an irreducible native polynomial the Galois action on the roots is transitive. The absence of intermediate fields makes the action primitive, by the subgroup–block correspondence. Frobenius at the witness prime has a cycle of length $q$. Raising it to the least common multiple of the other cycle lengths leaves a single $q$-cycle, since those lengths are all below $q$. For large $d$ it fixes at least three roots. Jordan's theorem now gives a subgroup containing $A_d$. The native mod-two irreducibility provides the required irreducibility over the rationals on the continuing branch. [Jordan's theorem, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.5169)

## Distinguishing discriminant square classes

Fix any nonzero integer $a$ of bit length polynomial in the input bit length. For a new ordinary field let $\Delta$ denote the discriminant of its primitive native polynomial. On the continuing branch the raw polynomial is primitive. More generally scaling its coefficients by a nonzero rational scales the discriminant by a rational square. If $\Delta/a$ is a rational square, at any test prime $p\nmid 2aNQ$ the degree-$d$ discriminant modulo $p$ is either zero or has quadratic character $(a/p)$.

For monic polynomials of degree $d\ge2$ over $\mathbb F_p$, the two nonzero discriminant signs occur equally often. For a squarefree polynomial with $s$ irreducible factors the sign is $(-1)^{d-s}$, since Frobenius permutes the roots with $s$ cycles and acts on the Vandermonde determinant by its sign. The signed count is therefore $(-1)^d$ times the sum of the polynomial Möbius function over the monic degree-$d$ slice. It vanishes by the Euler product $\sum\mu(f)t^{\deg f}=1-pt$. The zero-discriminant probability is $1/p$. Conditioning on avoidance of the single root and allowing leading degree drop now give at most $1/2+O(1/p)$ for the test to accept the target sign or zero. Passing between a polynomial and its monic normalization changes the discriminant by a square.

Take $t=\lfloor cD^2\rfloor$ auxiliary primes between $n^A$ and $2n^A$, with $n$ the input bit length. Choose $A$ fixed and large enough to omit all primes dividing $2aNQ$, and then $c>0$ small enough that the product $J$ of the test primes satisfies $J\le\sigma^{1/2}$. This is possible because $\log\sigma=\Theta(DH)$ and $\log n\asymp\log\log N$. For all the $d$ Gaussian coefficients together the residue law modulo $J$ is within $O(dJ/\sigma)$ of uniform; the finite sampler adds at most $\exp(-n^{10})$. Under the uniform reference the distinct prime tests are independent. Multiplication of the local sign bounds therefore gives $\Pr(\Delta/a\in\mathbb Q^{\times2},\ \text{continuation})\le\exp(-\Omega(D^2))$. Using the long-prime-degree test instead bounds the probability of a continuing draw with no such witness by $\exp(-\Omega(D^2/\log D))$. Combining both with $a=1$ gives the full $S_d$ conclusion, since a square discriminant is exactly the condition that the Galois group be contained in $A_d$. These bounds are uniform in the admissible environment. [Native residue and finite-sampler estimates](keybreak-terminal-norm-floor.md) · [Exact local counts](../experiments/keybreak-native-splitting.py)

The same conclusion has a sharper exceptional probability using only pairs of primes. Put $P=\sigma^{1/2}/(4n)$ and use the primes in $[P,2P]$ excluding divisors of $2NQa$. There are $s\asymp P/\log P$ of them. This is an analytic test set and its enumeration is not charged to the algorithm. For distinct primes the joint modulus $m$ is at most $4P^2$. For one discrete Gaussian the Poisson summation formula gives, on residue $u\bmod m$, unnormalized mass
$$
 \frac{\sigma}{m}\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}
 e^{-\pi j^2\sigma^2/m^2}e^{2\pi i j u/m}.
$$
The sum differs from one by $O(\exp(-\pi\sigma^2/m^2))$, uniformly in $u$. The full-vector distance to uniform for one or two test primes, including the finite sampler, is thus $\eta=\exp(-\Omega(n^4))$. Use indicators for full-degree squarefree reductions with a long prime-degree factor, or for full-degree reductions with the sign opposite to $(a/p)$. Their expectations are at least $h\gg1/\log d$ for the first test and $h\gg1$ for the second. CRT and the pairwise approximation give absolute covariance $O(\eta)$ between distinct indicators. The sum has variance $O(s+s^2\eta)$ and expectation at least $sh$. Its probability of vanishing is $O((s^{-1}+\eta)/h^2)=\exp(-(1/2-o(1))\log\sigma)$. Both failure events of interest require zero witnesses. This proves an $\exp(-\Omega(DH))$ estimate for each. In particular repeated zero reductions of a discriminant equal to $a$ times a square cannot invalidate the bound: a witness requires a nonzero opposite sign. The small-prime proof above additionally gives polynomial-bit-length, polynomial-time verifiable certificates for the symmetric group from the factor degrees and an odd Frobenius permutation.

## Disjointness of several splitting fields

Let $T\le\exp(CH)$ be the number of draws. For each nonempty index set of size $j\le k$, expose the earlier draws first. If they all continued, take $a$ to be the product of their native discriminants, using $a=1$ for an empty product. Its bit length is polynomial in $n$ uniformly in the claimed range: the sampler caps coefficient lengths by a polynomial in $n$, the determinant formula does the same for discriminants, and $k$ is polynomial in $n$. The final draw uses fresh coefficient randomness, even if the environment was chosen from the public history. The previous sign bound applies to this particular product. There are at most $\sum_{j=1}^k\binom{T}{j}\le (1+T)^k\le\exp((Cc+o(1))DH)$ choices for $k\le cD$. Choose $c>0$ with $Cc<\liminf(\log\sigma)/(2DH)$. The pairwise-prime estimate gives simultaneous independence of the square classes within every such subfamily, except with probability $\exp(-\Omega(DH))$. The union bound for full symmetric groups applies once per field and costs $T\exp(-\Omega(DH))=o(1)$. A list of indices fixed before observing any draws costs only $2^k$ subsets, so that list can have length $c' DH$ for a sufficiently small fixed $c'>0$.

To finish, induct on the subfamily size. A product of symmetric groups of degrees at least five has elementary binary abelianization, one sign per factor. If the previous closures are linearly disjoint, their compositum's quadratic subfields are exactly those generated by the square roots of nonempty products of previous discriminants. The intersection of the next splitting field with that compositum is Galois over the rationals. Every nontrivial quotient of $S_d$, $d\ge5$, maps onto its sign quotient, because its only proper nontrivial normal subgroup is $A_d$. A nontrivial intersection would thus contain the new discriminant's square root, contradicting independence. The trivial intersection gives the direct product Galois group on adjoining the next Galois field.

This removes common intermediate fields, including ones in the normal closures, as a source of relation reuse among small groups of ordinary draws. Shared rational modular data, conditioning the polynomial coefficients across draws, and using a statistic of the entire exponentially large pool remain separate possibilities.
