# Pointwise rational marks for two quadratic characters

Suppose $p,q\ge13$ are distinct prime divisors of a positive integer $n$ of bit length $b$. Under Dirichlet GRH, for either $s\in\{-1,1\}$ there is a rational number $c>1$ of height $O(b^{10})$ such that
$$\chi_p(c)=\chi_p(c-1)=s,\qquad\chi_q(c(c-1))=-1.$$
Both $c,c-1$ reduce to nonzero elements at both primes. A list depending only on $n$ can be constructed in deterministic polynomial time and contains the witnesses for both choices of $s$ and every ordered pair of its prime divisors at least 13. Trial division handles the primes below 13. For balanced semiprimes with both prime factors growing, the required height improves to $O(b^4)$. Alpöge's November 20 result covers the Dirichlet GRH used below, including imprimitive characters. [Alpöge's theorem](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Independent kernel checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

## Small positive coefficients

Choose a prime $e\equiv1\pmod8$, coprime to $n$, with $\chi_p(e)=s$ and $\chi_q(e)=+1$. There is a nonempty coset of index 16 in $(\mathbb Z/8n\mathbb Z)^\times$ with these conditions. The residue class 1 modulo 8 has index four, and the two characters on different odd-prime components are independent and independently attainable. Choose a second prime $f\equiv1\pmod8$, coprime to $ne$, with $\chi_p(f)=s$, $\chi_q(f)=-1$, $\chi_e(f)=+1$. These define a nonempty coset of index 32 modulo $8ne$. Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan's Theorem 1.4 bounds the least prime in every prescribed fixed-index coset by $O_h((\log Q)^2)$ on Dirichlet GRH. Applying it successively gives $e,f\ll b^2$. The theorem supplies explicit constants, permitting a monotone majorant for $Q=8n$ and then $Q=8ne$. Finitely many moduli below its stated threshold can be included in the constants. [Lamzouri–Li–Soundararajan, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.3595#page=3)

Quadratic reciprocity gives $(e/f)=(f/e)=+1$ and $(-f/e)=+1$. The squarefree pairwise coprime coefficients of $w^2+fv^2=eu^2$ satisfy Legendre's criterion. Holzer's bound supplies a nonzero primitive integral solution $B=(u,v,w)$ with $|u|\le\sqrt f$, $|v|\le\sqrt e$, $|w|\le\sqrt{ef}$. All three coordinates are nonzero because $e,f$ are distinct primes. If $p,q$ exceed the absolute bounds for these coordinates, $c=e(u/w)^2$ already works and $c-1=f(v/w)^2$. Its reduced numerator and denominator are at most $ef=O(b^4)$. [Cochrane–Mitchell, Legendre's criterion and Holzer's bounds](https://www.math.ksu.edu/~cochrane/research/legendre.pdf)

## Avoiding vanishing coordinates at small prime divisors

Define $F(u,v,w)=eu^2-fv^2-w^2$ and let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ be its associated symmetric bilinear form, normalized by $\langle T,T\rangle=F(T)$. For $T\in\{0,\ldots,12\}^3$ put
$$B'=F(T)B-2\langle B,T\rangle T.$$
Expansion gives $F(B')=F(T)^2F(B)=0$. At each $\ell\in\{p,q\}$, the primitive vector $B$ is nonzero modulo $\ell$ and the three diagonal coefficients are units. Each coordinate $B'_i$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree two in $T$ over $\mathbb F_\ell$. When $B_i$ is nonzero, the coefficient of $T_j^2$ for $j\ne i$ is the nonzero $j$-th diagonal coefficient times $B_i$. When $B_i=0$, the coordinate is $-2\langle B,T\rangle T_i$, a product of nonzero polynomials.

The cube has 13 different values per coordinate modulo either prime. The elementary polynomial zero bound on a Cartesian grid bounds the fraction with $B'_i=0$ by $2/13$. Taking the union over three coordinates and the two primes leaves a positive fraction. For one choice of $T$ all three coordinates are therefore units modulo both $p,q$. Write $B'=(u',v',w')$. Each coordinate is $O(b^4)$ from $e,f=O(b^2)$, $|B_i|=O(b^2)$ and bounded $T$. Set
$$c=e(u'/w')^2=1+f(v'/w')^2.$$
It has the required characters and height $O(b^{10})$. The public algorithm can simply enumerate all reduced rationals $c>1$ with numerator and denominator at most a suitable constant times $b^{10}$. It needs no factor of $n$ or local character evaluations. The alternative of enumerating the small prime coefficient pairs, the Holzer box, and the fixed cube is also polynomial in $b$.

For the [Legendre-family search](legendre-average-factoring.md), the same conic parametrization $a=cu^2$, $a-1=(c-1)v^2$ applies to these rational $c$. The degree-four bound is unchanged and the resulting mark heights are polynomial in $b,H$. In the balanced dyad $p,q\in(X,2X]$, $H=X^{o(1)}$, so all selected conic marks of distinct rational values remain distinct modulo either prime for large $X$. The fixed pair of local target sets can be chosen independently of the other prime by taking the union of exceptional starting sets over the polynomially many public pairs of conics.

The finite check tested both signs for all ordered pairs of different primes from 13 through 89. All 684 cases supplied the prescribed rational characters. In 18 cases the initial primitive conic point had a nonunit coordinate and the finite cube supplied a replacement. The largest reduced height encountered was 2,405,344,977.
