# A class-group quotient controls the remaining square tests

Let \(K=\mathbb Q[X]/P\) have degree \(d\), given by a monic irreducible integral polynomial. Given nonzero elements written in its power basis and a finite list \(S\) of rational primes including 2, one can compute the full field-square kernel and a root for every basis product in time
\[
  2^{O(d+h_S)}\operatorname{poly}\!\left(\text{explicit input size}+\sum_{p\in S}\log(2p)\right),
 \qquad h_S=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}
 \frac{\mathrm{Cl}(K)}{2\mathrm{Cl}(K)+\langle[\mathfrak p]:\mathfrak p\mid p,\ p\in S\rangle}.
\]
The algorithm takes the fields, marks and primes as input. It terminates without knowing \(h_S\), a maximal integral basis, or a factorization of the marked norms or the polynomial discriminant. Adding primes can reduce the residual rank even when the unit rank or the full class-group 2-rank is large. ## What remains after the local tests

Start with products having even valuation at every finite prime ideal. For such a product \(z\), impose positivity in every real embedding and require that \(z\) be a square in every completion over every \(p\in S\). These tests are linear conditions on binary product masks, and every field square passes.

For the entire group of even-valuation squareclasses, the classes passing the tests are canonically dual to the displayed class-group quotient. To see this, attach the Kummer character of \(K(\sqrt z)/K\) to a class, with the trivial character for a square. At an odd place strip off an even power of a uniformizer. The square root of the remaining local unit gives a split or unramified extension by Hensel lifting. Positivity splits the real places, and the local tests split every dyadic place. Hence the extension is unramified everywhere. Global reciprocity gives a character of the ordinary ideal class group. At each tested prime ideal its Frobenius is trivial, so the character factors through the stated quotient. Conversely any quadratic character of this quotient gives an everywhere unramified extension splitting at the listed places and the real places. It has a Kummer representative. An odd valuation of that representative would force even relative ramification index, which excludes it at every finite place. The Kummer character of this representative recovers the original class-group character. On any supplied list of marks, the remaining classes form a subspace of this dual and have rank at most \(h_S\). ## Testing every branch over a rational prime at once

For a field \(F\) let \(g_F(p)\) be the number of prime ideals above \(p\). To test a masked product \(z\), apply the primitive quadratic-cover construction to \(K[Y]/(Y^2-z)\), scaling \(z\) by a nonzero rational square first to clear denominators. Its rational characteristic polynomial factors into two degree-\(d\) factors exactly for a field square. In that case every local test passes. Otherwise the cover is the field \(E=K(\sqrt z)\). Each prime ideal over \(p\) in \(K\) contributes exactly two primes of \(E\) if split and one otherwise. Consequently the simultaneous local-square test is
\[
                  g_E(p)=2g_K(p).
\]
This counts all residue degrees and applies at wild primes and at primes dividing the index of the displayed power order. At the real places the analogous equality is \(r_1(E)=2r_1(K)\). Alternatively, the signs of the original columns can be evaluated directly at the isolated real roots of \(P\).

The prime count uses only a \(p\)-maximal order \(B\). Compute it by repeatedly replacing an order by the multiplier ring of its \(p\)-radical until the order stabilizes, using rational lattices. On the finite algebra \(B/pB\), the dimension of the fixed space of \(x\mapsto x^p\) is \(g_F(p)\). Its reduced quotient is the product of the residue fields, each with one-dimensional fixed space. A fixed nilpotent vanishes. Conversely, when a residue class is fixed, raising any lift to \(p^j\ge[F:\mathbb Q]\) kills the nilpotent Frobenius error and gives a fixed lift. Thus the fixed-space count works directly on the nonreduced algebra. Matrix arithmetic and binary powering suffice, with bit cost polynomial in the field presentation and \(\log p\). No splitting of a polynomial over a large finite field is needed for this count. ## Cost and factoring scope

Restrict the current basis separately at each rational prime. The adaptive binary kernel routine makes at most \(m2^r\) membership tests on a block with \(m\) input basis vectors and image rank \(r\). For odd \(p\) each completion contributes at most one unit-squareclass bit, giving \(r\le g_K(p)\). A dyadic completion \(F/\mathbb Q_2\) of degree \(n\) has \(n+1\) unit-squareclass bits. Indeed its principal unit group is the product of finite cyclic 2-power torsion containing \(-1\) and a free \(\mathbb Z_2\)-module of rank \(n\), and the residue unit group has odd order. Thus the dyadic block has rank at most \(d+g_K(2)\le2d\). Real places have at most \(r_1(K)\) sign bits. The separate block costs add. The final adaptive call uses exact field-square decisions and recovers roots, with residual rank at most \(h_S\). Products need only be formed from the original binary masks, keeping each query polynomial in the explicit input size. [Adaptive search](cogentic-adaptive-square-kernel.md) · [Local multiplicative squareclasses](https://magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/magma/handbook/text/415)

If the tested prime classes span \(\mathrm{Cl}(K)/2\mathrm{Cl}(K)\), all surviving products are field squares. For example, the primes up to the Minkowski bound suffice, since every ideal class has an integral representative of at most that norm. On a one-third factoring schedule with \(W=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\), the decision fits in \(\exp(O(W))\) whenever the explicit data and prime list fit in that time and \(d+h_S=O(W)\). This condition permits fields with many unit or class squareclasses if the tested prime classes account for them. In the exact replay, the globally nonsquare class of 17 over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-51})\) survives the tests at 2 and 13, and the test at 3 eliminates it. All 1,120 comparisons with real embeddings and PARI local powers agreed. [Local restriction replay](../experiments/cogentic-local-square-restriction.py)

## An unconditional bound when the normal closure has 2-power degree

Suppose the normal closure of (K/\mathbb Q) has 2-power degree, (K) is unramified outside a finite set \(\Sigma\) of rational primes, and (K) has a unique prime above some \(p\in S\). Let \(\kappa_p(\Sigma)\) be the dimension of the kernel of restriction to \(\mathbb Q_p^\times/\mathbb Q_p^{\times2}\) on the rational squareclasses spanned by \(-1\) and the primes in \(\Sigma\). Then
\[
                         h_S\le [K:\mathbb Q]\,\kappa_p(\Sigma).
\]
The set \(\Sigma\) is used in the bound. The local kernel algorithm still takes only \(S\) as its list of rational primes.

Take the elementary abelian class field \(E/K\) attached to the displayed class-group quotient and let \(M\) be its normal closure over \(\mathbb Q\). Over the normal closure of \(K\), the field \(M\) is a compositum of elementary abelian extensions obtained by base change from the conjugates of \(E/K\). Thus \(G=\mathrm{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)\) is a 2-group. The normal closure is unramified outside \(\Sigma\), because \(E/K\) is unramified and composita of unramified local extensions are unramified. Write \(H=\mathrm{Gal}(M/K)\), and let \(D\le G\) be a decomposition group above \(p\). A quadratic character of \(G\) trivial on \(D\) corresponds to a rational quadratic field split at \(p\); its squarefree radicand is supported on \(\Sigma\) and a sign. The image of \(D\) in the Frattini quotient \(G/(G^2[G,G])\) therefore has codimension at most \(\kappa_p(\Sigma)\). By the Burnside basis theorem, one can generate \(G\) by generators of \(D\) and at most \(\kappa_p(\Sigma)\) extra elements. [Burnside basis theorem](https://web.mat.bham.ac.uk/D.A.Craven/docs/lectures/finitegroups2010.pdf)

The unique prime condition gives \(G=HD\), since primes above \(p\) correspond to double cosets \(D\backslash G/H\). Choose a representative \(u\in D\) for each right coset \(Hu\). For a generator \(s\) of \(G\) and such a representative \(u\), let \(v\) represent \(Hus\). The elements \(usv^{-1}\) generate \(H\) by following the cosets of the successive prefixes of a word in the generators and their inverses. When \(s\in D\), this element lies in \(H\cap D\), which acts trivially on \(E\) since the prime over \(p\) splits in \(E/K\). Each extra generator contributes at most \([K:\mathbb Q]\) generators of \(\mathrm{Gal}(E/K)\), proving the bound.

For \(p=2\) and \(2\in\Sigma\), the classes of \(-1\) and \(2\) are independent over \(\mathbb Q_2\), giving \(\kappa_2(\Sigma)\le|\Sigma|-1\). If \(\Sigma\) contains an odd prime \(q\equiv3\) or \(5\pmod8\), its class adds a third independent bit, since neither \(q\) nor \(-q\) is a square unit modulo eight. Then \(\kappa_2(\Sigma)\le|\Sigma|-2\).

### The binomial first field on the raw schedule

For \(d=2^k\), \(k\ge1\), and an odd integer \(a\) with \(1\le a<m\), take \(N=m^d+2a\) with \(m\) odd. Whenever the schedule chooses degree \(d\), the [raw radix construction](cogentic-monic-pair.md) uses this base \(m\) and gives \(F=X^d+2a\) as its first polynomial. This field has a unique prime over 2 by Eisenstein, and its normal closure is the splitting field obtained by adjoining the \(d\)th roots of unity and a \(d\)th root of \(-2a\), of 2-power degree. The polynomial discriminant is supported on \(2a\). Thus the explicit one-prime set \(S=\{2\}\) gives
\[
                            h_{\{2\}}\le d\,\omega(a),
\]
where \(\omega(a)\) counts distinct prime factors. The residual rank is zero for \(a=1\) and for prime \(a\equiv3,5\pmod8\). For example, the field of \(X^4+10\) has class group cyclic of order four, and the class of its dyadic prime generates that group. PARI verifies these two claims with `bnfcertify` and `bnfsunit`.

For arbitrarily large powers of two \(d\), there are whole intervals of bases for which these inputs have scheduled degree \(d\). Indeed, let \(j\) be the least positive integer with \(2^j>d^3j\), and set \(M=2^{d^2j}\). For odd \(M<m<2M\), one has \(M^d<N<(2M)^d\), since \(m^d+2a<(m+1)^d\). The binary bit length \(\ell\) of \(N\) satisfies \(d^3j<\ell\le d^3j+d\). Minimality gives \(2^{j-1}\le d^3(j-1)\) and, because \(d\) is a power of two, \(2^j-d^3j\ge d^3\). Thus \(j\) is the bit length of \(\ell\), and \(d^3\le\ell/j<(d+1)^3\) gives the required schedule.

For a fixed such base, all but a vanishing fraction of odd choices of \(a<m\) satisfy \(\omega(a)\le3\log\log m\). Indeed, \(\sum_{a\le m}2^{\omega(a)}\le \sum_{b\le m}\lfloor m/b\rfloor\le m(1+\log m)\) by counting squarefree divisors. On the schedule \(d\log\log m=O(W)\), so these choices give \(d+h_{\{2\}}=O(W)\). A positive fraction of odd choices in \([m/2,m)\) are squarefree, since the upper bound for the excluded fraction is \(\sum_{n\ge3}n^{-2}+o(1)<1\) by counting multiples of every odd square up to \(m\). Thus there are choices with both the stated rank bound and squarefree \(a\) in that interval. At each odd prime dividing such an \(a\), Eisenstein gives tame total ramification and discriminant exponent \(d-1\), hence \(\log|\Delta_K|=\Theta(d\log m)=\Theta(\log N)\). This makes the dyadic bound smaller than the available bound from the full class number.

The density statement concerns the binomial choices of \(a\) for each base. For example, if \(3\nmid m\), restricting to odd \(a\equiv1\pmod3\) still leaves a vanishing exceptional fraction and makes \(N\) composite by divisibility by 3. The consecutive-degree field \(G=F+(X-m)X^d\) and general radix inputs still need a residual rank bound. A supply of relations that split every composite input also remains open.
