# Single-input half-width schedule with a narrower public Gaussian

For a general integer \(N\), the half-width quadratic-class construction, with the modifications below, gives the conditional manuscript bound
\[
 L_N[1/3,\,2.009433036410336\ldots]
\]
in expected bit time. The hypotheses are Dirichlet GRH and the analytic inputs of the pinned November 26 KeyBreak preprint used by the previous half-width construction. A fixed rational schedule at coefficient width \(V_{\max}^{1.51}\), up to polynomial factors in the bit length, has ceiling **2.0100**. The limiting coefficient uses widths strictly above \(V_{\max}^{3/2}\). The cost includes the public fields and the private samples on the current input. The bound is asymptotic and the full factoring theorem is a manuscript argument. ## Which probability estimates change

Take a fixed \(\chi\in(3/2,2)\), set \(\epsilon=2-\chi\), and use \(\sigma=2^{\lceil\chi\log_2 V_{\max}\rceil} n^{10}\) for the public coefficient Gaussian, where \(n\) is the bit length. Recompute the deterministic reference heights and point densities with this \(\sigma\) in (G.25). Every top box has a fixed positive log-size fraction of the largest box, so \(\sigma\ge V^{2-\epsilon-o(1)}\) and \(\sigma=V^{O(1)}\) at its local \(V\). The public top moments require control of smoothness at two evaluations of the *same* field. For the narrower law the pointwise two-value Gaussian approximation (G.19) can fail. The restricted smooth tests admit a different argument.

1. For an ordinary parent the input field has no prescribed algebraic root. For the aggregate incidence of a fixed incoming child, the two marks determine the same root event in their evaluation image. Restrict the Poisson fiber quotient to that image, before analyzing either its numerator or denominator; division by the child preserves both separate step frequencies and endpoint-to-period ratios. The same kernel-basis bound and short-mode cutoffs hold in both cases. The smallest allowed angle sine is \(V^{-\zeta}\) up to constants for any sufficiently small fixed \(\zeta>0\).
2. On the Cartesian support progressions, minor-arc savings sum over the modes with at least one fixed-power height. For the others, let \(T\) be the minimizing rational height and \(q\) the denominator of its approximant. The arithmetic count of modes with \(qT\le U\) in either coordinate is at most \(V^{\epsilon^2+o(1)} U^{\epsilon+o(1)}\). The smooth prefix sum in that coordinate gains \((qT)^{-1/2}\), with an arbitrarily small fixed-power GRH error on the stated major arcs. The product over the two coordinates and absolute summation give \(V^{-(1-2\epsilon)+o(1)}+O(X^{-c})\) before the Gaussian variation. The latter costs \(V^{2\zeta+o(1)}\). Choose \(0<2\zeta<1-2\epsilon\). The resulting absolute error for each retained pair is a fixed negative power of \(V\).
3. For a rational-good parent or incoming region the diagonal, repeated-denominator and small-determinant pairs have fraction \(V^{-c'+o(1)}\). This follows by counting partners per primitive mark in the *same* congruence lattice. All reference densities are \(\ge e^{-O(H)}=V^{-o(1)}\), where \(H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}\). Therefore the additional normalized moment error tends to zero, including for the bounded-ratio incoming weights. The ideal part of G.9 supplies the remaining \(D^{O(1)}\) relative upper bound. The one-point kernel basis has lengths \(O(V)\) and satisfies its original smoothing estimate. Internally, keep the original pointwise pair comparison, with \(\chi v_0>2v_1\) in log-size units. For root flatness the modified initialization checks \(\sigma\ge n^6Y_1\) in place of the printed three-prime check in (H.1). The interior and incoming arguments in G.6–G.12 condition on a single root, and parent divisibilities occur inside the tests. Single-root flatness follows by reducing one independent coefficient modulo that prime. The total variation cost of conditioning the finite-sampler error is \(O(Y_1 e^{-n^{10}})\). The unconditional initialization rejection probability on the continuing no-factor path is \(O(1/\sigma)+O(e^{-n^{10}})\), negligible against a polynomial per-field hit probability. An arbitrary tuple of three unrelated roots could require the larger original width; that separate probability estimate has no place in the modified argument. ## Passing from smooth moments to completed rows

The interior deletion recurrence G.11 continues to give a child-bad mass smaller than any fixed negative power of \(D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}\). For the elliptic recognition charge, also delete a candidate whenever its tested quotient contains an exceptional numerical child prime above \(B\), including at even valuation or at a prime terminal in the field. The formal incidence counts cover these cases, including raw-content incidences. The one-point bounds (G.51) and (G.55), root flatness, and the rational second moment (G.14) charge this extra deletion by its within-dyad exceptional fraction times \(D^{O(1)}\). That fraction is \(\exp(-\Omega(\sqrt{\log B\log\log B}))\), so it survives the interior recurrence. For the remaining recursive child-bad contribution at the top, use Cauchy–Schwarz under the incoming child-root law. The capped elliptic test is applied to the resulting good candidates, with bounded small-factor handling and exact factor-list checks. [KeyBreak, (G.46)–(G.55)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119) · [Public elliptic deletion and recognition](keybreak-public-elliptic-recognition.md)

For each parent outside the deterministic exceptional set in its dyad, apply Paley–Zygmund using the one-point mean and ordinary second moment. A Markov bound removes the large bad-incidence events at smaller probability cost. This gives a per-field hit probability at least \(D^{-C_0}\) and a polynomial lower bound on the survivor fraction on a hit. A polynomial pool of independent full public environments covers the retained primes simultaneously in every prescribed private dyad. The miss probability per prime is at most \(\exp(-D^c)\) with a sufficiently large fixed \(c\), while \(\log Y_0=O(DH)\). All failed searches and amplified quotas are charged. Each top prime and its entire subtree stay in one field, so good large-ideal valuations cancel in that field. The rational columns are shared, including one for each public \(Q_j\). Field \(j\) has its own column for the parity of the signed occurrence sum \(E_j\), as well as separate good small ideal, signed norm, and character columns. Even occurrence makes the leading-coefficient factor \(A_j^{-E_j}\) in the norm identity a square, so stored factorizations of the primitive form values determine the true norm squareclass. The ideal and character conditions give a rational squareclass in each field. The separate signed norm-square conditions then give a square because the degrees are odd. Compute the exact roots in the integral coordinates \(\beta_j=A_j\alpha_j\), clearing negative occurrences and using the derivative factor of (E.16)–(E.19). Evaluate them through their verified unit localizations, multiply by the square root of the global rational correction, and verify the modular square. Separate field certificates, cumulative polynomial-bit probes and accelerated Hensel reconstruction retain their cost bounds after multiplication by the polynomial pool size. Restricting to split primes retains this bound up to a constant since the splitting density tends to one half. Multiplying over the \(\Theta(D)\) ordered slots gives a bias negligible relative to the inverse genus index. This yields the staged lower bound, and the prefix upper counts allow unrestricted prime sets. The public search receives only the numerical leading coefficient of each private square class. Field choices, recognition, row retention and exact extraction depend on those public values and public coins, so they preserve the ideal private conditional signs, with the existing full-transcript total variation transfer for the actual sampler. The first-split and restart argument then gives complete factorization. ## Integer scales and cost

Fix a rational schedule before letting \(N\) grow. Use \(d,t,e_B,e_0,V(e),c(e,\lambda)\) from the tiered integer construction, with \(\gamma=S-\alpha\) and \(T_H=\lfloor(A_0+\eta)dt\rfloor\), where \(A_0=\delta^{-3}(1+\chi v_0)\). The order-\(dt\) padding absorbs the \(O(d+\log n)\) factors in (G.57), as well as the \(o(dt)\) error in the leading log size. The largest raw norm bound follows from \(2v_0<1\). At \(E_j=\lfloor a_j e_0\rfloor\) use the local ratio \(1+(\lambda_T-1)a_j\), and route its children through the common first internal cutoff \(c(e_0,\lambda_T)\). The unrounded local child expression increases in \(a_j\) eventually: the positive coefficient of \(a_j\) is of order \(dt\), whereas the intercept is of order \(t\). Floors introduce at most a lower-order log error, and for every proper knot the limiting child is strictly below the first internal cutoff. The last knot uses that cutoff exactly. Each fixed knot's parent-to-box, parent-to-child, and box-to-child log gaps and the excess of twice the box log over the parent log are fixed positive multiples of \(\log M\), where \(M=\lfloor N^{1/d}\rfloor\). This gives the corresponding scale separations and negative-power rational averaging errors. The reference log heights after the allowed divisions are comparable to \(d(e+t)\), with cutoff logs comparable to \(e+t\), uniformly in the two internal ranges and over the fixed top knots. Thus (G.26) holds at the recomputed scales. The upper smoothness argument follows from the defining ceiling for \(c(e,\lambda)\) and (G.57)'s reference-height upper bound.

For an internal exponent \(e\ge e_B\) write \(g=\alpha e+\gamma e_B\). In the low range the unclamped map has slope \(\alpha/\lambda_L<1\). Its value at \(e_B\) is below \(e_B\) by \((\eta/\lambda_L)t+O(1)\), so each unclamped step decreases by at least a fixed multiple of \(t\). The final clamped step decreases whenever \(e>e_B\), even if the decrease is only one bit. For an unclamped low step the gap \(g-c(e,\lambda_L)\) is bounded below by \([(\lambda_L-1)S\beta-(A_0+\eta)]t/\lambda_L-O(1)\), and at a clamped step the gap is at least \((S-1)e_B\). The high range \(e>t\sqrt d\) has a uniform contraction and a box-to-child gap of order \(e\), since \(t/e\le1/\sqrt d\). It changes to the low range at most once, and the low contraction toward a fixed point below the clamp then gives \(O(\log D)\) total levels. The same geometric and mean bounds apply to the box in which a prime is actively processed, independent of the tier that produced it.

For the internal mean, an upper rational smoothness loss in units of \(t\) is \((\delta^{-3}+g/(dt))/(3\beta)+o(1)\), uniformly, using the full internal rational value \(|G(P)|\ll M V(e)\) and the cutoff \(B\). The algebraic loss is at most \(\lambda_L/3+o(1)\) throughout, since \(\lambda_H<\lambda_L\). The resulting lower log-mean bound contains the bottom coefficient \(2\eta+o(1)\) and the extra term \([2\alpha-1-O(1/d)](e-e_B)/t\), which is nonnegative eventually for each fixed schedule. The \(O(1/d)\) slope is kept in this comparison because \(e/t\) can grow. For costs, separately, in the low range \(g/(dt)=O(d^{-1/2})\); in the high range the largest box determines the rational and algebraic upper charges. The larger of the valid bounds on the two sides controls the full search, including the crossing. The separate-period GRH error has a strict bottom margin, and its additional term proportional to \(d(e-e_B)\) improves the margin higher up. The inverse-density and mesh factors have smaller logarithms in this comparison. For the ordinary unpaired private shape, allocate \(L_j=f_jL_x+O(1)\) bits to the finitely many groups with total \(L_x=\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor-2\). Here \(L_x=k_0e_0+O(k_0)\) and \(E_j=\lfloor a_j e_0\rfloor\). In the local (H.18) recipe take \(k'_j=\lceil L_j/E_j\rceil=(f_j/a_j)k_0+O(1)\), let \(m_j\) be its bit length, and set \(k_j=k'_j+\lceil k'_j/m_j\rceil\). Thus \(k_j-(f_j/a_j)k_0=O(k_0/\log k_0)\), and the summed occurrence cap has linear slack from \(\sum_j f_j/a_j=W/M_{\rm priv}<2\). For \(S_j=k_j E_j-L_j\) and \(J_j=\lfloor 2(S_j+k_j)/k_j\rfloor\), one has \(\lfloor k_j J_j/2\rfloor\in[S_j,S_j+k_j]\). Also \(S_j\ge k'_j E_j/m_j\) and \(k_j/k'_j\to1\), so \(2m_j(J_j+1)\ge E_j\) eventually. The band width \(J_j+1=O(E_j/\log k_0+E_j/k_0+1)=o(E_j)\) ensures disjoint complete bands inside the strict parent separations, including the lowest fixed positive knot. The product threshold loses at most \(2k_j\) bits per group. The extra prime slots change the leading log shape loss by \(O(k_0)\), and the bit-length factors contribute \(O(k_0\log\log k_0)=o(H)\). These terms are covered by the fixed cost padding at sufficiently large \(N\). The conclusion is an eventual integer schedule, without a numerical first valid input size. For \(\chi=1.51\) the strict rational caps on staged screening and low raw search are \(2.009928951\) and \(2.009929108\), respectively. All other charges are smaller, giving the fixed \(2.0100\) ceiling. In the limiting model \(b+p(u)\le C\) and \(6b(C-b)\ge 3\chi u^2/4+4\sqrt C\), with \(p(u)=2/(9u^2)+2u/3\). At \(\chi=3/2\) these have the common stationary coefficient in \((2.00943303641033,2.00943303641034)\). For each positive excess take a fixed \(\chi>3/2\), a sufficiently fine fixed grid and positive rational paddings. The nonactive guards have strict room at the endpoint; the positive perturbations open the geometric and low-mean equalities. To obtain one algorithm, enumerate syntactically valid encodings of fixed rational schedules, with no test of their eventual analytic validity. Give encoding \(j\) a fresh bounded simulation of \(2^{r-j}\) steps in phase \(r\), charging setup and verification, and include a deterministic factoring backup with a doubling budget. A fixed valid schedule of expected work \(E_j(N)\) has failure probability at most \(1/4\) in each independent phase after its budget reaches \(4E_j(N)\). Since phase costs double up to polynomial simulation overhead, the geometrically weighted tail converges. The backup covers every input outside a fixed schedule's eventual range without requiring its threshold to be known; only verified splits or a certified complete factorization are accepted. For each fixed index its startup multiplier is constant in \(N\), so taking the leading upper limit for that index and then the infimum gives the limiting coefficient without using \(\epsilon=1/2\) in a Fourier estimate.
