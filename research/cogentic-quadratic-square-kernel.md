# Exact square relations in a quadratic field

For any nonsquare $D\in\mathbb Q^\times$ and explicit list $z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)^\times$, two binary eliminations compute a basis of every mask whose product is a field square. The algorithm also returns an exact root for each basis mask. The construction uses the quadratic norm and the standard norm-one descent identity, together with gcd-free rational squareclasses. Its operations are deterministic and polynomial in the explicit input size. [Implementation and exhaustive replay](../experiments/cogentic-quadratic-square-kernel.py)

## Rational squareclasses by gcd

Represent a nonzero rational $x=a/b$ with $b>0$ by the signed integer $ab$, which differs from $x$ by the square $b^2$. Start with the distinct absolute values above one. Whenever two distinct current integers $u,v$ have $g=\gcd(u,v)>1$, replace them by $g,u/g,v/g$, dropping ones and duplicates. Every original absolute value remains a product of powers of current integers. The sum of logarithms of the distinct integers decreases by at least $\log g\ge\log2$ at each step. The final integers are pairwise coprime and can be used to recover all exponents by exact division.

The rational squareclass column consists of a sign bit and the exponent modulo two at each final integer that fails the exact integer square test. A final integer can be composite or contain a square divisor. If it is nonsquare, at least one of its prime valuations is odd, and no other final integer shares that prime. Thus these nonsquare classes are independent along with the sign class. All rational square relations are exactly the binary nullspace of these columns, with no prime factors required.

## Two eliminations and a root formula

Use the rational procedure on the signed norms $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(z_j)$ to compute a basis $v_1,\ldots,v_r$ of masks having square norm. Every field square has square rational norm. For each basis mask, form its product $\beta_i\in K^\times$. If $N(\beta_i)=t_i^2$ for $t_i\in\mathbb Q$, the quadratic identity gives

$$ (\beta_i+t_i)^2=(\operatorname{Tr}(\beta_i)+2t_i)\beta_i. $$

Choose between $t_i$ and $-t_i$ so that $s_i=\operatorname{Tr}(\beta_i)+2t_i$ is nonzero. This is possible because the square norm is nonzero. The class of $\beta_i$ in $K^\times/K^{\times2}$ is the image of the rational class of $s_i$. For a rational element, taking the sign with nonzero $s_i$ also avoids a zero numerator.

For a nonzero rational $s$, the equation $(a+b\sqrt D)^2=s$ forces $2ab=0$. Hence $s$ is a square in $K$ exactly when $s$ or $s/D$ is a square in $\mathbb Q$. The kernel of the rational-to-field squareclass map is the line generated by the class of $D$.

Apply rational squareclass elimination to $s_1,\ldots,s_r,D$. Project the resulting binary kernel onto the first $r$ coordinates, then replace each unit vector by its original mask $v_i$. Projection is injective because $D$ is nonsquare in $\mathbb Q$. It is surjective onto the field square kernel because the squareclass of the product of two masks agrees with the class of their XOR product. Repeated factors differ by squares. Every original square mask therefore occurs, and the output masks are independent.

For each returned mask, recompute its product $\beta$, choose $t$ as above, and let $s=\operatorname{Tr}(\beta)+2t$. Find $w\in K$ with $w^2=s$ by exact rational square tests on $s$ and $s/D$. Then $(\beta+t)/w$ is an exact root. The implementation checks its square. Negative rationals and nonfundamental radicands require the same operations.

## Cost and replay

Each subset product uses at most $n$ multiplications in the explicit basis $1,\sqrt D$. On clearing each input coordinate's denominator, coefficient bit lengths grow at most linearly in the sum of operand bit lengths plus $O(n(1+\log H_D))$, where $H_D$ bounds the numerator and denominator of $D$. For example one may bound each step by multiplying the two-dimensional coordinate vector by an integer matrix after clearing the denominators of the next factor and $D$. The gcd refinement has at most the sum of the input bit lengths many splitting steps, and its pair searches and exact divisions are polynomial. There are at most $n$ norm basis products and $n$ final basis products. Gaussian elimination and the explicit roots therefore remain within polynomial bit work and output size. For an explicit list of $\exp(O(W))$ elements with $\exp(O(W))$-bit coordinates, the resulting bound is $\exp(O(W))$. An intersection of such square kernels on common mask coordinates can also be computed by binary elimination.

Run `python3 -B experiments/cogentic-quadratic-square-kernel.py`. The replay exhausts 14,336 subset products with PARI over $D=2,18,-1,-5,-20,3/2,-3/2$. Each list includes signed rational, pure radical, and mixed elements. It also checks a gcd-free computation with a shared 234-bit composite, recognizes $-1$ as a square in $\mathbb Q(i)$, and rejects $2+\sqrt{-5}$ despite its square norm. The squareclass kernel takes nonzero field entries, as required for multiplicative squareclasses. The implementation does not use ideal valuations, a factor base, or integer factorization.
