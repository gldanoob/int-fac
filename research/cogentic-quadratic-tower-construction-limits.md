# Limits on constructing quadratic tower fields for arbitrary inputs

The descent introduced in `664976e` recovers square kernels from a supplied tower under its stated support hypotheses. Constructing each such field with a common root modulo an arbitrary input faces a density obstruction for quadratic compositions and a necessary modular-root condition for general small-height towers. [Quadratic tower descent](cogentic-quadratic-tower-square-kernel.md)

## Scarcity of quadratic compositions

Let \(X\le N<2X\), \(L=\log X\), \(W=L^{1/3}(\log L)^{2/3}\), and \(V=L^{2/3}(\log L)^{1/3}\). Suppose \(f\in\mathbb Z[T]\) is monic irreducible of degree at least two and coefficient height \(H\le\exp(CV)\) for a fixed \(C\). The model is a rational polynomial composition \(f=g\circ h\) with \(\deg g=2\) and \(\deg h\ge1\), and an integer \(m\) with \(0<|f(m)|\le X^\mu\) and \(N\mid f(m)\), for fixed \(\mu\ge1\). The polynomials and argument can depend on \(N\). At most \(X^{\mu/2+o(1)}\) integers in the dyad have such a representation. In particular the count is \(X^{1/2+o(1)}\) when \(f(m)=N\) or \(0<|f(m)/N|\le X^{o(1)}\) uniformly.

Translate and rescale the inner polynomial so that \(h\) is monic and \(h(0)=0\). The adjusted outer polynomial is monic since \(f\) is monic. For each root \(\eta\) of the adjusted \(g\), the monic polynomial \(h-\eta\) divides \(f\) over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\). Its roots are algebraic integers, so its coefficients are algebraic integers. The nonconstant coefficients of \(h\) are rational and hence integral. Its constant is zero, and the constant of \(h-\eta\) gives that \(\eta\) is integral as well. It follows that \(g=T^2+aT+b\) has integer coefficients. Now \(b=f(0)\). If \(r\) is the least positive index where \(h_r\ne0\), then \(f_r=a h_r\). This gives \(|a|,|b|\le H\) directly from the height of the expanded polynomial. No height bound on the original compositional factors is needed.

The integer \(t=h(m)\) satisfies \((2t+a)^2=4f(m)+a^2-4b\), so \(|t|\le X^{\mu/2+o(1)}\). There are \(X^{o(1)}\) choices of \(a,b\) and at most \(X^{o(1)}\) positive divisors of each nonzero \(t^2+at+b\) of size at most \(X^\mu\), by the divisor bound. Counting these possible divisors proves the assertion, independently of the number of choices of \(h\), its degree, or \(m\). The same proof works when the bound on \(|f(m)|\) is \(X^{1+o(1)}\). For instance, if \(d=\deg f\) grows on the one-third schedule and \(m=\lceil N^{1/d}\rceil\), then \(|f(m)|\le(d+1)Hm^d=X^{1+o(1)}\). The counting argument leaves noncompositional relative quadratics and larger evaluated multiples open.

## A bottom quadratic discriminant and its modular image

Let \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\), with \(\alpha\) a root of any monic irreducible \(f\in\mathbb Z[T]\) of degree \(d\) and height \(H\). Suppose \(K\) contains a quadratic subfield \(E\) with fundamental discriminant \(\Delta\), as it must for a nontrivial quadratic tower. Relative discriminants give \(|\Delta|^{d/2}\le |\operatorname{Disc} K|\le|\operatorname{Disc} f|\). Applying Hadamard's inequality to the Vandermonde matrix of the roots gives \(|\operatorname{Disc} f|\le d^d M(f)^{2d-2}\), where \(M(f)\) is the Mahler measure. The usual Jensen and Parseval bound gives \(M(f)\le\|f\|_2\le\sqrt{d+1}H\). In particular
\[
 |\Delta|\le d^2(d+1)^2 H^4.
\]
This bound holds for all primitive monic models of \(K\). For example, putting \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt N)\) at the bottom for squarefree \(N\) requires \(H\ge N^{1/4}/\sqrt{d(d+1)}\), ruling out the one-third height for growing scheduled degree. The radical field defined by \(T^{2^r}-N\), when irreducible, contains that bottom field regardless of the choice of primitive generator.

Suppose the tower presentation supplies \(\Delta\) and a power-basis expression for \(\beta=\sqrt\Delta\in K\), with coordinate bit lengths polynomial in the field description. If \(f(m)\equiv0\pmod N\) and \(f'(m)\) is a unit modulo \(N\), the pair supplies a certified modular square root of \(\Delta\). Since \(\beta\) is integral, \(\beta f'(\alpha)\in\mathbb Z[\alpha]\). This follows from \(\mathcal O_K\subseteq\mathbb Z[\alpha]^\vee=f'(\alpha)^{-1}\mathbb Z[\alpha]\), where the superscript denotes the trace dual. Thus rational polynomial multiplication and monic reduction give an integral representative \(U\) of \(\beta f'(\alpha)\). Evaluate \(U(m)/f'(m)\bmod N\) and verify that its square is \(\Delta\bmod N\). These operations use bit time polynomial in \(d,\log H,\log N,\log(2+|m|)\) and the supplied representation lengths. They require no integral-basis computation. The discriminant inequality alone does not find the subfield or its embedding in a bare input polynomial. [Derivative clearing for power orders](cogentic-monic-index.md)

There is an existence variant without the derivative test. If \(N\) is squarefree and \(|f(m)|=N\), the principal ideal \((m-\alpha)\) has absolute norm \(N\). The quotient \(\mathcal O_K/(m-\alpha)\) is then canonically \(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\). Each of its additive prime components has cardinality \(p\), and the identity generates each component additively. The bottom element therefore has an image modulo \(N\). A supplied integral basis, multiplication matrix for \(m-\alpha\), and coordinates for \(\beta\) also make this image computable by integer linear algebra. A singular root of a bare power-order polynomial supplies no such basis or map.

## Small square classes on rough inputs

For an odd \(N\) whose prime divisors all exceed \(\exp(cW)\) with fixed \(c>0\), existence of a small nontrivial modular square class follows independently of field construction. If \(s\) is the number of distinct prime divisors of \(N\), then \(s=O(L/W)\). Take \(s+1\) distinct primes of size polynomial in \(L\), all coprime to \(N\). Their vectors of Legendre signs at the distinct primes dividing \(N\) are linearly dependent over \(\mathbb F_2\). A nonempty dependency gives a squarefree product \(D>1\) with \(\log D=O((L/W)\log L)=O(V)\). It is a square unit at every prime divisor of \(N\), hence a square unit modulo each prime power and modulo \(N\) by Hensel lifting and CRT. The fundamental discriminant of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) is \(D\) or \(4D\) and has the same modular square property.

The proof uses the individual Legendre signs at the unknown prime divisors and supplies neither the dependency nor its square root from the unfactored input. It also gives no tower extensions above the quadratic field or productive square relation. The scarcity result for compositions and this necessary modular-root condition leave growing towers with general lower-field coefficients unresolved.
