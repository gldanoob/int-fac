# Public identity rows and an extra descent level

A complete ordinary relation gives a public row with known modular square root one. Such rows can remove many parity coordinates before collecting private rows. For triangular pivots produced by the existing special-prime lattice, building them ahead of time pays for a larger population than an added descent level would visit. The quotient operation itself gives a useful adaptive certificate. This comparison uses the existing one-field relation model. [KeyBreak (H.21), (E.27) and Theorem E.5](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=136)

## Public square rows and private dependencies

At a primitive mark $P=(a,b')$, let $\xi_P=a-b'\theta$ and $G(P)=Qa-rb'$, so the evaluation map satisfies $\psi(\xi_P)=G(P)/Q\pmod N$. The exact identity

$$1=\frac{Q}{G(P)}\,\psi(\xi_P)\pmod N$$

gives a public row as soon as both the signed rational factor list for $G(P)$ and the algebraic factor list for $F(P)$ are verified. The source already uses this identity for each internal atom. A public row need not have zero rational exponent parity. Its complete product evaluates to one, so its public square root is one even if both sides have nonzero parities. All reference leading coefficients, derivatives and denominators use the ordinary unit checks. [KeyBreak, Appendix E.6](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=95)

Choose $B'=e^{(b'+o(1))H}<B=e^{(b+o(1))H}$, with $H=(\log N)^{1/3}(\log\log N)^{2/3}$. Split the parity coordinates into the small coordinates up to $B'$ and the large coordinates above it. Keep polynomially many other coordinates, for the occurrence, signs, characters and the exceptional primes of the order. For a good large prime the norm parity is the sum of the good ideal parities at its degree-one labels, by the norm identity in Lemma E.1. It can be omitted from the elimination and checked through that identity. This applies to the primitive marks and the same one field. [KeyBreak, Lemma E.1](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=87)

For a good ideal $(p,s)$ with $B'<p\le B$, an algebraic pivot is a public identity row for a mark with $p\mid F(P)$ once at the prescribed root and both $F(P)/p$ up to sign and $|G(P)|$ smooth at $B'$. Its large part is exactly the desired coordinate. The corresponding rational pivot has $p\mid G(P)$ once, with its cofactor and $|F(P)|$ smooth at $B'$. For that construction the rational parent condition replaces the algebraic root condition. The two cutoffs and all factor lists are checked exactly.

If a completed private row has large support contained in the available pivot labels, multiply its representation by one public identity for each odd large coordinate. This clears those parities with the same private square residue. A dependence among the projected private rows must contain at least one private index. The public square roots of the auxiliary rows are already known, and the chosen identities and dependency use only the public transcript. Thus under the source's exact reconstruction on the resulting square classes, the private roots still split with the probability of Theorem E.5. The number of atoms needed for the auxiliary rows is at most the number of odd coordinates cleared. In a completed bounded-depth tree this adds only its ordinary multiplicity factor. [KeyBreak, Theorem E.5 and Appendix H.8](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=93)

This gives an adaptive stopping rule. Project a verified private row when all the required pivots are available, keep its independent private index, and stop at the first binary dependence among projected private rows. If every accepted row clears its large support, $e^{(b'+o(1))H}$ private rows suffice by the size of the remaining coordinate list. Missing pivots can instead cause a row to be rejected. A cap plus the original factoring schedule as fallback gives a correct finite procedure on every tape. Improving the worst-case expected coefficient requires a lower bound for the coverage of large coordinates actually visited, a uniform accepted-row rate, and a charged budget for constructing the pivots. Merely measuring a high public rank in an individual epoch supplies a certificate for that epoch.

## Work under the same triangular proposal law

Suppose all rational cofactors of the private tree already use $B'$, so no rational pivots are needed. The potentially new algebraic pivots are precisely the former terminal algebraic labels that an extra active level would eliminate. Indeed, an added child search for $(p,s)$ with next cutoff $B'$ asks for exactly the algebraic pivot mark described above. Inserting that mark now or recording its public identity and applying it later gives the same parity reduction. This equivalence makes no new claim about the distribution of good parents with the smaller cutoff. If that last-box supply is proved, its bad-label estimate can be used for the added child requests. There is no need to cover every possible large label in advance. [KeyBreak, (G.5), (G.44), (H.15)](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=119)

Let $s'$ be the sum of the rational and algebraic inverse-density coefficients at the pivot box, and let $e^{(v+o(1))H}$ be its coordinate size. If a positive fraction of the large labels is built in advance using separate uniform requests, the original bounded-work ledger pays $b+s'$. The on-demand version pays $b'+s'$ under the same per-label capped success estimate. There are $e^{(b'+o(1))H}$ retained requests times the subleading multiplicative tree and launch factors. A complete sweep of the pivot box starts at coefficient $2v$. At a prime of size $B$ the source's mean condition already requires $2v-b\ge s'$, so scanning the entire box cannot beat the same leading prebuild ledger. A positive density of available pivots can help if uncovered labels are excluded from private rows. That filtering changes which inputs reach the new level and needs a weighted incidence proof, as with an ordinary newly added level. This is a comparison of these cost formulas, not an algorithmic lower bound on generating public relations. [KeyBreak, Proposition G.13](https://crypto.sjtu.edu.cn/factoring.pdf#page=124)

For a numerical comparison, keep the public field height and former terminal cutoff of the [2.157 schedule](keybreak-private-elliptic-schedule.md#a-2157-retune-with-public-elliptic-recognition). Write $d\sim\delta D$, $D=(\log N/\log\log N)^{1/3}$, and $v_0=\alpha/(2\kappa)$ for the leading top box ratio $\log V_0/\log M$. In the terminal pivot box the combined norm-height coefficient on the $DH$ scale is $K+\delta v$, where $K=(2+2v_0)/\delta$. The rational value contributes $1/\delta$, the algebraic coefficient width contributes $(1+2v_0)/\delta$, and the algebraic mark contributes $\delta v$. Dividing out the incoming prime changes the height by $O(H)$. The KeyBreak Dickman ledger therefore uses $s'\ge(K+\delta v)/(3b')$. Eliminating $v$ via the mean condition yields

$$s'\ge \frac{K+\delta b/2}{3b'-\delta/2}.$$

At $\delta=1.3609$, $\kappa=.8127$, $\alpha=.5001$ and $b=.898534225$, the resulting on-demand bound $b'+s'$ is at least $2.155606168\ldots$ at $b'=b$. Its derivative with respect to $b'$ at $b$ is $-.871429334\ldots$ and decreases as $b'$ is lowered in the feasible range. Holding these public scales fixed, lowering $b'$ by $.005$ already gives an on-demand lower ledger of $2.160033487\ldots$ and a prebuild ledger of $2.165033487\ldots$, before positive mean margins and caps. This excludes a material improvement by this local substitution on that schedule. New public field heights and cutoff laws require their own estimate.

Keeping rational cofactors smooth only at $B$ in the earlier private levels could avoid increasing their rational rejection cost. It then requires rational pivots for the larger primes too. The existing algebraic incoming measure in G.11 does not control the incidence of numerical factors of $G(P)$ in those earlier rational cofactors. A guarantee for that variant needs a separate rational incidence bound and the cost of the rational pivot searches. General high-rank ordinary pools with multiple large coordinates are outside the triangular comparison.
