# Correlated samples for modular states on the degree ladder

A deterministic list of \(e^{O(H)}\) coefficient tuples can reproduce all bounded-dimensional affine residue states used by the degree ladder. It also matches any individual additive phase of the coefficients to the requested exponential precision. This controls the modular conditions in the score. Preserving the actual two-mark smoothness terms requires a further estimate, even for a short list that matches an arbitrarily prescribed \(e^{O(H)}\) collection of additive phase means. ## One list for all small residue states

Let the active integer coefficient intervals be \(I_j\) of lengths \(L_j\le e^{O(H)}\), with \(J=O(d)\) coordinates. Translate their origins to zero and pad the \(j\)th interval to the next power of two \(Q_j\ge L_j\). Construct an \(\epsilon\)-biased set on \((\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)^J\), where \(Q=\max_j Q_j\), and reduce its \(j\)th coordinate modulo \(Q_j\). A nontrivial character of the product of the smaller cyclic groups pulls back to a nontrivial character. The construction has size polynomial in \(J\log Q\) and \(\epsilon^{-1}\), and can be enumerated deterministically in polynomial time in these quantities. Keep the points whose representatives all lie in the respective intervals. [Jalan and Moshkovitz, *Near-Optimal Cayley Expanders for Abelian Groups*, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01149)

For any real \(\theta\), the normalized interval weight times the phase \(e(\theta t)\), where \(e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)\), has Fourier absolute sum \(O(\log(2Q_j))\) on the \(j\)th cyclic group. Its Fourier coefficient at \(k\) is the geometric sum
\[
 \frac1{L_j}\sum_{t=0}^{L_j-1}e\big((\theta-k/Q_j)t\big).
\]
Keep the two closest frequencies bounded by one and bound the others by \(O(Q_j/(L_j h))\) at distance index \(h\). Since \(Q_j<2L_j\) for \(L_j>1\), the claimed sum follows. Singleton axes can be removed. The normalized product weight times any coefficient phase \(e(\sum_j\theta_j t_j)\) therefore has Fourier absolute sum at most
\[
                    \prod_j O(\log(2Q_j))=e^{O(d\log(2H))}=e^{O(H)}
\]
on the fixed schedule. The product weight has mean one under the ambient uniform law. Its expectation under the biased list differs by at most \(\epsilon e^{O(H)}\), as does the numerator for any coefficient phase. The weight is constant on the accepted part of the list, so normalizing gives the claimed uniform phase error there. Take \(\epsilon=e^{-C'H}\) with \(C'\) sufficiently large for the desired precision.

A \(k\)-dimensional affine state over \(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z\) with fixed \(k\) has \(M^k\) Fourier phases, each of the preceding form. Thus for all \(M\le e^{O(H)}\) the state distribution of the same list has arbitrarily small error \(e^{-CH}\) in total variation. The state can include row evaluations and derivatives, the pair of coordinates for the marked image in \(B_s/MB_s\), and any fixed number of coefficient residues. This applies to arbitrary affine linear forms, which is stronger here than matching a fixed number of coefficient marginals. Fixed origins and prescribed coefficient progression steps just change the phase frequencies.

For only the finitely many score states, there is also an elementary construction using their exact conditional probabilities from the ladder's state convolution. Index the listed state indicators by \(h\), with probabilities \(p_h\). For \(K\) independent full tuples, the expected sum over \(h\) of squared errors in the empirical probabilities is at most \(\#\{h\}/K\). Fix the entries of the sample tuples by conditional expectation in their original interval product. The conditioned means and second moments in that expression use the single-tuple conditional indicator probabilities, because the different tuple draws are independent. Each coordinate has \(e^{O(H)}\) possible values, so this gives a deterministic list of size and construction cost \(e^{O(H)}\) at error \(e^{-CH}\). This argument requires a conditional evaluator for every listed statistic along the entire fixing process.

## A genuine band obstruction to finite congruence and phase data

Consider an allowed ladder box with two consecutive active degrees, and fix two distinct admitted marks \(a_1,a_2\). Write \(b_i=Z+a_i\) and \(n_i=m-a_i\). For consecutive active degrees \(j,j+1\), the determinant of their evaluation columns, including the common factor \(g\), is
\[
                   g^2n_1n_2 b_1^j b_2^j(a_2-a_1).
\]
Fix any \(e^{O(H)}\) coefficient phase probes, including all the characters of a prescribed menu of small states. The widths can use sufficiently large one-third guards for this menu. Choose \(P=e^{O(H)}\) above the band, \(Z+A\), the small moduli, and large enough that \([P,2P]\) contains more primes than the total number of coefficient frequencies in the probes plus the prime divisors of the displayed determinant. This is possible by the prime number theorem. Put \(\delta=e^{-O(H)}<1/(8P)\), small enough for the desired phase precision.

For a coefficient frequency \(\theta\) with \(\|\theta\|>\delta/P\), there is at most one prime \(p\in[P,2P]\) for which \(\|p\theta\|<\delta\). Indeed, if two distinct such primes gave nearest integers \(u,v\), their cross difference would be an integer of magnitude at most \(4P\delta<1\). The equal fractions \(u/p=v/q\) would then be an integer, contradicting the condition on \(\theta\). Choose a prime \(p\) outside all these exceptions and the determinant. Set all coefficient residues modulo \(p\) to constants, choosing the two consecutive coefficients using the invertible evaluation matrix so that both marked values vanish modulo \(p\). In every coordinate draw uniformly on the resulting progression inside its original interval. Neither marked value can be a band product, since it has a prime divisor above the band. This is a distribution on actual polynomial tuples with the original degree, congruences modulo \(g\), cofactor, and real-root margins.

The new law still matches the listed phase means. If \(\|\theta\|>\delta/P\), the geometric sums on both the original interval and the new progression are \(O(P/(\delta L_j))\), since the progression has \(\asymp L_j/p\) points and \(\|p\theta\|\ge\delta\). If \(\|\theta\|\le\delta/P\), couple the original uniform interval to the selected representative from each complete block of \(p\) integers. The phase varies by \(O(\delta)\) inside the block and the incomplete end costs \(O(p/L_j)\). The guards can ensure that these errors, summed over all coordinates, are at most \(e^{-CH}\). Products of phases compare by telescoping. All retained small moduli are coprime to \(p\), so a direct residue count gives the same result for their bounded-dimensional states. A phase multiplied by a small state indicator can also be expanded into \(e^{O(H)}\) phases before choosing \(p\). The restricted progression box itself admits an explicit \(e^{O(H)}\)-support list preserving any such finite menu by the interval sampling argument above.

The original product box can have simultaneous band probability \(e^{-O(H)}\) at these marks. For instance, take the \(B_0\) stage and choose two unprotected admitted marks at bounded distance inside a cell, using the cell's positive density after removing the \(\ell\)-root classes. In the uniform \(B_0\) law, split the normalized mark pair by the common residue modulo \(a_2-a_1\) and use an interior square panel of relative mass \(e^{-O(H)}\). The resulting two scalar progressions have coprime residues after the fixed exclusions. All primes dividing their bounded difference lie in the original \(P_0\) for sufficiently large inputs. The square-root-margin scalar estimates and the principal saddle term give each progression band density \(e^{-O(H)}\) under Dirichlet RH. The ladder lattice coupling transfers their product with arbitrarily small \(e^{-CH}\) error. Hence matching the finite phase data gives no inference of even a positive two-mark band count on the correlated list. ## What the full band test would need

On one retained mark class, pad the physical scalar interval to a cyclic period \(R_i\) and expand its actual band indicator \(F_i\) into additive Fourier coefficients. The error of a two-mark average with a retained row predicate is a weighted sum over both spectra, with weights \(\widehat F_1(u)\widehat F_2(v)\) and the corresponding masked coefficient phase errors. The elementary bound \(\|\widehat F_i\|_1\le\sqrt{R_i\mathbb EF_i}\) can be exponential on the \(V\) scale, while the list above only controls individual phase errors on the \(H\) scale. A bound on this weighted sum, or a different estimate for true simultaneous band membership on the sampled coefficient tuples, is still needed to transfer the nonnegative score. The forbidden-prime example concerns the information in the specified probes. It leaves arithmetic estimates that use the joint smooth-product structure available.
